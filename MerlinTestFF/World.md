# World

## Time and place

The trilogy takes place in the founding years of Hogwarts, in an early medieval Britain inspired by the late tenth century. The date is a reference for building the setting. The story leaves the year unstated and uses fictional rulers and borders. Familiar names such as York, Wessex, Winchester, and London keep the geography readable. People in the story refer to places and communities by their own names, and the narration uses **the South** and **the North** for the wider regions. *England* and *Scotland* are reserved for the historical frame or a possible epilogue.

### The South

The South is a recently consolidated, relatively centralized kingdom that is expanding beyond its existing borders. The crown identifies young witches and wizards and can train them for military service. Their families may gain protection, resources, or privileges in exchange for loyalty and service. The same arrangement can give a family a place of safety and give the crown a claim on what their child does with their magic.

**Morgana leads a coven and advises the southern king.** The king supplies soldiers, resources, and royal protection. Morgana brings magical expertise and people who can serve in his army. Other members of the coven may advise nobles and gather information across several courts. London is a possible base, and *Queen of London* is a possible title among magical people. The king and Morgana each have their own ambitions and reasons to value the alliance. A family driven out by a local lord could genuinely benefit from Morgana's protection and still have to decide whether to accept its terms.

### The North

The North is a patchwork of villages, clans, regional communities, and possibly one or two small kingdoms. Local leaders govern their own people, with no single authority across the region. Neighbours share some customs and cultural ties and can also feud or compete. A community might resist southern expansion, negotiate, pay tribute, or accept an alliance. The four founders have personal origins and loyalties of their own, still open for development.

Southern pressure could bring some northern communities together over time. A possible epilogue looks across generations to the kingdom eventually called **Scotland** and the persistence of its distinct identity. Some areas may be drawn into the southern kingdom while others forge closer ties with one another. The school and the war are part of the backdrop to that longer history.

### Ireland and Scandinavia

Ireland and the Scandinavian territories sit outside the main North–South conflict. Ireland has competing Gaelic kingdoms and Norse-influenced coastal settlements. Trade, travel, settlement, and warfare tie Scandinavian communities to northern Britain. These connections can shape individual characters, settlements, and alliances as the story needs them.

## The founding of Hogwarts

**Godric starts the school. Godric, Rowena, Helga, and Salazar found Hogwarts together.** Godric leaves the South after seeing young magical people trained for the crown's wars. He starts a small, independent school in the North where they can learn magic without being pressed into service. He is a formidable fighter and can teach students how to defend themselves. His objection is to conscripting children. The other three join later, each with their own reasons. Their disagreements about admission, ancestry, security, and what the school owes its students and neighbours become harder to resolve as war approaches.

The school has its own relationship with the surrounding Highland communities. Who helps it settle there, what the founders promise their neighbours, and the reason for the precise site are open. The independence that makes Hogwarts a refuge also leaves it responsible for negotiating its own safety.

The founders seek out young people who show signs of magic and can take them on personally. The first cohorts can include students of different ages, with Merlin arriving around thirteen or fourteen. An early house can grow out of a particular founder agreeing to teach a student. The Sorting Hat can be made during the founders' lifetime while they still choose pupils themselves. The Book of Admittance and Quill of Acceptance are in place by the time the castle is complete. The Hogwarts Pensieve predates the school, and the account that the founders found it at the site is an in-world legend. ## Magic in the world

### Power, intention, and cost

Magical ability is innate and can take many forms. Strong emotion can bring it to the surface in someone who has yet to learn deliberate casting. A frightened child might want to escape and tear the latch out of a door. With training, a caster can direct magic toward a particular result. A wand helps focus it, and an incantation gives the intention a repeatable form. Casting without speaking and casting without a wand are separate skills, and an exceptional caster can master both. Some spells also require a particular feeling or motive.

One person can channel only so much power at once. Even a rested, determined caster cannot lift an entire mountain. Casting also demands effort, and fatigue makes it harder to sustain a spell or keep it precise. Training improves how much power a caster can use reliably and how efficiently they use it. See [[Power system]] for the casting rules and examples.

### Working model for the source of magic

**Magic is a form of energy already present in the world.** The working rule is that casting transfers or transforms energy. A witch or wizard is born able to draw on that energy, and individual magical power is how much they can channel and control. A wand helps direct the flow. The strain comes from channeling and controlling magic, so a caster can tire while plenty of energy remains available around them.

Lifting a stone transfers energy into raising it. Once the stone rests on a ledge, the caster can stop. Holding it suspended can continue to demand effort because the caster has to maintain the effect. For ordinary casting, ambient magic can be plentiful enough for a lesson to exhaust the students without exhausting the room. A possible way to make large enchantments is to gather and bind energy gradually. The founders might enchant a castle together over months, within the limits of what each can channel at one time. Whether heavy use can deplete magic locally and how bound energy is stored or released are open.

### Spell languages and traditions

Magic and wand use in Britain reach back beyond Roman influence in Rowling's account. The Ollivanders' own tradition credits ancestors arriving with the Romans and selling wands to British wizards who already made their own. Uagadou gives a canonical example of a tradition that teaches deliberate magic through gestures. The Latin, mixed, and non-Latin incantations in the books leave room for several traditions of spellcasting. [Ollivander](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/mr-ollivander) · [Uagadou](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/uagadou) · [Spell etymology](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/the-etymology-of-harry-potter-spells)

One option is for the founders to use Latin to record and share some repeatable spells. Some formulations could have arrived with the Romans, and others could have been written down later. Regional traditions could pass on related magic through local words, gestures, or demonstration. A founder might teach an incantation for an effect a student already recognizes from home. Hogwarts gives those methods a place to meet.

### Taboos and old magic

The founders' era has no British Ministry of Magic, modern Auror office, or International Statute of Secrecy. The later dates are **1692** for the Statute taking effect, **1707** for the Ministry, and **1717** for the formal classification of the Unforgivable Curses. In the trilogy, what people teach, forbid, or tolerate can depend on a school, household, community, or court. Feared magic and local taboos can already exist. Herpo the Foul's basilisk and Horcrux are precedents for dark practices long before Hogwarts. [Statute of Secrecy](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/some-rules-about-magic-its-important-to-bear-in-mind) · [Ministry](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/ministers-for-magic) · [Unforgivable Curses](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/event/unforgivable-curses-made-illegal/) · [Herpo the Foul](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/character/herpo-the-foul/)

The invention dates of **Imperius, Cruciatus, and the Killing Curse** are open, so any of them could be known by the founders or Morgana's coven. Imperius controls actions and can be resisted. Cruciatus requires a genuine desire to cause pain. The Killing Curse requires enough power to cast and has no ordinary counter-curse, though it can miss or strike an intervening object. Which curses appear in the story and what people call them are open. [The three curses](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/spells/the-unforgivable-curses) · [Killing Curse in the novels](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/magic/killing-curse/)

The ancient **Fidelius Charm** gives the setting a precedent for magic that depends on a voluntary choice. Imperius cannot force a Secret Keeper to disclose the protected information. Magical vows and willing sacrifice offer other precedents for magic whose effect depends on the circumstances and commitments involved. Prophecies can go unfulfilled, and acting on a prophecy can create the conditions for it to come true. The precise magic behind Merlin and Arthur's return is open. See [[Merlin-Brainstorm]] for the prophecy, the promise to return, and Merlin's memory arc. [Secret Keeper](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/secret-keeper) · [Unbreakable Vow](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/magic/unbreakable-vow/) · [Trelawney's first prophecy](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/magical-miscellany/sybill-trelawneys-first-prophecy)

### Other limits and precedents from canon

- Food can be summoned, transformed, or increased from what already exists. Creating edible food from nothing is one of the limits described by Gamp's Law. The story can use the limit without assuming anyone in the founders' era already names it that way. [Gamp's Law](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/gamps-law-of-elemental-transfiguration/)
- Magic can prolong a life, preserve or alter memories, and leave an imprint of someone who has died. Returning the dead to ordinary life remains beyond the magic demonstrated in the books. The Pensieve, magical vows, and soul magic each offer a different precedent for the cycle. Its exact mechanism is still a decision for the trilogy. [Pensieve](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/pensieve) · [Limits of healing and resurrection](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/illness-and-disability) · [Horcruxes](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/objects/horcrux)
- House-elves and goblins have magical practices of their own. House-elves can Apparate where human casters cannot, and Godric's goblin-made sword gives the school a direct connection to goblin craft. Brooms are already available. Floo powder is a later invention, dated to the thirteenth century. [House-elves](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/creatures/house-elf) · [Godric's sword](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/the-sword-of-gryffindor) · [Floo powder](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/floo-powder)

## Flying brooms and games

Flying brooms and broom racing already fit the era. *Quidditch Through the Ages* cites an illustration of broom riders from **962** and places the first annual Swedish broom race in the tenth century. The first surviving account of the game at **Queerditch Marsh** is from the eleventh century. It describes a ball, trees used as goals, and enchanted rocks. The exact date people started playing is open. The live Golden Snidget first enters a match in **1269**, and the enchanted Golden Snitch comes later. [Early flying brooms](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/source/other-potter-books/qa/qa1/) · [Ancient broom games](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/source/other-potter-books/qa/qa2/) · [Queerditch Marsh](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/witches-who-shaped-quidditch) · [The Snitch](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/source/other-potter-books/qa/qa4/)

Early Hogwarts students could play informal games with rules brought from home. One northern precedent is **Creaothceann**, a game in which riders try to catch falling stones in cauldrons strapped to their heads. An eleventh-century Gaelic poem records it, leaving room for an older local tradition. A game with a ball and trees for goals could also foreshadow Quidditch. The particular game played at Hogwarts is open. [Creaothceann](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/creaothceann/)
