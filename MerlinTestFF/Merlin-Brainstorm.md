# Merlin Brainstorm

## The premise

The story begins in the early years of Hogwarts, while the four founders are still looking for students. Merlin joins as a boy with no memory of having lived through the Arthurian era, roughly five centuries earlier. We follow him as he grows into the wizard the world will remember. How he returns is still open. He could be reborn, or Past Merlin could have deliberately reset himself.

## The cycle

Britain is caught in a recurring conflict between a great villain and a hero. A prophecy foretells the villain’s downfall. The villain tries to prevent it and, in doing so, chooses and creates their own nemesis. Acting on the prophecy helps make it come true.

The Arthurian legend and the Hogwarts-era story could be two turns of the cycle. The Harry Potter story could be a later one. The Zelda comparison is about familiar roles and a recurring evil appearing in different eras. In the final reveal we discussed, Harry is Arthur reborn and Voldemort is the latest incarnation of that evil. Harry can already be Arthur reborn when Voldemort’s actions make him the Boy Who Lived.

## The Hogwarts trio

Merlin, Arthur, and Guinevere could form an early mirror of the Golden Trio. Arthur would occupy the Harry role, the boy everyone expects great things from. Merlin would be more like Ron within the group, while remaining the protagonist of this story.

If Arthur and Guinevere also return without their memories, the three can become friends at Hogwarts before they know what they meant to each other in another life. By the time the old plan surfaces, their present-day friendship gives Merlin a personal stake in what he does with it.

Other figures from the Round Table, such as Lancelot, Tristran, and Cadogan, could return as friends or fellow wizards and discover that they were part of the same plan. The mirror could extend to Ron as Merlin reborn and Hermione as Guinevere reborn. The image of the greatest wizard who ever lived coming back as Ron and befriending Arthur again on a train is part of the fun.

## The old plan and the theme

Past Merlin or Arthur may have devised a plan to make their next lives the final turn of the cycle. Young Merlin gradually uncovers it and has to decide whether to follow it. Recovering his old memories could explain a leap in magical ability and might even be something Past Merlin set out to make happen.

> How much power do you give something just because it tells you what comes next?

The villain gives a prophecy power by treating it as something they must act on. Merlin faces a version of the same choice when his past self hands him a plan for his life. By then, young Merlin has his own friendships and reasons for deciding what should happen next.

## A possible ending

Merlin makes a sacrifice believing it will end the cycle. It can still save the people he loves and give them a genuine happy ending. The final line reveals that the cycle eventually begins again.

> And they all lived happily ever after.
>
> Until, nearly a thousand years later, the Boy Who Had Lived was born.

The extra *had* makes Harry’s familiar title a clue to the life he already lived as Arthur.
