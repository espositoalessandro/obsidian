# Merlin Brainstorm

## The premise

The story follows Merlin as a real beginner at Hogwarts, around thirteen or fourteen, among the early students recruited during Hogwarts' founding years, in an unspecified early medieval period. He has no memory of his life in the Arthurian era roughly five centuries earlier and has to learn magic for himself. We eventually follow him into becoming the wizard later generations remember.

He could grow up in a remote coastal village somewhere in Britain. Strange things have already happened around him. One version has him trying to make sense of those signs without knowing he has magic. He could also come from a family of wizards. His ancestry, the village, and what first attracts a founder's attention are open.

Salazar is a strong candidate to come to the village. Merlin ends up in Slytherin because he **convinces Salazar to take him on**. His place in the house starts with a teacher choosing a student for reasons we can show in the scene. One possibility is that Merlin realizes Salazar needs recruits, then talks him into showing him some magic worth leaving home for. Salazar could be impressed that a teenager noticed what he wanted and made a case for himself. Another founder could still be the first to discover Merlin. The invitation gives Merlin a reason to go to Hogwarts that belongs to him before he meets Arthur.

## The South, the North, and Morgana's coven

**Revised political and social premise (November 2026):** Hogwarts is **not** a northern resistance headquarters. The northern kingdoms fight over land, sovereignty, and political independence; Hogwarts is concealed from northern and southern rulers and exists to **preserve magical people and knowledge**. See [[World]] for the full political model and the emerging history of isolation.

The story takes inspiration from late-tenth-century Britain without naming an exact year or faithfully mapping borders. Recognizable places such as York, Wessex, Winchester, London, Alba, Strathclyde, and Moray keep geography intelligible. We use **the South** and **the North** as loose geographic and political shorthand, rather than prematurely calling the two regions *England* and *Scotland*. *Eragon* (Galbatorix) and *The Witcher* (Nilfgaard, politically influential sorceresses) are useful inspiration, not exact world models.

**The South is a centralized, expansionist monarchy** with territorial and administrative ambitions independent of its witches and wizards. Its conventional army remains essential. **Morgana leads a coven and advises the king**, providing magical knowledge, agents, and trained practitioners. London and the informal title *Queen of London* remain possibilities. Morgana's project promises magical people legal protection and tolerance that many lack elsewhere, **in exchange for loyalty, supervision, and potentially compulsory royal service**. Families can benefit genuinely while also being controlled. The king and Morgana are partners, not a ruler and a mere puppet.

**The North has real political and military organization, not just scattered peasants and villages.** **Alba** is the major northern kingdom; **Strathclyde** is another organized but more exposed kingdom; **Moray and the Highland territories** have regional rulers and varying autonomy; Norse and coastal powers have their own interests. This is **not** the abandoned plan to assign four kingdoms to four Hogwarts founders. No single northern monarch governs everyone. Northern powers are strong enough to resist but not necessarily willing to cooperate; their conflicts, terrain, and different interests matter. We do not need four separate royal-court subplots.

**The North's political independence does not make it safe for magical people.** Persecution is inconsistent and localized. Some villages fear, blame, or expel witches and wizards; others value their magic or protect them. No blanket claim that every Muggle hates magic, and no need for systematic modern witch-hunts. This contrast is central: the **North may grant political freedom without personal safety; the South may grant personal safety without political freedom**.

**Magical people are scarce; trained practitioners are scarcer still.** The exact ratio is open (one per thousand is only a tentative possibility). Innate ability can manifest as uncontrolled emotional magic, and many people lack the training needed to use it reliably. The South deliberately trains magical recruits, but even trained casters cannot replace conventional armies. Magic supports battles, intelligence, logistics, healing, and defence; it has costs and limits. In the North, instruction may rely on families, apprentices, independent masters, and local customs, so opportunity varies greatly.

**Godric leaves the South after witnessing magical children trained for royal war.** He begins a hidden, independent place of refuge and teaching. Rowena, Helga, and Salazar join him and **the four together establish Hogwarts**, an institution whose first purpose is the preservation of magical lives and accumulated knowledge. The founders are not appointed representatives of northern kingdoms. **Hogwarts is secret even from northern kings**; a very small circle of trusted people may know of it, and fewer still know its location. Its protections can already be exceptionally strong. It is **not officially involved in the war**, and no climactic siege of Hogwarts is planned. The founders can nevertheless disagree over whether and how to intervene when the war affects students and families.

**Hogsmeade is a promising linked development, not a fully plotted subplot.** Preserve canonical **Hengist of Woodcroft** as its founder, fleeing Muggle persecution. An all-magical settlement may grow near Hogwarts as a refuge for adults and families while Hogwarts shelters and teaches children. Why the locations are close, when the village forms, and how it shares in Hogwarts' concealment are still open. This may be among the earliest examples of a community choosing to live apart from non-magical society.

**Isolation is an emerging direction, not a completed tenth-century international policy.** The trilogy can show the intellectual and social origins of the eventual wizarding world's withdrawal. Persecution encourages concealment; forced service encourages autonomy; the sanctuary provides another way to live. This choice offers safety and exacts costs, especially for those who favour coexistence. The **International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy remains centuries later (1689–1692)**. No single mass memory-erasure of all Muggles is established; selective charms, hidden places, reduced contact, and later formal regulation are possible stages.

**Ireland and Scandinavian territories remain independent background powers.** Ireland has Gaelic kingdoms and Norse-influenced settlements; Scandinavian communities connect to northern Britain through travel, trade, settlement, and war. They need not become separate major plotlines.

### Long-term historical direction

The South's expansion may lead Alba, Strathclyde, and neighbouring rulers to cooperate more closely while preserving their distinct interests. The narrative should not imply northern Britain had no kingdoms or political identity before the war. A distant epilogue may use *Scotland* as a recognizable later name without attributing the creation of Scotland to Hogwarts or claiming southern Britain conquered all of it.

The priority is a usable, textured political background for character choices, not a comprehensive history of every local ruler or campaign.

## The trilogy

**Book one begins inside a hidden sanctuary.** Merlin learns magic, meets the founders and the young cast, develops a relationship with Arthur, and begins to understand the divide between northern persecution and southern royal protection. A local adversary, possibly an agent or emissary of Morgana, remains an option, but should operate through **events outside or around the hidden school** rather than publicly confronting a famous northern resistance institution. The school's location and existence need not become known to the southern crown.

**Book two brings the wider war into the characters' lives, not an official war into Hogwarts.** Students' families, recruitment, persecution, and political pressure can change what the founders believe the school should do. Merlin and Arthur may reach their happiest and closest period before their choices pull them apart. By the end they drift toward opposed positions; the decisive rupture is open.

The founders' disagreements may grow into Salazar and Godric's serious falling-out. **The Chamber of Secrets is present and used as a secret teaching space** for subjects Salazar believes the other founders would forbid; that secrecy may contribute to, but is not yet the settled cause of, the rupture. An earlier plan placed Helena's death, the Bloody Baron's death, and Rowena's final illness here: **that is superseded**. Helena's diadem may already have been stolen when the trilogy begins, while the later deaths happen **outside the books**. A proposed theft concerning Helga remains **an uncommitted idea**, not a required event.

**Book three brings open war outside Hogwarts.** Merlin and Arthur become ideological and personal enemies, Morgana is defeated, and the truth of the recurring prophecy emerges. Arthur's response to victory and his possible pursuit of vengeance matter as much as the military victory. Merlin recovers earlier memories and faces a choice about whether to begin again. The great battle need **not** be another Battle of Hogwarts. A small, unrelated background reveal may casually identify the quiet Hogwarts caretaker as a former baron, foreshadowing the Bloody Baron without depicting his later crimes.

The books can cover several years. Their boundaries follow the emotional and political escalation rather than a fixed school-year formula.

## The prophecy and the cycle

> How much power do you give something just because it tells you what comes next?

A ruler hears a prophecy about someone who could bring them down and takes steps to prevent it. By acting out of fear, they choose their own enemy and give that person a reason to fight them. The prophecy can promise the power to defeat someone and leave open what the person does with that power afterward.

The preferred direction is for **Arthur to occupy the same role each time**. He's the person singled out by the prophecy, including in a later life as Harry. He can defeat the ruler who came after him and then become dangerous himself. Earlier, we tried an Arthurian cycle in which Arthur occupied Voldemort's position and his son Mordred was the child he targeted. That's a genuine parallel in Malory and useful source material. To give Arthur the same role in every life, the fic's version of Camelot would need to adapt that episode or draw on it in a different way. We also talked about a Zelda-like recurrence of heroes and evil. How literally different antagonists share an identity is open.

In the Hogwarts era, **Morgana hears the prophecy and acts against Arthur**. She could be the magical Queen of London, giving her a power base away from Hogwarts. She needs a convincing reason to identify Arthur as the boy in the prophecy. The exact sign, her first move against him, and the prophecy's wording can develop later. We can echo the structure of Trelawney's prophecy, including the idea that trying to ward off a threat helps create it and that either adversary can bring the other down. Morgana and Arthur's conflict can unfold across the trilogy. After Arthur defeats her, there's still a story about what he does with the anger and fear that brought him that far. Vengeance is one possibility. His descent should feel like a series of choices and have more to it than a hunger for power.

For the Harry Potter parallel, Voldemort learns the opening of a single prophecy through Snape. It speaks of a child born at the end of July to parents who had defied him three times. Harry and Neville both fit that description. Voldemort chooses Harry, and the part he missed says he'll mark the child as his equal and that one will die at the other's hand. Harry learns the full prophecy in his fifth year. His willingness to walk into the forest and die gives the fic an existing answer to the cycle's question about what the chosen boy does when it matters. [Trelawney's first prophecy](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/magical-miscellany/sybill-trelawneys-first-prophecy), [Neville and the prophecy](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/what-if-neville-was-the-chosen-one).

## Merlin's memories and the choice to try again

A working explanation for their return is that Merlin and Arthur made a promise in an earlier life to try again if they failed. Arthur may have died after a conflict with Morgana, leaving Merlin to carry out their plan alone. Merlin might have bound himself, Arthur, and some of the people of Camelot to keep returning. Their circumstances and ages when they meet can vary. They could have met as adults in an earlier era, with Arthur possibly a Roman commander, and meet as students in this one. Their bond is deep each time and can take different forms.

**Merlin eventually recovers the memories of his previous lives in every cycle and can choose whether to begin again.** He remembers all the different relationships he has had with Arthur and why each of those lives gave him a reason to try again. He may also recover knowledge that changes what he can do with magic. How the return works, what happens to his powers, how many lives he remembers, and how his memories come back are open. Our starting point is a young Merlin living this life as his own.

At Hogwarts, Merlin ultimately chooses to let go. The fact that he remembers, or that he remembers earlier, is insufficient to explain that decision. **Something about this life has to change what he chooses.** We’re leaving that difference open so it can grow out of writing the characters. An earlier idea was for Arthur to ask Merlin to let him go. A conversation like that could belong to the ending if the story earns it, but it cannot substitute for something materially different about this life.

One possible result is that Merlin releases the people of Camelot from the spell that keeps bringing them together under familiar names and circumstances. He gives up the certainty of another life in which he can find Arthur and try to save him. The old pattern of a prophecy and a ruler acting out of fear can still recur. Harry grows up as Harry Potter, and what he chooses to do with his life is how the pattern finally ends. Merlin's sacrifice, and what that sacrifice might be, are still open.

## Merlin and Arthur

The relationship is important across their lives, and the Hogwarts story can grow into a romance between them. The opening can give each boy his own reasons to be at Hogwarts, his own friendships, and his own problems with the school. **Gon and Killua in Hunter × Hunter** are a reference for two boys whose bond develops while they have their own reasons to take on an adventure. Gon is also a reference for how seemingly innocent qualities can coexist with rage and a capacity to frighten the people who love him. Killua brings a darker history and a different kind of composure. Those dynamics are inspiration for Merlin and Arthur, and their backgrounds and personalities are ours to develop. Harvey Dent in *The Dark Knight* is another reference for a man whose real virtues can coexist with a turn toward vengeance.

Merlin and Arthur could dislike each other at first. A rivalry that grows into trust and then romance is on the table, including an enemies-to-lovers direction if it feels right once they're on the page. The friendship needs a reason of its own in this life. One possible turning point is Arthur standing up for Merlin when he is treated unfairly, then telling him privately what he still thinks Merlin did wrong. Merlin could respect the honesty and seek him out again. What actually earns their trust is open.

Their arcs can **cross**. Merlin starts out comfortable with choices Arthur would balk at. He might have little hesitation about using lethal force, though his actual history and limits are open. He remains ambitious and interested in power as he grows more thoughtful about what he does with it. Arthur can have recognizably Gryffindor qualities, including courage, directness, and conviction. Later on, those qualities can coexist with increasingly frightening choices. Both need depth beyond their house and their broad direction of change.

They gradually move toward common ground and might get together during a genuine honeymoon period, when they enjoy each other's company and feel like they're on the same side. Then they slowly separate in what they're willing to do, even if the love is still there. They could partly teach each other the lessons that set those changes in motion. One possible later beat is Arthur defending a choice with an argument he learned from Merlin and expecting him to understand. The crossing is an arc to explore, with room to discover what changes each of them and how much of themselves they keep.

## Founders' Hogwarts

Godric begins a **hidden sanctuary** for children at risk of persecution or conscription; Rowena, Helga, and Salazar expand it into Hogwarts. Its purpose is **preservation and education**, not directing a northern rebellion. The founders and the school are politically independent, and Hogwarts' existence and location are unknown to the great majority of both northern and southern society. The four founders do not represent four kingdoms. They can admit students of different ages, including teenagers, and the trilogy may span several school years. Their exact recruitment system, size, supporting staff, and the rules for keeping parents informed remain to be designed.

Professor Binns says the founders sought out young people who showed signs of magic and brought them to the castle. Rowling's supplementary account places the Book of Admittance and the Quill of Acceptance at the completed castle in the founders' lifetime. The Sorting Hat's song says the founders originally chose students themselves and enchanted Godric's hat to keep choosing after they were gone. The Hat can exist in this era and the founders can still take students on personally. House membership can begin as a relationship with the founder who chooses to teach you. Merlin belongs to Slytherin. Arthur as a Gryffindor is a fitting direction. [The Book and Quill](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/the-quill-of-acceptance-and-the-book-of-admittance), [the Sorting Hat](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/the-sorting-hat).

- **Salazar Slytherin** values ambition and resourcefulness and increasingly argues that magical education should remain with wholly magical families. Fear of persecution, betrayal, and the danger of exposing a hidden school can inform—but not justify—his distrust of Muggle-born pupils. He privately teaches a selection of students in **the Chamber of Secrets**, including magic the other founders would not approve. This is a meaningful story location and possible factor in his break with Godric. The basilisk's introduction and the precise reason for the final quarrel remain open. [The origins of Hogwarts](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/origins-of-hogwarts-school-of-witchcraft-and-wizardry).
- **Godric Gryffindor** valued courage and was an accomplished duellist. The goblin smith and king Ragnuk made Godric's sword to his specifications. Rowling cites Excalibur as an inspiration for the sword. Godric and Salazar were close friends in the school's early days. The collapse of a friendship between people who built something together could be part of the school story. [The Sword of Gryffindor](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/the-sword-of-gryffindor), [the Hat's song in Book Five](https://www.cjvlang.com/Hpotter/unicode/hatsong5.html).
- **Rowena Ravenclaw** values intelligence and learning and is close to Helga. Her daughter Helena has **likely already stolen the diadem and fled** before our opening, a private hurt we need only hint at. **Rowena is not dying during the trilogy.** The future Bloody Baron appears as the school's quiet, unobtrusive caretaker, and his earlier noble title may be mentioned casually in Book III. Helena's murder and his suicide belong to the canonical future, not to scenes we plan to dramatize. [Ravenclaw's diadem](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/objects/ravenclaws-diadem).
- **Helga Hufflepuff** was willing to teach all the students and treat them equally. She was known for food magic, some of her recipes survived in the Hogwarts kitchens, and official lore credits her with providing refuge for house-elves. [Helga Hufflepuff](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/characters-and-pets/helga-hufflepuff).

The **Room of Requirement** is understood in our story as an unforeseen result of Hogwarts' interlocking enchantments. It can exist without a founder having deliberately built it, and it need not be more than a passing reference. The castle is exceptionally well protected and can still have secrets beyond its creators' understanding. **Hogsmeade** may grow close by as Hengist of Woodcroft's refuge for persecuted magical families. Hogwarts' own secrecy may foreshadow the much later international turn toward isolation.

The Ministry of Magic dates to 1707, the Wizards' Council is attested by 1269, and the Unforgivable Curses were formally classified in 1717. The founders' era gives us room to write magical Britain without a modern Ministry, Auror office, or shared legal category of Dark wizard. Later sources call the ancient Herpo the Foul a Dark wizard, so feared practices can already exist. The founders can bring different customs and taboos into Hogwarts and disagree about what should be taught or how magic should be used. A spell that binds someone to an oath could be accepted in one court and condemned at the school. What Merlin and Arthur grew up believing about magic can come into play here. [Ministers for Magic](https://www.harrypotter.com/writing-by-jk-rowling/ministers-for-magic), [history of the Ministry](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/2003/07/01/history-of-the-ministry/), [the Unforgivable Curses](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/event/unforgivable-curses-made-illegal/), [Herpo the Foul](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/character/herpo-the-foul/).

## Arthurian material and the wider cast

Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* is a useful reference for Camelot. The Arthurian-era story can stay closer to the medieval legends, while the Hogwarts-era characters get fresh circumstances. Morgana can be Arthur's half-sister in the earlier life and meet him as a stranger in the later one. In Geoffrey of Monmouth she's a healer associated with Avalon. In Malory she tries to put her lover Accolon on the throne by giving him Excalibur and its scabbard and setting him against Arthur. Later she takes the scabbard that protects Arthur from bleeding and throws it into a lake. Her last appearance is on the barge taking the mortally wounded Arthur to Avalon. Malory leaves her later fate open. Arthur's reported burial and the hope of healing leave room for different readings of his end. Merlin could have good reason to fear Morgana and still have to make sense of her being the one who comes for Arthur at the end. [Morgan le Fay](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/theme/morgan.html), [Arthur's departure for Avalon](https://www.telelib.com/words/authors/M/MaloryThomas/prose/lemortedarthur/lemortedarthur_2105.html).

**Mordred** is Arthur's son by his half-sister Morgause, a different character from Morgana. In Malory, Merlin tells Arthur about a child born on May Day who will bring him down. Arthur has the noble babies born that day set adrift, and Mordred survives. Years later, while Arthur is fighting Lancelot in France, Mordred falsely announces Arthur's death and seizes the throne. He tries to marry Guinevere, who takes refuge in the Tower of London. Arthur and Mordred deal each other fatal wounds. Mordred is a model for how someone can survive a ruler's fear and still grow into a man whose pursuit of power hurts others. [Malory's May Day episode](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_1/Book_1/Chapter_27), [Arthurian character reference](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/text/frequently-asked-questions.html).

**Guinevere** is Arthur's queen, and the exposure of her affair with **Lancelot** splits Camelot. **Gawain** is Arthur's nephew and the knight of the Green Knight story. **Tristan**, also called **Tristram**, is the knight of the tragedy with Isolde and King Mark. **Kay** is Arthur's foster brother in Malory, and **Bedivere** returns Excalibur to the lake. **Galahad**, Lancelot's son, is the chief Grail knight in Malory, with Percival and Bors also attaining the Grail. These are reference points for deciding who belongs in the fic. Lancelot, Tristram, Cadogan, and others could recur as friends or fellow wizards tied to the old plan. [British Library introduction to Arthur](https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/king-arthur-fable-fact-and-fiction), [Arthurian character reference](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/text/frequently-asked-questions.html).

The earlier idea of Merlin, Arthur, and Guinevere forming a Hogwarts trio is still available. The parallel could eventually extend to **Harry as Arthur, Ron as Merlin, and Hermione as Guinevere**. Harry as Arthur is central to the proposed ending. The full trio mapping and Guinevere's Hogwarts role are possibilities. Each friendship needs to feel like something these people built in this lifetime.

### Names and Wizarding World references

In *Philosopher's Stone*, Ron offers Harry a duplicate Morgana Chocolate Frog card. The early games and old Pottermore describe **Morgana, or Morgan le Fay**, as a medieval Dark sorceress, Arthur's half-sister, and an enemy of Merlin. The licensed Hasbro card adds **Queen of Avalon**, skilled healer, and bird Animagus. Avalon could belong to the earlier lifetime, with London as her seat during the Hogwarts era. The source material leaves room for our version of how she and Merlin became enemies. [Morgana in the novels](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/character/morgan-le-fay/), [the different card biographies](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/2016/12/29/famous-wizard-cards/#morgan-le-fay).

Merlin and Arthur have no established hereditary surnames in the medieval Arthurian tradition. **Ambrosius** is another name Geoffrey of Monmouth gives Merlin. **Pendragon** is traditionally Uther's acquired epithet, and some retellings also apply it to Arthur. Wizarding World material generally uses Merlin and King Arthur.

## A possible ending

A possible historical epilogue could look beyond the immediate conflict to northern powers' later evolution and the persistence of Scotland as a distinct identity. *Scotland* may appear as a familiar later name; **Alba and other organized northern powers already exist during the books**, and Hogwarts is not the origin of Scotland.

Another possible historical echo is Hogwarts' legacy of **magical self-separation**: a hidden school and a nearby refuge such as Hogsmeade help establish a way of living that centuries later becomes formalized under the International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy. The trilogy should not leap ahead and erase magic from every Muggle's memory or establish the Statute centuries early.

> And they all lived happily ever after.
>
> Until, nearly a thousand years later, the Boy Who Had Lived was born.

The extra *had* points to the lives Harry already lived as Arthur. The choice to end the spell can coexist with another prophecy taking hold centuries later. The reader already knows what Harry does when his turn comes. The exact shape of Merlin's immediate ending, including whether the fairy-tale line fits it, can emerge from the story.
