## Innate magic and spellcasting

Witches and wizards are born with magical power. The same power can take many forms. Strong emotions can bring it to the surface before a person has learned to cast deliberately. The effect may follow an immediate desire, but its form is unpredictable. A frightened child who wants to escape might tear the latch out of a door without meaning to.

Deliberate casting channels magic through an intention. A wand focuses the magic, and an incantation gives the intention a precise, repeatable form. Some spells also require a particular feeling or motive.

Casting without speaking and casting without a wand are separate skills. An exceptional witch or wizard can do both, especially with familiar magic. A wand and incantation can still help an experienced caster with unfamiliar or delicate work.

## Limits and fatigue

- **There is a limit to how much power one person can channel at once.** Intention directs the power they can bring to a spell. Lifting an entire mountain exceeds what a single caster can channel, even at full strength.
- **Casting takes effort.** Demanding spells and prolonged casting can leave a person physically exhausted. Fatigue makes it harder to keep a spell precise or sustain it. A wizard might catch a falling beam for a few breaths and struggle to hold it until everyone gets clear.
- **Training improves control and usable power.** A skilled wizard can channel more of their power reliably and achieve more with less effort. Instead of holding the whole beam in the air, they might shift one end onto a pillar and let it bear the weight.
