# Characters

> Character bible for the Merlin / early-Hogwarts trilogy. See [[Merlin-Brainstorm]], [[World]], and [[Power system]] for the wider plot, politics, and magic.
>
> **Status key:** **Established** = a decision for this story; **Working direction** = a preferred but changeable idea; **Open** = not decided. Canonical future events are identified separately from what the trilogy will actually depict.
>
> **Continuity note (21 November 2026):** Our latest discussions supersede an older possibility in [[Merlin-Brainstorm]] in which Helena's death, the Bloody Baron's death, and Rowena's final illness occurred in Book II. They **do not need to happen during the trilogy**. The caretaker is a minor living character; his former title may be revealed casually in Book III. Likewise, a theft involving Helga is still only an old possibility, not a required character arc.

## Present cast — central characters

### Merlin
**Established:** Protagonist. A boy of roughly thirteen or fourteen among Hogwarts' first cohorts, with no conscious memory of his earlier Arthurian life. He genuinely begins as a student: no instant mastery of magic, no awareness of a grand plan, and no reliance on having already lived. He becomes a **Slytherin** through Salazar's interest in him; our preferred recruitment scene has Merlin **convince Salazar to accept him** rather than passively receiving an invitation. His initiative and ambition should be visible from the beginning.

Merlin is not a Harry-like pure-hearted hero. He is curious, ambitious, power-conscious, and initially more comfortable than Arthur with morally harsh choices. He may be willing to kill when he believes it necessary, though the precise circumstances and personal boundaries remain **open**. He can admire Salazar's knowledge and teaching without ultimately agreeing with his exclusionary ideology. The Chamber of Secrets, where Salazar teaches selected students privately, is a natural place for their relationship to develop; whether Merlin is invited and what he learns there remain scene-level decisions.

**Established arc:** Merlin and Arthur form the emotional core of the trilogy. Their bond develops from independent lives and possible friction into trust and romance. Merlin moves toward greater care, restraint, and self-examination, even as he remains ambitious. He eventually recovers memories of earlier lives and recognizes a recurrent pattern of prophecy, chosen heroes, threatened rulers, and self-fulfilling violence. Remembering the past is **not** itself enough to solve the cycle. His final choice must arise from something this life changes in him.

**Working direction:** Merlin has repeatedly chosen to begin again because of a promise or bond with Arthur. Ultimately he may release that cycle, accepting that he cannot keep manufacturing another chance to save Arthur. The mechanism, precise sacrifice, and ending are **open**.

**Open:** Family and ancestry (magical or non-magical), birthplace (a remote coastal village is one option), first manifestation of magic, who initially discovers him, the exact recovery of memories, and the limits of his past-life knowledge. 'Ambrosius' is a historical/literary name for earlier Merlin, not yet his fixed surname in this life.

### Arthur
**Established:** Merlin's principal counterpart, love interest, and eventual ideological antagonist. **Gryffindor** is our chosen House direction. He begins with recognizable courage, conviction, openness, and a stronger instinct than Merlin to reject ruthless measures. He should not be flatly innocent or morally infallible; the virtues that make him admirable can also make his later convictions frightening.

**Established arc:** Arthur and Merlin grow toward one another, potentially move through a real period of happiness as a couple, and then gradually drift apart as the war intensifies. By Book III they become enemies, without the relationship having ceased to matter. Their moral trajectories cross: Merlin grows more thoughtful about power, while Arthur becomes increasingly capable of justifying violent or unforgiving choices. Arthur's fall should come through decisions, loss, fear, and vengeance—not a sudden personality reversal or simple hunger for power. Harvey Dent is a tonal reference; Gon/Killua are references for the development of the boys' bond, **not** prescribed character copies.

**Established story function:** Morgana hears a prophecy relating to Arthur and acts against him. The attempt to prevent the prophecy helps create the very adversary she fears. Arthur will be instrumental in Morgana's eventual defeat; what he does afterward matters just as much as defeating her.

**Working direction:** Arthur may be the recurring 'chosen' figure across lives, with Harry Potter occupying an analogous role much later. In an earlier Arthurian cycle he may himself have been the ruler who feared a prophesied child, Mordred. How literally those identities, lives, and cycles connect is **open**.

**Open:** Parentage and home, childhood experience, why he comes to Hogwarts, his first relationship with Merlin, the moment their values divide decisively, and the precise act that makes them enemies. 'Pendragon' is an Arthurian name/epithet, not necessarily a hereditary surname for the Hogwarts-era boy.

### Morgana (Morgan le Fay)
**Established:** Major antagonist of the present trilogy. Leads a **coven** of witches and serves as counsellor and magical ally to the monarch of the expansionist South. She is not simply the ruler's obedient servant: the king has armies, administration, and political ambitions of his own, while Morgana brings magical power, knowledge, agents, and her own objectives.

The alliance has a genuine appeal. Morgana can offer witches and wizards protection from hostile local authorities, refuge, and status—but southern protection also creates obligations to the crown, including military service and the training of magical children as instruments of war. She should be convincing to some magical families, not merely an obviously deceitful villain. Her coven's members may be embedded in noble courts.

**Established arc:** Morgana is the first in this cycle to hear or act on a prophecy concerning Arthur. Her attempts to avert it make Arthur her enemy. She is defeated in Book III, but her defeat does **not** automatically end Arthur's danger or the larger pattern of prophecy and revenge.

**Working direction:** London may be her political base, and other magical people may refer to her as the **Queen of London**. Her relationship to her earlier self and what she remembers are **open**. In the older Arthurian life she may have been Arthur's half-sister; in this life she and Arthur begin as strangers.

**Open:** Exact origin, powers, coven membership, place in the court, prophecy wording, method for identifying Arthur, and whether/how she understands previous lives. 'Morgan le Fay' may function as an alternate or historical name.

### The southern monarch — name undecided
**Established role:** The ruler of a newly consolidated, expansionist southern kingdom (not a historical English king named as fact). Controls military and civic power, and allies with Morgana for magical expertise. His court increasingly recruits and trains magical children for service. He pursues his **own** political ambitions and should not be reduced to Morgana's puppet.

**Open:** Name, age, dynasty, temperament, private relationship with Morgana, military policies, and fate. The kingdom and its borders are fictionalized rather than mapped directly onto a historical reign.

## Present cast — Hogwarts founders

### Godric Gryffindor
**Established:** **Godric starts the original independent school** after leaving the South, where he saw young witches and wizards being prepared for compulsory royal service. Rowena, Helga, and Salazar subsequently join him; **all four together found Hogwarts**. Godric is an exceptional fighter and duellist, not a pacifist. He opposes the conscription and instrumentalization of children, not the teaching of defensive magic. His original school becomes **a concealed sanctuary from both royal control and local persecution of magical people**. Hogwarts is not a northern military alliance or resistance headquarters; its existence and location remain secret to most northern and southern rulers. **Preservation of magical lives and knowledge** is the institution's first purpose.

Godric values courage, directness, and the defence of others. His House is Arthur's natural place. The famous goblin-made sword belongs to his traditional lore. He was once especially close to Salazar; their friendship and shared project make the eventual split more painful.

**Working direction:** In Book II he and Salazar may quarrel so severely that the conflict becomes physical. They may disagree over which magic ought to be taught, what protection requires, and whose children deserve an education; the war increases pressure on these disputes. Godric may feel torn between maintaining the school's secrecy and intervening against suffering outside, without making Hogwarts formally join the war.

**Open:** Birthplace, social standing, early allies, precise location of the original small school, and the incident that finally destroys his friendship with Salazar. He need not symbolize a separate northern kingdom.

### Salazar Slytherin
**Established:** Hogwarts co-founder, teacher and selector of Merlin, and the founder most associated with ambition, resourcefulness, and a strong belief in magical ancestry. He must be a **formidable, genuinely compelling teacher**, not a caricature from the beginning. Merlin can learn a great deal from him and understand some of his fears before recognizing the damage of his ideology. In a world where many Muggles fear magic and Hogwarts must remain hidden, Salazar can point to real dangers of betrayal or exposure, but those fears do not justify denying Muggle-born children protection or education.

**Established for our story:** **The Chamber of Secrets exists and will be used.** It begins, or at least functions, as a secret place where Salazar privately teaches selected pupils magic the other founders would not approve of. This teaching gives the Chamber a real purpose in the story. It can become one element in his eventual fallout with Godric, but **the Chamber alone is not the settled cause** of the rupture.

**Canon/future trajectory:** Salazar increasingly distrusts Muggle-born students and wishes to limit access to magical education; he eventually leaves Hogwarts. The Basilisk becomes part of the Chamber's dangerous legacy. Our preferred approach allows the Chamber to develop from private classroom or headquarters into something more threatening as Salazar's position hardens.

**Open:** His ancestry and place of origin, precisely what lessons he teaches, when and how he built the Chamber, whether Merlin attends those lessons, when the Basilisk is installed, how the other founders discover the secret, and the final trigger for his departure.

### Rowena Ravenclaw
**Established:** Co-founder celebrated for intelligence, learning, and wisdom; close to Helga. Her enchanted **diadem** is associated with heightened wisdom. Her daughter is **Helena Ravenclaw**, later known as the Grey Lady.

**Current preferred continuity:** By Merlin's arrival, **Helena has already stolen the diadem and fled**, leaving Rowena with a private loss that she does not openly explain. We might merely notice that Rowena never wears the famous diadem, hear a fragment of a conversation, or learn of the theft much later. The characters do **not** need to witness Helena's theft and her death; indeed, we prefer not to tell that complete tragedy.

**Important:** **Rowena is not dying during the trilogy.** In canonical later events she sends the future Bloody Baron to retrieve Helena when she is near death, but that encounter can occur **after the books**. It is not part of the planned Book II founder crisis.

**Open:** How Rowena handles the loss, what she tells the other founders, how much she knows about the caretaker's past, and whether Merlin ever learns the truth.

### Helga Hufflepuff
**Established:** Co-founder who emphasizes fairness, generosity, and teaching students irrespective of ancestry. Skilled in food-related magic; some of her preparations survive through Hogwarts' kitchens. She provides refuge and work for **house-elves** at the school. Her close friendship with Rowena can show the founders as people with private loyalties, rather than only abstract House ideals.

Her values differ from Salazar's more exclusive approach to admissions. Her willingness to accept and support all students is essential to the institution Godric began.

**Open:** Her exact origin, family, political position, contribution to the wider war, and response to the breakup among the founders. An older brainstorm proposed a **robbery involving Helga** during Book II, but no object, perpetrator, or plot is established; **do not treat this as a locked event**.

## Present cast — recurring and minor characters

### Hogwarts caretaker — the future Bloody Baron (personal name undecided)
**Established:** A **minor recurring side character**, comparable in narrative prominence to Argus Filch in the original books. He is the **living Hogwarts caretaker** during Merlin's school years: quiet, reserved, taciturn, and not particularly hostile to students. He may appear in corridors, tend to the castle, or perform small practical tasks, but he **does not receive a dedicated subplot** or become a prominent tragic viewpoint character.

**Working backstory:** He was once a baron or nobleman who relinquished his title, family, and former place in society out of devotion to **Helena Ravenclaw**. Helena left, stealing the diadem and fleeing without him. This was his choice; his sacrifices do not create a claim on Helena's affection. His own temperament and the depth of his attachment can remain largely unseen.

**Established reveal plan:** Only **casually, around Book III**, someone (possibly Rowena) mentions that the caretaker *used to be a baron* and gave up everything for someone he loved. No theatrical unmasking, no explanation of what readers are meant to infer, and **no portrayal of Helena's murder or his suicide within the trilogy**. Readers who know Harry Potter may recognize him; others just learn a sad fragment of a background character's life.

**Canon/future:** Later, when Rowena is dying, she sends him to find Helena. He kills Helena after she refuses to return, then takes his own life and becomes the Bloody Baron, carrying chains in remorse. That future is **not** a story we need to dramatize.

**Open:** Personal name, specific duties, why Rowena employs him, when Helena left, and the exact wording of the Book III allusion. Do **not** casually call him 'the Bloody Baron' in the current-era narration.

### Helena Ravenclaw — future Grey Lady
**Established identity:** Daughter of Rowena Ravenclaw; later the **Grey Lady**, the ghost associated with Ravenclaw House. She steals her mother's diadem and flees; in the later canonical tragedy the Baron finds and murders her.

**Current preferred use:** **Absent or peripheral**, with the theft already behind us when the trilogy begins. Her story is primarily an unseen wound in Rowena and the caretaker's lives; it should not grow into a major subplot merely because readers know the canon. We might never meet Helena directly. Her death is not planned for our books.

**Open:** Whether she appears in person or only by reference, the amount of information readers receive, and precisely when she departed.

### Morgana's coven — individual members unnamed
**Established collective role:** Morgana leads witches who offer magical expertise, information, and political influence across the southern kingdom. Some may advise noble families or act as agents. They can represent the benefits *and* costs of court patronage.

**Open:** Individual characters, hierarchy, internal disagreements, powers, and personal loyalties. No member's name or arc has been approved yet.

### A southern emissary / local antagonist — unnamed
**Working direction, not yet cast:** Book I may feature an emissary or agent connected to Morgana, whose presence gives the story a contained conflict **outside or on the fringes of the secret school**. The agent should not casually know Hogwarts exists or where to find it. This person might genuinely offer northern magical families protection under the southern king, allowing Merlin and Arthur to understand why some people accept the bargain.

**Open:** Identity, allegiance, objectives, whether this is a member of the coven, and whether the character survives or recurs.

### Hengist of Woodcroft — Hogsmeade's founder (appearance undecided)
**Canonical figure / working worldbuilding direction:** Founder of Hogsmeade, a magical settlement traditionally linked to his flight from Muggle persecution. Our preferred explanation connects Hogsmeade's growth near Hogwarts to the same need for refuge, but **does not** make the four founders replace Hengist as its founder.

**Possible function:** A refugee or organizer who establishes a home for adult witches, wizards, and families near the hidden castle, complementing Hogwarts' mission to preserve and teach magical children. The village may gradually become a significant all-magical community. He does **not** need to become a prominent character, or even to appear directly, if the story works better with the village remaining a background development.

**Open:** His age, personality, whether he meets the founders, when Hogsmeade begins, how the settlement protects its secrecy, and whether Merlin encounters him.

### Peeves — potential background presence
**Optional:** Canonical Hogwarts poltergeist. A possibility raised during castle worldbuilding is that he was already present near the school's foundation, emerging from the unruly magical atmosphere of a school full of children. This can parallel the Room of Requirement as an unintended expression of the castle's accumulated enchantments, but **his appearance and origin are not story decisions**.

## Past cast — previous Arthurian life

> The earlier Arthurian era is roughly five centuries before the Hogwarts setting. These characters and relationships provide the history underlying the cycle, but **a traditional legendary role does not automatically mean the same person must appear as a Hogwarts student**. The mechanism of return—rebirth, memory erasure, magical continuity, or something else—is not fixed. What *is* fixed is that present Merlin starts without memories of these lives.

### Merlin Ambrosius (earlier Merlin)
**Established conceptual role:** The previous Merlin is one of the central figures bound up with Arthur and the recurring attempt to escape prophecy's destructive cycle. He may have arranged, continued, or accepted a magical process that allows them to meet again, deliberately leaving the next Merlin without memories.

**Working direction:** With each recurrence Merlin later remembers former lives and must decide whether to continue the pattern. A promise with Arthur, followed by Merlin surviving or losing him, may be the emotional origin of the decision to try again.

**Open:** The precise relationship to Merlin's present body and identity, how much power and knowledge persists, his role in Camelot's destruction, the exact old promise, and what 'Ambrosius' signifies.

### Arthur Pendragon (earlier King Arthur)
**Established conceptual role:** The earlier king whose bond with Merlin is central to the repeating story. The Arthur of the legends need not be identical in temperament to the Hogwarts boy, though the emotional and moral patterns echo across lives.

**Working direction:** In one older cycle Arthur may have become the very sort of fearful ruler he initially opposed, acting against a child mentioned in a prophecy and creating a future enemy. This is a model for the Hogwarts Arthur's eventual transformation.

**Open:** Earlier relationship to Merlin, actions against Mordred, timing and manner of death, and whether the Pendragon name is used in-story as a title or name.

### Morgan le Fay (earlier Morgana)
**Established conceptual role:** The Arthurian counterpart of the present Morgana, with a complicated history as Arthur's enemy and a figure linked to Avalon. The traditional story allows her to be Arthur's half-sister and an opponent of Merlin; she is not necessarily identical to her current self in motives or knowledge.

**Working direction:** The folklore of healing, magical power, political manipulation, and Avalon can complicate a simple good/evil interpretation. Her role in Arthur's final passage to Avalon is available material, not a fixed scene.

**Open:** How she relates to the present Morgana, whether she returns consciously, and her exact relationship to the first failed cycle.

### Mordred
**Arthurian reference — relevant to the prophecy theme, not confirmed present-day cast.** In Malory, Arthur is told that a child born on May Day will cause his destruction; he attempts to prevent it, Mordred survives, and the two eventually destroy one another. That is the strongest earlier parallel for the self-fulfilling prophecy: a ruler manufactures an enemy by trying to escape a prediction.

Traditionally Mordred is Arthur's son by **Morgause**, not by Morgana. The fic may adapt the older family relationships to preserve the desired recurring roles. Whether a Mordred counterpart appears during the Hogwarts trilogy is **open**.

### Morgause
**Arthurian reference, not confirmed Hogwarts-era cast.** Arthur's half-sister in Malory and Mordred's mother; **distinct from Morgana**. Include her in the backstory only as needed for the chosen Mordred/Arthur adaptation.

### Guinevere
**Arthurian reference / possible recurring character.** Traditionally Arthur's queen and central to the Camelot/Lancelot rupture. An earlier idea put **Merlin, Arthur, and Guinevere** together as a Hogwarts friendship trio, with a distant echo of Harry, Ron, and Hermione. **Her appearance at Hogwarts is not decided**; do not impose a love triangle on the central Merlin–Arthur romance without a later story decision.

### Lancelot
**Arthurian reference / possible recurring character.** His relationship with Guinevere traditionally contributes to Camelot's rupture. He could reappear in the Hogwarts-era circle as a friend or wizard connected to previous lives, but that remains **optional**, with no assigned House, age, or character arc.

### Other Arthurian names held in reserve
These are reference points from earlier brainstorming, **not an approved Hogwarts ensemble**:

- **Gawain:** Arthur's nephew in legend, associated with the Green Knight.
- **Tristan / Tristram:** Knight associated with Isolde and King Mark; a possible recurring companion.
- **Kay:** Arthur's foster brother in some Arthurian traditions.
- **Bedivere:** Returns Excalibur to the lake in the traditional account.
- **Galahad:** Lancelot's son, prominent in Grail stories.
- **Percival and Bors:** Grail knights in medieval accounts.
- **Cadogan:** A name raised as a possible familiar knight or school-era friend, without a settled role.

## Distant future / thematic echoes — not current cast

### Harry Potter
**Working thematic direction:** Many centuries later, Harry is another figure caught in a similar cycle of prophecy and a ruler trying to prevent his downfall. The possible final line about **'the Boy Who Had Lived'** hints at Arthur's repeated lives, but the exact metaphysics remain open. Harry's canonical decision to face death rather than perpetuate domination may answer a question that this trilogy raises.

### Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger
**Optional parallel only:** An earlier proposal maps **Ron to Merlin** and **Hermione to Guinevere**, completing the possible trio echo. This is **not established** and does not require literal reincarnations, characters at Hogwarts in the founders' era, or additional epilogue scenes.

## Relationships and timeline guardrails

- **Merlin ↔ Arthur:** The main romance and the trilogy's emotional spine; friendship/romance and eventual ideological conflict must be earned through choices in this life.
- **Merlin ↔ Salazar:** The student's respect for a brilliant but increasingly troubling teacher; secret Chamber lessons are a useful possibility.
- **Godric ↔ Salazar:** Former friends and co-founders; their eventual split can reflect wider arguments over power, blood ancestry, safety, and education.
- **Rowena ↔ Helga:** Close friendship; a humanizing anchor among the founders.
- **Rowena ↔ Helena:** Daughter has already disappeared with the diadem in our preferred version; Rowena's grief/secrecy can remain subtle.
- **Caretaker ↔ Helena:** A past unreciprocated attachment and a relinquished title, treated as background; only the Book III hint is needed.
- **Morgana ↔ southern king:** Political partners with separate agendas; neither is merely an extension of the other. Their offer of official protection for a persecuted magical minority comes at the price of obedience, central control, and militarized training.
- **Morgana ↔ Arthur:** Prophecy drives her to persecute or target him; he eventually defeats her, then must decide what kind of man victory will make him.
- **Book I:** Merlin's arrival, friendships, teaching, an optional local emissary acting outside the hidden castle; Hogwarts and the founders remain the immediate world. Hogwarts exists to safeguard rare, often untrained magical children from local persecution and coerced service, **not to direct the northern resistance**.
- **Book II:** Founder disagreements and war deepen. Merlin and Arthur grow close then begin to separate. Salazar's secret teaching and eventual rupture can feature prominently. **Do not schedule Helena's murder, the caretaker's suicide, or Rowena's death here.**
- **Book III:** Merlin and Arthur become opponents; Morgana is defeated, the pattern behind prophecy becomes clearer, and the caretaker's former baronial title can be disclosed with one casual remark. The great war's climactic battle need not be a second Battle of Hogwarts. An emerging preference for magical isolation may be expressed through Hogwarts' continued secrecy and Hogsmeade's development; the worldwide Statute of Secrecy belongs centuries later.
