The first thing Taiga understood was that Leo had lied.

Not intentionally, probably. That would have required Leo to know that the phrase *my house* suggested one building with one front door and some reasonable relationship between the people living inside it.

Manuel slowed beside a wide metal gate and tapped the horn once. A moment later the gate opened from inside, revealing a paved courtyard surrounded by walls, doors, stairs and sections of building that did not quite match one another. Some parts were painted more recently than others. One flight of stairs disappeared upward along an exterior wall. Another door stood open beneath a small covered area with chairs gathered around a table. Plants crowded one edge of the patio in mismatched pots.

Taiga looked from one side of the property to the other.

Leo was already smiling.

“What?”

“This is your house?”

“Part of it.”

Taiga turned toward him.

“Part.”

“Yeah.”

“You said house.”

“It is a house.”

“There are stairs going into another house.”

“That’s Tío’s.”

“So another house.”

“It’s all the same property.”

“That makes it worse.”

Manuel laughed from the front seat even though Taiga was reasonably sure he had not understood the English. Leo translated while Manuel pulled farther in, and Manuel laughed harder.

“Great,” Taiga said. “He’s enjoying this.”

“He likes you.”

“He barely knows me.”

“That hasn’t stopped anybody here before.”

The car had not fully stopped before a woman appeared from one of the doors.

Taiga knew immediately that she had to be Carmen. Leo had shown him pictures, but the pictures had not captured the speed. She crossed the courtyard while Manuel was still turning off the engine, spoke to him through the open window, looked into the back seat at Taiga, then switched attention to Leo without visibly pausing between any of it.

“Julio, *get his bag.* Manuel, *don’t leave the car there.* Taiga, welcome.”

The last word came in English.

Taiga opened the door.

Carmen was shorter than he had expected. That did nothing to reduce the sense that the entire courtyard had reorganized itself around her arrival.

“Buenas tardes,” Taiga said. “Mucho gusto.”

Her expression changed immediately.

“*Oh.*”

Leo got out behind him.

“*I told you.*”

“*You told me he was studying. You didn’t tell me he was going to arrive speaking.*”

“*I said he was studying Spanish.*”

Carmen turned back to Taiga and spoke more slowly.

“*You understand me?*”

Taiga caught that.

“*A little.*”

He pinched two fingers together.

“Un chin.”

Carmen laughed and looked over his shoulder.

“Mami!”

Taiga closed his eyes briefly.

Leo leaned toward him.

“You did that to yourself.”

“I hate you.”

“I know.”

By then Manuel had opened the trunk. Leo reached for Taiga’s suitcase, but Manuel moved it out of reach with one hand and said something that made Leo protest.

“What?”

“He says I already carried enough bags at the airport.”

“He carried the suitcase.”

“Yes.”

“That was the only bag.”

“I know.”

Manuel lifted the suitcase as if it weighed nothing and walked toward one of the doors.

Taiga watched him go.

“Your dad is huge.”

Leo grinned.

“Yeah.”

“And your mom is terrifying.”

“Also yeah.”

“Julio.”

Carmen had heard that one.

Leo straightened.

“I said nothing.”

Taiga looked at him.

“You answered in English.”

“Survival instinct.”

A girl stood in the doorway behind Carmen, watching Taiga with the specific concentration of someone who had already formed several questions and was deciding which one would cause the most damage.

Leo noticed her.

“Dani, *this is* Taiga.”

“*I know.*”

“*You haven’t met him.*”

“*I’ve seen pictures.*”

“*That’s not meeting.*”

Dani stepped outside. She looked sixteen in the way Taiga had started recognizing at Olympus whenever somebody’s younger sibling visited: old enough to want everyone to stop treating them like a child and young enough to make adults regret agreeing.

“Hi,” she said in English.

“Hi.”

Her English was not as effortless as Leo’s, but it was comfortable.

She looked at Taiga’s hair.

Then his face.

Then Leo.

“So you’re actually real.”

Leo sighed.

“What did you think?”

“I didn’t say I thought anything.”

“You just said actually.”

“Because now I know.”

Taiga nodded.

“That makes sense.”

Leo looked at him.

“Do not encourage her.”

Dani ignored him.

“Did he kiss you first, or did you kiss him?”

Carmen turned around so quickly that Taiga almost laughed.

“Daniela.”

“*What? They’re already boyfriends.*”

“*That doesn’t mean you ask that in the first thirty seconds.*”

“*Why not? It already happened.*”

Leo covered his face.

Taiga looked at Dani.

“I kissed him.”

Leo dropped his hands.

“That is not the whole story.”

“It answers the question.”

Dani nodded, apparently satisfied.

“Okay.”

Carmen stared at all three of them.

Taiga liked Dani a little more than he wanted to admit.

Before anybody could continue, a boy ran across the courtyard from the direction of the stairs and went straight to Leo.

“Julio!”

Leo turned in time to catch him around the shoulders.

“*Hey.*”

The boy started talking immediately, fast enough that Taiga got almost nothing beyond Leo’s name and something about tomorrow. Leo answered in the same language without changing rhythm, crouching slightly so they were closer to the same height.

Taiga watched.

The kid stopped long enough to notice him.

Leo pointed.

“Antonio, *this is* Taiga.”

Antonio looked at Taiga with complete seriousness.

Then he asked Leo something.

Leo laughed.

“What?”

“He wants to know if you’re faster than me.”

Taiga looked at Antonio.

“Yes.”

Leo turned.

“Excuse me?”

Taiga crouched enough to address Antonio directly.

“Sí.”

Antonio grinned.

Leo pointed between them.

“This relationship is already unacceptable.”

A man’s voice came from above.

“Taiga!”

Taiga looked up.

A broad man leaned over the upstairs railing.

“*There he is!*” he called, and then continued in Spanish at a speed that removed Taiga from the conversation almost immediately.

Leo answered.

The man laughed, disappeared from the railing and reappeared on the stairs moments later, still talking.

“That’s Tío Ramón,” Leo said quietly.

“I guessed.”

“How?”

“Instinct.”

Leo laughed.

Ramón reached the courtyard, shook Taiga’s hand with both of his and launched directly into something involving the airport, traffic and, unless Taiga had misunderstood very badly, a truck that had once caught fire.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“Help.”

“He says he once got from the airport to here in thirty-five minutes during a storm while the highway was blocked.”

From somewhere behind Ramón, a woman said something without raising her voice.

Ramón stopped.

Leo smiled.

“And Tía says it was fifty minutes, there was no storm, and the highway wasn’t blocked.”

The woman came down the stairs as Ramón was still arguing his version of events.

“Isa,” Leo said. “Tía.”

She greeted Taiga in Spanish and asked about the flight. He answered in the simplest sentence he could manage.

“Largo, pero bien.”

Long, but good.

Isa nodded and said something else Taiga missed before Carmen called them toward the house.

“*Come,*” Carmen said, already moving. “Mami *is waiting.*”

Taiga looked at Leo.

“She’s waiting?”

“You’re the event tonight.”

“I don’t like that.”

“You’ll survive.”

“That’s what you said before I got here.”

“I said it might be fun.”

“Worse.”

Leo took his backpack from the car and nudged Taiga forward.

They crossed the patio.

Only then did Taiga fully register the architecture. Carmen and Manuel’s section had its own door and windows. Another entrance led toward what Leo had called Tío’s. Mercedes’s part sat closer to the center, older in small ways Taiga could not have named if asked: the shape of the doorway, the tile near the threshold, the furniture visible inside.

It was not one house, and somehow it also was. Taiga understood why Leo had failed to explain it.

Mercedes sat near the open kitchen with a glass of something cold beside her. She did not get up when they entered. Everybody else seemed to move toward her eventually, which probably made standing unnecessary.

Leo went to her first.

“Abuela, *this is* Taiga.”

She touched his cheek, said something that made him roll his eyes, and then looked past him at Taiga.

Taiga stepped forward.

“Buenas tardes, Doña Mercedes. Mucho gusto.”

Mercedes looked at him for a long second.

Then at Leo.

Then back at Taiga.

Her smile was slow.

“*You studied.*”

Taiga understood because she said it carefully.

“*A little.*”

“*More than* Julio *told me.*”

Leo objected immediately.

“*I told everybody he was studying.*”

“*You said he knew some words.*”

“*I do know some words.*”

Mercedes waved one hand toward Leo without looking at him.

“*He talks too much.*”

Taiga understood that too.

He smiled.

“Sí.”

Leo stared.

“Oh, great.”

Mercedes laughed.

That was the beginning of the problem.

Carmen said something to Leo from the doorway. Taiga caught *hora* and nothing useful around it.

Leo looked at him.

“Dinner in about an hour.”

Taiga checked the time almost automatically. It was a little after seven.

“Good.”

“You want to put your stuff upstairs?”

“I want to stop smelling like an airport.”

Leo laughed.

“That too.”

Manuel had already carried the suitcase into Carmen and Manuel’s part of the house. Leo shifted Taiga’s backpack higher on his shoulder and led him through the living room, then toward a staircase Taiga had not noticed from the courtyard.

“You’re upstairs?”

“My room is.”

“Of course it is.”

“What does that mean?”

“This place keeps producing stairs.”

“It’s a house.”

“It’s several houses pretending to be one.”

Leo smiled and kept climbing.

The upper floor was quieter. The noise from the courtyard followed them, but at a distance now, softened by walls and the turn in the stairs. Leo opened the last door along the short hallway and stopped immediately.

“Oh, come on.”

Taiga looked past him.

“What?”

“She moved my desk.”

The room was unmistakably Leo’s even before Taiga knew what half the things in it meant. A framed football photograph hung near the desk, with two medals looped over one corner of a shelf and an old pair of boots pushed beneath the window. Across from them sat a gaming setup that had clearly been assembled over years rather than bought all at once: monitor, headset, controllers, cables, a chair that had survived at least one repair.

Taiga stepped inside.

“You play?”

Leo turned.

“Games?”

“Yes.”

“Obviously.”

“That was not obvious.”

“You never asked.”

“You talk constantly.”

“That doesn’t mean I’ve submitted a complete inventory of my personality.”

Taiga looked back at the desk.

One controller had worn rubber on the sticks. A stack of old game cases shared a shelf with schoolbooks and a small speaker. Beside the monitor was a photograph of a younger Leo in a football kit with several other boys.

Taiga pointed at one of them.

“Enzo?”

Leo looked.

“Yeah. We were fifteen, I think.”

“You look twelve.”

“Fuck you.”

Taiga smiled and turned toward the rest of the room.

That was when he saw the beds.

Leo’s normal single bed had been pushed against the wall. Beside it, the sofa had been opened into a second sleeping surface and moved close enough that the two mattresses almost met. One sat slightly higher than the other, leaving a narrow seam between them.

Both had fresh sheets.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“What the fuck is this?”

Leo covered his face.

“Moma.”

“That doesn’t answer the question.”

“It answers every question.”

“You have two beds.”

“I had one bed and one sofa.”

“Now you have one badly constructed double bed.”

“Yes.”

Taiga pressed one hand against the sofa mattress.

It was noticeably firmer.

“Why are they different heights?”

“Because one is a bed and one is a sofa.”

“Your mother did this?”

“She said my bed sleeps one person.”

Taiga looked at him.

“And?”

“And there are two of us.”

That was apparently the entire logic.

There had been no conversation about whether Taiga would sleep somewhere else, no awkward explanation waiting for him on arrival. Carmen had counted people, counted beds and solved the problem.

Taiga looked at the seam again.

“You’re sleeping on that side.”

“This is my room.”

“You’re the host.”

“That sounds like discrimination.”

“Society rules.”

Leo dropped Taiga’s backpack beside the wardrobe.

“There’s space for your stuff there. Moma cleared it.”

“She moved your desk and cleared my stuff.”

“Yes.”

“You’re never finding anything again.”

“I know.”

A pair of fresh towels had been folded on the chair beside the door. Taiga picked one up.

“Bathroom?”

“Across the hall.”

“Good.”

Leo pointed.

“Hot water takes a second.”

“I’ve survived worse.”

“You say that now.”

Taiga took his toiletries from the backpack and headed for the door.

Leo leaned against the frame.

“Need anything?”

“A different family.”

“You’ve been here twenty minutes.”

“Exactly.”

Leo laughed.

The bathroom was ordinary enough that Taiga felt relieved by it. He closed the door, put his things beside the sink and looked at himself in the mirror.

Travel had settled on him in layers. His hair had flattened in the wrong places, his shirt smelled faintly of airplane cabin and warm car, and the back of his neck still felt sticky from the humidity outside.

The shower helped more than he expected.

For ten minutes there was only water, tile and the muted sound of voices somewhere beyond the door. Nobody was asking him anything. Nobody was speaking too quickly for him to follow. Nobody was discovering that Leo’s foreign boyfriend had apparently learned enough Spanish to become tonight’s entertainment.

By the time he stepped out, the flight felt farther away.

He dried off, changed into clean clothes and spent another minute at the sink making his hair look less like he had slept against an airplane window for several hours.

When he returned to Leo’s room, Leo was sitting at the desk with one of the old controllers in his hands.

Taiga stopped in the doorway.

“You started without me.”

Leo looked at the controller.

“It isn’t connected.”

“Still rude.”

“I was checking if it works.”

“Does it?”

Leo pressed a button.

Nothing happened.

“No.”

Taiga dropped the towel over the chair.

“Good system.”

“Shut up.”

He had enough energy now to look around again without feeling like the room was another thing happening to him. It was strange seeing Leo reduced to objects that had existed before Olympus: old football photographs, schoolbooks, games, a desk his mother had apparently moved while he was gone.

Leo caught him looking.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That keeps happening.”

Taiga walked over and hooked two fingers into the front of Leo’s shirt.

“Hi.”

Leo smiled.

“Hi.”

Taiga kissed him.

It was brief, but still longer than the airport one. There was no crowd behind them this time and no suitcase moving toward a car.

When they separated, Leo rested his forehead against Taiga’s for a second.

“Better?”

Taiga knew what he meant.

“Yeah.”

A voice rose from downstairs.

“Julio!”

Leo closed his eyes.

Taiga smiled.

“That lasted.”

Leo called something back.

Another answer came from below.

He looked at Taiga.

“Dinner.”

Taiga glanced toward the door.

“Already?”

“It’s been almost an hour.”

Taiga checked the time again.

Leo was right.

He looked once more at the room, then at the hallway beyond it.

“Fine.”

Leo took his hand for the first few steps toward the stairs.

“Round two.”

“I hate that you called it that.”

---

Dinner did not begin so much as accumulate.

Taiga had expected a recognizable start. There was a table, technically. There were also people eating before other people sat down, somebody carrying another dish from Carmen’s kitchen, Antonio leaving halfway through and returning with a toy, Dani appearing with two glasses and no explanation for where she had been, and Ramón telling a story loudly enough that Taiga could follow his presence even when he could not follow a single word.

They ended up around the larger table near Mercedes’s kitchen, with two extra chairs dragged in from the patio.

Taiga sat between Leo and Isa.

He suspected Leo had arranged that deliberately.

The food helped.

There was rice, stewed chicken, beans, avocado and fried plantain, plus two other things Taiga did not know the names of and stopped trying to identify after Carmen put more food on his plate before he had finished the first serving.

“*I can do it,*” he said.

“*I know.*”

She added more anyway.

Leo leaned closer.

“She’s going to feed you until you stop moving.”

“I’ve noticed.”

Carmen caught them looking at her.

“*What? He needs to eat.*”

Taiga understood that much without help.

“*I am eating.*”

Carmen pointed at his plate like the evidence supported her.

“*Not enough.*”

Taiga understood that too.

“I’m not that hungry.”

Leo wisely declined to translate that one.

Across the table, Manuel asked Leo something about Olympus. Before Leo could finish answering, Ramón cut in with a comment that sent Carmen straight back into the argument they had apparently been having before Taiga understood any of it.

Taiga caught almost none of the words. What he could follow were the reactions: Carmen corrected Ramón twice, Ramón answered both times and kept going, and Mercedes watched the exchange with the expression of somebody who had seen it before.

Then Dani leaned toward Taiga.

“Do you sleep in the same bed at university?”

Taiga stopped with his fork halfway to his mouth.

Leo choked.

Carmen closed her eyes.

“Daniela.”

Dani looked genuinely confused.

“*What? I want to know where he sleeps.*”

“*At night,*” Leo said.

“*That doesn’t answer it.*”

“*That was intentional.*”

Dani looked back at Taiga.

Taiga put the fork down.

“None of your business.”

She considered that.

“Okay.”

And that was it. Dani took no offense and offered no argument; she simply went back to eating.

Taiga glanced at Leo.

Leo was trying not to smile.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Your face.”

“My face is innocent.”

“No part of this family is innocent.”

Mercedes noticed Taiga looking around the table.

“*What did he say?*”

Leo translated.

Mercedes smiled.

Then she asked Taiga something.

Too quickly.

He caught *Julio*, *español* and something that might have been *enseña*.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“She asked if I’m teaching you well.”

Taiga answered before Leo could do anything else.

“No.”

The table laughed.

Mercedes repeated the question more slowly.

Taiga understood enough this time.

“*He is a bad teacher,*” Taiga said carefully. “*He knows when something is wrong, but he doesn’t know why.*”

Leo turned toward him.

“That was a whole sentence.”

“Shut up.”

Mercedes listened, then said something with such a neutral expression that Taiga initially assumed it was harmless.

Carmen put a hand over her mouth.

Isa looked down at her plate.

Ramón laughed loudly enough to scare Antonio.

Leo went red.

Taiga looked around.

“What?”

“No.”

“What did she say?”

“Nothing.”

“Leo.”

“Eat.”

Dani was openly delighted.

“She said maybe your mouth is better at things that don’t need grammar.”

Taiga stared at Leo.

Leo stared at Dani.

“Why would you translate that?”

“He asked.”

Mercedes was still eating.

Taiga looked at her.

She raised her glass slightly.

Taiga started laughing.

“Oh, fuck you.”

“I didn’t say it!” Leo said.

“That’s worse.”

Mercedes asked what Taiga had said.

Dani translated *that* too.

Carmen finally gave up and laughed with everyone else.

Taiga made a decision then: Doña Mercedes was dangerous, and he liked her.

The conversation moved on before he fully recovered.

Ramón wanted to know about soccer. That much Taiga understood because *fútbol* solved several problems at once. Manuel asked something about the championship. Antonio wanted to know whether Taiga had really scored in the final, which meant Leo had apparently been talking about him.

Taiga answered where he could.

Yes, they had won.

Yes, he had scored.

No, he was not better than Leo at everything.

“Yes,” Leo said in English, “thank you.”

“I said not everything.”

“You could have stopped earlier.”

Ramón started another story, this one involving football.

Leo sighed before the second sentence.

“No.”

Ramón pointed at Taiga and continued.

The story concerned a match from years ago in which Ramón had, according to his own version, scored from almost midfield against a team that had not lost in two seasons.

Carmen interrupted.

Leo translated automatically.

“Moma says it was inside the box.”

Ramón objected.

Isa added something.

Leo smiled.

“Tía says it was a penalty.”

Ramón looked betrayed.

Manuel said something.

Leo laughed harder.

“What?”

“Papá says Tío missed the first penalty and they let him retake it because the goalkeeper moved early.”

Ramón spread both hands as if this improved the story, then continued arguing his case while the others corrected him from three directions at once.

By the time dinner ended, Taiga’s head felt full. Not in a bad way; there had simply been too much to process at once.

Spanish had stopped being individual sentences and turned into currents. He could follow one if somebody addressed him directly, especially if they slowed down. The moment two people spoke across the table, his understanding collapsed. When three did it, he stopped trying.

Leo translated less than Taiga expected.

At first Taiga thought he had forgotten. Then he realized Leo was choosing. If someone asked Taiga something, Leo helped. If Ramón and Carmen were arguing about a story Taiga had no reason to care about, Leo let them.

It made the whole thing easier.

Eventually chairs began scraping backward and people started moving again. Taiga thought dinner was over until he realized Mercedes and Ramón were still sitting at the table, Carmen was packing food into containers, Manuel had wandered outside, Dani had taken Antonio somewhere, and Isa was washing something despite Carmen telling her not to.

Nobody announced an ending. Dinner simply dissolved into other activities.

Leo touched Taiga’s shoulder.

“Come on.”

“Where?”

“My room.”

Taiga looked toward the patio.

“Are we allowed to leave?”

Leo laughed.

“Yes.”

“Are you sure?”

“No one is holding you hostage.”

“Your mother put food on my plate four times.”

“That’s hospitality.”

“It was an attack.”

“Come on.”

Taiga followed him.

---

When they went back upstairs after dinner, Taiga no longer had enough energy to inspect anything.

He closed Leo’s bedroom door behind them and stood there for a moment while the noise from downstairs softened into something he could finally ignore.

Leo dropped onto the edge of his bed.

Taiga sat on the sofa mattress beside him.

For a few seconds neither of them said anything.

Then Taiga leaned back on his hands.

“Your family is fucking insane.”

Leo smiled.

“Yeah.”

“You say that like you’re proud.”

“I am.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo’s expression was completely uncomplicated.

Taiga thought about dinner: Carmen making sure his plate never stayed empty for long, Dani asking exactly what she wanted to know, Ramón’s football story surviving increasingly destructive corrections, and Mercedes detonating the table while looking innocent.

“Your grandmother is dangerous.”

“Extremely.”

“Your sister asks too many questions.”

“She likes you.”

“That’s not reassuring.”

Leo laughed.

Then he looked at Taiga a little more carefully.

“Was it too much?”

Taiga considered lying, not because the truth was bad but because the truth required more than one answer.

“I understood maybe thirty percent of dinner.”

Leo nodded.

“That’s more than I expected.”

“Fuck you.”

“I’m serious. Seven people talking over each other is hard even when you’re fluent.”

“You were all talking about different things.”

“That happens.”

“Ramón changed stories halfway through telling them.”

“That also happens.”

“I still don’t know where he lives.”

Leo pointed vaguely upward and behind them.

“That way.”

“That is not a location.”

“You’ll figure it out.”

Taiga stared at him.

Leo smiled.

“Too much?”

Taiga let himself fall backward across the sofa bed.

“A lot.”

Leo waited.

Taiga turned his head.

“Not bad.”

That was enough.

Leo leaned over and kissed his forehead.

Taiga shoved him away.

“Don’t get sentimental.”

“You flew to another country to meet my family.”

“I came for the weather.”

“Sure.”

A few minutes later they finally dealt with the sleeping arrangement properly. The difference in mattress height was worse when they were lying down, and the narrow seam between them seemed specifically designed to catch an elbow or a hip.

Taiga pushed at the sofa with his foot.

“Closer.”

“It’s already touching.”

“It’s pretending to touch.”

Leo got up and helped him move it another few centimeters.

“There.”

Taiga tested the gap with one hand.

“Still terrible.”

“You can sleep on the floor.”

“You can sleep on the floor.”

“This is my room.”

“We covered this.”

By the time they changed for bed, the family had grown quieter by degrees, though never completely silent. Voices still traveled through the open window now and then, followed by a laugh or a door closing somewhere below.

Taiga lay on the firmer mattress.

Leo took the bed.

Within ten minutes, Leo had shifted close enough that the distinction barely mattered.

Taiga fell asleep with Leo’s hand resting against his stomach and somebody laughing somewhere beyond the window.

---

Saturday morning began before Taiga wanted it to.

He woke to movement outside the room, rolled toward Leo and immediately found the seam between the mattresses with his elbow.

“Fuck.”

Leo opened one eye.

“Told you.”

“You were supposed to be on that side.”

“I moved.”

“Why?”

“You steal blankets.”

“I do not.”

Leo looked down.

The entire blanket was on Taiga’s half.

“That proves nothing.”

A cupboard closed somewhere in the hallway. Someone called for Julio from outside. Another voice answered before Leo could.

Taiga looked at the ceiling.

“What time is it?”

Leo reached blindly for his phone.

“Eight twenty.”

“Why is everyone awake?”

“It’s Saturday.”

“That makes it worse.”

Leo laughed and pushed himself upright.

By the time they reached the kitchen, the compound had already become a logistics problem.

Carmen was standing near the counter with a list in one hand and coffee in the other. Manuel was checking his pockets. Ramón had appeared from somewhere and was insisting he knew exactly where to find something Carmen needed for tomorrow. Isa stood beside the counter drinking coffee while he talked.

Dani came in through the patio door wearing shoes and carrying a bag.

“*Where are you going?*” Leo asked.

“*Out.*”

“*With?*”

“*People.*”

“*That is not an answer.*”

“*It’s enough of one.*”

Taiga looked between them.

Antonio crossed the patio carrying a ball under one arm and something wrapped in foil in the other. Mercedes sat at the table drinking coffee as if the activity around her belonged to a different weather system.

Carmen looked at Taiga.

“*Did you sleep?*”

He understood that.

“Sí. Bien.”

She smiled.

“*Coffee?*”

“Yes.”

That answer required no Spanish.

Leo reached for a cup.

Carmen handed Taiga one first.

“Favorite child already,” Taiga said.

Leo looked wounded.

“You have been here one night.”

“Efficient.”

Manuel walked into the kitchen again.

“*Keys?*”

Carmen looked at him.

“*You had them.*”

“*I know.*”

Dani pointed toward the counter.

The keys were beside his coffee.

Manuel picked them up, gave Dani a small nod and walked back out.

Breakfast spread itself across the morning while plans for Sunday happened around it. Taiga caught *cumpleaños* often enough to know when Leo’s birthday had entered the conversation, even when everything around the word escaped him.

Carmen needed groceries, Ramón was apparently handling chairs, Isa had somewhere to go before lunch, Dani had already made plans, and Antonio wanted to know whether tomorrow involved cake.

Leo answered three different people while buttering bread.

At one point Carmen asked him to carry a folded table from one side of the patio to another. Before he finished, Ramón called him upstairs for something Taiga did not understand. Leo went up, came back with a box, put it in Manuel’s car, then returned to his coffee and discovered it had gone cold.

Taiga watched all of it.

At Olympus, Leo’s tendency to be everywhere had always seemed like Leo being Leo.

Here it looked almost architectural.

He moved through the property the same way Antonio did, through doors and between people without needing to ask where he belonged. Everybody called him Julio. Everybody expected he knew where things were. Most of the time, he did.

Taiga finished eating.

“Do you need help?”

Leo looked at him.

“With what?”

Taiga gestured at everything.

Leo followed the gesture and laughed.

“No. This is just morning.”

“That is not reassuring.”

“It’ll calm down.”

Taiga did not believe him.

Then, somehow, Leo was right.

Carmen and Manuel left first, taking the grocery list and two bags with them. Ramón and Isa followed later with Antonio, after a ten-minute discussion about where they were going that Taiga never fully understood. Dani disappeared through the gate after calling goodbye from halfway across the courtyard.

Within twenty minutes, the compound emptied so completely that Taiga noticed the absence of sound before he understood what had happened.

Mercedes remained at the patio table with another coffee.

Taiga looked around.

“That’s it?”

Leo stretched.

“For now.”

“This place can do this?”

“Do what?”

“Shut up.”

Mercedes heard the tone if not the words and smiled.

She said something to Leo.

Leo laughed.

“What?”

“She says we should leave before Moma gets back and finds more work for us.”

Taiga looked at Mercedes.

“Gracias, Doña Mercedes.”

Mercedes waved them away.

Leo reached for Taiga’s hand.

“Come on.”

“Where?”

“Outside.”

“We are outside.”

“More outside.”

“That sounds stupid.”

“You’ll see.”

---

The neighborhood made more sense on foot.

From inside the car, Taiga had registered roads and houses as a gradual retreat from the city. Walking beside Leo turned them into a place.

The street outside the compound was quiet compared with the roads they had taken from the airport, but it was not empty. A motorcycle passed them. Two kids kicked a ball against a wall farther down. Somebody had music playing from an open doorway. A dog watched Taiga with mild suspicion from beneath a parked car and decided he was not worth standing up for.

Leo walked without thinking about the route.

“This way.”

“Where are we going?”

“Nowhere important.”

“Great tour.”

“I told you. Today is the local version.”

They passed a small colmado where Leo knew the man behind the counter well enough to greet him without slowing down. The man called him Julio and asked something about university.

Leo answered.

Taiga caught *campeones*.

The man looked at Taiga.

Then back at Leo.

Taiga caught *novio* too.

He looked at Leo.

Leo was smiling.

“What did he say?”

“He asked if you’re the boyfriend.”

“And?”

“I said no. You’re my accountant.”

Taiga stopped walking.

Leo laughed.

“I said yes.”

“Asshole.”

The man behind the counter called something after them.

Leo answered over his shoulder.

“What was that?”

“He said you look serious.”

“He’s observant.”

“I said you’re worse than you look.”

Taiga shoved him.

They kept walking.

Leo pointed out places without giving them the kind of explanation a tour guide would have. A corner where people gathered in the evening. A bakery his mother liked. A small shop that had changed owners twice but somehow still looked exactly the same. A house where an older cousin used to live before moving away.

Then Taiga recognized a storefront.

He slowed.

Leo kept going for two steps before noticing.

“What?”

Taiga pointed.

“That’s it.”

“What?”

“The bad empanadas.”

Leo looked at the shop.

Then at Taiga.

His face brightened.

“You remember that?”

“You called me from here.”

“Yeah.”

“You said they were terrible.”

“They are.”

“Then why are we going inside?”

Leo was already turning toward the door.

“Tradition.”

Taiga stared.

“That isn’t a reason.”

“It’s the best reason.”

The empanadas were not terrible, but they were not especially good either. Taiga told Leo exactly that.

Leo looked offended on behalf of the establishment.

“I said they were terrible.”

“They’re fine.”

“No. You need to commit.”

“You’ve been insulting them for years.”

“Yes, affectionately.”

“That’s not how food works.”

“It is here.”

They ate outside under shade while Leo pointed farther down the road with the unfinished half of his empanada.

“There’s a court that way. We used to play there sometimes.”

“Soccer?”

“Anything. Mostly soccer.”

“Show me.”

It was not much of a field. More of an open hard-packed space beside a small court, with enough room for kids to turn it into whatever game they needed. A group was using part of it when Taiga and Leo arrived, and Leo knew two of them by name.

One recognized him first.

“Julio!”

Leo raised a hand.

A few minutes later, Taiga found himself standing beside the fence while Leo explained in Spanish who he was to people Taiga did not know. He heard *Olympus*, *fútbol*, his own name and, again, *novio*.

One of the older boys looked at Taiga and said something.

Leo laughed.

“What?”

“He wants to know if you’re actually faster than me.”

Taiga looked at Leo.

“This is becoming a theme.”

“You told Antonio yes.”

“Because it’s true.”

“It is not.”

The boy was still waiting.

Taiga answered with the vocabulary he had.

“Sí. Más rápido.”

Leo looked betrayed.

The boy laughed.

Taiga smiled.

They did not stay long, which helped.

Leo could have spent an hour there without effort, Taiga suspected. He knew people. People knew him. Nobody seemed surprised that he was back or that he had brought someone with him.

But after a few minutes Leo touched Taiga’s shoulder and they kept walking.

The farther they moved from the compound, the more Taiga understood why the morning had not bothered Leo in the slightest.

This place did not require Leo to enter social situations. He already existed inside them.

A man sitting outside a house knew his name.

A woman crossing the road asked something about Carmen.

Someone on a motorcycle lifted a hand in greeting.

None of it became a conversation unless Leo wanted it to.

For the first time since Taiga arrived, the social density felt loose instead of concentrated.

They eventually stopped near a shaded wall at the edge of a quieter street.

Leo leaned back against it.

Taiga stood beside him.

“So?”

“So what?”

“My neighborhood.”

Taiga looked around.

“It’s quieter than your house.”

Leo laughed.

“Low bar.”

“I mean it.”

“I know.”

Taiga looked toward the street they had just walked down.

“I thought you grew up in the middle of the city.”

“I mean, kind of. Santo Domingo is complicated.”

“This doesn’t feel like the center.”

“It isn’t.”

Leo explained the westward sprawl in the vague way someone explained geography they had never needed to define for themselves. La Guáyiga. Pedro Brand. The city spreading outward. Family who had been there before half the things around them.

Taiga listened.

“Do you miss it?”

Leo looked at him.

“When I’m at Olympus?”

“Yeah.”

“Sometimes.”

He thought for a moment.

“I miss people more than places, usually. But then I come back and there’s stuff I forgot I missed.”

“Like bad empanadas.”

“Exactly.”

“That’s depressing.”

“It builds character.”

Taiga smiled.

Leo nudged his shoulder.

“And I like Olympus.”

“I know.”

“It’s not one or the other.”

“I didn’t say it was.”

“I know.”

They stayed there a little longer without needing to fill the silence.

That was different too.

At the house, silence had felt temporary, something the next person would interrupt eventually. Out here it simply existed between them, and neither seemed in a hurry to break it. Eventually Leo looked at his phone.

“We should probably go back.”

“Why?”

“Moma will be back eventually.”

“That is not a reason.”

“It is if we want to survive tomorrow.”

Taiga frowned.

“What happens tomorrow?”

Leo smiled.

“My birthday.”

“I know that.”

“Then why did you ask?”

“What are we doing?”

“During the day?”

“Yes.”

“You’ll see.”

Taiga stared.

“I hate surprises.”

“You like some surprises.”

“No.”

“You came here.”

“That was scheduled.”

Leo laughed.

“Fine. I’m taking you somewhere. Just us.”

“And then?”

Leo’s smile changed.

“Then family.”

Taiga thought about Friday night.

“Worse than dinner?”

“Bigger than dinner.”

“That isn’t the same answer.”

“No.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo kissed his cheek.

“Tomorrow.”

---

Mercedes was still home when they returned.

She had moved from the patio table to a chair nearer the shade, with a small radio playing beside her and something folded in her lap. Taiga could not tell whether she was repairing it, sorting it or simply holding it.

She looked up when they entered.

“¿Comieron?”

Taiga understood immediately.

“Yes.”

Then corrected himself.

“Sí.”

Mercedes asked what.

Taiga looked at Leo.

He knew this one.

“Empanadas.”

Mercedes made a face.

Then said the name of the place.

Taiga recognized it.

He pointed at Leo.

“*He likes them.*”

“*I do not.*”

“You keep going back.”

Mercedes asked something else.

Leo laughed.

“What?”

“She says that means I like them.”

Taiga looked at her.

“Exacto.”

Mercedes smiled as though Taiga had made measurable progress.

They sat with her for a while because there was simply nowhere they needed to be.

Mercedes asked Taiga about the neighborhood, speaking more slowly when she wanted him to answer and normally when she was talking to Leo. Taiga caught enough to respond in pieces. The rest Leo filled in.

At some point another woman stopped by through the gate, greeted Mercedes, greeted Leo and inspected Taiga with obvious curiosity before saying something that included *novio*.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“Again?”

Leo smiled.

“Neighborhood efficiency.”

The woman stayed ten minutes, drank half a coffee, left something in Mercedes’s kitchen and disappeared again.

Taiga watched her go.

“Who was that?”

Leo thought.

“Family friend.”

“You hesitated.”

“She might technically be related.”

“How do you not know?”

“I know who she is. I don’t know the exact branch.”

“That sounds fake.”

“It’s not.”

“Your family is badly documented.”

“We need Caleb.”

Taiga laughed.

The quiet did not end all at once.

Dani came back first.

She crossed the courtyard, saw Taiga sitting with Mercedes and stopped.

“You’re still alive.”

“So far.”

“Good.”

Then she went inside.

A little later Antonio appeared through the gate ahead of Ramón and Isa. He went directly to Leo, showed him something Taiga could not see and spent five minutes explaining it with the intensity of a legal argument.

Leo listened with full attention, asked questions, and let Antonio correct him when he got something wrong.

Then the kid noticed Taiga and moved to sit beside him instead.

Taiga looked down.

Antonio looked back.

“What?”

Antonio said something.

Taiga caught *Julio* and *mañana*.

Leo translated from across the table.

“He wants to know what you’re giving me tomorrow.”

Taiga looked at Antonio.

“That’s confidential.”

Antonio frowned.

Leo translated.

Antonio asked another question.

“What now?”

“He wants to know if he can know because he won’t tell me.”

Taiga considered him.

“No.”

Antonio accepted this and stayed in the chair anyway.

Taiga shifted slightly.

Antonio did not.

Leo was smiling.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“He’s sitting on me.”

“He’s next to you.”

“Same problem.”

Antonio said something else.

Leo laughed.

“He says you complain a lot.”

Taiga looked at the kid.

“You understood that?”

“No.”

“Then stop helping him.”

By late afternoon, the property had repopulated.

Manuel returned carrying more bags than seemed necessary and immediately realized one of them belonged in Ramón’s section. Carmen came in behind him with a box Leo tried to inspect before she slapped his hand away.

“*Tomorrow,*” she said.

That word Taiga understood.

Ramón brought in folded chairs and claimed there had been none available until he personally persuaded someone. Isa corrected the number of phone calls involved. Dani came back outside once the food appeared. Another cousin Taiga had not met stopped at the gate to tell Carmen something and stayed twenty minutes.

The compound became noisy again, but it no longer sounded like one continuous wall.

Taiga could separate pieces now. Ramón was telling something near the patio table while Carmen called instructions from one doorway to another. Dani switched into English when she wanted to tell Taiga something directly, and Antonio could apparently say Julio’s name from any point on the property and expect an answer. Isa crossed through with a stack of plates, Mercedes remained in the middle of everything without looking remotely hurried, and Leo moved between them all.

At one point Carmen called him to hold something while Manuel adjusted a folding table. Thirty seconds later Dani asked where a charger was. Antonio wanted him to see whatever he had been explaining earlier. Ramón needed help moving chairs.

Leo answered everyone.

Taiga watched for a moment.

Then Carmen handed him one end of the table.

“Taiga.”

He understood the gesture even if he missed the sentence.

“Yeah.”

He took it.

Leo looked over.

“You don’t have to—”

“I have hands.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

“Move.”

Together they carried the table across the patio.

Carmen pointed where she wanted it.

Taiga put it down.

She nodded.

“*Perfect.*”

Taiga understood that too.

By evening, he knew where the glasses were in Mercedes’s kitchen, which felt more significant than it probably should have.

Dinner was smaller and less organized than the night before. People ate at different times because half the food was apparently preparation for Sunday and therefore forbidden. Taiga did not ask why there were already so many chairs.

He had learned enough to stop asking. Eventually, the patio settled into something slower without ever becoming empty.

Taiga and Leo escaped to the bedroom before anyone could invent another reason for them to stay outside.

Leo closed the door.

Taiga looked at him.

“You said today would calm down.”

“It did.”

“For like four hours.”

“That counts.”

“This family has a different definition of everything.”

Leo sat on the bed.

“Days. Calm. Houses.”

“Teaching.”

“Rude.”

Taiga changed his shirt and sat beside him.

The two mattresses had separated slightly again.

He pushed them together with his foot.

“Your engineering is failing.”

“My engineering?”

“Your family’s.”

“Moma’s.”

“Same institution.”

Leo laughed and lay back.

Taiga followed, choosing the sofa side this time because at least he knew what kind of terrible it was.

Outside, somebody moved a chair across the patio.

Taiga looked toward the window.

“So tomorrow is bigger.”

“Yeah.”

“How much bigger?”

Leo considered.

“That depends.”

“On what?”

“Who comes.”

“You don’t know who’s coming to your own birthday?”

“I know some people.”

“Some.”

“Family doesn’t really RSVP here.”

“Of course they don’t.”

Leo smiled.

“But during the day, it’s just us.”

Taiga turned toward him.

“Where?”

“No.”

“Leo.”

“You’ll see.”

“I can leave.”

“You are in my house.”

“Apparently that means very little.”

Leo laughed and reached across the seam between the mattresses.

Taiga took his hand.

For a while neither of them said anything.

The house was quieter now, though never fully silent. Voices drifted through the open window and faded again. Somewhere outside, Carmen called something to Manuel. Ramón laughed at his own story. A door closed.

Taiga thought about the previous twenty-four hours.

He still could not have drawn a map of the property without inventing at least one staircase. He understood maybe half of what people said directly to him and much less when they forgot to slow down. He had met enough relatives and almost-relatives that the distinction was already becoming suspicious.

And yet the place felt less confusing than it had when the gate first opened.

People had started becoming people instead of noise.

Taiga squeezed Leo’s hand.

“Happy birthday tomorrow.”

Leo looked over.

“That’s cheating.”

“What?”

“You’re early.”

“I’m going to sleep. It counts.”

“It absolutely does not.”

“Your calendar rules are stupid.”

“You started this.”

Taiga smiled.

Leo shifted closer until the seam between the mattresses pressed against both of them.

“Tell me tomorrow.”

“Maybe.”

“You have to.”

“We’ll see.”

Leo kissed him once, then settled beside him.

Outside, the compound slowly quieted around them.

Tomorrow, apparently, it would fill all over again.
