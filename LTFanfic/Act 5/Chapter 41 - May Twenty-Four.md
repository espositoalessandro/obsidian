Taiga woke to sunlight, voices somewhere below and the uncomfortable realization that Leo had managed to occupy more of Taiga’s side than physics should have allowed.

One of Leo’s legs was hooked over his. His face was buried against Taiga’s shoulder, and sometime during the night he had stolen enough of the blanket that Taiga’s right foot was cold.

Taiga looked at the ceiling.

“Birthday privilege lasts until midnight tonight.”

Leo made a noise without opening his eyes.

“That was not a sentence.”

“It was.”

“You’re asleep.”

“Also yes.”

Taiga tried to move his leg.

Leo tightened his hold.

“Move.”

“No.”

“You’re twenty now. Act like an adult.”

That got one eye open.

“You remembered.”

Taiga stared at him.

“I wished you happy birthday six hours ago.”

“Technically after everybody else.”

“You’re lucky I did it at all.”

Leo smiled and closed his eye again.

Taiga waited, but Leo made no effort to move.

“Are you getting up?”

“No.”

“You made plans.”

“Future Leo made plans.”

“Future Leo is an asshole.”

“I’m twenty. Respect your elders.”

Taiga shoved him.

Leo laughed into the pillow.

The noise downstairs had changed since Saturday. Yesterday morning had sounded like people organizing things. This morning sounded like people who had already organized them and were now finding reasons to reorganize them anyway. Chairs scraped across the courtyard. Carmen called something from below. A male voice Taiga did not recognize answered. Someone laughed near the gate.

Leo’s phone vibrated on the bedside table, then again, followed by two more notifications in quick succession.

Taiga looked at it.

“You have twelve notifications.”

Leo did not move.

“Thirteen.”

“Good for me.”

“Fourteen.”

“That’s enough.”

Leo reached blindly for the phone, missed it, then finally dragged it under the blanket with him.

Taiga watched his face brighten from the screen.

“Messages?”

“Yeah.”

“Anyone important?”

Leo scrolled.

“Everyone thinks they’re important today.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

“It’s mostly people saying happy birthday.”

Another message arrived.

Leo smiled.

Taiga looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You’re staring.”

“You look stupid.”

“It’s my birthday.”

“That doesn’t make you less stupid.”

Leo put the phone down and rolled closer.

“You’re mean to me on my birthday.”

“I’m consistent.”

“That’s true.”

He kissed Taiga once, still half-asleep, then again because apparently the first one had not satisfied some private birthday requirement.

A knock sounded against the door. Neither of them moved, and a second knock followed a moment later.

“Julio.”

Carmen. Leo closed his eyes.

“See?”

“Your mother is calling you.”

“She knows.”

“Clearly.”

“Julio.”

This time the door opened.

Taiga pulled the blanket higher on instinct even though they were both wearing clothes.

Carmen stood in the doorway carrying two mugs.

Her eyes moved from Leo to Taiga, then settled on her son.

“*Happy birthday.*”

Leo’s expression softened immediately.

“Gracias, Moma.”

Carmen crossed the room, bent down and kissed his forehead. Then she put one mug on the bedside table and handed the other to Taiga.

“*Coffee. Your father bought bread. Abuela says you have ten minutes before she comes upstairs herself.*”

Leo sat up.

“*That is terrorism.*”

“*Then move.*”

Taiga took the mug.

“Gracias.”

Then she left.

Leo stared at the open door for a second before reaching for the coffee.

Taiga swung his legs off the sofa bed.

“You have ten minutes before your grandmother invades.”

Leo grabbed the mug.

“She would.”

“That I believe.”

---

Breakfast took longer than ten minutes because everybody who crossed the courtyard had apparently been waiting for an opportunity to say happy birthday again.

Manuel hugged Leo hard enough to lift one shoulder off the ground. Dani handed him a wrapped package the size of a book and warned him not to open it until later because she wanted to leave before he could complain. Antonio had made something out of folded paper that seemed to require a five-minute explanation. Ramón announced his congratulations as if the rest of the compound had somehow missed the date. Isa kissed Leo’s cheek and gave him a small envelope.

Mercedes waited for him instead, which somehow made the whole thing more suspicious.

Leo approached her carefully.

“Abuela.”

She looked at him, then at Taiga, then back at Leo.

“*Twenty years.*”

Leo nodded.

“Sí.”

Mercedes sighed dramatically.

“*And still no improvement.*”

Taiga laughed before Leo could translate.

Mercedes noticed.

“*He understood.*”

“*Of course he did. You’re predictable.*”

Mercedes ignored him and pulled him down far enough to kiss both cheeks.

Then she looked at Taiga.

“*Take care of him today.*”

Taiga caught enough of that to answer.

“Sí.”

Leo looked between them.

“I don’t like how quickly this happened.”

Taiga sat down.

“You introduced us.”

“That was my first mistake.”

Breakfast was bread, eggs, cheese, fruit and coffee, but it kept expanding because Carmen kept bringing things out and because people kept wandering through. Taiga recognized one woman from the afternoon before and still could not remember whether she was technically related.

Leo seemed to know, at least.

At some point Carmen put another plate in front of him.

Leo looked at it.

“Moma, we’re leaving.”

“*You can leave after you eat.*”

“*I am eating*.”

“*That is why there is food in front of you.*”

Taiga drank his coffee.

Leo looked at him.

“Help.”

“No.”

“You’re supposed to take care of me today.”

“I am. Eat.”

Carmen pointed at Taiga.

“*Listen to him.*”

Leo stared at both of them.

“This house has turned against me.”

Taiga took another sip of coffee.

“Eat.”

“Worst birthday.”

Manuel laughed without knowing why.

---

They escaped a little before ten, although not before Carmen tried to send a bag of food with them. Leo refused it, so she put it directly into Taiga’s hands instead.

Taiga accepted it.

“You’re weak,” Leo said as they walked toward the gate.

“She feeds me.”

“She feeds everyone.”

“Exactly. I’m protecting the relationship.”

“With my mother.”

“Yes.”

“Great.”

Outside the compound, Leo stopped.

“Okay.”

Taiga looked at him.

“What?”

“I need you to trust me.”

“No.”

“You don’t even know what I’m asking.”

“That has never improved anything.”

Leo smiled.

“We’re going into the city.”

“That was already obvious.”

“Fine. I’m not telling you everything else.”

“You say that like I care.”

“You do.”

Taiga adjusted the small backpack on one shoulder.

Leo’s eyes dropped to it.

“What’s in there?”

“Stuff.”

“You brought stuff?”

“Yes.”

“What stuff?”

“None of your business.”

Leo narrowed his eyes.

Taiga started walking.

“Come on, birthday boy.”

Leo caught up.

“You’re suspicious.”

“You’re annoying.”

“Those aren’t mutually exclusive.”

They made their way toward the city without anybody driving them, which Taiga appreciated. Leo handled the route automatically, switching between directions, transport and small comments about where they were going.

By the time the streets changed around them, the morning had become hotter.

Taiga noticed the buildings first.

Color, balconies, stone, narrow streets and the sudden increase in people who were obviously not from the neighborhood they had left behind. Cameras appeared. So did groups following guides.

Leo looked over.

“You’re doing the tourist face.”

“I don’t have a tourist face.”

“You absolutely do.”

“What does it look like?”

“Judgmental.”

“That’s just my face.”

“Exactly.”

They came out near Parque Colón.

Taiga stopped long enough to look around properly.

The cathedral dominated one side. People crossed the square in every direction. Pigeons moved with the confidence of animals that had never experienced consequences.

Leo watched him.

“So?”

Taiga looked at him.

“So what?”

“First impression.”

“There are too many pigeons.”

Leo laughed.

“I brought you to the oldest part of the city and your review is pigeons.”

“They’re aggressive.”

“They’re birds.”

“One of them is looking at me.”

“It’s a pigeon.”

“It knows.”

Leo shook his head and kept walking.

Taiga followed.

This was not yesterday’s neighborhood. Here Leo actually had to decide where to go. He knew the area, but not in the same unconscious way he had known every turn near the house. He checked streets, changed direction once, then admitted he had been wrong before Taiga could accuse him.

“I thought it was the next one.”

“You’re the guide.”

“I’m not a guide.”

“You kidnapped me.”

“You agreed to come.”

“Under false pretenses.”

“You knew we were going somewhere.”

“That’s not information.”

Leo pointed ahead.

“Calle Las Damas.”

Taiga looked.

“And?”

“It’s old.”

“That’s your explanation?”

“It’s very old.”

“You’re terrible at this.”

“I told you I’m not a guide.”

They walked anyway.

Leo knew pieces of history in the way people often knew the history of places they grew up around: badly organized, attached to school trips, mixed with personal complaints. He remembered being dragged through one building in the heat when he was thirteen. He remembered Enzo getting yelled at for touching something he had been specifically told not to touch. He remembered a teacher making them write an essay about colonial architecture and half the class copying from the same website.

Taiga listened.

“That sounds more useful than the official version.”

“It is not.”

“It explains why you hate museums.”

“I don’t hate museums.”

“You complained for ten minutes.”

“I complained about that museum.”

“You’ve complained about three buildings.”

“Because I have memories.”

Taiga smiled.

“That’s called hating museums.”

They passed the Fortaleza Ozama and Leo slowed.

“You want to go in?”

Taiga looked toward the entrance.

“Do you?”

Leo made a face.

“No.”

“Then no.”

“You came all the way here.”

“I didn’t come here for the fort.”

Leo looked at him.

Taiga kept walking.

After a second, Leo followed.

“You say things like that and then act like I’m the corny one.”

“I said I don’t care about a fort.”

“Exactly.”

“That is not romantic.”

“To you.”

“Your standards are low.”

“They’re adapted.”

They wandered without much structure after that.

Leo bought water from a small shop and immediately complained about the price.

Taiga looked at the bottle.

“How much did they charge you?”

Leo told him.

Taiga stared.

“You got robbed.”

“I know.”

“You speak the language.”

“That doesn’t protect you from tourism.”

“You should be embarrassed.”

“I am.”

They found shade in Plaza de España and stayed longer than either of them intended.

Leo sat beside Taiga on a low wall, elbows resting on his knees. People crossed in front of them. A child chased bubbles from somebody’s toy. Music drifted from somewhere nearby and disappeared whenever the wind changed.

Taiga looked at the Alcázar.

“You’ve been in there?”

“Twice.”

“School?”

“Once. The other time family.”

“Good?”

“It has rooms.”

Taiga turned.

“Excellent review.”

“I’m trying not to ruin your sense of wonder.”

“You never gave me one.”

“That’s true.”

Leo leaned against his shoulder.

Taiga let him.

They stayed there until hunger made the decision for them.

Leo rejected the first place they passed because it was “for people who wanted to photograph lunch more than eat it.”

Taiga looked through the window.

“There are normal people in there.”

“They’re being financially abused.”

“You just paid tourist price for water.”

“That’s why I know.”

The second place was apparently too expensive.

The third had “bad vibes,” which Leo could not define.

Taiga stopped.

“You have rejected three restaurants.”

“Two restaurants and one trap.”

“Pick something.”

“I am picking.”

“No. You’re eliminating.”

“That’s part of picking.”

“Leo.”

“Okay, okay.”

They ended up walking a few streets away from the busiest part of the zone to a small place Leo said somebody from school had brought him to years earlier.

“Is it good?”

“I remember it being good.”

“How long ago?”

“Four years?”

“That’s useless.”

“Do you want lunch or an audit?”

“Both.”

The food was good enough that Taiga stopped complaining.

Leo noticed.

“You like it.”

“It’s fine.”

“You’re eating fast.”

“I’m hungry.”

“That means you like it.”

“That’s not how food works.”

“It is.”

Taiga pointed his fork at him.

“Birthday rules don’t change physics.”

“They change interpretation.”

“That’s worse.”

Leo grinned and reached for Taiga’s drink.

Taiga slapped his hand away.

“Get your own.”

“I have one.”

“Then use it.”

“Yours looked better.”

“They’re the same.”

“I wanted confirmation.”

“You’re twenty and still annoying.”

“You keep mentioning my age like I’m supposed to have transformed overnight.”

“I was hoping.”

“Sorry.”

They ate slowly after the first ten minutes, because neither had anywhere else to be.

That was new enough to feel noticeable without requiring comment.

At Olympus, even when they went somewhere together, there was usually a next thing waiting: class, training, homework, somebody else, the walk back, a deadline. Here the next thing was simply whatever Leo had refused to tell him.

Taiga looked at the backpack beside his chair.

Not yet.

Leo caught the movement.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That bag is suspicious.”

“It’s a bag.”

“You brought it for a reason.”

“Water.”

“You bought water.”

“Then I failed.”

Leo narrowed his eyes.

Taiga kept eating.

---

They reached the Malecón later in the afternoon.

The sea appeared before Taiga expected it to, broad and bright beyond the road. Wind cut through the heat just enough to make walking easier.

Leo looked pleased with himself.

Taiga looked at the water.

“Okay.”

“That’s all?”

“It’s the ocean.”

“Yes.”

“I’ve seen one.”

“You flew over this one yesterday.”

“Then I’ve seen it twice.”

Leo shoved his shoulder.

Taiga smiled.

They crossed toward a quieter stretch and eventually found somewhere to sit facing the water.

Traffic moved behind them. The wind made conversation slightly more annoying and Leo’s hair impossible.

Taiga watched him try to fix it twice.

“Give up.”

“I look insane.”

“You always look insane.”

“Birthday.”

“Still.”

Leo pushed his hair back again.

The wind immediately destroyed the effort.

Taiga laughed.

“Fuck you.”

“There he is.”

Leo leaned back on his hands.

For a while they watched the water.

Taiga had expected Leo to keep talking. Leo usually filled empty space unless he was tired, irritated or deliberately giving somebody room. Instead he seemed content to sit there with the wind and the traffic and Taiga beside him.

Taiga looked at the backpack again.

Leo noticed this time.

“You’re doing it again.”

Taiga sighed.

“Fine.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You clearly have something in there.”

Taiga pulled the backpack into his lap.

Leo sat up.

“What is it?”

“Stop asking.”

“It’s my birthday.”

“That argument is getting old.”

“It is literally one day old.”

Taiga unzipped the bag.

The book fit tightly between his water bottle and the folded food Carmen had forced on them.

He pulled it out.

Leo stopped talking.

The cover was simple. Dark, with no writing on it.

Taiga held it out.

“Here.”

Leo looked at the book, then at him.

“What is that?”

“A book.”

“I can see that.”

“Then why did you ask?”

Leo took it carefully.

The expression on his face had already become too serious for Taiga’s comfort.

“Open it.”

Leo did.

The first photo was not particularly good, which was exactly why Taiga had chosen it.

It was one of the earliest team pictures from the semester, taken after training when nobody looked ready for a camera. Leo was half turned toward somebody outside the frame. Enzo had one arm raised for no identifiable reason. Taiga himself looked irritated.

Leo laughed.

“Oh my God.”

“Keep going.”

The next pages were better.

A picture from campus. One from a team dinner. Another that had apparently been taken by Caleb because Taiga did not remember posing for it and Leo was leaning into the frame from behind him.

Leo looked up.

“Where did you get these?”

“People have phones.”

“That is not an answer.”

“I asked.”

“You asked people for photos?”

“Don’t make it weird.”

“I’m not making it weird.”

“You’re making the face.”

“What face?”

“The one where you’re about to say something weird.”

Leo looked back down.

There were championship photos later. One from Camp Buddy. A blurry shot from a bus that Taiga had nearly deleted because Leo’s eyes were closed, except the sunlight had hit his face in a way Taiga liked and he had kept it instead.

Leo stopped on that one.

“You took this.”

Taiga looked at the sea.

“Maybe.”

“When?”

“No idea.”

“You know exactly when.”

“I don’t.”

Leo smiled.

Taiga could feel it without looking.

Leo turned another page.

There were no captions, dates or explanations, only photographs. Some were good and some were stupid; one was almost entirely Enzo’s hand because he had apparently ruined the picture at the last second, while another showed Leo holding something Taiga could no longer remember as he argued with somebody off-camera.

The last one held the arcade photo. It was the first photo of just the two of them.

Taken from behind, both were leaning toward the shooter screen, Taiga’s plastic rifle angled left and Leo’s right, their shoulders almost touching.

Beside it, on the last empty page, Taiga had put one line.

*Sometimes I forget there was a version of Olympus where you weren’t here yet.*

Leo stopped.

Taiga looked toward the beach.

There was a lot of sand. Enough, probably, to bury himself in before Leo finished reading the sentence a second time.

“You remembered that.”

Taiga kept looking at the water.

“Unfortunately.”

“Taiga.”

“Don’t make it worse.”

Leo laughed quietly, but when Taiga finally glanced over, his expression had gone soft in exactly the way Taiga had been trying to avoid. Also, his eyes had gone suspiciously shiny.

Leo closed the book.

“You made me a photo book.”

“I ordered a photo book. I didn’t bind it by hand.”

“That distinction is really important to you.”

“Yes.”

“You went through all these pictures.”

“I had time.”

“You asked people for some of them.”

“They sent files. It was not difficult.”

“And you put that in there.”

Taiga frowned.

“It’s one sentence.”

“I know.”

“Don’t make it worse.”

Leo looked at him.

“Thank you.”

Taiga’s shoulders dropped slightly.

“Yeah.”

“I love it.”

“Good.”

“I’m going to show everyone.”

“No.”

Leo burst out laughing.

“No?”

“That is not a family presentation.”

“Moma is absolutely going to see this.”

“She can see it when I go back home.”

“Abuela too.”

“Absolutely not.”

“Enzo is in it.”

“That makes it worse.”

Leo put the book carefully beside him, away from the edge.

Then he leaned over and kissed Taiga.

Taiga let him.

The kiss lasted long enough that somebody walking past whistled.

Taiga pulled back and looked toward the sound.

Leo laughed.

“Tourist face.”

“I’m going to throw you into the ocean.”

“You’d have to catch me.”

“I’m faster.”

“Still false.”

Taiga reached for him.

Leo got up immediately.

“Don’t.”

“You said catch.”

“I was speaking conceptually.”

“Too late.”

Leo backed away laughing, and Taiga stood as if he intended to chase him. They made it only a few steps before Taiga remembered the book still sitting on the wall and pointed back at it.

“Birthday boy. Your emotional evidence.”

Leo retrieved it instantly.

“See? You care.”

“I paid for it.”

“Even better.”

Taiga rolled his eyes.

They started walking again.

Leo carried the book under one arm.

He did not put it in the backpack.

---

By the time they started heading home, Taiga had walked enough that he stopped pretending the heat was irrelevant.

Leo looked entirely too pleased about this.

“You’re sweating.”

“So are you.”

“Yes, but I’m used to it.”

“That doesn’t make your sweat better.”

“I never said it did.”

“You were thinking it.”

“You’ve learned my face.”

“Unfortunately.”

They stopped for something cold before leaving the center.

Leo checked his phone while they waited.

The screen filled with messages.

Taiga saw the number.

“Jesus.”

“It’s fine.”

“You have forty-seven unread.”

“Some are group chats.”

“That’s not better.”

Leo opened one message, typed something quickly and put the phone away.

“Done.”

“You answered one.”

“I’ll answer later.”

Taiga watched him take another drink, then let it go. It was his birthday; forty-seven messages seemed reasonable enough.

---

They knew something had changed before they reached the gate because the music was audible from halfway down the street.

Taiga stopped and looked at him.

“You said dinner.”

Leo listened.

“I may have used dinner as a category.”

“That is not a category.”

“It is for tonight.”

The gate opened before they reached it.

Antonio appeared first.

“Leo!”

He ran toward them, only to stop when he noticed the book under Leo’s arm and immediately ask about it. Leo lifted it out of reach.

“*Later*.”

Antonio objected at once.

Taiga smiled.

The courtyard looked like Saturday had reproduced.

More chairs had appeared. Two folding tables were covered with food, plates, cups and containers Taiga did not recognize. A speaker sat near one wall. A cooler was open beside Manuel. Someone had hung decorations across the covered part of the patio, not enough to transform the place but enough that nobody could pretend this was an ordinary Sunday.

There were already people Taiga had never seen.

Carmen came through one of the doors carrying a tray.

“Julio!”

Leo lifted both hands.

“*I’m here*.”

She crossed toward him, kissed his cheek again and immediately took the book from under his arm.

Taiga stared.

Leo looked at him.

“I told you.”

“That was robbery.”

Carmen had already opened it.

“*What is this?*”

Leo answered in Spanish.

Taiga understood his own name.

Carmen looked up at him, and whatever passed across her face made Taiga briefly consider leaving. Then she smiled.

“*Beautiful.*”

“...Gracias.”

She turned another page.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“This was supposed to be private.”

“She’s my mother.”

“That is not a defense.”

“It is here.”

Carmen reached the championship photos and called for Manuel.

Taiga closed his eyes.

Leo put an arm around his shoulders.

“You’re adorable when you regret life choices.”

“I’m killing you.”

“Later. There are guests.”

That part became obvious almost immediately.

People were arriving through the gate in pairs and groups, sometimes carrying food, sometimes drinks, sometimes nothing. Some hugged Leo. Some slapped his shoulder. Older women kissed his cheek and then looked directly at Taiga with open curiosity. Children moved between legs and chairs with no interest in introductions.

Taiga lost track after the fifth new person.

Leo tried to keep up with the introductions at first.

“This is my cousin—”

Another person interrupted before he finished, and when Leo started again with somebody else, a new arrival cut across that one too. He finally laughed and gave up.

“Never mind. You’ll learn.”

“That’s reassuring.”

“You learned the house.”

“Barely.”

“You’ll be fine.”

An older man Taiga had not met took Leo by the shoulders.

“Julio.”

Leo smiled and answered in Spanish.

Taiga caught something about university and football.

The man looked at Taiga.

Leo said his name. Then novio.

Taiga had started recognizing that word too quickly.

The older man shook his hand with both of his and said something long.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“He says welcome and that anybody who can tolerate me deserves food.”

“That I agree.”

Leo translated.

The man laughed and moved on.

Taiga looked at him.

“Who was that?”

Leo paused.

“My mother’s cousin.”

“You hesitated.”

“I was checking which side.”

“Your family tree needs a database.”

“I told you.”

A hand landed on Leo’s other shoulder.

“BIRTH CRIMINAL.”

Taiga turned.

Enzo stood there holding a paper bag in one hand and wearing sunglasses despite the fact that the sun was already dropping behind the walls.

Leo stared.

“What does that mean?”

“I don’t know yet.”

“You shouted it.”

“I committed before I developed the concept.”

Leo hugged him.

Enzo hugged back harder.

Then Enzo stepped away and looked at Taiga.

“You survived.”

“Barely.”

“Excellent.”

“You sound disappointed.”

“I had money on Thursday.”

Leo took the paper bag.

“What is this?”

“Nothing.”

“That is obviously something.”

“It is a legally neutral package.”

Taiga looked at Enzo.

“You two use legally wrong in exactly the same way.”

Enzo pointed at Leo.

“He learned it from me.”

“That explains a lot.”

Leo looked offended.

“I speak better English than both of you.”

“Impossible,” Enzo said. “I have confidence.”

“That is not language skill.”

“It is in America.”

Enzo took off his sunglasses and immediately walked toward Mercedes.

“Abuela!”

Mercedes looked up.

“Enzo.”

He bent down so she could kiss his cheek.

Then she said something that made him put one hand over his heart.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“What did she say?”

“She asked if he came because he loves me or because there’s food.”

Enzo answered from across the patio in English.

“Both!”

Mercedes laughed.

Dani appeared behind him.

“*You’re loud*.”

Enzo turned.

“*You got taller*.”

“*No*.”

“*You did*.”

“*No*.”

“*This is why nobody trusts science*.”

Dani walked past him.

Enzo looked at Taiga.

“She missed me.”

“She absolutely didn’t.”

“She did spiritually.”

Leo handed Enzo back the paper bag.

“What did you bring?”

“You can open it later.”

“You told Antonio later too,” Taiga said.

Leo pointed.

“See? Birthday oppression.”

Enzo looked at the photo book Carmen was still holding with Manuel now leaning over her shoulder.

His eyes narrowed.

“What is that?”

“No,” Taiga said.

Enzo’s face lit up.

“Oh, that’s definitely something.”

“Stay away.”

“Taiga made it,” Leo said.

“I ordered it.”

Enzo walked toward Carmen immediately.

Taiga grabbed the back of his shirt.

“No.”

Enzo looked over his shoulder.

“Why are you ashamed of love?”

“I’m not.”

“That grip says otherwise.”

Leo was laughing too hard to help.

Taiga let go.

“Fine. Go.”

Enzo did.

He reached the first team picture and immediately pointed.

“That was after Mercer made us run because somebody was late.”

Leo looked at him.

“You were late.”

“That detail is not relevant to the photograph.”

He turned another page.

“Oh, I hate this one.”

“Good,” Taiga said.

“You can see my hand.”

“You ruined the picture.”

“I improved the composition.”

Enzo kept going.

When he reached the Camp Buddy photo, his expression shifted just enough for Taiga to catch it. Enzo looked at him, then at Leo.

“Okay.”

Taiga narrowed his eyes.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Don’t do that.”

Enzo closed the book and handed it back to Leo.

“It’s good.”

Taiga stared.

“That’s it?”

“What do you want? Tears?”

“No.”

“Then we agree.”

Leo held the book against his chest.

Enzo looked at him.

“Put it inside before someone drops sauce on it.”

For once, that was sensible.

Leo disappeared upstairs with it.

Taiga watched him go.

Enzo leaned beside him.

“Good gift.”

“Yeah.”

“I mean it.”

“I know.”

Enzo looked toward the stairs.

Then at Taiga.

“You’re still weird.”

“Fuck off.”

“Missed you.”

---

The party grew without announcing that it had started.

There was no moment when everybody gathered and somebody said dinner was ready. Food simply became available in increasing quantities. Plates appeared. People ate a pastelito while standing and then forgot they were eating because somebody had arrived. Someone opened the cooler. Someone else changed the music.

Carmen moved through everything.

Manuel stayed near the food for a while, talking to two men Taiga did not know, then vanished to bring more chairs from somewhere. ---

By seven, the compound had become impossible to count. Taiga tried once, reached twenty-two, lost two children behind Ramón and gave up when another group arrived.

“This family is ridiculous.”

The music got louder after dark.

Dani complained about one song and then somehow became responsible for the speaker for twenty minutes. Enzo intervened twice and was removed from authority both times.

Leo found him.

The cake did not arrive when Taiga expected it to.

Mostly because he had stopped expecting anything to arrive on schedule.

By nine-thirty, people were still eating. At ten, somebody brought out another tray of food and Taiga briefly wondered whether the cake had been canceled.

Then the music stopped.

That got everybody’s attention faster than shouting would have.

Carmen appeared carrying the cake with Isa beside her, and the courtyard reorganized itself almost instantly. People crowded closer as phones came out; Antonio tried to claim a place in front until Dani shifted him sideways, while Leo was pushed into position behind the cake.

Taiga stayed with everyone else as the first song began. He recognized what was happening, but not the song itself. Everyone around him clearly did.

The melody spread across the courtyard immediately, older voices and younger ones joining without needing instruction. Carmen sang. Manuel sang. Mercedes sang from her chair. Enzo sang loudly enough to become individually identifiable, which seemed unnecessary.

Taiga listened closely enough to catch Leo’s name, birthday words and something about happiness. Behind the candles, Leo’s smile widened every time somebody got louder.

Taiga kept waiting for the part he knew, but the song reached its end without ever finding it. People clapped, and before he could decide whether that was it, the familiar melody of Cumpleaños feliz started.

Taiga stared. Apparently there were two birthday songs. Of course there were fucking two.

Across the cake, Leo caught his expression and nearly laughed.

Taiga narrowed his eyes at him.

Leo only smiled wider.

The second song finished under another round of clapping and overlapping voices. Leo started to lean toward the candles, but Antonio shouted something that stopped him halfway.

“What?”

Antonio repeated it, and Leo laughed before closing his eyes. Apparently he had forgotten the wish.

He stayed like that longer than Taiga expected, then opened his eyes and blew out the candles to another burst of cheering. Phones went up almost immediately.

Leo barely had time to straighten before Carmen was beside him. Manuel joined from the other side. Dani pulled Antonio forward. Mercedes stayed in her chair and ordered people around until somebody moved the cake table enough to fit her into the frame.

By eleven-thirty, Taiga had stopped trying to follow every conversation.

Progress.
