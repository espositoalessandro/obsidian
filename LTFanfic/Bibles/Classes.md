# Olympus Fanfic — Academic Program Bible
## Taiga and Leo — Revision Canon

This document defines the academic structure to use when revising Acts I–IV and planning Act V.

The purpose is not to turn the fic into a college-procedural story. Classes should provide ordinary-life texture, recurring social spaces, workload pressure, and occasional character opportunities. Soccer and romance remain the main framing elements; academic detail should stay readable and selective.

---

# 1. General principles

- Olympus follows a U.S.-style semester system.
- A four-course semester is the default load used in the story.
- Taiga begins university **undeclared** and uses his first semester to test different kinds of academic work.
- By the end of the fall semester, he chooses **Computer Science**.
- His spring schedule therefore becomes visibly more technical and STEM-focused.
- Not every course needs equal page time.
- The story should only dramatize classes when they provide one or more of:
  - useful character interaction;
  - a change of routine;
  - a memorable incident;
  - academic pressure;
  - a place to expose Taiga or Leo's strengths/flaws;
  - continuity for friendships outside soccer.
- Avoid repeatedly writing generic lecture scenes merely to prove that the characters attend class.

---

# 2. Taiga — Fall semester

Taiga starts Olympus undecided about his major. His first-semester schedule should feel deliberately mixed rather than random.

## Foundations of Computer Science

**Primary instructor:** Professor Anika Rao  
**Graduate assistant / lab support:** Dev  
**Recurring classmates:** Lina Park; Leo may overlap in parts of the course depending on section/lab structure.

### Function in the story
- Establishes Taiga's existing practical programming ability.
- Shows that he is not a total beginner, but still lacks some formal CS foundations.
- Gives Rao a reason to notice that Taiga asks unusually useful design questions.
- Becomes the strongest academic argument for declaring Computer Science.

### Useful subject matter
- variables and state;
- functions;
- recursion;
- references / aliasing;
- arrays / lists and simple collections;
- searching and introductory sorting algorithms;
- binary search;
- insertion sort and Quicksort at an introductory level;
- asymptotic complexity at a basic level;
- decomposition;
- choosing a representation rather than merely making code run.

### Continuity boundary
Formal graph representations and graph algorithms are **not** part of Taiga's fall Foundations course in the story. Trees, graphs, BFS / DFS and shortest-path material belong to **Data Structures in spring**, after Taiga has declared Computer Science.

### Taiga's profile in the course
- Fast at implementation.
- Strong practical intuition.
- Occasionally overengineers.
- Can become impatient with abstractions that seem unnecessary until he understands what problem they solve.
- Enjoys being challenged more than he initially admits.

## Calculus I

**Instructor:** Professor Mei Chen.

### Why this replaces Statistics
The current manuscript uses Statistics and Quantitative Reasoning in fall but later places Taiga directly into Calculus II. Replacing Statistics with Calculus I creates a clean prerequisite chain and avoids inventing placement credit solely to repair continuity.

### Function in the story
- Establishes Taiga's quantitative ability.
- Gives him a mathematically demanding course that is distinct from programming.
- Provides groundwork for Calculus II in spring.

### Useful subject matter
- limits;
- derivatives;
- rates of change;
- optimization;
- related rates;
- introduction to integration late in the term.

### Revision note
Existing Statistics scenes should be converted rather than mechanically deleted when they already serve a useful character purpose. Regression/statistics-specific dialogue can be replaced with Calculus I material while preserving the surrounding interaction.

### Professor continuity
The current **Professor Bell** attached to Statistics should not survive in this role. Bell is better retained elsewhere only if needed; see the continuity section below.

## General Chemistry I + Lab

**Lecture instructor:** Professor Dalia Hassan.  
**Lab graduate assistant:** Seth Miller.

### Why Chemistry replaces Writing Seminar
Chemistry adds a recurring academic setting in which things physically happen rather than every class being lecture, discussion, or typing.

It also allows the story to establish on-page the later anecdote that Leo once burned his sleeve in chemistry lab.

### Function in the story
- Gives Taiga and Leo a hands-on academic environment.
- Creates opportunities for lab partners, minor accidents, waiting, measurement, procedural frustration, and unexpected downtime.
- Exposes personality through practical work rather than dialogue alone.

### Useful subject matter
- measurement and significant figures;
- stoichiometry;
- atomic structure;
- bonding;
- solutions;
- thermochemistry;
- gases;
- introductory equilibrium concepts near the end of term.

### Taiga in lab
- Understands procedures quickly.
- Strong at measurement and sequence.
- Can become impatient with repetitive procedural safeguards.
- Wants to understand *why* a step matters rather than perform it blindly.
- May need to learn that experimental work cannot always be debugged as cleanly as code.

### Leo in lab
- Competent but more distractible socially.
- Can talk while working and occasionally lose track of a physical detail.
- The established sleeve-burning incident should occur here on-page.
- The accident should be minor and mundane, not a dramatic lab disaster.

### Revision requirement
Remove the existing Writing Seminar as one of Taiga's courses and redistribute or repurpose useful secondary characters/scenes from that course where appropriate.

## Political Institutions

**Instructor:** Professor Hall  
**Recurring classmate:** Nora

### Function in the story
This course should remain in fall even though Taiga later chooses a STEM-heavy path.

It reveals that Taiga's intelligence is not limited to coding or mathematics. He enjoys:
- attacking assumptions;
- testing premises;
- spotting contradictions;
- defending a position;
- forcing another person to justify a claim.

This is the best academic space for developing his friendship with Nora.

### Taiga's profile in the course
- Strong ideas.
- Fast conclusions.
- Sometimes fails to show intermediate reasoning.
- Can treat discussion like combat.
- Learns to make the reasoning between premise and conclusion visible.

### Hall's key teaching note
Hall's recurring criticism is essentially:

> I don't need you to think more slowly. I need you to show me the bridge.

This belongs to **Hall**, not Bell.

### Narrative value
Keeping one non-STEM course in fall makes Taiga's later choice of CS stronger. He chooses CS despite discovering that he is good at and genuinely enjoys another kind of intellectual work.

# 3. Taiga — Spring semester

By spring, Taiga has chosen Computer Science. The schedule should therefore become intentionally STEM-heavy.

**Lecture professor:** To be named.

### Function in the story

- Strongest recurring classroom overlap between Taiga and Leo.

### Useful subject matter
- linked lists;
- stacks and queues;
- trees;
- hash tables;
- graphs;
- BFS / DFS;

- complexity;
- implementation tradeoffs.

### Owen's role
Owen should be standardized as a **teaching assistant / lab instructor**, not Professor Owen.

He can:
- run labs;
- answer questions;
- leave comments on assignments;
- send overly long announcements;
- fight with classroom projectors;
- know students personally enough to become recurring.

A separate professor should own the formal lecture section.

# 4. Academic arc across the year

The two semesters should tell a visible story.

## Fall: exploration

Taiga takes:
- Foundations of Computer Science;
- Calculus I;
- General Chemistry I + Lab;
- Political Institutions.

The mix lets him discover:
- he likes programming enough to study it formally;
- he can handle university mathematics;
- he is competent in hands-on science but does not necessarily love it most;
- he genuinely enjoys intellectual argument outside STEM.

The point is **not** that CS is the only thing Taiga can do.

The point is that he actively chooses it.

## Spring: commitment

Taiga takes:
- Data Structures;
- Discrete Structures;
- Calculus II;
- General Chemistry II + Lab.

The schedule now looks like someone who has committed to a technical path.

This should be visible without characters repeatedly explaining it.

# 5. Nora after Political Institutions

Removing Comparative Political Theory from spring is intentional.

Nora should remain in Taiga's life **outside class**.

This improves their friendship because it demonstrates that they did not only interact because a timetable forced them into the same room.

Possible spring contexts:
- coffee;
- lunch;
- study breaks;
- campus events;
- mutual complaints about finals;
- Nora asking Taiga a football question;
- Taiga asking about one of Nora's classes;
- Oliver updates;
- invitations through Nora's wider friend group.

Do not invent another shared course solely to keep Nora present.

# 6. Leo — academic continuity rules

Leo is also a Computer Science student, but Taiga and Leo should **not** have identical schedules.

They may overlap in:
- Foundations of Computer Science, depending on lecture/lab section;
- General Chemistry I/II if both need the science sequence;
- Data Structures in spring.

They should also have independent courses and obligations so the university continues to feel larger than their relationship.

Leo's full timetable does **not** need to be designed unless a later scene requires it. The important continuity rule is that his university life remains independent from Taiga's: in spring, **Data Structures** is the main explicitly shared class, while other Leo coursework can remain off-page or be named only when story-relevant.

### Established Leo academic traits to preserve
- competent but not pristine programmer;
- code often works before it becomes elegant;
- may duplicate logic rather than abstract immediately;
- can overschedule himself;
- has group-project problems;
- can become genuinely irritated with reckless collaborators;
- sometimes loses track of practical details when doing too many things at once.

These flaws should remain visible in Acts III–IV.

# 9. Classes as character tools

Classes should be chosen for what they reveal.

## Foundations of CS
Reveals Taiga's technical instinct and tendency to overengineer.

## Calculus
Reveals discipline and quantitative confidence without requiring social drama.

## Chemistry
Reveals patience, procedural habits, practical mistakes, and Taiga/Leo differences in a physical environment.

## Political Institutions
Reveals Taiga's appetite for argument and creates Nora as an intellectual peer.

## Data Structures
Shows Taiga and Leo working in the same field while approaching problems differently.

## Discrete Structures
Shows that formal proof can frustrate Taiga in ways programming does not.

---

# 10. Revision guideline

When revising Acts I–IV, do not mechanically add classroom references every few chapters.

Instead:
- preserve enough mentions to establish the schedule;
- dramatize only the academically useful scenes;
- allow weeks to pass without seeing every course;
- let assignments/exams occasionally affect plans;
- make schedules constrain characters naturally;
- avoid retroactively inventing shared academic memories unless they have been seeded earlier.

Academic life should feel continuous even when it is off-page.
