# Act V — Structural Bible

Working structural bible for Act V.

This is **not yet a chapter-by-chapter outline**. It defines the act's thematic conflict, emotional logic, pacing, relationship function and major structural phases. Individual scenes, relatives, locations and exact chapter breaks can be developed later.

Act V is intended to be **at least as substantial as Act I, potentially longer**. The central pattern must emerge slowly enough that it feels like character discovery rather than diagnosis-by-plot.

---

# 1. Core Function of Act V

Acts I–IV are primarily Taiga-led development.

- **Act I — Introduction / belonging**
  - Taiga enters Olympus.
  - Core question: can he build a life here?

- **Act II — Insecurity / visibility**
  - Taiga's fear of being seen and socially exposed damages his closeness with Leo.
  - Core question: what happens when people notice something important about him?

- **Act III — Past / jealousy / recognition**
  - Taiga confronts Kieran and his own past.
  - Leo confronts jealousy and uncertainty about his place beside Taiga.
  - Mutual romantic and sexual attraction becomes explicit.

- **Act IV — Frustration / competition / definition**
  - Compressed conference run.
  - Taiga struggles with failure, pressure and recovery.
  - Taiga and Leo kiss and begin explicitly dating.

- **Act V — Leo / availability / being needed**
  - The narrative center shifts decisively onto Leo.
  - Taiga enters Leo's home world.
  - Leo's previously scattered traits become one coherent pattern.
  - Core question:

> **If Leo stops being constantly useful, available and emotionally productive for the people he loves, will he still be loved?**

Act V should explain Leo without reducing him to a diagnosis, a hidden fake personality, or a single traumatic cause.

The goal is not to reveal that Leo's existing personality is false.

The goal is to reveal **the cost of a real strength that has become compulsory**.

---

# 2. Leo's Core Pattern

Leo genuinely:
- likes people;
- likes crowds;
- likes talking;
- likes helping;
- likes remembering things about people;
- likes being involved;
- likes making plans with others;
- likes family life;
- likes being useful;
- likes being the person who shows up.

None of this should be retconned into performance.

The problem is not:

> Leo pretends to be social.

The problem is:

> **Leo has difficulty distinguishing "I want to show up" from "I am responsible for showing up."**

His instinct is to respond to interpersonal demand by moving toward it.

When someone needs something:
- he helps;
- he explains;
- he reassures;
- he translates;
- he includes;
- he fixes;
- he appears;
- he says yes.

When something relational feels unstable:
- he talks more;
- he tries harder;
- he tries to restore equilibrium.

This is one coherent behavior expressed across family, friendship and romance.

---

# 3. Existing Breadcrumbs That Act V Must Pay Off

Act V should feel like an explanation of things the reader has already seen, especially in Act II.

Established material includes:

## Social battery / five-minute resets
Leo genuinely enjoys crowded social environments.

However, when the accumulated input becomes too much, he sometimes slips away privately for roughly five minutes, gets silence, then returns.

Important:
- this is not crowd aversion;
- this is not evidence that his social personality is fake;
- the reset is a pressure-release valve;
- at Olympus, almost nobody knows about it;
- Taiga knows because he found Leo alone and Leo asked him not to tell anyone.

Act V should gradually reveal that the five-minute reset is not an isolated quirk.

It is evidence of how often Leo operates near his social limit.

## Overscheduling
Act II establishes that Leo can:
- promise multiple people different things;
- discover conflicts after the fact;
- look busy enough that others assume he has a plan;
- make cheerful answers about the future because they stop people asking.

He is **not an obsessive planner**.

His problem is not control through itineraries.

His problem is **overcommitment through people**.

## Family
Existing breadcrumbs establish:
- his mother checks on practical details repeatedly;
- his sister is close enough to recur naturally in conversation;
- his father expresses affection through practical behavior such as making breakfast;
- home is loud;
- extended family presence can be intense;
- Leo describes the environment affectionately;
- the family is supportive and involved;
- his mother calls him **rayito de sol**.

The family should not be rewritten into antagonists.

## Prior relationship
Leo's previous push-pull relationship reinforced his instinct to:
- talk;
- fix;
- reassure;
- chase;
- restore contact.

He has consciously learned one boundary from it:

> He will not keep chasing someone who repeatedly pulls him close and then pushes him away.

He has **not** yet learned the deeper lesson:

> Other people's emotional equilibrium is not always his responsibility.

Act V is where that second lesson becomes visible.

---

# 4. Thematic Image — Sunshine

Leo's mother calls him **rayito de sol**.

This can become an important thematic image.

Leo really is warm, energetic and socially generous.

The problem is not that the sunshine is fake.

The problem is that, somewhere along the way, Leo internalized something close to:

> **The sun is supposed to stay up.**

He can feel tired.

He can need silence.

He can not know what he wants.

He can not want to go somewhere.

He can disappoint somebody mildly.

He can stop performing energy.

Act V should allow Leo to discover that **sunset is not failure**.

Do not overstate this metaphor in dialogue or narration. It is primarily an authorial thematic guide.

---

# 5. Why This Happens Now

The central structural question is:

> Why does Leo confront this now, specifically because Taiga is there?

The answer:

## Taiga collapses Leo's worlds into one

Until Act V, Leo can largely compartmentalize.

At Olympus he is:
- Leo;
- student;
- footballer;
- social connector;
- friend;
- boyfriend.

At home he is:
- Julio;
- son;
- brother;
- grandson;
- nephew;
- cousin;
- old friend;
- familiar neighborhood figure.

For the first time, Taiga enters **Julio's world**.

Leo cares enormously about the visit going well.

He wants:
- Taiga to like Santo Domingo;
- Taiga to feel comfortable;
- his family to like Taiga;
- Taiga to understand where he came from;
- old friends and relatives to meet him;
- his birthday to be good;
- family time after months away;
- time alone with Taiga;
- time with people who have missed him.

The result is not that Taiga creates Leo's problem.

Taiga's presence creates enough overlapping demands that **Leo's existing strategy becomes impossible to hide**.

---

# 6. Taiga as Trigger, Not Therapist

Taiga must not "solve" Leo through superior emotional insight.

His role is observational and relational.

He notices what Leo cannot initially see because Taiga is:
- comfortable with saying no;
- resistant to unnecessary dependence;
- intolerant of being babysat;
- capable of sitting in silence;
- increasingly capable of telling someone directly when he is angry or uncomfortable;
- not someone who needs constant emotional maintenance.

Taiga should initially interpret Leo's activity practically.

At first:

> Leo is doing all this because I'm a guest.

That is a reasonable conclusion.

Taiga is:
- in another country;
- meeting a large family;
- navigating language differences;
- learning a city;
- unfamiliar with household rhythms.

Leo naturally helps him.

## Crucial progression

As Taiga becomes comfortable, **Leo stops babysitting him**.

Do not have Leo keep hovering over Taiga after it is no longer necessary.

That would be out of character for both of them.

Taiga quickly becomes more independent because he hates unnecessary dependence.

He learns:
- who people are;
- where things are;
- basic household routines;
- enough Spanish / contextual language to navigate simple interactions;
- local routes and familiar places;
- how to function around the family without Leo translating every moment.

Leo recognizes that Taiga is fine and backs off.

But the energy does not disappear.

It goes somewhere else.

Leo:
- helps his sister;
- handles something for his mother;
- agrees to an aunt's request;
- answers a cousin;
- meets an old friend;
- fixes somebody's practical problem;
- joins another family plan;
- reshuffles his own time around other people.

This gives Taiga the crucial realization:

> **So it wasn't because of me.**

That is the point where the pattern becomes observable.

---

# 7. Leo's Family Environment

Leo's home should feel:
- crowded;
- affectionate;
- loud;
- porous;
- multigenerational or extended-family-heavy;
- socially alive;
- difficult to reduce to a neat household unit.

The intended emotional comparison is the **Casa Madrigal feeling from Encanto**:
- people always around;
- relatives appearing naturally;
- shared food;
- overlapping conversations;
- weak boundaries between private and communal space;
- family members knowing one another's business;
- affection expressed through involvement.

Do **not** literally reproduce Encanto or build a magical-house analogue.

The eventual family structure should be culturally plausible for a Dominican Santo Domingo setting.

Possible spatial models to consider later:
- large family house with several generations;
- relatives occupying different floors;
- nearby family homes with constant movement between them;
- a mixture of blood relatives, godparents and family friends treated socially as family.

The house should function as a social ecosystem.

## Family tone

The family is not the villain.

They may be:
- pushy;
- intrusive;
- loud;
- opinionated;
- aggressively supportive;
- comfortable assuming Leo will participate.

But they also:
- love him deeply;
- miss him;
- want to feed him;
- want time with him;
- are proud of him;
- are excited about Taiga;
- are not deliberately trying to exhaust him.

A critical late-act realization should be:

> **Some of the pressure is real. Some of it is self-imposed because Leo rarely gives them the chance to accept "no."**

The family may discover Leo's boundaries at the same time Leo begins discovering them himself.

---

# 8. Julio vs. Leo

Taiga already knows Leo's legal given name is **Julio**.

Act V must not treat this as a factual reveal.

The emotional discovery is hearing the name live inside its real context.

At Olympus:
- almost everyone calls him Leo.

At home:
- family naturally calls him Julio.

Taiga gets to experience:
- the voice Leo responds to at home;
- childhood stories attached to Julio;
- habits that predate Olympus;
- family jokes;
- photographs;
- old football history;
- Enzo's shared pre-university context;
- versions of Leo that Taiga did not create and cannot fully possess.

This supports the act's larger purpose:

> Taiga is not merely visiting Leo's country. He is meeting the person Leo was before Olympus.

---

# 9. Relationship Function of Act V

Act IV establishes:

> **I want you.**

Act V should deepen this into:

> **I can rest beside you.**

Until the weekend trip, Taiga and Leo have never truly gone anywhere alone.

There are always other people.

- Olympus: teammates, classmates, roommates, friends.
- Camp Buddy: staff, scouts, chores, Kieran, shared spaces.
- Leo's home: family, old friends, obligations.
- Even many private moments happen between responsibilities.

The short trip in Act V becomes the first environment containing only:

> **Taiga + Leo**

with no one else waiting for either of them.

This is a new intimacy threshold.

It does not require another formal romantic milestone.

Its importance comes from the absence of external demand.

---

# 10. Act Length and Calendar Shape

Act V should cover roughly **five weeks**.

Current calendar entry point:
- **Fri May 22, 2026** — Taiga arrives to begin the visit.
- **Sun May 24** — Leo's birthday.

Do not lock an exact Act V end date yet.

Working shape:

| Phase | Approximate duration | Function |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Arrival / birthday / immersion | ~1 week | Establish Leo's home world as joyful and desirable |
| Taiga becomes independent | ~1 week | Remove "hosting Taiga" as explanation for Leo's energy use |
| Pattern recognition / denial | ~1 week | Taiga sees aggregate cost; Leo rationalizes individual demands |
| Short trip away together | 2–3 days | Environmental proof: pressure drops |
| Return home / active growth | ~1.5–2 weeks | Leo experiments with boundaries in the environment that triggers the pattern |

The act should likely support **14–17 chapters**, possibly more if scene density justifies it.

Do not force the chapter count in advance.

The important requirement is pacing:
- pattern before explanation;
- repetition before confrontation;
- proof before acceptance;
- practice before resolution.

---

# 11. Phase One — Arrival / Birthday / Honeymoon

### Approx. May 22 onward

Taiga arrives shortly before Leo's birthday.

The first stretch should feel overwhelmingly positive.

Goals:
- introduce Santo Domingo through Leo rather than tourism exposition;
- introduce the house as a living ecosystem;
- allow Taiga to meet family gradually;
- show Leo at home;
- let Taiga hear **Julio** naturally;
- establish childhood / teenage traces of Leo;
- make Taiga's temporary dependence on Leo completely reasonable;
- make Leo's energy look like one of his greatest strengths.

## Leo's birthday — May 24

The birthday is an important family/social event.

It should:
- justify Taiga's timing;
- bring multiple parts of Leo's home life together;
- make Taiga's presence as boyfriend concrete;
- remain joyful rather than functioning as the immediate crisis.

Do **not** reveal the act's central problem too aggressively during the birthday.

The reader should primarily think:

> Of course Leo comes from this.

The family environment should explain his warmth before it explains his exhaustion.

---

# 12. Phase Two — Taiga Integrates

Taiga should become increasingly autonomous.

Examples of progression:
- knows where breakfast things are;
- knows which relative is being discussed without needing explanation;
- survives simple conversations without Leo;
- spends time with Leo's sister / cousin / relative independently;
- knows how to get somewhere familiar;
- understands household patterns;
- can be left alone without becoming a hosting problem.

Leo responds appropriately:
- he stops managing Taiga's adaptation;
- he trusts him;
- he returns attention to the wider family/social ecosystem.

This phase is where Taiga begins seeing the transfer of energy.

At first, this should remain neutral or funny.

Leo is simply:
- busy;
- wanted;
- involved;
- useful.

Do not tell the reader what it means yet.

---

# 13. Phase Three — Pattern Recognition

The pattern becomes visible through accumulation.

No single request should look unreasonable.

That is essential.

Each individual explanation makes sense:

> My sister needs help.

> My aunt asked.

> I haven't seen him since Christmas.

> Mom needs me to pick something up.

> My cousin wanted to go.

> I said I'd help.

> They're family.

Taiga sees the **aggregate** before Leo does.

Potential indicators:
- Leo says he is tired, then agrees to another thing;
- Leo's five-minute resets happen more often;
- he becomes shorter / snappier when depleted;
- he forgets something minor;
- two commitments collide;
- he automatically starts solving the collision instead of dropping one;
- a small disappointment from someone else affects him disproportionately;
- he claims he is resting while still participating socially;
- he keeps answering demands because each one is individually defensible.

## Taiga's first attempts

Taiga should not immediately deliver a psychological interpretation.

His first observations are concrete:

> "You look fucking tired."

Later:

> "You know you don't have to do everything people ask you to do, right?"

Leo initially resists because from his perspective Taiga is misreading affection as burden.

A useful core disagreement:

> **Leo:** They're my family.

> **Taiga:** I know.

Taiga is not asking Leo to withdraw from them.

Leo hears criticism of the relationships themselves because he does not yet separate:
- loving someone;
- helping someone;
- being constantly available to someone.

This produces a **small but real confrontation** before the trip.

Do not make it a giant rupture.

They love each other and are already capable of repair.

The conflict is:
- Taiga sees a pattern;
- Leo sees unrelated normal interactions;
- Leo believes Taiga is overinterpreting;
- Taiga cannot prove an aggregate problem from inside the same environment.

---

# 14. The Weekend Trip — Structural Hinge

The short trip should last **2–3 days maximum**.

Destination is not yet locked.

Likely requirements:
- reachable without an expensive international flight;
- plausible for two university students without much money;
- enough physical separation from the household / old social network to reduce demand sharply;
- visually and emotionally distinct from Santo Domingo.

The trip is **not the final resolution**.

It is the act's natural experiment.

## Function

Before the trip:

> Taiga sees the pattern. Leo does not.

During the trip:

> **Leo experiences the absence of the pattern.**

After the trip:

> Leo can finally recognize it happening.

That is the structural hinge of Act V.

---

# 15. What Changes on the Trip

This is the first time Taiga and Leo are truly away together with nobody else.

Remove:
- family;
- teammates;
- classmates;
- camp staff;
- old friends;
- people expecting attendance;
- people needing help;
- household social monitoring.

What remains:
- Taiga;
- Leo;
- ordinary practical needs.

Taiga is unusually undemanding.

He:
- says when he wants something;
- says when he does not;
- tolerates silence;
- can entertain himself;
- does not interpret every mood as relational danger;
- does not need Leo to create a good time every minute;
- will directly say if something is wrong.

Leo therefore has almost nothing to monitor.

## The change should appear physically before intellectually

Possible signs:
- Leo sleeps longer;
- talks more slowly;
- leaves his phone somewhere without noticing;
- checks messages less;
- misses a vague plan and does not immediately replace it;
- sits doing nothing;
- does not disappear for his usual five-minute reset;
- becomes less irritable;
- stops scanning for who needs what;
- loses track of time harmlessly.

Important:

> **The five-minute reset disappears because there is nothing to reset from.**

That is one of the strongest pieces of evidence available.

Eventually Leo notices.

A possible conceptual beat:

> "I haven't done the balcony thing once."

Taiga does not need to say "I told you so."

The evidence belongs to Leo's body.

---

# 16. The First Experience of Pressurelessness

The trip should establish something Leo may never have experienced in this form:

> Being close to someone he loves without needing to anticipate them.

This can initially feel strange.

Leo comes from an environment where prolonged silence can imply:
- nobody is home;
- something is wrong;
- the social system has stopped.

With Taiga, he can discover:

> **Silence does not mean absence.**

Taiga can be beside him without asking anything from him.

That gives silence a new meaning:

> Taiga is here.

This is a major relationship development.

Leo begins to understand why he increasingly seeks Taiga out when depleted.

Taiga does not require:
- humor;
- energy;
- constant talking;
- emotional performance;
- perfect hosting;
- immediate repair.

Taiga can think Leo is being an asshole and still remain beside him.

That directly challenges Leo's old relational conditioning.

---

# 17. Reframing the Prior Relationship

The deeper ex history can be revealed during or after the weekend realization.

Do not bring the ex physically into the plot unless a later outline creates a compelling reason.

The purpose is retrospective coherence.

Leo can begin to recognize:

- his ex withdrew;
- Leo chased;
- his ex became jealous;
- Leo reassured;
- his ex destabilized closeness;
- Leo tried harder to stabilize it;
- Leo became accustomed to treating another person's emotional state as a problem requiring action.

This should reframe the Act II joke about a missed text becoming a three-day crisis.

It also reframes Pinetree.

Leo consciously understood:

> I will not chase someone who keeps pushing me away.

Now he can begin understanding the broader pattern:

> **Why do I assume closeness means I have to keep the other person okay?**

Do not make the ex the sole origin of the behavior.

The relationship **reinforced** something already compatible with Leo's family/social role and natural temperament.

---

# 18. The Important Realization

The weekend does not prove:

> Leo's family is the problem.

It proves:

> **Leo's level of internal pressure is environmentally responsive.**

Same Leo.

Same boyfriend.

Same summer.

Same country.

Remove everyone else's expectations for forty-eight hours and:
- he is less tired;
- less reactive;
- less vigilant;
- less compelled to disappear;
- more capable of doing nothing.

This gives Leo reason to reconsider Taiga's earlier observations.

Crucially, Leo reaches this conclusion because **he experiences the contrast himself**.

Taiga provides the trigger.

Leo owns the realization.

---

# 19. Return Home — Where the Real Growth Happens

The trip should end around the structural midpoint / later midpoint of the act, not at the end.

They return to Leo's house.

Immediately:
- someone calls for Julio;
- family wants stories / photos;
- somebody needs something;
- a message arrives;
- old social patterns resume.

Before the trip, only Taiga could see Leo's operating system.

After the trip:

> **Leo can feel it switching back on.**

This begins the active growth phase.

---

# 20. What "Fixing It" Means

The goal is **not**:

> Leo learns to say no.

That is too simple.

The deeper goal is:

> **Leo learns to know why he is saying yes.**

Healthy outcomes include all three:

> "Yeah, I want to."

> "Not today."

> "No."

His generosity should remain intact.

His family involvement should remain intact.

His social energy should remain intact.

What changes is the automatic assumption of obligation.

---

# 21. Growth Must Be Messy

Do not resolve this in one conversation.

Leo should experiment.

Possible progression:

## First attempts
He says no but overexplains.

> "I can't because Taiga and I were going to—"

Then recognizes that he is constructing an external justification.

Eventually:

> "Actually, no. I just don't want to go."

That should feel enormous.

## Later attempts
- says yes automatically;
- realizes it later;
- goes back and changes the answer;
- feels guilty;
- discovers the relationship survives.

## Failure
After several good days:
- he overcommits again;
- comes home exhausted;
- Taiga notices;
- Leo catches Taiga noticing.

The significance is that **Leo has started recognizing the pattern himself**.

## Family response
Some relatives may:
- complain;
- tease;
- ask why;
- be mildly disappointed.

But the world does not collapse.

Others may simply say:

> Okay. Tomorrow.

That can be destabilizing in a useful way.

Leo discovers that people often could have tolerated boundaries all along.

---

# 22. Family's Part in the Growth

The story must hold two truths simultaneously:

1. Leo's family really can be demanding, intrusive and accustomed to his availability.
2. Leo has also trained them to expect that availability by rarely refusing.

Do not absolve the family of all contribution.

Do not blame them for everything.

The family system and Leo's own behavior have reinforced each other.

A particularly useful realization for a parent / close relative could be:

> They did not fully understand how tired Leo became because Leo rarely showed them.

Conceptual line:

> "He's always been like that. If everybody else was having fun, Julio was happy."

The hidden question:

> What if Julio wasn't?

The relative may genuinely not know.

Not because they never cared.

Because Leo rarely gave them the information.

---

# 23. Taiga's Growth Inside Leo's Arc

Act V is Leo-centered, but Taiga should still develop.

His function is not static support.

Taiga must learn:
- how to support without taking control;
- that seeing a pattern does not mean he can force Leo to acknowledge it;
- to tolerate Leo rejecting an observation;
- to remain present without escalating every disagreement;
- that helping someone can mean allowing them to make the same mistake twice;
- to distinguish concern from management.

This matters because Taiga himself hates being controlled.

He should not become controlling when Leo is struggling.

A major sign of Taiga's growth:

> He can say "You're doing it again" without turning that sentence into a demand that Leo immediately obey him.

---

# 24. Relationship Lesson

Leo's earlier relationship taught him:

> If someone I love is upset, I need to fix it before they leave.

Taiga can challenge this simply by being Taiga.

Taiga may:
- be annoyed;
- say so;
- need space;
- call Leo an asshole;
- remain in the relationship;
- sit beside him afterward;
- continue loving him.

Leo gradually learns:

> **Someone can be unhappy with me and still love me.**

This is one of the deepest Act V relationship lessons.

It should emerge through repeated behavior rather than one perfect speech.

---

# 25. Desired End-State for Leo

By the end of Act V, Leo should have a coherent self-understanding.

He should understand that:
- his sociability is real;
- his generosity is real;
- his family love is real;
- his exhaustion is also real;
- needing temporary quiet does not invalidate being social;
- being useful is not the price of belonging;
- he does not have to fix every interpersonal disturbance;
- other people can tolerate disappointment;
- he can choose whether he wants to participate;
- rest is not abandonment;
- silence with Taiga is not emptiness;
- someone can love him while requiring nothing from him.

He should **not** be cured.

He should still:
- overschedule occasionally;
- say yes too fast;
- help too much;
- enjoy being involved;
- need resets;
- get tired;
- make mistakes.

The difference is awareness and choice.

---

# 26. Desired End-State for Taiga and Leo

By the end of Act V, their relationship should have crossed a new intimacy threshold.

Not necessarily:
- engagement;
- permanent promises;
- another large romantic label.

Instead:

> They have learned that the relationship can contain rest.

Taiga knows:
- Leo does not need to perform around him;
- Leo's silence does not mean withdrawal;
- helping Leo sometimes means doing nothing.

Leo knows:
- Taiga can be disappointed without leaving;
- Taiga can coexist with him without requiring entertainment;
- he is allowed to be boring, tired, quiet or unavailable;
- Taiga remains.

Core relationship result:

> **Act IV: I want you.  
> Act V: I can rest beside you.**

---

# 27. Possible Final Symbolic Beat

A late-act scene should demonstrate growth through something tiny.

Someone calls for Leo / Julio from elsewhere in the house.

Old Leo would:
- get up;
- answer automatically;
- ask what is needed;
- go.

Newer Leo can remain where he is and answer simply:

> "I'm not coming."

No elaborate excuse.

No fake assignment.

No promise to compensate later.

Then he stays.

The importance is not refusal itself.

The importance is:

> **He believes the relationship will survive his absence.**

This is a possible endpoint image, not mandatory final prose.

---

# 28. Cultural / Setting Guardrails

Before detailed outlining:
- research Dominican family structures and household norms rather than importing a generic "Latino family" template;
- research Santo Domingo neighborhoods, transportation, food, weather, speech rhythms and everyday social life;
- avoid turning the setting into tourist exposition;
- show Leo's Santo Domingo through places that matter to him personally as well as major public locations;
- build family members as individuals rather than functions in Leo's psychology;
- preserve variation inside the family;
- avoid treating loudness, closeness or multigenerational living as inherently dysfunctional.

The cultural environment should feel like a real home that Leo loves.

The conflict is about **how Leo participates in relationships**, not about Dominican culture being pathological.

---

# 29. Vacation Guardrails

The short trip should be:
- **2–3 days maximum**;
- affordable;
- geographically plausible;
- already desired for ordinary couple reasons, not prescribed as therapy;
- the first trip they take entirely on their own.

Do not characterize Leo as obsessive about itineraries.

He may:
- know some places he wants to see;
- have loose ideas;
- improvise;
- change plans;
- forget practical details.

The emotional function is not learning spontaneity.

It is experiencing **low interpersonal demand**.

Destination can be chosen later after research.

---

# 30. Pacing Guardrails

Act V must take its time.

Do not:
- show Leo visibly breaking down immediately after Taiga arrives;
- make Taiga identify the entire pattern in the first week;
- explain the five-minute resets too early;
- make the birthday secretly miserable;
- make the family obviously toxic;
- make every family scene serve the conflict;
- front-load the ex backstory;
- make the weekend a magical cure;
- end immediately after the weekend realization;
- make Leo's growth perfectly linear.

Do:
- spend real time enjoying the house and family;
- let mundane scenes accumulate meaning;
- make Taiga's increasing independence visible;
- let Leo's energy redirect naturally;
- make individual demands reasonable;
- allow the aggregate to become the problem;
- let Taiga notice before he understands;
- let Leo resist before he has evidence;
- use the weekend as contrast / proof;
- return home for substantial post-realization material;
- let Leo practice new behavior repeatedly.

The reader should ideally be able to look backward and think:

> It was there from the beginning.

---

# 31. Structural Summary

Act V progression:

> **immersion → belonging → independence → redistribution → pattern → denial → confrontation → separation from pressure → recognition → explanation → return → practice → relapse → choice**

Or emotionally:

> Taiga meets Julio's world.  
> Taiga sees what Julio gives it.  
> Leo refuses to believe the giving costs him.  
> They leave the world briefly.  
> Leo discovers how different he feels without its demands.  
> They return.  
> Leo learns that love survives when he stops giving everything automatically.

Core Act V statement:

> **Leo does not need to become less loving.  
> He needs to stop treating love as a permanent obligation to be available.**
