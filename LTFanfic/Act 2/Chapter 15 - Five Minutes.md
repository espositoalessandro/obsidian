When Taiga returned to Olympus, it looked smaller. Not physically—the tower remained obnoxiously tall, the Academic Zone still contained enough pale stone to build a medieval city, and his residence hall still required three separate turns from the station even though Taiga knew perfectly well where it was—but the campus no longer expanded in his head every time he looked at it.

He knew where things ended now. He knew which paths mattered, which doors stuck in cold weather, that the central kiosk would reopen at seven tomorrow and the smaller dining hall would probably have soup because Monday was soup day. 

Taiga opened the room 317 door at four-twenty and found Caleb kneeling beside the mini-fridge with a thermometer in one hand.

“Of course.”

Caleb looked over. “Hi.”

“Hi. What happened?”

“Nothing.”

“Then why are you measuring the refrigerator?”

“I unplugged it before leaving.”

“Okay.”

“It needs to stabilize before I put food back.”

Taiga looked at the three containers sitting on Caleb's desk. “You brought food from home.”

“Yes.”

“Your mother?”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

Caleb looked at the containers. “Too much.”

Taiga smiled. “Good break?”

Caleb stood. “Mostly. Maya came for New Year. My mother asked about graduate school four times.”

“You are a freshman.”

“I know.”

“Did you tell her that?”

“Yes.”

“Did it work?”

“No.”

“Excellent.”

Caleb put the thermometer on the shelf, then looked at Taiga's suitcase.

“How was Camp Buddy?”

“Still there.”

“Yoichi?”

“Also still there.”

“Yuki?”

Taiga narrowed his eyes. “Why do you remember the dog before half the people?”

“Dogs are easier.”

“Fair.”

Five minutes later, Caleb asked whether Taiga had used the last of the printer paper before break. Normal life resumed, and Taiga unpacked.

Soccer resumed Tuesday. Training was training: cold, wet, and forty minutes of Taiga discovering that three weeks away had not magically improved his first touch.

Mercer told everybody they looked like they had eaten too much over break. Enzo accused him of profiling; Mercer told Enzo specifically that he had eaten too much over break, and Enzo admitted this was true.

They ran. They played. Taiga got nutmegged once and successfully pretended it had not happened. 

The locker room afterward was nearly empty by the time Taiga finished showering. He had stayed under the hot water longer than necessary because the walk back to Residence was going to be miserable and heat was currently free.

When he came out, towel around his waist, most of the team had already gone. Scott was closing his locker, Aaron was arguing with someone on the phone about train tickets, and Leo stood three lockers down from Taiga wearing black training shorts and nothing else, his hair still wet from the shower and darker at the ends.

Taiga opened his locker.

Behind him, Leo said, “Shit.”

Taiga did not turn. “What?”

“I did this to myself.”

“Useful explanation.”

“My chain. Hang on.”

Metal clicked faintly as Leo continued talking. “I took it off before training and somehow managed to get the clasp twisted into the little loop, and now my fingers are frozen and every time I try to fix it I make it worse. This is why tiny engineering should be illegal. Nobody needs objects this small.”

Taiga turned. Leo was holding a thin silver chain between both hands, a small flat pendant hanging at the bottom.

“Give it.”

Leo looked up. “You can fix it?”

“It's a clasp, not surgery.”

“That is exactly the confidence I need right now.”

He handed it over. The chain had twisted around itself near the clasp, and Taiga worked it loose in three seconds.

“There.”

“You're a hero.”

“No.”

Leo took it, looked at the clasp and then at his own hands. “Okay, second problem.”

Taiga already regretted participating. “What?”

“Can you get it? I swear my fingers stop functioning below five degrees, and if I drop this thing again I'm going to step on it and then I have to explain to my mom why I destroyed something she has asked me not to destroy approximately eighty times.”

Taiga stared. “You want me to put it on you.”

Leo blinked, “Yeah?”

Taiga held out his hand. “Turn around.”

Leo smiled. “See, this is why I keep you around.”

Leo sat on the bench and turned his back to him. Taiga stepped closer and lifted the chain around his neck, his fingers catching damp hair.

“Hold this.”

Leo gathered it forward with one hand. Taiga found the clasp—tiny hook, tiny loop—and his knuckles brushed the back of Leo's neck.

Leo went still. Taiga's hand stopped too.

“Everything good?” Leo asked.

“Yeah.”

Taiga tried the clasp again. It slipped.

“Shit—”

The necklace dropped, and Taiga lunged for it before it could hit the floor. His hand closed around the chain at the exact same moment the towel around his hips came loose.

It hit the floor.

Leo turned at the sudden movement and froze. His eyes were met with Taiga's nakedness basically 20 centimeters away.

"Oh-"

He turned back with the same speed

Taiga grabbed the towel from the floor and wrapped it back around his waist. “Fuck.”

For a few seconds neither spoke. Then Leo, still facing the lockers, asked, “Did you catch it?”

Taiga looked at the chain still clenched in his hand. “Yeah.”

“Good.”

Taiga stepped behind him again. “Stay still.”

“Trying.”

Leo's ears were definitely red. Taiga's face, though, was probably redder.
This time the clasp closed.

“Done.”

Leo touched the pendant. “Thanks.”

“Yeah.”

Taiga stepped back and found his shirt. Leo stood, then seemed to reconsider looking directly at him and reached for his own shirt instead. They dressed in silence.

A few seconds later Leo said, “For the record, I blame the necklace.”

Taiga looked over. “For what?”

“Uh, for flashing me?”

Taiga felt the heat coming back again.

"I didn't flash you."

"I know, that's why I blamed the necklace!"

Taiga stared at him, then laughed once. “Fine.”

Leo smiled, still visibly embarrassed. “Excellent. Official cause established.”

They finished dressing.

Apollo parties were less terrible than Taiga expected and worse than Leo apparently believed.

The common lounge had been rearranged, with couches pushed against walls and tables covered with snacks. Music was loud enough to matter but not loud enough to make conversation impossible, and people from soccer, track, swimming, volleyball and at least two sports Taiga could not identify occupied every visible surface. The House sun emblem above the fireplace had acquired a paper party hat.

Taiga stopped inside the doorway.

“Forty-one!”

Enzo appeared immediately.

“No.”

“You came!”

“I can leave.”

“Too late.”

Enzo handed him a plastic cup. Taiga looked at it.

“What is this?”

“Punch.”

“Alcohol?”

“Some.”

“How much?”

Enzo frowned. “This is why nobody invites engineers to joy.”

“I'm not an engineer.”

“Exactly. Even worse.”

Taiga smelled the cup. Mostly fruit. He took a small drink, found it too sweet, and kept it anyway.

Aaron was near the kitchen talking to Sofia. Gav sat on the arm of a couch with the expression of someone who had already decided when he was leaving. Scott was playing cards with two people from track, Mia stood beside the window arguing with another CS student about whether a game counted as strategy if the correct strategy was obvious, and Leo was near the center of the room talking to three people from volleyball and somebody Taiga recognized vaguely from the library. Dark green sweater, that silver chain visible at the collar.

Leo saw him and lifted one hand over the crowd. Taiga lifted his cup back, then Leo returned to the conversation and Taiga went toward Aaron.

The next hour happened without requiring him to survive anything. He talked to Aaron about absolutely nothing; Sofia told him a story about her younger brother breaking a window with a football and attempting to blame weather; Scott taught Taiga a card game badly enough that Gav eventually intervened; Enzo changed the music three times and was booed twice. At some point somebody from track recognized Taiga from the first-team fixture and said, “You're the redhead winger, right?” Taiga answered yes. That was the entire interaction.

Leo passed through the room repeatedly. Sometimes they spoke, sometimes they didn't. Once he appeared beside Taiga long enough to steal a chip from his plate.

“Get your own.”

“These taste better because they're stolen.”

“That's not a thing.”

“Crime adds seasoning.”

Then someone called Leo from across the room and he disappeared again. Later, a woman from the track team caught Leo by both shoulders and pulled him into a photograph with six people. Taiga watched long enough to see Leo throw an arm around her shoulders for the picture, then looked back at Sofia's story.

At ten-fifteen, the room became louder without the music changing. More people had arrived, the heat had built, and someone had opened the kitchen window despite snow outside. Taiga's social battery, which had apparently been issued at forty percent capacity, reached zero.

He did not announce it. He simply left the lounge. He didn't go back to the room, though.

The upstairs hall was quieter. He passed the empty film room and reached the small balcony door at the end of the corridor, which stood slightly open with cold air moving through the gap. Taiga pushed it wider and stopped.

Leo was already outside, standing with both forearms resting on the railing and looking over the dark Sports Zone. No coat, just the green sweater.

Leo looked over. Surprise crossed his face for a second, then a smile.

“Hey.”

Taiga stepped outside and pulled the door mostly closed behind him. “You're going to freeze.”

“Probably.”

“Then why are you out here?”

Leo looked toward the windows behind them, where music thumped faintly through the glass.

“I'm just taking some fresh air.”

“I thought crowds were your fresh air.”

Leo laughed. “Sometimes even I need a pause.” Then he turned to the railing.

Taiga looked at him for a few seconds.

“Okay. I'll leave you alone, then.”

Taiga was about to go back inside when Leo turned suddenly and grabbed his sleeve.

“No, no, it's okay. You can stay if you want. I don't mind.”

Taiga stared at him without answering for a second, then moved beside him at the railing. The balcony overlooked the practice fields, where snow remained in thin gray-white lines along the edges nobody had walked. The pitch lights were off, and without them the Sports Zone looked unfamiliar. Smaller.

Leo rubbed both hands along his sleeves.

“When it gets too loud. Or too many people are trying to talk to me at once. After a while I just disappear for five minutes and then go back.”

Taiga looked at him. “You?”

Leo laughed. “I know. Devastating brand damage. Please keep this confidential.”

“You spend your entire life making noise.”

“Exactly. Sometimes I need to stop hearing myself.”

Leo exhaled into his hands. “Okay, this was a better plan in October.”

“Go inside.”

“In four minutes.”

“You're timing it?”

“Not literally.”

Taiga glanced over. Leo smiled.

“Usually.”

“Of course.”

They stood quietly for thirty seconds, maybe forty. Leo watched the empty field, the silver pendant resting against his sweater.

“Everything good at your place?” Leo asked.

“Yeah. You?”

Leo nodded. “Yeah. Loud. My aunt stayed six days, which means our kitchen became a competitive sport. My sister stole the scarf back twice. My dad pretended he wasn't happy I was home and then made breakfast every morning before anyone else woke up. So, normal.”

Taiga smiled.

Leo glanced over. “She's sixteen, by the way.”

“The scarf?”

Leo laughed. “Okay, deserved. No, my sister.”

Taiga snorted. “She likes soccer?”

“She likes making fun of soccer. Different activity.” Leo smiled. “She comes to games if she's bored enough. Then she spends ninety minutes sending me messages like *that guy's hair is terrible* and *why did you kick it there*. Very high-level analysis.”

“Sounds useful.”

“She once told me I run weird. I thought about it for a week.”

Taiga laughed harder, and Leo looked pleased.

Then the balcony door opened behind them and a guy Taiga didn't know leaned out.

“Leo, we're starting teams.”

Leo turned. “Yeah, I'll be in.”

“Okay.”

The door closed. Taiga looked at Leo.

Leo followed his gaze. “The chain? Yeah. Graduation gift.”

He lifted the pendant slightly. It was the outline of a sun.

Taiga nodded toward it. “It's starting to look like a theme for you.”

Leo laughed. “It kind of is. My mom has always called me *rayito de sol* because of my hair.”

He rubbed the pendant between his fingers. “Little ray of sunshine.”

Taiga chuckled. “Seems appropriate.”

“What do you want to do?”

Leo was quiet. “In summer?”

“Summer. After. Whatever.”

Leo exhaled slowly. “That's the problem.”

Taiga turned. Leo kept looking outward.

“Everybody thinks I have a plan because I always look busy. Those are different things.”

Taiga almost laughed, then didn't.

“I like CS. Mostly. I like building stuff. I like when something actually works and I can point at it. Theory can go fuck itself on some days, but whatever. And I love soccer. Obviously. But I'm not sitting here thinking I'm definitely going pro, because I'm not insane.” Leo paused. “So then people ask what comes after and I say something cheerful enough that they stop asking.”

“You don't know.”

Leo looked over. “Not really.”

“Huh.”

“Nothing.”

“It absolutely did not mean nothing.”

Taiga smiled. “I thought you had everything figured out.”

Leo laughed once. “Why?”

“You act like it.”

“No, I act like I know what I'm doing for the next forty-five minutes. Very different skill.”

Taiga laughed, and Leo did too. Then quiet returned.

“I didn't know either,” Taiga said.

Leo looked at him. “I know.”

“No, I mean—” Taiga stopped. “Before Olympus. I stayed at Camp Buddy because it was easier than choosing anything.”

Taiga nodded. “Right.”

Leo smiled faintly. “So maybe we're both screwed.”

“You're more screwed.”

“Why?”

“You have to report to your mother.”

“Fuck you.”

Taiga laughed.

The balcony door opened again. Enzo this time. He looked at both of them, then at Leo.

“You died?”

Charades lasted forty-seven minutes. Taiga hated every second.

This was a lie. He hated approximately thirty-two minutes; the remaining fifteen were objectively funny.

Leo was terrible. Not mediocre. Terrible. He tried to act out *The Godfather* by pretending to hold a baby and then making an incomprehensible horse motion that resulted in Sofia shouting, “ARE YOU GIVING BIRTH TO A PONY?” Leo collapsed onto the floor laughing hard enough that he could no longer continue.

Taiga's team lost because Enzo interpreted Taiga's attempt at *Titanic* as “rich people drowning.” Technically correct.

By midnight, the party had thinned. Taiga left before cleanup could become a moral obligation. Leo was in the kitchen helping collect cups and looked up as Taiga passed.

“Leaving?”

“Yeah.”

“Okay. Hey—thanks for coming.”

Taiga stopped. Leo was holding a trash bag.

“I'm telling the entire team.”

“I trusted you.”

“Mistake.”

“Cruel.”

“Good night, sunshine.”

Leo laughed and flipped him off. “Night, forty-one.”

Taiga left.
