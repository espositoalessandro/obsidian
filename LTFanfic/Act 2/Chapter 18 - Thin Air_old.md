Mercer announced the exchange program at the end of Thursday training, which meant everyone was tired enough to react honestly.

“Next month,” he said, standing in front of the tactics board while twenty-three players attempted to remove shin guards without looking like they were removing shin guards, “we start the intercollegiate training exchange.”

Enzo lifted one hand. Mercer ignored him.

“We’re spending a weekend at Pinetree. Joint sessions Friday and Saturday. Full match Sunday.”

Enzo’s hand went higher. Mercer still ignored him.

“Pinetree is in the mountains. Pack for actual winter.”

“How high?” Aaron asked.

“About sixteen hundred meters.”

Aaron’s expression changed slightly. “Seriously?”

“You’ll survive.”

“I know.”

“Then stop looking like I told you we’re training on Everest.”

Aaron smiled. “Just asking.”

Mercer tapped the board. “Pinetree’s coach is Daniel Hale. His sessions will not look like ours. Do not spend the weekend asking why Mercer does something differently. I know I do something differently. That is why you are going.”

Gav nodded once. Scott added the schedule to his Oracle, Leo looked interested, and Taiga looked at the word *Pinetree* beneath the weekend dates.

Mountains sounded better than the city.

---

Four weeks later, the bus left Olympus at seven on Friday morning. This was considered an act of violence by most of the squad.

Enzo boarded carrying a pillow the size of a small child. Gav stared at it.

“You packed that?”

“I have needs.”

“You have a neck.”

“Not after three hours on this bus.”

Scott took the seat behind them and immediately put headphones on. Aaron sat near the front with his backpack under his knees, travel mug in the holder, everything arranged before half the team had boarded.

Taiga took a window seat halfway back. Leo appeared in the aisle.

“Anyone sitting there?”

Taiga looked at the empty seat. “No.”

Leo sat. That was apparently all the ceremony required now.

The city thinned slowly: concrete gave way to low suburbs, then highway, then long stretches of bare winter fields. Leo slept for twenty-three minutes with his head tipped against the seat, woke when the bus hit a pothole and immediately started complaining that the suspension had attacked him.

Two rows ahead, Evan turned around. “You’ve been awake eight seconds.”

“I suffered while unconscious.”

“Tragic.”

Enzo held his giant pillow over the seat. “This is why preparation matters.”

Gav pushed it back down. “No one wants your pillow.”

By the time the road began climbing, snow sat in the tree shadows.

---

Pinetree looked like somebody had built a university where a ski lodge should have been: dark timber buildings, stone foundations, training fields cut into the slope below campus, pines everywhere. The air was cold enough that the first walk from the bus to the lodge made Enzo announce he had been betrayed by global warming.

Scott said, “You are carrying a pillow.”

“That is unrelated.”

Hale met them outside the training building. He was taller than Mercer, grayer and quieter.

“Welcome to Pinetree. Bags in the lodge. Boots on in forty minutes. Mixed groups.”

No speech. No school-pride performance. Mercer looked pleased by this, which was worrying.

The first session explained why.

Hale rotated everyone—not just teams, but roles. Taiga started left wing, moved right, then played underneath the striker. Gav spent twenty minutes at center-back. Evan played as a nine and complained exactly once before discovering he enjoyed bullying center-backs.

Aaron started in his usual fullback role. Then Hale pointed toward the middle.

“Bennett. Central.”

Aaron stopped. “Central midfield?”

“Yes.”

“I don’t really play there.”

“That’s why you’re going there.”

Aaron stared. “Right.”

He went, and the role punished hesitation immediately. At fullback, most of the field lived in front of him; central midfield turned the game all the way around him. Check shoulder. Receive. Know where pressure is before the ball arrives.

Aaron’s first touch went backward when the forward option was open.

Hale said, “Scan earlier.”

Aaron nodded. Next time he scanned too late and got pressed from behind. Ball gone.

Scott’s voice detonated from the mixed-team goal. “TURN OUT! SECOND BALL!”

They recovered. Hale moved on. Aaron looked toward Mercer, but Mercer was talking to a Pinetree assistant and did not rescue him.

The next rotation moved Aaron wide again, then central, then fullback on the opposite side. By dinner, he was quiet.

---

The lodge common room filled after eight. Pinetree and Olympus players occupied the same couches, tables and floor space with the awkwardness of people who had kicked each other for three hours and were now expected to share snacks.

Micah Reed, Pinetree’s central midfielder, sat across from Gav arguing about whether Olympus built too slowly from the back. Noah Brooks called Olympus “too pretty.” Enzo took this as a personal insult despite not being a tactical system. Scott played cards with two Pinetree defenders and spoke fewer words in forty minutes than he had shouted during the last ten minutes of training.

Taiga ended up near the fire with Leo. Outside the windows, snow had started again.

Leo looked at the trees. “I get it more now.”

“What?”

“Why you go back.”

Taiga looked at him. Leo caught the expression.

“I mean the place. Not whatever happened there.”

Taiga looked at the fire. “It’s hard to explain.”

“Then don’t.”

A pause settled between them.

“There’s a lake,” Taiga said.

Leo smiled. “I know.”

“Not like a little one.”

“Okay.”

“Big enough that mornings look different depending on wind.”

Leo waited.

“There’s a dock.”

“I know that too.”

“Who told you?”

“You.”

“When?”

“Train.”

Maybe. Leo remembered things.

“The cabins are worse than these.”

“That I believe.”

“Walls are thin.”

“Enzo would die.”

“He would get murdered.”

“By you?”

“Within a week.”

Leo laughed.

Aaron came into the room looking tired. Leo lifted a hand.

“You alive?”

“Yeah.”

“You vanished.”

“Called Sofia.”

Enzo looked over from the card table. “How is she?”

Aaron’s face improved immediately. “Good. She says I’m being dramatic.”

“Wise woman,” Gav said.

Aaron gave him the finger and sat down. The room moved on.

---

Saturday morning began with role rotations again. Aaron received central with a Pinetree player already closing him, turned into pressure and lost it.

Scott shouted from behind, “BENNETT, CHECK SHOULDER!”

Aaron reset. Next possession he checked early, saw pressure and played first time.

Too safe.

Hale stopped him. “You had space.”

“I thought you wanted quicker.”

“I want information. Quicker is not the same thing.”

Aaron nodded. “I know.”

Hale looked at him for half a second. “Then use it.”

They went again.

Taiga recognized the pattern before lunch. Aaron wanted the rule to stay still. Every correction became the new rule: hold, then go; play quicker, then keep it; scan, then stop overthinking. Hale was not contradicting himself. The picture had changed. Aaron knew that intellectually, but his body still wanted one correct answer.

During the afternoon game, Hale moved him central again. Aaron laughed without humor.

“Sure.”

Two minutes later, Leo pressed him. Aaron tried to turn, Leo took the ball cleanly and played forward. Goal.

Leo jogged backward. “Sorry.”

Aaron looked at him. “For what?”

Leo slowed. “Nothing.”

“Then don’t say sorry.”

“Okay.”

Next possession, Aaron fouled Jonas Keller late. Jonas went down. Whistle. Aaron walked away.

Jonas stood. “What the hell?”

Aaron turned. “You stepped across.”

“Because I had the ball.”

“I know.”

“That’s a foul.”

“I know.”

“Then what are you arguing?”

“I’m not.”

Taiga stepped between them. “Then stop.”

Aaron’s head snapped toward him. “I said I know.”

“Great. Use one of the words you know and move on.”

Aaron stared. Jonas gave Taiga a look that suggested Olympus was exhausting.

Hale blew the whistle. “Bennett. Two minutes.”

Aaron walked off and looked at Mercer once. Mercer pointed toward the bench. Nothing else.

---

The second session should have reset things. It did not.

Aaron played fullback again—his actual position—and still looked half a second late. Scott shouted constantly behind him.

“STEP!”

Aaron stepped.

“HOLD!”

He stopped.

“BACK POST!”

He recovered too late. The cross came through. Goal.

Scott slapped both gloves together. “Bennett, inside earlier!”

“I heard you.”

“Then move earlier.”

Aaron turned away. Taiga saw his shoulders go tight.

Evan jogged across during the reset. “Forget the last one.”

Aaron nodded. “I know.”

Evan looked at him. “Okay.”

That should have ended it. Instead, three plays later Aaron hesitated again.

After the whistle, Taiga said, “You’re waiting for someone to tell you what to do.”

Aaron looked at him. “I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I’m trying not to get corrected every thirty seconds.”

“Then read the play.”

Aaron laughed once. “Right. Easy for you.”

Taiga frowned. “What’s that supposed to mean?”

“Nothing.”

“Then don’t say it.”

Aaron picked up his bottle. “Forget it.”

He walked away.

---

That evening, people gathered around the firepit behind the lodge. It should have helped: food, heat, no drills, no coaches except Mercer and Hale talking near the doors.

Pinetree players mixed with Olympus again. Noah argued with Enzo about pressing systems. Micah told Gav that Olympus still played too slowly; Gav told him Pinetree confused running with progress. For ten minutes it almost felt normal.

Aaron sat across the fire, quiet. Taiga ignored him.

Then Aaron said something about the afternoon session. Taiga corrected him, Aaron corrected the correction, and the argument began from there. Nobody noticed the exact moment it stopped being about football.

“You act like every answer is obvious,” Aaron said.

“It was obvious.”

“To you.”

“Yes.”

Aaron laughed. “There. That.”

Taiga leaned forward. “What?”

“You do this every time. Somebody gets something wrong and you look at them like they’re an idiot for not seeing what you saw.”

“That’s not what happened.”

“You literally just did it.”

“Because you were waiting for Mercer to tell you whether to breathe.”

Aaron’s face changed.

Gav said, “Taiga.”

Taiga ignored him. Aaron looked into the fire.

“At least I don’t act like everyone else is stupid when I’m having a bad weekend.”

Taiga laughed. “Maybe because sometimes you make everybody else deal with it instead.”

Aaron looked up. “What the fuck does that mean?”

“You know exactly what it means.”

“No. Say it.”

Taiga did. “You’re so fucking terrified of being the weak link that you make everybody else pay for noticing.”

The firepit went quiet. Aaron’s face twisted, humiliation arriving before anger.

“Yeah?” he said. “And you’re so fucking terrified of people noticing anything about you that one comment turns you into this.”

Taiga went still. Aaron saw it. Regret flashed across his face.

Too late.

Leo, who had been standing near Gav, stepped closer. “Aaron.”

Aaron looked away from him. Taiga’s pulse hammered.

“What comment?”

Aaron shook his head. “Forget it.”

“No. You wanted to say it.”

“I said forget it.”

“Fucking say it.”

Aaron looked back at him. “I’m talking to you lately and half the time he’s there too.”

Leo frowned. “I’m standing here because everyone is standing here.”

“I know.” Aaron rubbed both hands over his face. “That’s not—fuck.”

Taiga’s voice dropped. “Keep going.”

Aaron looked at him. “Every time I turn around lately, you two are together. Training. The volleyball game. Apollo. Now this trip. I say one thing to you and somehow I end up arguing with both of you. I don’t know what the fuck that means, and I don’t care. I’m just tired of feeling like I’m arguing with both of you every time I argue with one.”

“That’s bullshit,” Taiga said.

“Maybe.” Aaron looked exhausted now. “But it’s what it feels like.”

Leo said, “There isn’t a side.”

Aaron looked at him. “Doesn’t feel like that from here.”

Taiga stood. “Don’t drag him into this.”

Aaron laughed once. “There. Exactly.”

Taiga stepped forward. Gav moved too—not much, but enough.

Aaron’s anger flickered. Taiga saw the weakness in it and hit anyway.

“You know what? Maybe if you spent half as much time reading the fucking game as you do checking whether Mercer thinks you’re failing, you wouldn’t feel like everybody was against you.”

Aaron flinched. Taiga kept going.

“You want someone to tell you the answer every time. Then when they don’t, you get pissed at whoever looks like they already know it.”

“Taiga,” Gav said again.

Aaron stood. “Fuck you.”

“Yeah. Great answer.”

“At least I’m not terrified of whatever the fuck this is.”

He gestured once, vaguely, between Taiga and Leo.

The world narrowed. Leo’s face changed. Aaron looked sick the second the words were out.

“Taiga, I—”

“Don’t fucking talk to me.”

Taiga grabbed his coat from the chair. Enzo said his name; Taiga ignored him. Leo said, “Taiga.”

Taiga was already walking.

---

Away from the fire, the cold hit hard enough to make his eyes water. Taiga walked past the lodge toward the service path, snow crunching behind him.

“Taiga.”

He kept walking.

“Wait.”

“Go back.”

Leo did not.

Taiga reached the edge of the training fields and turned. “What?”

Leo stopped several steps away. His coat was open, his scarf still back by the fire; he had followed too quickly to grab it.

“I’m not here to ask what Aaron meant.”

“Great.”

“I’m serious.”

“Then why are you here?”

Leo stopped. “Because you left.”

Taiga looked away.

Of course.

“You looked like you were about to punch Aaron or punch a tree,” Leo continued. “Both seemed bad.”

“Go back.”

“Can you give me thirty seconds?”

“No.”

Leo’s jaw set. “I don’t care what Aaron thinks it looks like.”

Taiga laughed once. “Exactly.”

“What does that mean?”

“He says that shit in front of everybody and then you come running after me.”

“I came because you were upset.”

“That doesn’t make it better.”

“Why?”

Taiga had no answer he could say. Leo stood in the snow waiting.

Taiga’s voice sharpened. “You’re making it worse.”

Leo’s expression shifted. “How?”

“By being here.”

Silence.

Leo looked at him for several seconds. “Okay.”

He turned. Something in Taiga caught.

“Why do you always do that?”

Leo turned back. “Do what?”

“Follow.”

Leo stared. “Because you’re my friend.”

Taiga looked away.

“That’s it,” Leo said. “You leave angry, I check you’re not doing something stupid. That’s the conspiracy.”

“Stop.”

“You asked.”

“And I said stop.”

Leo’s voice rose. “Fine. But don’t make me into the problem because Aaron pissed you off.”

Taiga snapped back toward him. “You think that’s what this is?”

“I don’t know what this is. You won’t tell me.”

“Then leave.”

Leo went quiet. Taiga breathed hard.

“I don’t fucking need you following me.”

Leo looked at him. There it was: no joke, no easy exit.

“Fine.”

Taiga’s chest tightened. Leo nodded once.

“You don’t.”

He turned.

“Leo.”

Leo stopped. Taiga had nothing. Leo waited a second anyway, then started walking again.

Taiga said, “Whatever.”

Leo turned back. Now he was angry.

“Don’t do that.”

“What?”

“Say my name and then act like you didn’t want me to stop.”

Taiga stared.

Leo shook his head. “I came out here because I care about you. You can be pissed about that. But I’m not apologizing for it.”

Taiga could not answer.

“And I’m not standing here while you treat me like I did something wrong.”

Leo turned and walked back toward the lodge. This time Taiga did not stop him.

---

The forest became quiet again. It did not help.

Taiga stood beside the dark field until the cold got through his coat. Aaron’s words kept returning—not the details, but the gesture. *Whatever the fuck this is.*

The rink. The lab. Coffee. The balcony. Leo beside him on the bus. Leo following him into the snow.

Taiga started walking, not toward the lodge at first but along the service path until the trail lights ended. Pine and cold and dark water that was not there flashed through his head anyway. Camp Buddy. Keitaro refusing to leave that summer.

Taiga turned around and went back.

---

The lodge had changed. People avoided looking at him with the deliberate skill of people who were absolutely looking at him. Gav stood near the fireplace with Scott. Enzo sat on the arm of a couch. Aaron was gone. Leo was gone.

Taiga crossed the room. Enzo stood.

Taiga looked at him. Enzo lifted both hands. “I’m not asking.”

“Good.”

“Aaron’s in his room.”

“I don’t care.”

Enzo nodded.

At the stairs, Gav said, “Match briefing is eight tomorrow.”

“Yeah.”

“That’s it.”

Taiga nodded once.

Upstairs, his room was empty except for Enzo’s giant pillow on the other bed. Taiga sat.

His Oracle buzzed.

**Mercer:** breakfast 7. briefing 8. bus to pitch 8:45. kickoff 11.

Replies arrived.

Scott: 👍

Gav: 👍

Enzo: **alive against all odds**

Leo: 👍

Taiga stared at Leo’s reaction. No private message.

He opened their chat. The last exchange was from that morning.

**Leo:** are you awake

**Taiga:** unfortunately

**Leo:** outside?

Taiga typed **sorry**, deleted it, typed **you didn't do anything**, deleted that too, and put the Oracle down.

The door opened. Enzo came in, closed it quietly and sat on his bed. For a while neither spoke.

Then Enzo said, “I’m not asking.”

“I know.”

Another silence.

“Aaron feels like shit.”

“Good.”

Enzo looked at him. Taiga looked away. Enzo did not argue.

After a moment he said, “Leo too.”

Taiga’s jaw tightened. “Enzo.”

“Done.”

He turned off his lamp. Taiga lay down while wind moved through the trees outside.

Tomorrow they had to play Pinetree. Eleven o’clock. Full match. Olympus shape. Olympus positions. Rules that stayed rules. Everything Aaron had wanted all weekend.

For the first time since arriving, Taiga wanted to go home.
