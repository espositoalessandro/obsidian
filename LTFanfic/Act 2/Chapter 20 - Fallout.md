Taiga woke at six-thirteen, not because of the alarm but because Enzo rolled over and the bed frame complained.

For a few seconds he did not know where he was. Dark ceiling, cold air, open window. Then Pinetree came back all at once: Aaron, the firepit, Leo in the snow.

Taiga shut his eyes.

Bad choice.

He opened them again. Across the room, Enzo was still asleep. The Oracle on the bedside table showed no notifications, but Taiga looked at it anyway.

Nothing.

He had told Leo to leave him alone. Leo was leaving him alone.

At six-thirty, Enzo’s alarm went off. He killed it immediately. Neither of them moved.

Then Enzo said into the dark, “You awake?”

“No.”

“Great.”

Silence.

Enzo sat up, his hair looking like somebody had attacked it. “Breakfast in thirty.”

“I know.”

“Mercer will murder us if we’re late.”

“I know.”

“Cool.”

He stood. Taiga closed the window.

---

The hallway smelled like coffee before they reached the stairs, and the voices downstairs were low for a team breakfast.

Leo was sitting with Scott and Gav. Aaron sat at another table with untouched coffee. Enzo moved toward the food, and Taiga followed.

Eggs, toast, fruit. He took more than he wanted.

Match. Fuel. Simple.

He sat with Enzo. Gav joined them, then Scott. Leo stayed where he was.

Breakfast happened in fragments. Enzo complained about the altitude; Scott corrected him; Gav told Scott nobody needed a physiological lecture at seven in the morning; Scott continued anyway.

Across the room, Aaron stood with his tray, looked toward Taiga’s table, then changed direction and sat with two Pinetree players. Nobody commented.

Leo got up for coffee and passed behind Taiga’s chair. No touch, no joke, no morning.

Taiga stared at his plate while Enzo kept talking.

---

Mercer’s briefing was at eight. Pinetree gave Olympus a small meeting room beside the indoor hall: twenty-three players, whiteboard, projector, chairs too close together.

Leo sat beside Gav, Taiga beside Enzo, Aaron at the end of the second row.

Mercer stood at the front. “Good weekend?”

Silence.

Enzo raised one thumb. Mercer looked at him. Enzo lowered it.

Mercer turned to the board. “Fine.”

He pulled up Pinetree’s shape. “Four-three-three out of possession. Mid-block. They’ll push us outside and trap on the second pass.”

Normal.

Taiga focused on left-sided progression, weak-side rotation, Pinetree’s fullback stepping high and the space behind.

Mercer pointed at the right side. “Bennett. You start.”

Aaron nodded.

“Mendez, high and wide first fifteen. Don’t come inside too early.”

“Got it.”

“Akatora. Same on the other side. Their six tracks ball-side. If you both collapse inward, you help him.”

“Okay.”

Mercer looked around the room. “Communication has been poor all morning. I don’t care why. You have ninety minutes to be adults.”

That was all. Then he went back to set pieces.

---

Warm-up began at nine-fifteen. The air was bright and cruel: blue sky, snow along the edges, mountains clear behind the far stand.

Passing patterns came first. Taiga’s group was Taiga, Leo and Gav.

Gav played Taiga. Taiga returned to Leo. Clean. Again. No words.

On the third repetition, Leo said, “Set.”

Taiga set. Leo moved. Fine.

Next rotation, Taiga overhit a pass. Leo stretched and kept it in.

Nothing.

Mercer shouted, “Akatora. Pace.”

Taiga nodded. “Yeah.”

Scott’s voice carried from the defensive group on the next pitch. “STEP! HOLD! SECOND BALL!”

Normal football noise.

That helped more than anything else.

---

The match started at eleven. For the first ten minutes, Olympus played like everybody wanted permission before doing anything.

At eight minutes, Taiga saw Leo start a diagonal run. The window opened. Taiga hesitated for half a second, which was enough for Pinetree’s six to step into the lane. Window gone. Taiga played backward, and Leo reset.

At eleven, Aaron received under pressure from Micah. He held too long; Micah nicked it; Pinetree broke.

Scott exploded behind them. “RECOVER! INSIDE! BACK POST!”

The shot went wide. Gav clapped once.

“Reset.”

Aaron nodded.

Pinetree scored at seventeen. Not because of one person, but because Olympus defended like six people had different versions of the plan. Switch, overlap, cross low, finish.

One-nil.

Scott slapped his gloves together. “WAKE UP! NEXT ONE!”

Taiga jogged back to halfway. Leo was already there. Their eyes met. Neither spoke.

Whistle. Kickoff.

---

At twenty-eight, Leo finally beat his man. Taiga moved inside. Leo looked up, saw the near-post run and crossed late enough for the ball to be blocked.

Corner.

Leo looked toward him. “Sorry.”

“Fine.”

At thirty-one, Taiga lost the ball trying to force a pass inside. Aaron recovered with a clean tackle.

“Good,” Taiga said.

Aaron looked at him. No answer.

Pinetree’s second came at thirty-nine: corner, first contact, second ball, shot through bodies.

Two-nil.

Mercer finally shouted, “Wake up!”

Everybody knew he was not talking about sleep.

---

Halftime was worse because Mercer stayed calm.

The locker room door closed.

“What is this?”

Nobody answered.

“I’m not asking about the score.”

Still nothing.

He pointed toward the pitch. “You trained with these people for two days. You know more about how they move than you know about half the teams in our league, and you are communicating less than you did Friday.”

Gav said, “We’re hesitating.”

“Yes.”

Scott said, “Second pass is slow.”

“Yes.”

Mercer looked at Aaron. “You’re playing like every possession is a test.”

Aaron swallowed. “Yeah.”

“Stop.”

“Okay.”

“Mendez. You’re waiting for certainty before you play.”

Leo nodded. “Yeah.”

“Stop.”

“Okay.”

Then Taiga.

“Akatora. Same thing.”

Taiga looked up. “I know.”

Mercer stared at him. “Good. Then fix it.”

Gav stood before Mercer finished and looked around the room. “Talk.”

Scott stood, then Aaron, then Leo. Taiga stood last.

They went back out.

---

Olympus improved. Not dramatically, but enough.

Aaron played forward. Leo attacked space. Taiga stopped waiting for perfect windows.

At fifty-three, Taiga found Leo with a diagonal. Leo controlled, turned and played inside. Shot saved. Corner.

Clean. Familiar.

At fifty-nine, Aaron got booked for a late challenge on Micah. Yellow. Aaron stepped away immediately.

“Sorry.”

Micah nodded. No argument.

At sixty-eight, Taiga overlapped. Leo had the ball, the lane opened, and Leo saw him—but cut inside instead.

Lost it. Counter.

Taiga sprinted fifty meters back. When the ball went out, he turned.

“Why didn’t you play it?”

Leo stopped. “I didn’t think you were going.”

“I was already running.”

“You stopped before.”

“That was first half.”

“I know.”

“Then play it.”

“Fine.”

Gav shouted from inside, “Enough.”

Both stopped. Pinetree throw. Play continued.

---

Olympus scored at seventy-four, and Aaron started it. He won the ball centrally and played forward immediately. Gav found Leo. Leo drove inside. Taiga stayed wide. Leo slipped the pass, Taiga crossed low and the substitute striker finished.

Two-one.

Instinct took over. Taiga turned toward Leo, who was already coming toward him and grabbed his shoulder.

“Good ball!”

Taiga laughed. “Good pass.”

Then the team arrived around them: Enzo shouting from the bench, Gav, Aaron, bodies and noise. Taiga looked across and caught Aaron’s expression.

Guilt.

The moment closed.

The equalizer never came. Pinetree sat deeper, Olympus pushed, legs slowed.

At full time: two-one.

Not a disaster.

Almost worse for that.

They had not been destroyed. They had simply been less than themselves.

---

Afterward, Micah found Aaron first. They spoke quietly. Taiga did not listen.

Micah came to him next. “Better second half.”

“Yeah.”

“You guys looked weird.”

Taiga’s face changed.

Micah added immediately, “On the field.”

Taiga looked toward the pitch. “Altitude.”

Micah smiled. “Keep telling yourself.”

Later, during the meal, Aaron approached Taiga once. Taiga saw him coming and his body went hard.

Aaron stopped two meters away. “Can I—”

“No.”

Aaron closed his mouth. “Okay.”

He left. Leo had seen. He did nothing.

---

Departure was scheduled for three. Bags came down, rooms emptied, and Enzo packed the giant pillow with ceremony.

Outside, the mountain afternoon had softened. Sun on snow, wind less sharp. The trail map stood beside the lodge, and Taiga looked at the overlook loop.

Friday felt like another month.

Coach Hale gathered both squads before the bus loaded. Short speech: thank you, good work, see you later in the semester.

Photos happened. Olympus. Pinetree. Then mixed.

Taiga stood in the second row. Leo ended up three people away. Aaron stood at the far end. Everybody smiled.

---

The bus smelled like wet coats and tired bodies. Taiga climbed aboard behind Enzo.

Halfway back, Leo was already sitting in the same row as Friday. Window seat. Bag on the aisle seat.

For one second neither moved. Leo’s hand went toward the bag.

Taiga said, “It’s fine.”

He kept walking.

Enzo sat two rows back and slid toward the window without comment. Taiga took the aisle seat.

Aaron boarded later and sat near the front beside Scott. Scott looked mildly surprised and also said nothing.

The bus pulled away. Pinetree disappeared in layers: lodge, fields, trail sign, forest, then road.

Enzo fell asleep twenty minutes in. Taiga opened his Oracle.

Team group.

**Enzo:** survived the mountain cult

**Scott:** academy

**Enzo:** cult energy

Leo reacted with a laughing face.

Taiga opened Leo’s private chat and typed:

**Taiga:** good pass on the goal

Deleted it.

Then:

**Taiga:** sorry about earlier

Deleted that too. He locked the screen.

---

At the service station, Taiga went to the refrigerator for water. Leo appeared on the other side of the glass.

Both stopped.

Ridiculous.

Leo reached for a bottle. Taiga moved his hand.

“Sorry.”

Leo shook his head. “You’re good.”

Two polite words. Taiga hated them.

Back on the bus, Leo’s bag was no longer on the aisle seat. The seat was open.

Taiga stayed beside Enzo. Leo put both headphones on.

---

By the time the city returned, it was dark. Enzo woke and looked outside.

“We’re close.”

“Yeah.”

He stretched carefully, then asked, “You okay?”

Taiga looked at him. Enzo held up one hand.

“Not asking about anything specific.”

“That’s still asking.”

“Fair.”

Taiga looked forward. Leo was staring out the window.

“No,” Taiga said.

Enzo went quiet, then nodded. “Yeah.”

No advice.

---

Mercer stood when they entered campus. “Tomorrow recovery at four. Film Tuesday. Get sleep.”

The bus stopped outside the athletic complex. Everyone stood. Bags, jackets, the aisle clogged.

Leo reached Taiga’s row while Enzo wrestled the giant pillow from overhead.

“Why did I bring this?” Enzo asked.

Gav, behind Leo, said, “You had needs.”

“Past me was selfish.”

Leo laughed. Small.

Taiga looked up. Leo looked down. Their eyes met.

“Night,” Leo said.

Not *forty-one*. Just night.

Taiga swallowed. “Yeah.”

Leo moved past.

By the time Taiga reached the door, Leo was already across the pavement, walking toward Central alone. Aaron stood near the baggage compartment. Taiga looked away.

No one followed anyone.

At least for tonight.
