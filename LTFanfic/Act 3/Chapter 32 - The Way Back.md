Taiga woke with a dry mouth, a dull headache, and the immediate certainty that opening his eyes had been a mistake. He did it anyway. Gray morning light filled Cabin Two, where his bag was mostly packed, his shoes were where he had left them, and a clean sheet covered the mattress. That last part took him back to approximately one in the morning, when he and Leo had discovered that changing bedding while slightly drunk was much harder than either of them had expected.

Taiga stared at the ceiling. Then remembered why they had needed a clean sheet. He closed his eyes again. Across the room, something rustled.

“Morning, Taiga.”

Taiga opened one eye. Leo was awake in his own bunk. His hair was everywhere and the blanket had fallen to his waist; he looked terrible. Taiga probably looked worse.

“You sound alive.”

“Barely functioning.”

Leo rubbed both hands over his face.

“My mouth tastes like firepit.”

“That isn't a taste.”

“It is today.”

Taiga pushed himself upright. His head objected, not violently but enough to make him stop for a second. Leo watched him.

“You good?”

“I'm never drinking Aiden's beer again.”

“You had two.”

“Exactly. Two was enough.”

Leo laughed and immediately regretted it. He put one hand against his forehead.

“Okay. No laughing.”

Taiga smiled. Leo pointed at him.

“That counts.”

“Not my problem.”

Silence settled, not awkward, not exactly normal either. Taiga looked at Leo. Leo looked back. Last night came back all at once—not the beer or the fall, but everything after. Taiga felt heat rise into his face despite the headache. Leo's mouth twitched. Taiga narrowed his eyes.

“If you say anything stupid, I'm going back to sleep.”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You're smiling.”

“I am allowed to smile.”

“Not like that.”

Leo's smile widened. Taiga reached for his pillow. Leo immediately raised both hands.

“Okay, okay. I'm done.”

Taiga left the pillow where it was. Leo lowered his hands. A few seconds passed. Then:

“Still okay?”

Taiga rubbed the back of his neck.

“Yeah. I'm still okay.”

Leo nodded.

“Me too.”

Leo stretched his legs over the side of the bunk.

“Although I still think you should be registered as a dangerous object.”

Taiga grabbed the pillow. Leo ducked. The pillow hit the wall.

“Worth it.”

---

The dirty sheet was rolled into a tight bundle inside the laundry bag. Taiga picked it up. Leo looked at him.

“You're taking that?”

“Do you want to leave it here?”

“Absolutely not.”

“Then yes.”

“We could bury it.”

Taiga stared. Leo considered.

“Probably bad for the environment.”

“Get dressed.”

Ten minutes later they left the cabin. Taiga carried the laundry bag. Leo carried both of their empty water bottles. The camp had the cold, peculiar quiet of a Sunday morning: no tools, no hammering, no Kieran, and nowhere they needed to be except breakfast. Taiga liked that less than he expected. The main lodge door was already open. Of course it was. Aiden was awake. Of course he was. Taiga walked in with the laundry bag. Aiden looked up from the kitchen. His eyes moved to Taiga. Then the bag. Then back.

“Rough night?”

Taiga nearly walked into a chair. Behind him, Leo made a strangled noise. Aiden frowned.

“The firepit. You both look awful.”

“Right. The firepit.”

Taiga kept moving.

“Blame the beer.”

Aiden stared at him.

“Yes. Beer.”

Leo turned away very quickly. Taiga could feel him trying not to laugh. He wanted to kill him. Aiden pointed toward the utility room.

“Leave the laundry by the washer. I'll run everything after breakfast.”

“I can do it.”

“You're leaving in a few hours.”

“So what?”

“So go eat.”

Taiga opened his mouth. Aiden pointed harder.

“Food first. Heroics later.”

Taiga gave up. He dropped the bag beside the washer. When he came back, Leo was sitting at the table with his forehead against one hand. Yoichi sat across from him looking offensively functional.

“You look like shit, Sunshine.”

Leo lifted his head.

“You're a wonderful friend.”

“I know.”

Taiga sat beside Leo. Yoichi looked at him too.

“Dynamite somehow looks worse.”

“Fuck you.”

“Good morning to you too.”

Yuki rested under Yoichi's chair, completely unaffected by alcohol, emotional breakthroughs, or human dignity. Yuri arrived next, took in Taiga and Leo, and smiled. Taiga immediately looked at his plate. There was no plate yet. Bad strategy. Yuri sat across from them.

“Did everyone sleep well?”

Leo coughed. Taiga glared at her. Yuri widened her eyes.

“What did I do?”

“Nothing useful.”

“I only asked about sleep.”

“Ask someone else.”

Her smile became unbearable. Goro entered carrying a pitcher of water. He set it on the table.

“Anyone who consumed alcohol last night should drink at least two glasses before coffee.”

Yoichi looked personally offended.

“Why are you saying that like we're being punished?”

“Because you are all dehydrated.”

Aiden came out with toast.

“He's been waiting to say that since six.”

“I have not.”

“You filled the pitcher at six.”

“Hydration is not time-sensitive.”

Yoshinori arrived last. He looked much better than Taiga expected. Too much better. Yuri stared at him. Yoshinori saw her. His mouth twitched. Yuri raised one eyebrow. Yoshinori looked away. Aiden started laughing. Goro closed his eyes.

“Not again.”

---

Lloyd looked almost offensively functional for someone who had matched Goro drink for drink for part of the night. Taiga stared at him.

“What?”

“You drank more than me.”

“I have architectural tolerance.”

Darius said:

“Support your partner.”

“I am.”

“With facts?”

“Yes.”

Leo laughed into his water. Breakfast helped. Water helped more. By the second piece of toast, the headache had backed off enough that Taiga could think without resenting the concept. Leo had color again. Yoichi had apparently never lost his. Yuri kept trying to make Yoshinori laugh by doing absolutely nothing. It worked twice. Goro refused to acknowledge either incident. The morning moved slowly. Nobody had work assignments. Nobody needed them. At nine thirty, Yoshinori stood.

“All right. You two should finish packing.”

Taiga looked at the clock.

“Train's not until two forty.”

“I know.”

“We have time.”

“I also know.”

Leo leaned toward Taiga.

“I think he's trying to avoid watching us pack in ten minutes at eleven twenty.”

Yoshinori pointed at him.

“Exactly what I'm trying to avoid.”

Taiga looked betrayed.

“You're supposed to be on my side.”

“I have watched you travel before.”

“That was once.”

“It was enough.”

Leo laughed. Taiga stood.

“Fine. We'll pack.”

---

Cabin Two looked different with the bags open, less temporary and more like a place they were actively leaving. Taiga hated packing because it required deciding where everything belonged. Dirty clothes. Clean clothes. Chargers. Toiletries. The hoodie from last night. He picked it up. Stopped. Leo looked at the hoodie.

“That survived?”

Taiga looked at the front.

“Barely survived.”

Leo started laughing. Taiga threw it into the dirty-clothes bag.

“You're paying for detergent.”

“I already paid for emotional damages.”

“You caused them.”

“That's highly disputed.”

Taiga zipped the bag. Leo folded a sweatshirt with surprising precision. Taiga watched him.

“You actually know how to fold.”

Leo looked offended.

“What did you think I did?”

“Shove everything into the bag.”

“I have standards.”

“You tried to jump three stairs last night.”

“That was unrelated to textiles.”

Taiga laughed. Leo smiled. Then went back to packing. A few minutes later, Taiga looked around at the empty bunk, cleared shelf and bathroom. No phone cable under his bed this time. Leo stood near the window. His phone was up. Taiga heard the camera sound.

“You took another picture.”

Leo lowered it.

“Yeah, I did.”

“Of the cabin.”

“Of the cabin.”

“Why that?”

Leo looked around.

“Because I want one.”

Taiga waited for a joke. None came. He looked at the room too. Two bunks. Wood walls. Cold window. The place where Leo had almost fallen over the first night trying to unpack and talk at the same time. The floor where they'd thrown pillows. The bed where— Taiga stopped that thought.

“Send it to me.”

Leo looked over.

“You don't have pictures of the cabin?”

“I didn't say I don't.”

“You want mine?”

Taiga shrugged. Leo smiled.

“I'll send it.”

---

They finished before ten thirty. Yoshinori would be unbearable about that. Taiga left both bags by the door. Leo put on his jacket. Then stood there.

“What now?”

Taiga looked at the clock.

“Still an hour before we leave.”

“Want to take a walk?”

Leo glanced toward the lake.

“Yeah. Let's go.”

They went outside. There was no bags or destination announced. They passed the field. The maintenance shed. The activity hall. Leo slowed near the dog run when Yuki came to the fence. Yoichi was inside brushing mud from one of her paws. Leo crouched.

“Hey, girl.”

Yuki shoved her nose through the gap. Leo scratched behind her ears. Yoichi looked up.

“You leaving already?”

“In about an hour.”

Yoichi grunted. Leo stayed crouched.

“I'm going to miss her.”

“You can miss me too.”

Leo looked at him.

“I'll consider it.”

“You're an asshole.”

Taiga leaned against the fence. Leo looked at Yoichi.

“Send me pictures.”

“I'm not promising that.”

“Of Yuki.”

“I know what you meant.”

“At least once a week.”

“You're demanding.”

“You have my number.”

Yoichi's face changed slightly. Taiga watched him scratch Yuki's neck.

“Maybe I'll send some.”

Leo smiled.

“That means yes.”

“It means maybe.”

“See you, Yoichi.”

Yoichi looked at him. No joke for a second. Then:

“Yeah. See you around, Sunshine.”

Leo stood. Yoichi looked at Taiga.

“You too, Dynamite.”

Taiga nodded.

“Don't let the fence fall over.”

Yoichi gave him the finger.

---

The pier was empty. Taiga had expected it to feel bigger, but it was still the same boards, the same lake and the same mountains. The water had gone almost silver under the late-morning sky. Leo walked to the end. Taiga followed. Neither sat. Leo put both hands in his jacket pockets.

“So this is where the week exploded.”

Taiga looked at him.

“Which time?”

Leo laughed.

“Fair point.”

They stood there. Taiga looked toward the opposite shore. Friday had felt enormous here. Now it was Sunday. Two days. That seemed impossible. Leo bumped his shoulder lightly against Taiga's. Taiga bumped back. After a minute Leo said:

“I get it now.”

Taiga glanced over.

“Get what?”

“Why you come back.”

Taiga looked toward camp. The lodge roof through the trees. Cabins. Field. Smoke-blackened firepit somewhere behind them.

“Yeah. I get it.”

Leo smiled.

“I'd come back too.”

The answer moved through Taiga before he had time to defend against it. He kept his eyes on the lake.

“You can.”

Leo looked at him. Taiga continued before the silence made it worse.

“You don't need preseason as an excuse.”

Leo's expression changed. Small. Warm.

“Good to know.”

Taiga nodded. They stayed another minute. Then Leo looked at the time.

“Yoshi's going to start searching for us.”

“He knows where we are.”

“That has never stopped responsible adults.”

“That's true.”

They walked back.

---

A message appeared on Taiga's phone while he was putting on his shoes. Kieran. Taiga stopped. Leo was across the cabin checking the charger pouch. He did not notice. Taiga opened it.

> Made it back yesterday.  
> Thanks for talking to me.  
> I mean it.  
> Take care, Taiga.

Taiga read it twice. Then he typed:

> You too.  
> Come back when you're ready.

He stared at the message, then sent it. The ache stayed small. Taiga put the phone away. Leo looked over.

“Everything good?”

Taiga nodded.

“It was Kieran.”

Leo waited.

“He made it home.”

“That's good.”

Taiga picked up his duffel.

“Yeah. It is.”

Leo did not ask anything else.

---

Before lunch, Lloyd cornered Leo with a printout of the expansion plan.

“That one?”

“Function hall.”

“Correct! And this?”

“Guest lodging.”

“Excellent.”

Darius looked at Taiga.

“He does this to new scouts.”

“He's not a scout.”

Lloyd looked offended.

“Temporary scout.”

Leo pointed at Taiga.

“Does that make him my counselor?”

“No,” Taiga said immediately.

Darius considered him.

“He would be bad at it.”

“Thank you.”

“That wasn't a compliment.”

They ate an early lunch. Aiden refused to let them leave on station food. That became a twenty-minute argument because Taiga pointed out they were taking a two-hour train, not crossing a desert. Aiden packed sandwiches anyway. Yuri added cookies. Goro removed half the cookies. Yuri put them back when he turned around. Leo watched the entire operation.

“I understand why nobody wins against her.”

Goro looked over.

“I win frequently.”

Yuri zipped the food bag.

“Of course you do, Dad.”

Goro frowned. Taiga took the bag before the argument could become permanent. At eleven twenty-five, both duffels were outside. Yoshinori looked at the time. Then at Taiga. Taiga pointed at him.

“Keep whatever you're thinking to yourself.”

Yoshinori smiled.

“I was only going to say you finished early.”

Taiga regretted everything. Leo laughed.

---

Goodbyes took longer than loading the car. Aiden went first. He hugged Leo without asking. Leo made a surprised noise, then hugged him back.

“Come back when I can feed you properly.”

Leo looked toward the lodge.

“You have been feeding me properly for nine days.”

“That was work food.”

“That was work food?”

Aiden patted his shoulder.

“You have much to learn.”

Then Aiden looked at Taiga. Taiga opened one arm. Aiden ignored the limitation and hugged him properly.

“Take care of yourself.”

“I do.”

“Better than usual.”

Taiga sighed.

“Fine. I'll try.”

Yuri hugged Leo next. She whispered something Taiga could not hear. Leo's face went bright red. Taiga immediately pointed at her.

“What did you say?”

Yuri stepped back looking innocent.

“Private conversation.”

Leo was trying not to laugh. Taiga looked at him.

“Tell me.”

“Absolutely not.”

Yuri hugged Taiga before he could retaliate.

“Come back soon.”

Taiga's arms closed around her automatically.

“Yeah. I will.”

She squeezed once. Then whispered:

“Try to get some sleep at university too~”

Taiga pulled back. Yuri was already escaping.

“Yuri, I swear to God.”

She laughed all the way to Aiden. Leo folded over beside the car. Traitor. Goro waited until the chaos passed. He held out his hand to Leo. Leo took it.

“You worked well this week.”

“Thank you, sir.”

“You are welcome at Camp Buddy again.”

Leo's expression shifted. He straightened.

“Thank you. I'd like that.”

Goro nodded once. Then he looked at Taiga. No hand. Taiga would have complained if Goro offered one.

“Do not wait until next preseason.”

Taiga frowned.

“For what?”

“To visit.”

“Oh. Right.”

Goro's expression softened slightly.

“Camp Buddy remains open to you even when there is nothing to repair.”

Taiga looked away.

“Yeah. I know.”

Goro put one hand on his shoulder, squeezed once, then removed it. Lloyd was next. He hugged Leo before Leo could decide whether that was happening.

“Come back! We still have half the camp to show you properly.”

“I thought I saw most of it.”

“You saw the practical parts. I mean the architecture.”

Darius held out his hand. Leo shook it.

“You worked well.”

Leo's expression changed slightly. He seemed to understand that from Darius, that was a lot.

“Thanks. I learned a lot.”

Darius looked at Taiga.

“Keep practicing.”

“At college?”

“Things break everywhere.”

Fair. Lloyd pointed at Taiga.

“And send pictures if Olympus has any interesting buildings!”

“No.”

“Please?”

“No.”

Darius said:

“He'll send them.”

Taiga stared at him. Darius's mouth moved slightly. Asshole. Yoichi arrived late because Yuki had apparently found mud. He stopped by Leo. No hug. Obviously. He held out his fist. Leo bumped it.

“Don't get soft on the train, Sunshine.”

“I'll try to survive.”

“Send me the football clips.”

“You mean the ones where I beat you?”

“You mean the ones where Dynamite carried your ass?”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“Do you hear jealousy again?”

Taiga looked at Yoichi. Yoichi flipped both of them off. Perfect. Yoshinori stood by the driver's door.

“All right. Before we miss the train because none of you know how to end a goodbye.”

Yuri waved dramatically. Aiden held up the food bag Taiga was already carrying. Goro told Yuri to stop standing in the driveway. Yoichi had Yuki beside him. Taiga looked at all of them. Camp Buddy. For years, leaving had always done something strange to him. Even when he knew he could come back. Especially then. He lifted one hand.

“See you.”

Aiden smiled. Yuri waved harder. Goro nodded. Yoichi shouted:

“Later, Dynamite!”

“You invited me.”

“Yoshi, can I ask you something?”

“Sit back.”

---

Taiga replied:

“So, about last night.”
