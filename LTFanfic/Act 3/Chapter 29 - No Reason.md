Thursday morning, Leo was still weird. Not dramatically.

That would have been easier.

He sat beside Taiga at breakfast like usual.

Stole one of Taiga's potatoes like usual.

Complained that Aiden had made coffee too strong, which was objectively wrong.

He laughed when Yoichi called the sponsor meeting “old-people homework.”

Everything worked. Just half a second late.

Taiga noticed because apparently he had become the kind of person who knew how long Leo usually took to laugh.

Annoying.

Across the table, Kieran was arguing with Yuri about whether faded paint counted as “vintage.”

Kieran already looked far less like a visitor than he had Monday.

Yuri held up an old wooden activity sign.

“It has character.”

“It has mold.”

“It has history.”

“It has moldy history.”

Aiden pointed a spatula at both of them.

“Nothing with mold is going back on a wall.”

Yuri gasped.

“You’re all philistines.”

Goro looked up from the folder beside his plate.

“It is a health hazard.”

“Two philistines.”

Kieran laughed.

Taiga did too.

Beside him, Leo took another potato.

Taiga slapped the back of his hand.

“Get your own.”

“You had four.”

“I had four because they were mine.”

“You still have three.”

“That is how subtraction works.”

Leo smiled.

Then Kieran looked over.

“You still count food too?”

Taiga turned.

“I do not count food.”

Leo held up three fingers.

“You absolutely count food.”

“I counted because you stole one.”

Kieran's mouth curved.

“You used to know exactly how many cookies were left in a bag.”

“That was survival.”

Leo laughed again.

Then Yoshinori stood with his clipboard.

“We have two jobs before lunch. Once those are finished, all four of you are free for the afternoon.”

Yoichi narrowed his eyes.

“You keep saying that like you're worried we'll hide and keep working.”

Yoshinori looked at him.

“I am worried *you* will.”

Leo leaned toward Taiga.

“He's got him.”

Taiga nodded.

“He’s got you completely.”

Yoichi threw a napkin at both of them.

---

The morning work was simple. Simple by Camp Buddy standards, anyway.

One section of the canoe rack needed reinforcement.

Two shutters still needed new hinges.

The rest was cleanup before the sponsor meeting.

Yoshinori put Taiga, Leo and Kieran on the rack while Yoichi took the shutters because he had complained loudest about being assigned “group labor.”

Nobody pointed out the logic.

Taiga held the replacement brace in place.

Leo crouched beside the lower bolt.

Kieran stood on the other side with the level.

“A little higher,” Kieran said.

Taiga lifted one end.

“Is that high enough?”

“A little more.”

Leo looked up.

“If he lifts it any higher, I lose the hole.”

Kieran lowered his hand.

“Right. Back down a little.”

Taiga moved.

Leo lined up the bolt.

“There, hold it.”

Taiga held still.

Leo tightened it.

Kieran checked the bubble.

“That’s good.”

They moved to the second brace.

It should have been awkward.

Three people around one narrow frame usually was.

Instead it worked.

Leo moved with Taiga's pace without needing instructions.

Kieran shifted his grip just before Taiga moved his weight.

Taiga compensated for both of them without thinking about it.

Kieran looked at the next bolt.

“Need the shorter one.”

Leo had already picked it up.

“Got it.”

Kieran smiled.

“You really do know what he means.”

Leo handed the bolt to Taiga.

“Most of the time.”

Taiga took it.

“Ninety percent, according to him.”

“At least.”

Kieran laughed.

Leo did not. Not immediately.

Taiga looked at him.

Leo was focused on the bracket.

“You good?”

Leo glanced up.

“I'm holding a rack.”

“That wasn't what I asked.”

“Then ask me when I'm not under a canoe.”

There was enough humor in it to pass.

Barely.

Taiga let it pass.

For now.

---

By eleven, the rack was solid.

Yoshinori tested it with both hands.

“It is not moving.”

Taiga wiped sawdust off his sleeve.

“It shouldn't.”

Kieran leaned against one post.

“That sounded familiar.”

Taiga looked at him.

“What sounded familiar?”

“You used to say that after fixing anything.”

Leo stepped away from the rack.

“He still does.”

Taiga looked between them.

Kieran smiled.

Leo did too.

Two people who knew different versions of him somehow reaching the same conclusion.

Taiga did not know why that bothered him.

Maybe because both of them looked pleased about it.

Yoshinori checked his watch.

“We are done.”

Yoichi appeared from behind Cabin Two-Seven carrying a screwdriver.

“That was fast.”

“You finished the shutters?”

“No. I abandoned them forever.”

Yoshinori waited.

Yoichi sighed.

“Yes, I finished the shutters.”

“Then we are done.”

Leo stretched both arms over his head.

“Actual freedom.”

Aiden came out of the lodge carrying a stack of folders.

“Until dinner.”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“We have six hours.”

“That sounds dangerous.”

“It should.”

Kieran smiled.

Yoichi looked toward the south cabins.

“If Sunshine makes me play soccer for six hours, I'm leaving.”

Leo pointed at him.

“You agreed.”

“I was outnumbered.”

“You were sitting at a table.”

“That’s exactly my point.”

Taiga started walking toward the cabins.

“Change first. You smell like wood treatment.”

Leo looked at his shirt.

“So do you.”

“Then both of us smell.”

Kieran laughed behind them.

Taiga looked over his shoulder.

“You too.”

Kieran sniffed his sleeve.

“That's fair.”

---

Lunch was quick because everyone wanted the lodge cleared before the sponsor arrived.

Goro had changed shirts.

That alone made the meeting feel serious.

Yuri had her hair tied back.

Aiden carried a tray of coffee into the office and complained that nobody important ever wanted tea.

Yoshinori stood by the door with three folders under one arm.

He looked at the four younger men.

“You are officially dismissed until dinner.”

Leo saluted.

“Understood. We’re gone.”

“You do not need to salute.”

“Too late.”

Yoshinori smiled.

“Please do not break anything expensive.”

Yoichi looked offended.

“Why are you looking at me?”

“Experience tells me to.”

A car rolled up the gravel road behind them.

Goro straightened.

Aiden looked through the window.

“That'll be them.”

Yuri waved the younger group toward the back door.

“Go have fun. Quietly.”

Leo whispered:

“Quiet fun.”

Taiga pushed him outside.

“Come on, move.”

---

The south field was barely a field.

Grass.

Two old wooden goals without nets.

One side sloped enough that Taiga immediately objected.

Leo immediately said it was perfect.

Yoichi looked at the ground like it had personally disappointed him.

Kieran stood with his hands on his hips.

“I remember this being bigger.”

“You were shorter,” Taiga said.

“That might be it.”

Leo dropped the ball between them.

“All right. Teams.”

Yoichi pointed at Taiga.

“I want Dynamite.”

“Absolutely not.”

“Why not?”

“Because then I get Kieran and the game lasts three minutes.”

Kieran looked at Leo.

“Thanks for that.”

“You know what I mean.”

“I really don't.”

Taiga folded his arms.

“Me and Kieran. You and Yoichi.”

Leo smiled.

“Exactly. That’s what I said.”

Yoichi stared at him.

“Why am I being punished?”

“Skill balance.”

“I have no skill.”

“That is the balance.”

Yoichi shoved Leo.

Leo laughed.

They used jackets for touchlines.

There was no referee.

That became a problem immediately.

Leo scored first.

Yoichi accidentally made a decent pass while trying to clear the ball, Leo caught it in stride, and Taiga was half a second too far left.

Leo put it inside the far post.

Then turned with both arms up.

“That’s one.”

Taiga pointed at Yoichi.

“That was luck.”

Yoichi looked proud.

“Still counts.”

Leo backed toward his side.

“Scoreboard doesn't care.”

“There is no scoreboard.”

“Then I'll remember for everyone.”

Taiga kicked a clump of dirt at him.

They restarted.

Kieran was not terrible.

That was inconvenient for Leo.

He was rusty, but he listened.

Taiga pointed once.

Kieran moved.

Taiga sent the ball into space.

Kieran reached it and pushed it back. Simple.

Enough.

Taiga shot.

Goal.

Kieran grinned.

Leo looked at both of them.

“Okay. So we're doing combinations now.”

Taiga walked backward.

“You scared?”

“I'm not scared.”

“Sounded scared.”

Yoichi looked between them.

“This is why sports people are unbearable.”

The next ten minutes were mostly Leo and Taiga trying to beat each other directly.

Leo nutmegged him once.

Taiga took the ball back thirty seconds later.

Yoichi committed something that would have been a foul in almost any organized sport.

Kieran fell over laughing before the play even finished.

Then the old rhythm showed up again.

Kieran had the ball near the side.

Taiga moved toward the middle.

Kieran looked at him. No call. No pointing. Just passed.

Taiga was already there.

One touch back.

Kieran returned it.

Taiga shot.

The ball hit the post and went in.

Kieran threw both hands up.

“Yes! Finally!”

Taiga laughed.

“That's two.”

Leo picked up the ball from inside the goal.

His smile was still there.

Thinner.

“Congratulations. You discovered passing.”

Kieran's expression changed.

Only a little.

Taiga caught it.

“So did you.”

Leo tossed the ball onto the grass.

“I know.”

Yoichi looked at him.

“Sunshine, chill.”

“What now?”

“Nothing. Keep playing.”

Leo stared at him.

Yoichi stared back.

Then shrugged.

“Kick off.”

The game restarted.

Leo played harder. Not dirty. Just harder.

He pressed Kieran sooner.

Closed Taiga faster.

Stopped laughing after every challenge.

Taiga noticed because Leo usually laughed through half of training.

At one point Kieran took a touch too long.

Leo stepped in.

Shoulder to shoulder.

Kieran lost balance and stumbled.

Taiga stopped.

“Hey, easy.”

Leo stopped too.

Kieran raised a hand.

“I'm fine.”

Leo stepped back.

“I didn't mean to hit you that hard.”

“You didn't.”

“You almost knocked him over,” Taiga said.

Leo looked at him.

“It was shoulder to shoulder.”

“It's a field behind a summer camp.”

“And we're playing football.”

Taiga stared.

Leo stared back.

Yoichi walked between them and tapped the ball with his shoe.

“You two done measuring dicks?”

Kieran made a choking sound.

Taiga looked away first.

“Just play.”

Leo exhaled.

“Fine. Let’s play.”

The rest of the match stayed physical. Not angry. Not exactly.

Taiga stopped trying to name it.

They finished four-three.

Leo and Yoichi won.

Leo acted like they had won a championship.

Yoichi claimed he had carried him.

Kieran dropped onto the grass.

Taiga sat beside him.

Leo stayed standing for a moment.

Then sat on Taiga's other side. Still there.

That mattered.

Yoichi remained on his feet because the grass was damp and apparently his dignity had limits.

Leo leaned back on both hands.

“Four-three, by the way.”

Taiga looked at him.

“You said you would remember for everyone. We know.”

“Just making sure.”

Kieran laughed.

“You were always like this?”

Taiga pointed at Leo.

“He’s worse.”

Leo turned.

“You met me in September.”

“Long enough.”

Yoichi snorted.

“That line's mine.”

For the first time all afternoon, Leo laughed without anything sharp under it.

Taiga relaxed before he realized he had been waiting to.

They stayed on the field a while longer, but nobody wanted another match.

Yoichi went to the lodge and came back with four bottles of water and a bag of chips he claimed Aiden had “voluntarily surrendered.”

Leo's knee rested against Taiga's while they passed the bag around.

Kieran took a chip and looked toward the old goal.

“SportsFest used to put the finish banner over there.”

Taiga followed his hand.

The post was gone now.

Only a darker patch in the grass showed where it had been.

“Yeah, right there.”

“You tore the banner down once.”

Taiga looked at him.

“I did not.”

Kieran started laughing.

“You absolutely did. You ran through it before they told you the race was over.”

“That wasn't my fault.”

Leo turned toward him.

“How is running through a finish banner not your fault?”

“They put it too low.”

Kieran covered his mouth with one hand.

“You tried to blame the wind.”

“The wind was involved.”

Leo laughed.

“Of course it was.”

Taiga reached for the chips.

Leo pulled the bag away.

“You lose snack privileges for that defense.”

“Give me the bag.”

“Not happening.”

Taiga leaned across him.

Leo twisted away, still laughing.

For a few seconds it was just them again.

Then Kieran said:

“He used to do that too.”

Leo's arm stopped halfway through moving the chips.

Taiga looked across at Kieran.

“Do what?”

“Act like something was stolen when it was already his.”

Kieran smiled, apparently pleased with the memory.

Taiga looked back at Leo.

Leo handed him the bag.

“Fine. Here.”

No joke attached.

Taiga took it.

Yoichi's eyes moved from Leo to Kieran and then to Taiga.

He said nothing.

That was worse than an insult.

---

They stayed on the field after the game. Not playing. Just existing.

The sponsor meeting kept the adults inside.

Nobody called them for anything.

Yoichi eventually went to get Yuki.

Leo lay flat on the grass despite spending five minutes earlier complaining that it was damp.

Taiga sat with his knees up.

Kieran picked at a loose thread on his sleeve.

For a while they talked about nothing important.

Soccer.

Camp.

Olympus.

The fact that Yoichi had somehow scored despite insisting he hated the game.

Then Kieran said:

“I forgot this could be fun.”

Taiga looked over.

Kieran was looking at the empty goal.

“You mean camp?”

“All of it.”

His voice had gone quieter.

Taiga did not answer immediately.

Kieran rubbed the thread between two fingers.

“I spent so long remembering the last day I was here that I think I forgot there were other days.”

Taiga looked toward Leo.

Leo was still on his back, one forearm over his eyes.

Taiga could not tell whether he was listening. Probably.

Kieran continued:

“I know coming back doesn't fix anything.”

Taiga looked at him again.

“No, it doesn’t.”

“I know.”

Kieran's mouth moved into a small smile.

“But I'm glad I did.”

That was harder to argue with.

Taiga looked toward the field.

“Yeah, I am too.”

Kieran turned.

Taiga regretted the answer immediately because Kieran looked too relieved.

He added:

“You worked. You didn't make shit weird.”

Kieran laughed softly.

“Very high praise.”

“Take it.”

“I will.”

Leo sat up.

“Yoichi's coming back.”

Taiga followed his gaze.

Yoichi was walking toward them with Yuki.

Conversation over.

Maybe that was good.

---

They spent another hour around the field. Not all soccer.

Leo tried to teach Yoichi how to juggle the ball.

Yoichi managed two touches and declared the exercise stupid.

Kieran got to five.

Leo got to thirty-seven before Taiga kicked the ball away.

That started another argument.

Then Yuki stole one of the jackets they had used as a touchline marker and ran with it.

Yoichi chased her.

Leo chased Yoichi.

Kieran laughed hard enough to sit down again.

Taiga ended up chasing all of them because the jacket was his.

By the time they got back to the lodge, the sponsor's car was gone.

Aiden was outside stretching his back.

He looked at Taiga's jacket.

There was grass stuck to one sleeve.

“Good afternoon?”

Taiga looked at Yuki.

“She committed theft.”

Aiden nodded.

“Sounds productive.”

Leo came up behind him.

“We won.”

Aiden looked at him.

“Against who?”

“Taiga, obviously.”

“That explains why you're leading with it.”

Taiga shoved Leo toward the door.

“Go drink water.”

Leo laughed.

He went.

Kieran followed more slowly.

As he passed Taiga, their eyes met.

He looked happier than he had Monday.

Taiga noticed.

He did not know what to do with that.

---

Dinner was louder than the sponsor meeting had apparently been.

Aiden complained that they had spent three hours discussing numbers nobody would remember next week.

Goro insisted the numbers mattered.

Yuri said the sponsor liked the preseason plan.

Yoshinori looked relieved enough that Taiga assumed the meeting had gone well.

Leo sat beside Taiga.

Kieran sat across from them.

Yoichi sat near the end with Yuki underneath.

Kieran asked Leo about Olympus soccer.

Leo answered.

Properly. No stiffness.

Taiga started to think maybe whatever had been wrong earlier had burned itself out on the field.

Then Kieran asked:

“Do you and Taiga always play on the same side?”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“Usually, yeah.”

Taiga tore a piece of bread.

“Same team.”

“I meant outside matches too.”

Leo smiled faintly.

“He hates losing enough that splitting us up is dangerous.”

Taiga pointed bread at him.

“You spent all afternoon trying to kill me.”

“You survived.”

“Barely survived.”

Kieran laughed.

Leo did too. Again, almost normal.

Taiga wanted to believe it.

So he did.

For dinner, at least.

---

Afterward, Taiga went to shower first.

He was covered in sweat, grass and whatever Yuki had put on his jacket.

Leo stayed outside with Yoichi.

Kieran was helping Aiden carry empty serving trays into the kitchen.

Taiga took his clothes and went back to Cabin Two-Twelve.

The shower took longer than usual.

Hot water.

Quiet. No Kieran. No Leo acting strange. No football.

For ten minutes, Taiga stopped thinking.

When he came out, Leo was not in the cabin.

Taiga dressed.

Sat on the bunk.

Checked his phone.

Nothing important.

Five minutes.

Ten.

Through the window he saw two figures cross the yard from the direction of the lodge.

Leo and Kieran.

They separated near the cabin path.

Kieran turned toward Two-Ten.

Leo kept coming.

Taiga looked back at his phone before Leo reached the door.

Then the door opened.

Leo came in.

Alone.

He shut the door behind him.

Taiga looked up.

“You showering?”

“In a minute.”

Leo crossed the room and took his clean clothes from the end of his bed.

Something was wrong again.

More wrong.

Taiga watched him open the bathroom door.

“Leo, wait.”

Leo stopped.

“What is it?”

“You good?”

Leo's shoulders moved with one breath.

“Yeah, I'm fine.”

Bullshit.

Taiga almost said it.

Then remembered Wednesday.

He had already pushed once.

Leo had asked for nothing.

So Taiga let him go.

“Okay. I’ll leave it.”

Leo went into the bathroom.

The shower started.

Taiga stared at the closed door.

Something had happened between the field and now.

He had no idea what.

---

Leo came out twenty minutes later.

Hair wet.

T-shirt sticking slightly to his shoulders.

He put his dirty clothes into the laundry bag and sat on his bunk.

Did not reach for the phone.

Did not say anything.

Taiga lasted maybe thirty seconds.

“You're acting weird.”

Leo looked over.

“I'm not.”

“Bullshit.”

That got a reaction.

Leo's jaw tightened.

Taiga sat up.

“There. That.”

“What do you want me to say?”

“The truth would be a nice change.”

Leo stared at him.

Taiga heard the sentence after it left his mouth.

Wrong wording.

Too late.

Leo laughed once. No humor.

“Okay. Fine.”

He looked down at his hands.

Then back at Taiga.

“Kieran told me you two used to be together.”

Everything inside Taiga stopped at once. Not his body. Just the noise.

“What did you say?”

Leo's eyes did not leave him.

“He told me you dated.”

Taiga felt heat climb his neck. Not embarrassment. Not exactly.

Something sharper.

“Kieran told you that?”

“Yeah, he did.”

“When did he tell you?”

“While you were showering.”

Taiga looked toward the door like Kieran might somehow be standing outside it.

He wasn't.

Leo continued:

“He said you were together before Camp Buddy.”

Taiga looked back at him.

“So what?”

The words came out harder than he meant.

Leo's expression changed.

“Why did you lie to me?”

That hit harder.

Taiga stood.

“I didn't.”

“You told me he was your best friend.”

“He was.”

“You know that's not the same thing.”

“It wasn't false.”

Leo looked away for one second.

Then back.

“I know.”

“Then don't call me a liar.”

“I didn't say you made something up.”

“You literally asked why I lied to you.”

“Because you gave me the version that left out the part you knew mattered.”

Taiga felt his shoulders tighten.

“Mattered to who?”

Leo went quiet.

Taiga stepped closer without meaning to.

“Why the fuck does it matter?”

Leo stood too.

“I don't know.”

“Then what are we doing?”

“I thought you trusted me.”

The room changed.

Taiga stared.

“What are you talking about?”

Leo swallowed.

“I thought you trusted me.”

Taiga could not believe what he was hearing.

“I told you about the journal.”

“I know.”

“I told you what I did to Keitaro.”

“I know.”

“I told you about Pinetree. I brought you here.”

“I know, Taiga.”

“Then don't stand there and tell me I don't trust you.”

Leo's voice rose.

“I didn't say you don't trust me at all!”

“That's what you're making it sound like.”

“No. I'm saying I saw something was wrong on Sunday, and I didn't ask because I thought if you wanted me to know, you'd tell me.”

“I did tell you.”

“You told me he was your friend.”

“He was my friend.”

“He was your boyfriend.”

The word hit the cabin walls.

Taiga flinched internally.

Leo's eyes flicked across Taiga's face.

His voice dropped again.

“And you knew that was different.”

Taiga looked away.

For the first time since the argument started, he did not have an answer ready.

Leo continued.

“I wasn't asking for every detail. I wasn't asking you to explain your whole life. I just…”

He stopped.

Taiga looked back.

Leo shook his head.

“Forget it.”

“No. Finish.”

Leo's mouth tightened.

“I believed you trusted me enough not to make me work around the truth.”

That hurt.

Enough that Taiga immediately got angry again.

“I wasn't making you work around anything.”

“You watched me talk to him for three days knowing I had no idea.”

“So what was I supposed to do? Give you a list of everyone I've dated before you're allowed to speak to them?”

Leo recoiled slightly.

“That is not what I said.”

“It sounds like it.”

“Because you're trying to make it ridiculous.”

“Because it is ridiculous.”

The sentence landed.

Leo went still.

Taiga heard himself.

He could have stopped.

He didn't.

“There was no reason to tell you.”

Leo stared at him.

The anger did not get louder.

That was worse.

“No reason.”

Taiga's stomach tightened.

“That isn't what I meant.”

“What did you mean?”

“That it was private.”

Leo nodded once.

“Right. Private from me.”

“That is not what I said.”

“You didn't have to.”

Taiga's jaw tightened.

“What the hell is that supposed to mean?”

Leo looked away.

For a second he seemed to be searching for words.

When he looked back, the anger was quieter.

“That I thought I mattered enough that you wouldn't have to decide what version of the truth was safe for me.”

“Then say that.”

“I just did.”

“No. You said there was no reason.”

Taiga dragged one hand over his face.

“This is fucking stupid.”

Leo laughed again.

Quiet.

“Yeah. Maybe it is.”

Taiga hated that answer.

“Why do you care?”

Leo looked at him.

For one second Taiga thought he might actually answer.

Then Leo's face closed.

“I don't.”

Bullshit.

Taiga knew it now. Not why.

But the answer was bullshit.

“Don't do that.”

“Do what?”

“Stand there acting like you're not pissed.”

“I am pissed.”

“Then tell me why.”

“I already did.”

“No, you told me about trust.”

“That is why.”

“It can't be the whole reason.”

Leo's eyes sharpened.

“Why? Because you decided it isn't?”

Taiga stopped.

Pinetree.

Wrong direction.

He heard it.

Too late to take back the last five minutes.

Leo looked exhausted now. Not physically.

Taiga knew what physically tired Leo looked like.

Leo sat back down.

“I don't want to do this tonight.”

Taiga remained standing.

“You started it.”

Leo looked up.

“You asked.”

That shut him up.

Leo pulled the blanket back.

Taiga stared at him.

“So that's it?”

“For tonight, yeah.”

“You're just going to sleep?”

“What do you want me to do?”

“I don't know.”

“Neither do I.”

Taiga looked toward the window.

Dark outside.

His reflection faint.

Leo lay down. Not facing him.

Taiga stood there another few seconds before sitting on his own bunk.

His chest felt too tight.

He was angry at Leo.

Angry at Kieran.

Angry at himself for not knowing which part deserved the most. Mostly he kept hearing one sentence.

*I thought you trusted me.*

Taiga turned off the light.

Neither of them said good night.
