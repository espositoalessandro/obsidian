Sunday morning began with rain. Not hard rain.

The patient kind.

It tapped against the cabin roof before sunrise and kept going after the sky had turned gray enough to count as morning.

Taiga woke to the sound of it.

For a few seconds he did not remember where he was.

Wood ceiling. Cold air above the blanket.

The smell of damp pine somewhere beyond the wall.

Then Leo moved across the room.

Taiga looked over.

Leo was awake.

Barely.

He had one arm hanging off the side of the bunk and his hair pressed flat on one side.

Taiga stared at it.

Leo opened one eye.

“What are you looking at?”

“Your hair lost a fight.”

Leo lifted one hand and touched the side of his head.

It made things worse.

Taiga laughed.

“Great. Helpful.”

“You asked.”

“I asked what you were looking at, not for a character assassination before seven.”

Taiga checked the time.

Six fifty-eight.

Close enough.

Outside, rain ticked steadily against the window.

Leo rolled onto his back.

“Please tell me rain cancels camp maintenance.”

“It changes camp maintenance.”

“That answer was significantly worse.”

Taiga sat up.

Leo pulled the blanket over his face.

“Wake me up when preseason becomes a desk job.”

“You study Computer Science.”

“I already have a desk job. I came here for lumber.”

“You complained about lumber yesterday.”

“I complained about mud inside my sock. The lumber was innocent.”

Taiga got dressed.

By the time he pulled on a sweatshirt, Leo had accepted that consciousness was mandatory.

He sat up slowly.

The blanket dropped.

His shirt had ridden up during the night. Just enough skin above the waistband.

Taiga saw it.

Looked away.

Better than February. Still not nothing.

Leo yawned.

“You okay?”

Taiga reached for his boots.

“I'm putting shoes on.”

“That was not what I asked.”

“Then ask better questions.”

Leo watched him for another second.

Taiga could feel it.

Then Leo smiled.

“Good morning to you too.”


Breakfast moved indoors around the weather.

The dining hall windows were fogged at the corners.

Aiden had eggs, toast, potatoes and a pan of something involving peppers.

Yoichi was already eating when Taiga and Leo came in.

He looked at Leo's hair.

Stopped chewing.

Then he grinned.

“Sunshine, your head looks like you slept in a hedge.”

Leo pulled out a chair.

“Good morning to you too.”

Taiga sat down.

Leo looked at him.

“And you're enjoying this.”

“I already had my turn in the cabin.”

“That does not make it better.”

Yuri came through the kitchen carrying mugs.

She saw Leo.

Her mouth opened.

Leo closed his eyes.

“Please don't.”

Yuri covered her smile with one hand.

“I was only going to say good morning.”

“No, you weren't.”

“Alright, maybe I was going to say your hair is adorable.”

Taiga took coffee.

Leo looked betrayed by the room.

Aiden set a plate in front of him.

“Eat. You can fix your dignity later.”

“Thank you.”

“There you go. Priorities.”

Goro sat at the end of the table with a weather report open on his phone.

Yoshinori had the clipboard.

Of course.

Taiga looked at it.

“What's the damage?”

Yoshinori glanced toward the windows.

“The lower trail can wait. The rain should stop around noon, but there is no reason to churn the drainage path into mud before then. We have more than enough indoor work.”

Leo sat beside Taiga.

“What kind of indoor work?”

“Main lodge windows, storage inventory, tool maintenance, and the kitchen shelves Aiden has been avoiding since Wednesday.”

Aiden looked offended.

“I haven't been avoiding them. I've been allowing the problem to mature.”

Yoichi snorted.

“You mean the shelf fell down.”

“One bracket failed.”

“It dumped six pans on your head.”

“Five pans.”

“Big difference.”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“I like it here.”

Taiga drank coffee.

“That says bad things about you.”

Yuri sat across from them.

“Oh, I think it says excellent things about him.”

Taiga did not like her tone.

Yuri continued:

“He fits in very naturally.”

Leo paused with his fork halfway to his mouth.

Taiga stared at his plate.

One normal sentence somehow landed worse than the teasing.

Yuri looked between them.

Then her smile returned.

“Also, he laughs at my jokes.”

“They're not jokes,” Taiga said.

“See? This is why I needed a new audience.”

Leo finally took the bite.

“I support the arts.”

Yuri pointed at him.

“That is exactly what I mean!”

Taiga regretted bringing him. Not actually.

That was the problem.


Yoshinori split them after breakfast.

Taiga expected Leo to stay with him.

The assumption happened before he noticed it was an assumption.

Instead Yoshinori said:

“Taiga, I need you with me in the main lodge. There are two window frames I would like you to check before we decide whether they need replacing.”

Taiga nodded.

Then Yoshinori turned.

“Leo, would you mind helping Yoichi with the maintenance room? Several hand tools need cleaning and oiling before we put them back into rotation.”

Leo looked toward Yoichi.

Yoichi looked back.

“Can he tell a wrench from a screwdriver?”

Leo leaned back in his chair.

“I can also identify several advanced objects, including hammers.”

Yoichi considered.

“Fine. Don't touch the power tools unless I tell you.”

“I know how to follow instructions.”

Taiga looked at Leo.

Leo caught it.

“What are you looking at?”

“I thought you'd end up on the windows.”

Leo shrugged.

“Apparently I've been promoted to wrench identification.”

Yoichi stood.

“Don't make me demote you.”

Leo grabbed his coffee.

“See you later.”

Taiga nodded.

“Try not to lose a finger.”

“That's almost concern.”

“It's paperwork.”

Leo smiled and followed Yoichi out.

Taiga watched them go.

Yoshinori picked up the clipboard.

“The windows are this way.”

Taiga looked at him.

Yoshinori's expression was neutral enough to be suspicious.

Taiga decided not to investigate.

He followed him out.

---

The main lodge smelled like old wood and rain.

Yoshinori had already moved the furniture away from the east wall.

Taiga checked the first frame. Fine.

Second frame.

Less fine.

He pressed the lower corner.

Soft.

“Water got behind this.”

“I thought so.”

“Seal failed.”

“Can we save the frame?”

Taiga checked farther up.

“Probably. Need to cut this section out.”

Yoshinori crouched beside him.

They worked quietly.

That was another thing Taiga liked about Yoshinori.

He could talk.

A lot.

Everybody knew that.

But he did not need to fill silence just because silence existed.

They removed the damaged trim.

Measured.

Marked.

Yoshinori handed him the pry bar without asking.

Taiga took it. Normal.

After ten minutes, Yoshinori said:

“Leo seems comfortable.”

Taiga nearly slipped.

Of course.

“He's been here a day.”

“That is true.”

“That is not enough time to decide anything.”

“I did not say it was.”

Taiga pulled another nail.

Yoshinori held the board steady.

“He is useful.”

“He plays soccer. He knows how to work.”

“I gathered that.”

Taiga looked at him.

Yoshinori's attention stayed on the frame. No smile. No Yuri bullshit. Just observation.

Taiga went back to the nail.

“He likes it.”

“I can tell.”

“That surprised me.”

“What surprised you about it?”

Taiga thought.

Because Camp Buddy was his.

Because Leo belonged to Olympus.

Because those had felt like separate facts until Friday.

“Don't know.”

Yoshinori did not accept the answer immediately.

But he did not challenge it either.

After a moment:

“I am glad you brought him.”

Taiga tightened his grip on the pry bar.

“Yuri already said enough about that.”

“I am not Yuri.”

“No shit.”

Yoshinori laughed.

Then:

“You do not bring many people here.”

That landed differently.

Taiga looked at the damaged wood.

“I brought nobody.”

“That was my point.”

No accusation. No implication beyond what existed. Still enough.

Taiga pulled the last nail.

The trim came free.

“Hold this.”

Yoshinori took it.

Conversation over.

Mercifully.

---

Through the open service door, Leo sat on a workbench with an old pair of pruning shears in his hands.

Yoichi stood beside him.

Talking.

Leo said something Taiga could not hear.

Yoichi shoved his shoulder.

Leo nearly dropped the shears.

Then laughed.

Yoichi laughed too. Actually laughed.

Not the mean bark he used when somebody embarrassed themselves.

A real one.

Taiga stared.

Yoshinori came up behind him carrying replacement trim.

“Would you hold the door?”

Taiga looked away.

“I've got it.”

He took the door.

---

The rain stopped at eleven forty.

By noon the camp had changed color.

Dark bark.

Wet roofs.

Puddles in every depression.

Clouds lifting from the ridge.

Aiden moved lunch outside under the dining hall awning because apparently weather had become a personal challenge.

Taiga sat on the steps with a bowl of soup.

Leo came over with Yoichi. Not behind him.

With him.

Yoichi had oil on one sleeve.

Leo had a dark smear across one cheek. Again.

Taiga looked at it.

Leo caught him.

“What are you looking at?”

“You're dirty.”

Leo touched the wrong cheek.

“Which side?”

“Your other cheek.”

He tried again. Still missed.

Taiga sighed.

“Come here.”

Leo leaned closer.

Taiga wiped the smear with his thumb and pulled his hand back.

“You got it now.”

Leo's face had changed color.

Slightly.

“Thanks for that.”

Yoichi looked between them, then went back to his soup with the faintest smirk.

Taiga ignored him.

His face said enough.

Leo sat beside Taiga.

Yoichi sat on Leo's other side.

Aiden passed with bread.

“Maintenance survived?”

Yoichi said:

“Sunshine's not completely useless.”

Leo took bread.

“I'm putting that on my résumé.”

“Then your résumé sucks.”

“Strong reference.”

Aiden looked at the two of them.

“Sounds like you figured each other out.”

Yoichi shrugged.

“He listens.”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“He said that yesterday too. I'm building a reputation.”

Taiga ate soup.

“That is a dangerous reputation.”

Leo smiled.

Yuri came out carrying a stack of bowls.

She looked at the three of them sitting in a row.

“Aw. Look at that.”

Taiga immediately tensed.

Yuri pointed at Yoichi.

“You made a friend.”

Yoichi nearly choked.

“Fuck off.”

Leo laughed.

Yuri looked delighted.

“I wasn't even talking about Taiga and Leo this time!”

“That doesn't make it better,” Taiga said.

“It makes it much better.”

Yoichi stood with his bowl.

“I'm eating somewhere else.”

Leo looked up.

“Where are you going?”

“Away from her.”

“That seems reasonable.”

Yoichi pointed at him.

“You can come.”

Leo looked at Taiga. Not asking permission. Just checking.

That distinction mattered.

Taiga nodded toward the path.

“Go. He'll get lost alone.”

Yoichi flipped him off.

Leo stood.

“I'll protect him.”

“From what?”

“The dangers of friendship.”

Yoichi shoved him toward the maintenance shed.

“Move, Sunshine.”

Leo went.

Taiga watched them cross the courtyard.

Yuri sat in the space Leo had left and held out the bread basket.

“Want another one?”

Taiga took a piece.

“Yeah, thanks.”

Yuri followed his gaze toward the maintenance shed.

Then, very gently:

“He really does fit in.”

Taiga's fingers stopped around the bread.

Yuri did not smile this time.

Taiga looked toward the maintenance shed.

Leo was saying something with his hands.

Yoichi was arguing back. Easy.

Like they had known each other longer than a day.

“Yeah, he really does,” Taiga said.

Yuri leaned back. No teasing. Just that.

“I'm glad.”

Taiga looked at her.

She stood before he could answer.

“Now I am going to bother my father.”

The afternoon belonged to the dock.

The rain had cleared enough for inspection.

Goro insisted on supervising the first ten minutes.

Nobody knew why. Probably authority.

He stood at the shore with his arms crossed.

“The damaged section remains off-limits until Yoshinori confirms the replacement boards are secure.”

Yoichi looked at the two bright ropes blocking three planks.

“Yeah. We can see that.”

Goro's eyes narrowed.

“I am stating it because somebody will inevitably decide the rope is a decorative suggestion.”

Yoichi looked at Taiga.

Taiga looked at Yoichi.

Both looked away.

Goro pointed.

“That is exactly why I am saying it.”

Leo covered a laugh.

Yoshinori arrived with the tool bag.

“Taiga, Leo, you can take measurements on the outer section. Yoichi, help me remove the damaged boards.”

Yuri came behind him with a camera.

Taiga stared.

“Why do you have a camera?”

“I need preseason documentation.”

“You need pictures of wood?”

“I need pictures of volunteers working.”

Her eyes moved toward Leo.

Taiga pointed at her.

“Do not point that thing at us.”

Yuri laughed.

“I haven't done anything!”

“You haven't done anything yet.”

Leo took the measuring tape.

“I think I'm starting to understand the system.”

“You're encouraging her again.”

“I haven't even spoken to her.”

“That doesn't matter.”

Yuri lifted the camera.

“Stand closer.”

Taiga nearly walked into the lake.

“I am not posing for this.”

“Taiga, I need both of you in the frame.”

“Take two pictures.”

“That defeats the purpose.”

“What purpose?”

“The documentation needs both volunteers.”

Leo looked at the dock.

Then at Taiga.

“I think arguing is making this take longer.”

Traitor.

Taiga stepped beside him.

Yuri lifted the camera.

“A little closer, please.”

“We're touching.”

Their shoulders were already together.

Yuri looked through the viewfinder.

“You are barely in the same frame.”

“This is a work photo.”

“Yes. A very cute work photo.”

Taiga felt Leo start laughing beside him.

“Stop laughing at this.”

“I'm trying.”

“You're failing.”

The shutter clicked.

Yuri looked at the screen.

“Aww~! That came out adorable!”

Taiga walked away.

Leo followed.

“What does it look like?”

“Do not ask.”

Yuri called after them:

“You both look adorable!”

Leo's ears turned pink.

Taiga's probably did too.

Yoichi shouted from the blocked section:

“Dynamite, quit flirting with Crazy Lady and bring the damn tape!”

Taiga turned so fast he nearly tripped.

“I'M NOT FLIRTING WITH ANYONE!”

Yoichi laughed hard enough to bend over.

Leo leaned on the railing.

Useless.

Completely useless.


The dock inspection took two hours.

Actual work saved them from Yuri. Mostly.

Taiga and Leo measured spacing, checked fasteners and marked two more boards for replacement.

The lake moved under them. Cold wind.

Sun breaking through clouds.

At one point Leo crouched beside the railing.

“Is this one supposed to move?”

Taiga stepped over.

“How much?”

Leo pushed.

The post shifted.

“Enough that I don't trust it.”

Taiga crouched beside him.

Checked the base.

“Bolts are loose.”

“Can we tighten them?”

“Probably. Hold it straight.”

Leo braced both hands around the post.

Taiga knelt.

The angle was awkward.

He reached around Leo's leg for the wrench.

His forearm brushed Leo's calf.

Nothing.

He tightened the first bolt.

Then the second.

Leo held the post.

Steady.

Taiga looked up.

Leo was looking down at him. Not at the wrench.

At him.

Taiga's stomach tightened.

Then Yoichi shouted from the shore:

“YOU TWO DONE OR YOU BUILDING A HOUSE OUT THERE?”

Leo looked away first.

“We are almost done!”

Taiga finished the bolt.

His hand slipped once.

Leo's fingers tightened around the post.

When Taiga stood, Leo's shoulder bumped his.

Taiga carried the wrench back.

Yoichi watched him approach. No grin.

Worse.

“What are you looking at now?”

Yoichi took the wrench.

“You ask that a lot.”

“Because people keep looking at me.”

“Maybe stop doing weird shit.”

“What weird shit?”

Yoichi glanced toward Leo.

Taiga followed the look.

Leo was checking the railing again.

“Nothing you need to worry about,” Yoichi said.

Taiga stared.

Yoichi's mouth twitched.

Asshole.

By late afternoon, the sky had cleared completely.

The lake reflected pale blue.

Taiga had just finished putting the dock tools away when Yoshinori found him and Leo outside the maintenance shed.

He held a folded piece of paper.

That was immediately suspicious.

Taiga looked at it.

“What did Aiden forget?”

Yoshinori smiled.

“More than one thing, apparently.”

Leo took the list when Yoshinori offered it.

Milk.

Eggs.

Onions.

Dish soap.

Coffee.

Rice.

Bread.

Something written at the bottom in Aiden's handwriting that looked like **good tomatoes**.

Leo frowned.

“How exactly are we supposed to evaluate whether the tomatoes are good?”

“Aiden says you will know.”

“That is not reassuring.”

Taiga took the list.

“There's a market near the bus stop.”

Yoshinori nodded.

“About twenty minutes on foot if you take the road rather than the trail. We could wait until tomorrow, but breakfast will be simpler if we have the milk and eggs tonight.”

Taiga looked toward the main lodge.

“Why can't Yoichi go?”

From inside the maintenance shed:

“Because I can fucking hear you.”

Yoshinori did not even turn.

“Yoichi is helping me finish the shelving.”

Leo looked at Taiga.

“I can handle a supermarket.”

“That remains to be seen.”

“I'm excellent at supermarkets.”

“You bought six kinds of cereal during finals.”

“They served different purposes.”

Yoshinori handed Taiga the camp card.

“Please try to return before six.”

Leo checked the time.

“Plenty of time.”

Taiga pocketed the card.

“Come on.”

The road into town curved away from the lake and gradually stopped looking like Camp Buddy.

Pines thinned.

Houses appeared.

Then a church.

A mechanic.

A pharmacy with a blinking green sign.

Leo walked beside Taiga with the grocery list open on his phone because he had taken a picture of the paper “in case weather happened.”

The weather did not happen.

“Do we really need two bags of rice?”

“Yeah, we need both.”

“That is a lot of rice.”

“There are six people eating at camp.”

“There are seven of us now.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo smiled.

“I count now.”

Something stupid warmed under Taiga's ribs.

He looked ahead.

“Unfortunately, you do.”

Leo bumped his shoulder lightly against Taiga's.

“That sounded like acceptance.”

“It is temporary.”

“Still counts.”

The supermarket sat at the far end of a small strip of shops.

Nothing special.

Automatic doors.

Fluorescent lights.

A row of carts with one bad wheel each.

Leo took one.

It immediately pulled left.

He looked at Taiga.

“Of course.”

“You picked it.”

“I picked a victim.”

“You can change it.”

“No. We have a relationship now.”

Taiga walked inside.

Leo dragged the crooked cart after him.


They split the list without actually splitting up.

Taiga handled things he knew Aiden would complain about if chosen incorrectly.

Leo handled things that came in boxes.

This division formed naturally.

Milk.

Eggs.

Rice.

Dish soap.

Coffee.

They argued about bread for forty seconds.

Leo picked up a loaf.

“This looks good.”

“It looks expensive.”

“Aiden wrote good bread.”

“He didn't write artisanal.”

“What if his standards are high?”

“They are. His budget isn't.”

Leo put it back.

“Fine. Practical bread.”

“That is exactly the problem.”

They reached produce.

Leo inspected tomatoes with inappropriate seriousness.

Taiga left him to it and went for onions.

He had three in the bag when he heard:

“Taiga?”

His hand stopped.

The voice was quiet.

Uncertain.

Familiar enough that Taiga's body knew before his brain finished catching up.

He turned.

Kieran stood at the end of the aisle with a basket in one hand.

For half a second, nothing fit.

Supermarket lights.

Onions.

Spring Break.

Kieran.

Then it did.

Badly.

Kieran looked older than the version in Taiga's head.

Of course he did.

His hair was a little different.

Face sharper.

Same eyes.

Same way of standing when he did not know whether he was welcome.

Taiga lowered the bag of onions.

“Kieran.”

Leo came around the end of the produce display with the cart.

He slowed when he saw Taiga's face. Not much. Just enough.

Kieran's eyes moved to him.

Then back to Taiga.

“I didn't expect to see you here.”

Taiga almost laughed at that.

“I could say the same.”

The answer came out flatter than he intended.

Kieran adjusted the basket against his hip.

“My aunt moved out here last year. I'm staying with her for a few days. I came in for groceries.”

That at least explained why the universe had decided to put Kieran in this specific produce aisle.

“I thought Camp Buddy was still closed.”

“It is, technically.”

Kieran waited.

Taiga could have left it there.

“Preseason started. Yoshinori's getting everything ready early, and I'm here for Spring Break.”

Kieran glanced toward the cart.

“So they put you to work.”

“Obviously they did.”

Something small moved across Kieran's face.

Almost a smile.

“That sounds about right.”

Taiga's shoulders tightened.

Kieran had said it like he knew.

He did know.

Leo remained beside the cart.

Quiet. Not awkwardly quiet.

Present.

Kieran's eyes moved to Leo again.

Taiga handled the introduction.

“This is Leo.”

Leo gave him an easy nod.

“Leo Mendez.”

“I’m Kieran.”

They shook hands.

Brief. Normal.

Leo's social expression stayed exactly where it needed to be.

Friendly enough.

Not curious enough to make the air worse.

Kieran looked between them.

“You're at Olympus too?”

Leo nodded.

“Same year. We play soccer together.”

“Computer Science too,” Taiga added.

Why had he added that?

Nobody asked.

Leo looked at him once.

Kieran did too.

“Sounds busy.”

“It is,” Leo said. “Spring Break was good timing.”

Kieran's attention returned to Taiga.

“How long are you staying?”

“Until Sunday.”

“Whole week?”

“That's usually what Spring Break means.”

Kieran paused, then let it pass.

“Right, fair enough.”

Taiga hated that more than if he had.

The old version of Kieran would have reacted.

Or maybe Taiga only remembered him that way because everything around those memories had teeth.

Kieran looked down at his basket.

Then back up.

“It's good that you're helping the camp.”

Taiga said nothing.

Kieran continued carefully.

“I heard Camp Buddy nearly went under after that term.”

That pulled Taiga's attention fully back.

“Who told you?”

“Nobody directly. People talk.”

Taiga's jaw tightened.

Kieran raised one hand slightly.

“I wasn't asking for details.”

Good.

Because Taiga was not giving them.

Kieran glanced toward the list in Leo's hand.

“You're probably in the middle of something.”

“We still have half the store.”

Leo looked at the cart.

“We have most of it.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo did not correct himself again.

Useful.

Kieran nodded.

“Then I won't keep you.”

For a second, it looked like he wanted to say something else.

Taiga could tell.

That was the horrible part.

He still knew that too.

Kieran settled for:

“It was good to see you.”

Taiga's grip tightened around the bag of onions.

He could lie.

Could say the same.

Could say nothing.

Instead:

“Yeah. You too.”

Not entirely true. Not entirely false.

That was probably the problem.

Kieran nodded once.

Then looked at Leo.

“Nice meeting you.”

“You too.”

Kieran stepped around them and continued toward the next aisle.

Taiga watched him go until the shelves blocked him from view.

Then he realized Leo was still holding the cart. Still saying nothing.

Taiga put the onions in.

“Did you find the tomatoes?”

Leo lifted the produce bag.

“I found some that meet Aiden's impossible standards.”

“Good, then we are done here.”

They moved on.

That was it.

Leo did not ask who Kieran was.

Did not ask why Taiga had gone rigid when he heard his voice.

Did not ask why Kieran had sounded like somebody who knew him.

He read the next item from the list.

“We still need coffee.”

“Other side of the store.”

“Lead the way.”

Taiga did.


They ran into Kieran once more near checkout. Not enough for another conversation. Just recognition across two lines.

Kieran lifted his hand.

Taiga nodded back.

Leo said nothing.

The cashier packed the groceries into four bags.

Taiga took two.

Leo took the other two.

Outside, the air had cooled.

They started back toward camp.

For the first five minutes, Leo talked about the bread. Not Kieran.

Bread.

“Aiden is absolutely going to tell us we bought the wrong one.”

“He'll eat it anyway.”

“That isn't the point.”

“It is when he's hungry.”

“He wrote *good bread* like we were entering a competition.”

Taiga adjusted the bag in his hand.

“He'll complain for thirty seconds, then make garlic bread.”

“That does sound useful.”

“That is what I keep telling you.”

They crossed the road.

Leo shifted one grocery bag higher against his hip. Still nothing.

Taiga had expected a question by now.

He was not sure why.

Maybe because anybody else would have asked.

Maybe because Taiga himself would have, depending on the person.

Leo didn't.

“This works.”

“I knew it.”

Almost suspiciously quickly.

Then looked away.

“Your friend has good priorities.”

“I asked one question.”

For several minutes, Kieran did not exist.

Then Kieran's name moved through Taiga's head.

He kept eating anyway.


After dinner, Taiga expected Leo to say something.

He did not.

They helped clear the table.

Yuri argued with Goro about a stack of old registration forms.

Yoichi disappeared toward the maintenance shed.

Aiden started prepping something for breakfast.

Leo dried dishes beside Taiga.

At one point their elbows knocked, and they shifted enough to keep working.

When the last plate went away, Leo stretched.

“My shoulders are filing a complaint.”

“You carried groceries for twenty minutes.”

“I also spent two hours on the dock.”

“That was before.”

“My body maintains records.”

“That sounds tragic.”

Leo smiled.

“I'm going to shower before Yoichi invents another task.”

“Go shower before he finds you.”

“I'll leave you some hot water.”

“There is enough hot water.”

“Then I'll leave you the *idea* of hot water.”

Taiga shook his head.

Leo left. Still no Kieran.

Taiga stood at the sink with the towel in his hand.

He should have been relieved.

He was. Mostly.

---

By ten, the cabin had gone quiet.

Leo sat cross-legged on his bed with the phone balanced against one knee.

Taiga had already showered.

He lay on top of the blanket, not tired enough to sleep.

The supermarket kept replaying in pieces.

Kieran saying his name.

Kieran saying *that sounds about right.*

Leo standing beside the cart.

Leo not asking.

That last part stuck more than it should have.

Taiga rolled onto his back.

Leo tapped something on the phone. Probably Enzo. Probably nonsense.

Taiga stared at the ceiling.

The words existed.

He did not like them.

That was not new.

He tried once.

Nothing came out.

Leo kept scrolling.

Taiga swallowed.

“...Leo?”

Leo looked over.

“Mm?”

Taiga almost aborted.

Could say never mind.

Could ask what Enzo wanted.

Could complain about tomorrow's work.

Instead he stared at the ceiling and forced the sentence through.

“...Thanks for coming.”

Leo's expression changed. Not dramatically. Just attention.

“To Camp Buddy?”

“Yeah, for coming here.”

Taiga picked at a loose thread near the edge of the blanket.

“You could've done something else for break.”

Leo set the phone down.

“I wanted to come.”

“Yeah, I know.”

Taiga hated how difficult the next part was.

He said it anyway.

“That's why I'm saying thanks.”

Leo was quiet for a moment.

Then:

“I'm glad you asked me.”

Taiga looked over.

Leo was smiling.

Small. No teasing. No making it bigger.

That helped.

Taiga nodded once.

“I'm glad you came.”

Leo's smile deepened.

“Me too.”

Taiga looked back at the ceiling before his face could do anything stupid.

Leo picked the phone up again.

A minute later Leo said:

“Enzo says Yoichi looks like he'd bite somebody.”

Taiga laughed.

“Tell him that's accurate.”

“Already did.”

Easy. Again.

---

Monday morning began with coffee and a vehicle Taiga recognized too quickly.

He and Leo had just stepped out of the dining hall.

Yoshinori was explaining the morning assignments to Yoichi near the lodge steps.

Yuri was carrying folded towels toward the cabins.

The engine came up the access road.

Taiga looked before anyone else did.

Dark car.

Same one.

His stomach dropped.

Leo followed his line of sight.

The vehicle stopped beside the main lodge.

The driver's door opened.

Kieran stepped out.

Yesterday he had been carrying groceries.

Today he had work boots on.

A duffel over one shoulder.

Gloves tucked into one hand.

Taiga stared.

Leo slowed beside him.

Recognition.

Nothing more.

Yoshinori turned toward the car.

And that was when Taiga noticed something worse.

He did not look surprised. Not even slightly.

Kieran closed the door and adjusted the duffel.

Yoshinori gave him a small, ordinary nod.

“Good morning, Kieran.”

Taiga looked at him.

Then back at Kieran.

His brain caught up.

“You knew he was coming?”

Yoshinori glanced at Taiga.

“He called last night.”

Taiga stared.

“And you didn't think that was worth mentioning?”

“You had already gone back to the cabin, and he asked whether he could volunteer for preseason work. I saw no reason to turn that into something larger before he had even arrived.”

There was something almost deliberate in the calmness of it.

Yoshinori had been there for enough of what happened years ago.

He had simply chosen, with infuriating scoutmaster neutrality, not to treat it as a crisis in advance.

Taiga was still deciding whether that made things better or worse when Kieran saw him.

Then Leo.

Kieran lifted one hand in a small, uncertain greeting.

“Morning, Taiga.”

Taiga looked at the duffel.

The gloves.

The boots.

Then back at Kieran.

“What the fuck are you doing here?”
