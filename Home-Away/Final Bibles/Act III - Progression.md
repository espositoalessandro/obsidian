This document records the narrative progression discussed while replanning Act III. It separates decisions from possibilities so later work can continue without reconstructing the conversation or treating suggestions as completed canon.

## 2. Why Act III needs a different purpose

The original draft devoted substantial space to physical awareness, uncertain touch, jealousy, and the eventual explicit acknowledgment of mutual attraction.

The Final rewrite has already moved much of that work into Act II. By the end of Chapter 20, Taiga and Leo both know that something romantic exists between them. Taiga has also explained the important history behind his reaction at Pinetree.

Act III therefore cannot use rediscovering attraction or establishing reciprocity as its main payoff.

The new direction is **Taiga's past-resolution arc**. Act II revealed the origins of his insecurities. Act III brings those insecurities into present situations where he has to make choices.

Acknowledgment changes the conditions. He can no longer experience the closeness entirely within a comfortable, unnamed flow. His choices now affect someone who knows the connection is meaningful.

## 3. The relationship state inherited from Act II

At the end of Final Chapter 20:

- Taiga acknowledges that he wants Leo around and that his feelings extend beyond ordinary friendship.
- Leo understands that he did not invent the significance of their closeness.
- Taiga explains his first relationship, the betrayal and rejection at camp, his treatment of Keitaro, and his fear of hurting people.
- Leo asks to remain involved in decisions about whether pursuing the connection is worth the risk.
- Leo asks that the presence of other people not make them retreat from how they treat each other.
- Taiga accepts deliberate affection and chooses to continue toward Leo.
- The conversation ends with warmth, relief, and an uneasy sense that their familiar relationship has changed.

This is a meaningful gain. It is not instant ease or complete readiness.

The next chapter should inherit the awkwardness as well as the permission to continue. Both boys still have to discover how to behave together after hurt, disclosure, and acknowledgment.

## 4. The central psychological movement

The agreed Act III question is whether Taiga can face the past and choose a present relationship without first repairing everything perfectly.

Relevant pressures, developed fully in the psychology bible:

- He fears being betrayed or misrepresented by someone important.
- He knows he has hurt people and fears repeating that behavior.
- His inner critic can turn a particular failure into a verdict about his character.
- He can defend himself intensely against an accusation, then condemn himself when he sees the other person's hurt.
- He has already improved substantially in friendship, belonging, and trust in new environments.
- Romance remains less practiced and more closely connected to the first relationship he believes he failed.

This is not a generic fear of losing independence or a fixed anxious/avoidant attachment pairing. He can enjoy affection, commitment, and being wanted. The difficulty must attach to a specific perceived threat or responsibility.

The narrative must distinguish Taiga's feared account of himself from an objective verdict. His restraint, initiative, and attempts at repair count as evidence of who he is too.

## 5. What Act III should accomplish

The intended gains are:

1. **A present decision about Kieran.** Taiga decides what connection, if any, he wants now instead of leaving their old relationship as an obligation he must resolve perfectly.
2. **More specific responsibility.** He can recognize neglecting Kieran without accepting responsibility for Kieran's framing and violence.
3. **A different experience of conflict.** He and Leo address a meaningful disagreement without the disagreement proving that closeness inevitably becomes retaliation, rejection, or destruction.
4. **Greater tolerance of disappointment.** Taiga begins allowing someone to be disappointed by an honest choice without treating that feeling as a problem he must eliminate.
5. **An active romantic choice.** He makes room for Leo and pursues something with him through behavior and initiative, beyond confirming that the feelings are mutual.

These gains can be partial and uneven. The act does not need to erase shame, make all trust effortless, or prevent later setbacks.

By the end, Leo should understand more clearly what Taiga is choosing to pursue with him. The exact agreement and milestones remain open.

## 6. What remains for Act IV

Act IV should test participation in an imperfect present relationship.

Material available for that act includes:

- Taiga's need to be useful, capable, and dependable;
- shame when he actually makes a consequential mistake;
- helplessness when someone he loves is hurt and he cannot fix it;
- accepting support when he feels that he has failed;
- expressing wants, taking initiative, and sustaining the relationship in ordinary life;
- the difference between making a romantic choice and consistently living with it.

The drafted Chapter 36, *Fault*, offers a relevant future test: Leo's injury leaves Taiga helpless; he tries to compensate through decisive action in the match, makes an error, and struggles with having failed other people's trust. This is useful source material, not a command to preserve the scene unchanged.

Later difficulty must contain evidence of Act III's progress. Asking for space and then returning voluntarily, acknowledging a specific mistake, or accepting care can distinguish a later crisis from Pinetree.

Do not make Act III solve the need for perfection so completely that Act IV has no meaningful pressure left. Equally, do not erase Act III's gains to create the later problem.

Dates, kissing, sexual intimacy, labels, and exclusivity are not assigned to acts by an inflexible quota. Their placement should follow the new story. Do not borrow a later major test just to fill the pre-camp interval.

A later public kiss may express initiative, joy, and commitment already developing. Respecting Leo's Chapter 20 request about their behavior around other people must begin earlier.

## 8. The main pacing constraint: approximately one month before camp

The current planning constraint is approximately a month between the post-Pinetree conversation and the Camp Buddy trip.

Exact dates and calendar reconciliation are not established here. Do not import the original draft's dates automatically.

A month needs visible progression. It cannot become either a long pause after acknowledgment or a sequence of physical-discovery beats already used in Act II.

**Crucial starting state:** Taiga is not ready to invite Leo to camp immediately after the conversation. He can become ready over the next couple of weeks. The invitation must be the result of that development.

The invitation is a substantial choice. It brings Leo into Taiga's emotional home, old relationships, and shared living space for an extended visit. That is a greater step than simply returning to class beside him.

## 10. First phase: rediscover their relationship

The first week has a real narrative job. They know more about each other and have accepted continuing, but familiar interaction may no longer feel effortless.

### Taiga's possible difficulty

- Leo knows vulnerable material Taiga normally keeps private.
- Taiga remembers hurting him and may worry about saying something wrong.
- Familiar affection now carries acknowledged romantic meaning.
- He may struggle to know how much initiative is welcome without wanting to reverse the direction they agreed on.

### Leo's possible difficulty

- He has been hurt and reassured, but may need time to regain spontaneity.
- He can try to give Taiga room without knowing how much room is useful.
- He may hesitate to assume that an old routine or teasing gesture is welcome.
- His accommodating instinct can make him more careful than he actually wants to be.

Neither needs to question the existence of the other's feelings for this awkwardness to work.

### What restores ease

Possible forms discussed include an ordinary disagreement, an involuntary laugh, an unplanned conversation, or a moment when their actual personalities break through the carefulness.

These are examples of function, not mandatory scenes.

The result is lived experience that they can still enjoy each other after everything they have said. Avoid making every interaction a discussion of Pinetree or an assessment of how well the repair is going.

## 11. Middle phase: company becomes an active choice

Classes, coffee routines, and soccer can place them together automatically. The next movement is Taiga choosing company that the schedule has not arranged for him.

Possible forms of progression:

- initiating contact rather than only responding;
- asking for time together;
- making space for Leo in an ordinary plan;
- rearranging a small opportunity when one of them is busy;
- including Leo in another part of his life and enjoying the result;
- allowing a preference for Leo's company to be visible.

These changes can be modest. They do not require a major romantic milestone or a crisis.

### Room 317 as a useful candidate

The existing Thai-food scene with Caleb and Maya was identified as material that may survive with a revised function.

Its useful action is a small invitation: there is food, Leo is there, and Taiga wants him to stay. Leo then gets along with Caleb and Maya and occupies Taiga's space without the evening becoming an emotional event.

Its value is inclusion and enjoyment. It should not replay discovering attraction or become a miniature version of the camp conflict.

The scene remains a candidate until the new chapter ladder is selected.

### Separate lives support the progression

Leo retains Enzo, other friends, projects, and obligations. Taiga retains coursework, soccer, Caleb, Nora, and his own plans. They will not always be available at the same time.

This creates ordinary opportunities to ask for company or choose another moment. Do not turn each scheduling difficulty into an abandonment test or use it to isolate either boy from the ensemble.

## 12. The invitation as an earned result

Taiga need not spend the month consciously preparing to invite Leo. The desire can become clear when holiday plans arise.

By then, he should have enough experience of choosing Leo's company that sharing camp becomes something he wants, despite the greater exposure involved.

The original call with Yoshinori can still provide a practical opening. It must not make the invitation feel arranged for them by the adults.

Taiga is inviting Leo specifically. The work, spare beds, and meal planning provide logistics; they should not erase the personal choice.

Readiness here is limited to this step. He may still be nervous about asking and unprepared for what Kieran's return will stir up.

Afterward, planning and anticipation can have their own value: shared practical decisions, jokes, expectations, and looking forward to something together. They do not need to create another crisis before departure.

The exact point when he asks, and how much preparation time remains, are open.

## 13. Covering the month without padding or premature escalation

Once the initial awkwardness is established, selective scenes and time jumps can show the pattern changing.

The month does not require:

- a separate psychological problem for every week;
- increasingly serious conversations in every scene;
- repeated confirmation that they like each other;
- escalating physical milestones to prove that something is happening;
- borrowing Kieran's conflict before he appears;
- bringing Act IV's major injury, helplessness, or performance test forward.

Affection can remain present. A gesture already established may become easier to accept or initiate. Its function is continuity and choice rather than another investigation of whether contact was intentional.

University life must continue to matter. Academic problems, soccer, other friendships, humor, and mundane enjoyment can carry scenes whose romantic consequence emerges indirectly.

The goal is an observable change from careful continuation to wanted inclusion, not an event quota.

### Zayne: recurring cameo and jealousy foreshadowing

**Agreed direction:** Retain Zayne across **at least two chapters** in the university section. His main romantic function is to provoke a small, comic reaction in Taiga when he teases or flirts with Leo. He does not need a separate jealousy subplot.

Zayne's academic role and ordinary campus presence can make the encounters feel natural. Keep his confidence, competence, and casual flirting. He is behaving like himself rather than deliberately manipulating the boys or testing their relationship.

An earlier appearance can establish familiarity and easy banter; a later appearance can carry the clearer reaction. This distribution is an option, not a fixed scene order. Two chapters are a minimum, not a maximum, and each appearance need not repeat the same jealousy beat.

#### The scale of Taiga's reaction

The preferred example discussed is Zayne teasing Leo in the library or another natural university setting. Taiga hurries them along because they have training, despite still having enough time.

Training is a real obligation. Taiga creates unnecessary urgency around it. Packing early, cutting an exchange short, or getting Leo moving slightly sooner can carry the reaction.

A clock or an already-established departure time can let the reader recognize the disproportion. Keep the beat brief. It needs no prolonged self-inquiry, confrontation, relationship-definition discussion, or explanation of the motive from Leo, Zayne, or the narration.

This is not another discovery that Taiga likes Leo. Avoid rebuilding the old extended sequence of competitive coffees, comparisons of attraction, or escalating territorial behavior around Zayne.

#### Cameo intent

The author intends the Leo/Zayne exchange as a nod to their pairing and interplay in the *Jock Studio* demo. Consult the relevant voicelines when writing their interaction. Readers who recognize the reference get an additional payoff; the exchange should also work as ordinary character comedy.

No specific voiceline or exact exchange has been selected here.

#### Foreshadowing Undertow's main arc

The cameo introduces jealousy through Taiga before the camp arc puts him on the receiving end of Leo's jealousy.

- With Zayne, Taiga briefly dislikes someone else occupying Leo's attention and uses a practical reason to redirect the situation.
- At camp, Taiga's involvement with Kieran can leave Leo feeling peripheral. That reaction intersects with history, expectations, and a concrete failure of consideration.
- A comfortable explanation may cover the uncomfortable feeling: training for Taiga; tiredness or competitiveness are possibilities for Leo, with exact behavior still to be developed.

The emotional pattern recurs while circumstances and consequences change. Zayne's teasing remains a harmless, comic encounter. Kieran carries the sustained history and relationship pressure.

The connection can remain with the reader. Taiga does not need to remember Zayne explicitly, diagnose the parallel, or explain the act's theme.

## 15. Camp Buddy: general purpose only

**The Camp Buddy chapter sequence remains to be laid out.**

Its general purpose is to bring Taiga's past and present into contact, allow him to make decisions about Kieran, and give the developing relationship with Leo a meaningful experience of difference from the old pattern.

Camp is already an emotional home and a place where Taiga has rebuilt trust. It should also remain a functioning community whose people have work, friendships, habits, and concerns beyond his relationship.

Useful existing foundations include:

- reopening and maintenance work;
- Taiga being capable and comfortable in familiar surroundings;
- Leo meeting people and finding his own place among them;
- shared work and ordinary proximity;
- Taiga enjoying bringing someone into this part of his life;
- friendships, food, jokes, free time, and periods of uncomplicated pleasure.

The current camp is financially stable. The visit's practical work is not another emergency rescue of Camp Buddy.

These are foundations and continuity principles, not a new daily itinerary.

## 16. Kieran's return: encounter with unresolved history

Kieran's presence should have significance independent of Leo's jealousy.

Possible elements already discussed:

- initial anger or discomfort;
- old familiarity and residual affection;
- recognizing that Kieran has changed;
- curiosity about what a present connection might look like;
- the temptation to make the original disaster turn out differently this time;
- a decision about the place Kieran may have in Taiga's present.

The exact emotional mixture and timing remain open.

Taiga can want to resolve the past without wanting Kieran romantically. Old ease does not automatically indicate romantic indecision.

Kieran should retain his own agency and reasons for returning. Remorse, reconnection with camp, and possible romantic hope are available from the existing draft, but their implementation remains to be assessed.

He should not exist solely as a device to provoke jealousy, become a villain to simplify the choice, or act as the interpreter who explains Leo to Taiga.

The old supermarket encounter and volunteer arrival are source material. They are not fixed as the new entry mechanism merely because they are already written.

## 17. The repeated relationship dynamic

The proposed connection to Taiga's first relationship is important:

Taiga becomes absorbed in something meaningful to him. The person beside him feels increasingly peripheral.

In the first camp term, that involvement was new friendship and belonging. In the proposed present arc, it may be resolving his history with Kieran.

Leo can sincerely support the reconciliation and still become hurt by how much room he is giving it. Taiga may assume that Leo is fine because he seems capable, sociable, and understanding.

For the new version, Leo needs some concrete basis for feeling neglected. Historical familiarity alone produces a different conflict.

Possible forms discussed include a shared plan being overlooked, a conversation running beyond what Taiga intended, or repeated attention to Kieran's comfort while Leo's is assumed. No incident has been selected.

Leo's jealousy can reach Taiga as something familiar and threatening. Taiga then has to distinguish the actual complaint from the old sequence he fears.

Neither boy must express the issue perfectly. Leo's accommodating tendency can delay recognition or admission of his own hurt. Taiga can hear an accusation more readily than a request for consideration.

## 18. Responsibility and the Kieran resolution

The general principles for the camp arc are:

- Taiga may acknowledge that he neglected Kieran.
- Kieran remains responsible for the framing and violence.
- Understanding a past grievance does not justify retaliation.
- Taiga does not owe a renewed romantic relationship as compensation.
- Forgiveness, reconciliation, friendship, and romantic reunion are distinct choices.
- Kieran may be disappointed by Taiga's answer.
- Taiga can care about that disappointment without making it his duty to remove it.
- Taiga's desire for redemption after harming Keitaro is different from an obligation to repair everything with Kieran.

The old draft has Taiga say that forgiveness happened earlier. Whether that remains the timing is open.

The intended closure is a present decision that no longer requires a perfect ending to the old relationship. It need not require forgetting, indifference, or absolute emotional comfort.

## 19. Conflict must have a limited job

The user expects a significant fight somewhere in the arc but does not want Act III built toward one enormous confrontation that resolves everything.

Avoid reproducing Act II's shape of mounting pressure, rupture, comprehensive disclosure, and a single emotional repair.

The fight can expose or address a specific problem between Taiga and Leo. It should not also be responsible for settling all of Taiga's self-understanding, Kieran's future, Leo's expectations, and the romance.

A strong working proposal is to place the fight early enough in the camp material that substantial life and follow-through remain afterward. This is not a locked placement.

Its intensity and content remain open. It does not need to exceed Pinetree to matter.

## 20. Distribute change across the arc

The broader progression should be carried by several choices and experiences rather than one explanatory conversation.

General structural principles discussed:

- Some romantic initiative happens before camp.
- Taiga and Kieran's relationship develops through its own encounters.
- Some understanding or boundary can precede the fight.
- The immediate argument has a specific present subject.
- Repair can happen in stages.
- Changed behavior and ordinary company continue after the argument.
- Kieran's resolution follows momentum already present in his own relationship with Taiga.
- A positive choice toward Leo arises from wanting him, rather than only compensating for a mistake.

These are principles for distributing causality, not an ordered camp scene ladder.

Taiga might acknowledge a failure of consideration before understanding every reason behind it. Leo might admit jealousy before he can express every expectation. They can regain some ease without producing a definitive account of themselves.

Kieran's boundary or closure should not feel like something Taiga does only because Leo becomes upset. Choosing Leo should not be reduced to the action required to settle their fight.

Ordinary camp activities should have their own value. Avoid turning every practical repair into an emotional metaphor or every social moment into a test.

## 21. What must change from the old camp progression

The assessed draft currently uses Kieran's old familiarity and Taiga's incomplete disclosure to accumulate Leo's jealousy. Taiga also explicitly reassures Leo that he will not be displaced.

That means the existing version cannot simply be relabeled as a story about actual neglect. Its mechanism needs to be reconsidered.

The original resolution concentrates several outcomes together: Kieran's romantic answer, his departure, Taiga recognizing Leo's jealousy, their repair, and mutual romantic acknowledgment.

Two changes are therefore necessary at the level of purpose:

1. Mutual acknowledgment cannot be the new discovery because Act II already established it.
2. The new arc should distribute its meaningful decisions rather than depend on one concentrated resolution.

Earlier disclosure of Kieran's identity was proposed as a way to explore difficulty even when Leo knows the truth. It remains an option, not a decision.

The ordinary-life and departure material in the old draft offers useful examples of consolidating change through enjoyment, small choices, and continuity. Exact physical milestones and exit logistics are not fixed by that observation.

## 22. Leo's own progression

Leo must remain a co-lead with needs, limits, and imperfect understanding.

The revised version does not establish the old development bible's ex-boyfriend backstory. Do not import that history to make him an expert at recognizing unhealthy dynamics.

Available movement:

- he tries to support Taiga and accommodate difficult circumstances;
- he notices his own discomfort later than he would like;
- he struggles to admit jealousy or distinguish support from suppressing a need;
- he eventually expresses hurt and asks for consideration;
- later development can help him ask more directly before resentment accumulates.

Jealousy does not entitle him to ownership or control. Equally, the lack of a formal label should not automatically invalidate a reasonable expectation they have actually created between them.

He must have relationships at camp that are not mediated entirely through Taiga. His rapport with Yoichi is useful existing material.

Taiga should also contribute pleasure, help, attention, and support to Leo's life. The relationship cannot become a sequence of Taiga's problems and Leo's perfect responses.

## 23. Romance and the act's ending

Act III needs a satisfying romantic consequence to its emotional movement.

By the end, Taiga should be doing something with his acknowledged feelings: initiating, making room, expressing a preference, agreeing on a direction, or another concrete choice that clarifies what he wants to pursue.

Starting to date is one possibility discussed. It is not a locked requirement. Neither is preserving the original arrangement of sexual intimacy before kissing and formal definition.

The intended ending can carry pleasure, initiative, and ordinary confidence. It need not be an emotional postmortem after a final fight.

If the act ends with departure or a return to Olympus, that transition can demonstrate that the change belongs to their lives beyond the camp setting. The exact ending event remains open.

The change should be visible in behavior that would have been harder at the beginning. Do not require a closing speech that explains the act's lesson.

## 25. Checks when building the actual chapter ladder

For each proposed chapter, establish:

1. What changes by the end?
2. Is that change new relative to the Final Act II ending?
3. Does it arise through behavior and events rather than explanation alone?
4. Is Taiga ready for the step at the point where it happens?
5. Does Leo retain his own perspective, wants, and life?
6. Does the chapter preserve existing progress while allowing difficulty?
7. Is ordinary university or camp life doing meaningful work?
8. Is this material appropriate here rather than borrowed from a later arc?
9. Does the chapter create or pay off its own movement instead of only feeding a future fight?
10. Which questions can remain open without making the chapter feel static?

Do not treat these as instructions to add ten beats. They are planning checks.
