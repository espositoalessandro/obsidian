# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Character Voices

This file defines **how recurring characters should sound in the final novel**.

The completed Draft is the primary evidence for who these characters became.

The existing development Bibles and source-game dialogue remain references, not automatic final targets.

---

# 1. Voice Model

For every recurring character, define four core dimensions.

## Sentence Shape
How do they build speech?

Consider:

- long vs short;
- polished vs messy;
- complete sentences vs fragments;
- filler;
- interruptions;
- corrections;
- repetition;
- certainty.

## Social Instinct
What do they instinctively do when another person speaks?

Examples:

- reassure;
- challenge;
- joke;
- clarify;
- redirect;
- fix;
- interrogate;
- withdraw;
- escalate;
- accommodate.

## Humor Mechanism
How does this person specifically produce comedy?

Avoid giving everyone the same sarcasm or sitcom timing.

## Failure Mode
What happens to their speech and behavior when they are:

- tired;
- embarrassed;
- angry;
- jealous;
- hurt;
- overwhelmed;
- exposed.

Characters often become most distinct when their normal social style stops working.

# 3. Leo Mendez

## Character Progression

### Core movement

**Automatic availability → chosen reliability.**

Leo begins from a genuine desire to be someone other people can depend on.

He is naturally:

- social;
- energetic;
- helpful;
- emotionally attentive;
- quick to include people;
- willing to show up;
- inclined to fix practical and emotional problems.

These are real strengths.

The flaw grows out of them.

Leo increasingly treats **being available** as evidence that he is reliable. If somebody needs something and he can theoretically help, his instinct is to say yes and work out the consequences afterward.

His late-novel growth is not learning to care less or learning the word "no" for the first time.

It is understanding that:

> **Being reliable does not mean being endlessly available.**

Sometimes being dependable requires choosing which commitments receive his time and accepting that another request will remain unanswered.

---

### Section I — Forty-One

**Social ease.**

Leo initially appears almost frictionless socially.

He is:

- energetic;
- curious;
- playful;
- inclusive;
- socially confident;
- comfortable initiating contact;
- good at remembering details;
- quick to involve people in conversation or activity.

His sociability is genuine.

Do not rewrite early Leo as secretly exhausted by every group situation in order to foreshadow Act V.

The imperfections are already present in smaller forms:

- imperfect planning;
- practical forgetfulness;
- code that works before it becomes elegant;
- enthusiasm outrunning organization;
- a tendency to throw himself into things.

At this stage, the costs are mostly comic or manageable.

---

### Section II — Past Mistakes

**Limits become visible.**

The private five-minute reset establishes that Leo does have limits.

He can genuinely enjoy:

- parties;
- crowds;
- teammates;
- conversation;
- meeting people;

and still occasionally need a few minutes alone.

This does **not** make him secretly introverted or socially performative.

The more important boundary appears at Pinetree.

When Taiga pushes Leo away and then calls him back, Leo refuses the push-pull dynamic.

His refusal matters because it proves that Leo already understands boundaries when another person is violating one:

> *I'm done following people who don't want me.*

This distinction should remain important later.

Act V is not about Leo discovering boundaries as a concept.

It is about applying them when **he is the person being asked for something**.

His earlier relationship history reinforces this boundary without needing to be explained during Pinetree. Leo has already learned that constantly chasing, fixing and reassuring another person can become destructive.

---

### Section III — Moving On

**Leo admits that he also wants to be chosen.**

Camp Buddy confirms that Leo's social confidence is real.

He can:

- enter Taiga's established world;
- form his own relationships;
- get along with Yoichi and the adults;
- function without Taiga acting as his social translator.

Kieran then exposes a less generous and more vulnerable side of Leo.

Leo is very good at:

- including people;
- giving attention;
- making room;
- making others feel wanted.

But he also wants:

- a special place;
- reassurance;
- exclusivity;
- evidence that his closeness to Taiga means something distinct.

His jealousy matters because it prevents him from becoming the endlessly accommodating social character.

He can become:

- insecure;
- territorial;
- unfair;
- sharp;
- embarrassed by his own reaction.

Eventually, he admits what is actually happening.

This is an important step toward asking for emotional needs rather than expressing them only through increased effort.

---

### Section IV — New Threshold

**Mutuality.**

Once Taiga and Leo's relationship becomes safer and more explicit, Leo is allowed to receive more.

Taiga:

- takes care of him;
- worries about his injury;
- supports him;
- initiates closeness;
- chooses him publicly;
- makes practical accommodations for him.

Leo is no longer always the socially competent person pulling Taiga forward.

The relationship becomes reciprocal.

The final rewrite should preserve some of Leo's existing imperfections here rather than smoothing him into an ideal boyfriend.

Light reminders may include:

- saying yes too quickly;
- overcommitting;
- trying to solve too much in football;
- imperfect planning;
- occasional depletion;
- needing Taiga's support;
- difficulty accepting that a problem cannot be fixed immediately.

Keep these light.

The reader should not already be able to diagnose the Act V problem.

---

### Section V — The Other Side

**The cost of usefulness becomes visible.**

Leo's home environment reveals the pattern at full scale.

His reflex is:

> someone needs something → Leo moves

He:

- helps;
- carries;
- explains;
- answers calls;
- joins errands;
- fixes things;
- volunteers;
- reassures;
- makes himself available.

When commitments collide, his first instinct is not to choose.

It is:

> **I can still fix this.**

If one plan is interrupted, he tries to move it.

If two promises conflict, he tries to preserve both.

If somebody is disappointed, he tries to make it up later.

The problem is not generosity itself.

The problem is that preserving every yes eventually makes him less reliable to the people and commitments he has already chosen.

### Social energy vs responsibility

The Las Terrenas trip is an important control case.

Away from family obligations, Leo can spend a day:

- meeting strangers;
- playing volleyball;
- dancing;
- talking;
- moving constantly;
- making new social connections;

and remain happy and energized.

Therefore:

**people are not the problem.**

The deeper exhaustion comes from carrying the belief that he must remain:

- useful;
- available;
- dependable;
- responsive;

to everyone at once.

### Self-perception

Leo is emotionally perceptive about other people much earlier than he is perceptive about himself.

He can often notice when somebody else is:

- uncomfortable;
- tired;
- withdrawing;
- upset;
- in need of reassurance.

He is slower to recognize the same limits in himself.

Before Act V, Leo already knows individual symptoms:

- he overbooks;
- he forgets commitments;
- he occasionally needs five minutes alone;
- he hates disappointing people;
- he tries to fix too much.

He has **not yet connected them into one coherent pattern**.

Act V is where those facts begin to form a single picture.

### Reliability

Leo's desire to be dependable should be partly conscious from early on.

He wants to be someone people can rely on.

Do **not** turn this into explicit psychological language such as:

> "My worth depends on being useful."

That would over-diagnose him.

The deeper mechanism should remain visible primarily through behavior.

### Final realization

The resolution is deliberately small.

Isa points out that:

- Ramón could have moved the table;
- Antonio's bicycle did not have to become Leo's responsibility;
- wanting to spend his own time on a game was itself a valid reason not to continue helping.

Later, Carmen asks Leo to come shopping.

Leo says no.

Nothing catastrophic happens.

Carmen accepts it.

Their relationship remains intact.

Later Leo still helps carry the groceries inside.

That is the endpoint:

**boundaries do not make Leo less helpful. They make helping a choice.**

---

### Stable traits

Leo's growth must not remove:

- sociability;
- enthusiasm;
- curiosity;
- generosity;
- playfulness;
- competitiveness;
- willingness to help;
- emotional attentiveness;
- love of crowds and activity;
- tendency to talk;
- desire to be involved.

The final version of Leo should still be recognizably the person who will:

- talk to strangers;
- volunteer;
- join the game;
- help a kid with a bicycle;
- get too excited about a musical;
- turn a simple explanation into a story;
- make friends quickly.

He simply becomes better at recognizing that:

- his plans matter;
- his rest matters;
- existing promises matter;
- disappointment cannot always be prevented;
- not every solvable problem is his problem to solve.

---

### Anger and failure mode

Leo should not default to explosive anger.

When genuinely:

- hurt;
- depleted;
- jealous;
- angry;
- overwhelmed;

his usual performance tends to **reduce**.

Expect:

- shorter sentences;
- fewer jokes;
- less verbal cushioning;
- more direct statements;
- quieter pride;
- sharper boundaries.

The absence of his normal enthusiasm can carry more weight than shouting.

---

### Foreshadowing rule

Sections III–IV may strengthen the already established pattern through small moments of:

- overbooking;
- saying yes too quickly;
- poor practical planning;
- trying to solve multiple things at once;
- social or football over-involvement;
- occasional depletion.

Keep the signals ordinary.

Do not turn them into repeated warnings that announce the final arc.

The pattern should become obvious **in retrospect**, not diagnostic on first appearance.

---

### Causal restraint

Do not invent one neat childhood cause for Leo's pattern.

His family environment may reinforce his habit of being available, but it should not be reduced to:

> his family relied on him, therefore he became this way.

Leo is naturally social, generous, energetic and competent.

The weakness grows organically out of those strengths, his experiences, and the rewards he receives for being the person who shows up.

# 7. Apollo Secondary Cast

The Apollo secondary cast does not need equal narrative weight.

Use three levels:

- **Full secondary profiles:** recurring enough that the reader should recognize them immediately in dialogue and group scenes.
- **Moderate profiles:** recurring characters with a clear social/football identity but no major arc.
- **Functional squad voices:** enough distinction to make the team feel populated without inventing subplots for everyone.

The goal is not maximal characterization.

The goal is to prevent the squad from collapsing into one shared register of dry competence and mild sarcasm.

---

## 7.1 Gav Brennan

### Status
**Full secondary profile.**

### Core distinction

**Gav stabilizes the group.**

He is not merely "the sensible one."

His strongest recurring instinct is to identify what the group actually needs and quietly move it back toward that point.

That may mean:

- restoring the tactical problem;
- stopping an argument from becoming useless;
- telling people to communicate;
- giving a teammate one actionable correction;
- letting a joke run until it actually becomes disruptive;
- taking responsibility without announcing himself as a leader.

This is why he reads as captain material.

### Sentence shape

Gav generally uses:

- complete sentences;
- short-to-medium length;
- little ornament;
- little filler;
- direct conclusions;
- explanation only when it helps someone act.

He is not as sparse as Scott.

He is not as literal or analytical as Caleb.

He is not as authoritative as Mercer.

Gav sounds like a teammate who has already understood the problem and wants everyone else to get on with solving it.

### Social instinct

**Stabilize first.**

When somebody speaks, Gav is likely to ask himself:

> What actually needs to happen here?

He may:

- clarify the practical issue;
- redirect nonsense;
- summarize where the group stands;
- tell someone what information matters;
- step between competing interpretations.

He should not constantly police fun.

The draft already shows that he can participate quietly in nonsense — for example, taking a photograph of Enzo asleep on the bus.

That matters.

Gav should feel capable of mischief without becoming performative.

### Humor mechanism

Gav's humor comes from:

- understated participation;
- refusing to exaggerate;
- calmly exposing the obvious flaw in somebody's argument;
- occasionally cooperating with a joke more than expected.

Avoid making every Gav joke:

> Enzo says something ridiculous → Gav says the sensible correction.

That becomes a template very quickly.

Sometimes he should:

- ignore the joke;
- quietly make it worse;
- take evidence;
- answer seriously for half a beat and then reveal he is participating.

### Football voice

On the pitch, Gav becomes more directive.

Use:

- names;
- positioning language;
- short tactical corrections;
- "talk";
- "hold";
- "again";
- specific descriptions of the next action.

His leadership should feel peer-based rather than coach-like.

He is inside the problem with everyone else.

### Failure mode

Under pressure, Gav becomes **more controlling and more directive**, not more theatrical.

Possible signs:

- starts solving for other people instead of only his own role;
- gives too many instructions;
- becomes impatient when teammates keep repeating the same mistake;
- assumes responsibility for restoring order even when the problem is not his alone.

Do not make him infallible simply because he is usually grounded.

His leadership should occasionally be incomplete or wrong.

### Emotional register

Gav is unlikely to conduct an emotional investigation.

Concern tends to appear as:

- staying nearby;
- asking one direct question;
- changing the practical situation;
- telling Mercer when a team problem has become serious;
- making space for someone rather than forcing disclosure.

### Independent texture

Retain and develop lightly:

- biology coursework;
- interest in sports medicine;
- independent social life;
- opinions that have nothing to do with keeping the team functional.

He should not exist only when somebody needs a sensible teammate.

### Distinguish from

**Scott:** Scott economizes speech; Gav coordinates people.

**Caleb:** Caleb clarifies facts; Gav clarifies action.

**Mercer:** Mercer directs from authority; Gav stabilizes from inside the group.

**Evan:** Evan gives experienced-player advice to individuals; Gav thinks more naturally about the group shape.

## 7.2 Aaron Bennett

### Status
**Full secondary profile.**

### Core distinction

**Aaron performs confidence more easily than he feels it.**

Normally he is:

- friendly;
- social;
- casual;
- playful;
- flirtatious;
- slightly cocky;
- comfortable inserting himself into conversation.

His insecurity becomes visible when he feels **evaluated**.

Pinetree does not reveal a completely different Aaron.

It reveals what his ordinary confidence is partially protecting.

### Sentence shape

Normal Aaron uses:

- casual short-to-medium sentences;
- contractions;
- slang;
- quick reactions;
- easy teasing;
- relatively little elaboration.

He does not build elaborate comic bits like Enzo.

He does not ramble through ideas like Leo.

He tends to react to the social moment that is directly in front of him.

When serious, however, he is capable of complete and surprisingly direct explanation.

His apology to Taiga is a useful model.

### Social instinct

**Engage and keep the interaction moving.**

Aaron tends to:

- joke;
- tease;
- flirt;
- comment;
- ask what someone is doing;
- make himself part of the conversation.

He likes social ease.

He also pays more attention than Gav or Scott to:

- whether he looks competent;
- whether somebody approves of him;
- whether he is being embarrassed;
- where he sits socially inside the group.

This does not make him shallow.

It makes evaluation a specific pressure point.

### Humor mechanism

Aaron's humor is:

- opportunistic;
- social;
- teasing;
- flirtatious;
- occasionally self-deprecating when he feels secure.

He uses whatever is currently happening.

He does not usually invent an entire comic reality around it the way Enzo does.

He can also miss a joke for a second, as in the early locker-room exchange with Taiga, which keeps him from feeling too slick.

### Confidence and Sofia

Aaron's flirting with Sofia is useful because it exposes the gap between his normal confidence and situations where he genuinely cares about the answer.

He may be perfectly comfortable flirting until Sofia turns the attention directly back onto him.

Do not make every Aaron scene about pursuing Sofia.

Their relationship should exist alongside:

- football;
- friendships;
- team travel;
- his own insecurity;
- ordinary social life.

### Football voice

Aaron is more reactive than Gav.

On the pitch he should sound like a fullback communicating with the nearest players:

- short calls;
- immediate options;
- warnings;
- overlap/hold decisions;
- frustration when the picture changes faster than he expected.

He is competent.

His Pinetree difficulty comes from **feeling tested on every possession**, not from secretly being a bad player.

### Failure mode

When Aaron feels publicly evaluated or exposed:

- humor becomes brittle;
- explanations become defensive;
- he may say "I know" before he has actually absorbed the correction;
- he overthinks;
- he starts treating each action as evidence of whether he belongs;
- he may redirect humiliation onto someone else.

At his worst, as at Pinetree, he can weaponize social information because he knows it will hurt.

That capacity should not be erased during the rewrite.

### Repair mode

Aaron's apology after Pinetree is equally important.

Once he decides to own what he did, he can be:

- direct;
- specific;
- uncomfortable without fleeing;
- willing to admit motive;
- willing to accept that forgiveness may not come immediately.

He does not need to make the apology charming.

That is one of his strongest differentiators.

The confident social character is capable of standing still inside consequences.

### Distinguish from

**Leo:** both are social, but Leo is more curious, inclusive, verbally expansive and emotionally perceptive. Aaron is quicker, more conventional and more sensitive to evaluation.

**Enzo:** Aaron banters; Enzo performs.

**Gav:** Gav stabilizes the room; Aaron wants to participate in it.

**Taiga:** both can become defensive under evaluation, but Aaron is far more socially motivated and much more concerned with public competence.

## 7.3 Sofia Navarro

### Status
**Moderate secondary profile.**

Sofia belongs to the wider Olympus athletic circle rather than the men's Apollo squad, but she recurs often enough around the team to require a clear voice.

### Core distinction

**Sofia punctures performance quickly and then moves on.**

She is confident enough that she rarely needs to prove she has won an exchange.

Examples already supported by the draft:

- preemptively shutting down Aaron's invitation before he makes it;
- telling Taiga to maintain his standards;
- allowing Enzo's seating schemes to remain Enzo's problem.

### Sentence shape

Use:

- compact statements;
- quick answers;
- practical vocabulary;
- little rhetorical decoration.

She should not debate for pleasure like Nora.

She should not riff chaotically like Mia.

### Social instinct

Sofia tends to:

- establish the boundary;
- identify the practical reality;
- call out something embarrassing;
- move the conversation forward.

Her confidence should come from not needing to linger after the punchline.

### Humor mechanism

Quick callout.

Preemptive shutdown.

Body/athletic reality defeating somebody's social performance.

Then she moves on.

### Athletic/academic texture

Her physiotherapy and football background should affect what she notices:

- soreness;
- movement;
- training load;
- bad recovery habits;
- injury risk;
- what somebody is physically doing rather than what they claim they are doing.

Do not turn her into the squad's free medical encyclopedia.

### Failure mode

Currently underdefined in the Draft.

Do not invent a major one before the rewrite needs it.

If she receives more page time, derive it from actual scenes rather than assigning her a generic hidden insecurity.

### Independence rule

Sofia must have moments that are not about Aaron.

Use:

- women's-team frustrations;
- physiotherapy coursework;
- friendships;
- training opinions;
- her own plans.

## 7.4 Evan Cole

### Status
**Moderate secondary profile.**

### Core distinction

**Experienced teammate who is comfortable telling Taiga something he may not want to hear.**

Evan's function is peer experience, not leadership of the whole group.

He can translate:

- team culture;
- positional habits;
- what Mercer means;
- what Taiga is doing better or worse.

### Sentence shape

Use:

- direct complete sentences;
- concrete football language;
- little filler;
- enough explanation to be useful;
- no lecture unless Taiga genuinely needs one.

### Social instinct

Evan tends to:

- offer the correction;
- needle someone who is taking themselves too seriously;
- treat improvement as ordinary rather than miraculous.

He is not intimidated by Taiga's temper.

### Humor mechanism

Experienced-player teasing.

He is more willing than Gav to poke directly at one person.

He does not need Enzo's performance or Scott's extreme economy.

### Example register

The existing line:

> "Quit looking like you're awaiting sentencing."

is close to the right function.

Evan notices Taiga catastrophizing and reduces it to something manageable without turning the exchange into therapy.

### Distinguish from

**Gav:** Gav coordinates the group. Evan advises the individual.

**Theo:** Theo is calmer and more contextual; Evan is more willing to needle.

**Mercer:** Evan can explain how something feels as a player. Mercer defines what the job requires.

## 7.11 Squad Density Rule

Not every named player needs an arc.

The final rewrite should instead make Apollo feel like a group where:

- the same people recur;
- voices have recognizable shapes;
- positional relationships repeat;
- players have conversations not involving Taiga;
- somebody may enter a scene, contribute two characteristic lines, and leave.

Repeated light presence is more valuable than giving every squad member one artificial spotlight chapter.
