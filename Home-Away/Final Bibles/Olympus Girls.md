# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Olympus Girls Bible

This file defines the **final-rewrite characterization and voice targets** for the recurring women in Taiga's Olympus social and academic circle.

It is intentionally separate from the development-era `Bibles/Characters.md`.

The completed Draft remains evidence for continuity and scenes that already work, but this file is authoritative when the final rewrite deliberately sharpens characterization.

---

# 1. Group Principle

These characters should not collapse into the same register of:

- smart;
- dry;
- competent;
- mildly sarcastic;
- socially perceptive.

Their intelligence should operate differently.

The core contrast is:

| Character | Default instinct |
| --- | --- |
| **Nora** | Challenge the argument until it actually makes sense |
| **Mia** | Find the workable solution and keep moving |
| **Sofia** | Cut through the performance and state the practical reality |
| **Rachel** | Verify before trusting the conclusion |

They do not need equal narrative weight.

Nora and Mia deserve stronger recurring identities.

Sofia is already sufficiently distinct and mainly needs consistency.

Rachel has little screen time and should remain lightly defined.

---

# 2. Nora

## Core role

**The Hermione-type intellectual.**

This means the archetype, not copied dialogue or behavior.

Nora is:

- prepared;
- academically serious;
- argumentative;
- principled;
- intellectually assertive;
- confident in her reasoning;
- occasionally bossy when she thinks the correct answer is obvious;
- genuinely interested in argument for its own sake.

Taiga argues because something bothers him.

Nora can argue because the argument itself is interesting.

---

## Academic instinct

Nora likes:

- having read the material;
- knowing what the professor actually assigned;
- defining terms correctly;
- following premises through;
- catching sloppy reasoning;
- asking what evidence supports a claim;
- continuing an argument after everyone else thinks it is over.

She may arrive with:

- the reading;
- the exact passage;
- an article;
- a screenshot;
- notes;
- the professor's wording.

Not because she is a walking encyclopedia.

Because she prepared.

---

## Social instinct

**Resolve the premise.**

When somebody says something she thinks is wrong or incomplete, Nora's instinct is to challenge it.

She may:

- ask why;
- identify the hidden assumption;
- offer the opposite case;
- distinguish two concepts somebody collapsed together;
- keep going because the issue is not actually resolved yet.

This can make her stimulating company for Taiga.

It can also make her exhausting.

---

## Weakness

Nora can become too convinced that if she intellectually understands the structure of a problem, she understands the situation itself.

She may:

- push a discussion farther than someone wanted;
- keep arguing after the emotional usefulness has ended;
- become bossy when she thinks the answer is obvious;
- treat resistance as another argument to solve;
- fail to notice that winning the point is no longer helping.

Do not make her cruel.

The flaw comes from overconfidence in reasoning.

---

## Sentence shape

Use:

- complete sentences;
- clear argument structure;
- distinctions;
- rhetorical questions;
- explicit premises;
- occasional lists or multi-part explanations when activated.

She should sound more formal than Mia or Sofia, but still like a student rather than a textbook.

---

## Humor mechanism

Nora's humor comes from:

- taking the argument one step farther;
- turning Taiga's own logic back on him;
- treating something absurd with academic seriousness;
- bringing receipts;
- refusing to let a bad premise survive because everyone else wants to move on.

Example energy:

> "That's not what I said."

followed by a detailed explanation of what she actually said while everyone else regrets creating the opportunity.

---

## Relationship with Taiga

Nora should be one of the few people who can reliably make Taiga enjoy being challenged.

Their arguments should sometimes feel competitive in a way that is actually pleasurable for both of them.

She should also be capable of being wrong.

Taiga conceding to Nora matters only if Nora must sometimes concede too.

---

## Stable traits

Keep:

- intelligence;
- confidence;
- political interest;
- enjoyment of debate;
- academic seriousness;
- ability to annoy Taiga productively.

Avoid:

- turning her into the group's therapist;
- making every line a correction;
- making her infallible because she prepared.

---

# 3. Mia

## Core role

**The chaotic weeb CS girl who is somehow still functional.**

Mia's chaos should have a specific identity.

She is not merely "messy."

She is:

- technically competent;
- pragmatic;
- fandom-brained;
- externally disorganized;
- fast-moving;
- comfortable with "good enough";
- capable of producing working results from circumstances that look disastrous.

---

## Weeb identity

Mia is genuinely into:

- anime;
- manga;
- games;
- fandom culture;
- seasonal shows;
- conventions;
- merch;
- figures;
- occasional gacha/mobile-game nonsense.

This should be texture, not every line.

Do not make her speak entirely in references.

The reader should know the interest exists even in scenes where no reference appears.

Possible recurring details:

- anime/game stickers on her laptop;
- convention plans;
- strong opinions about adaptations;
- manga volumes in her room;
- absurdly specific recommendations;
- occasional fandom references during technical conversations;
- mild financial irresponsibility around merch or gacha if useful.

Use sparingly.

---

## CS instinct

**Make it work and move on.**

Mia is comfortable with pragmatic solutions.

She may:

- accept duplicated logic if the deadline is tomorrow;
- tolerate ugly temporary fixes;
- start before the plan is perfect;
- procrastinate because last-minute success keeps reinforcing the habit;
- know exactly where something is inside apparent digital chaos.

Her environment may look catastrophic while still making sense to her.

---

## Sentence shape

Use:

- casual phrasing;
- quick reactions;
- internet-influenced language;
- abrupt topic shifts;
- weird but comprehensible analogies;
- short bursts when working;
- longer excited explanations when fandom activates.

She should not sound like Leo.

Leo rambles socially.

Mia ricochets.

---

## Humor mechanism

Mia's humor can come from:

- treating bugs as hostile entities;
- applying absurdly dramatic language to mundane technical failures;
- anime/game analogies;
- naming disasters;
- acknowledging terrible code without shame;
- moving on before anybody has finished reacting.

Example energy:

> "Why is this function called `finalFinal2`?"

> "Because `finalFinal` was wrong."

> "And `final`?"

> "We don't talk about `final`."

---

## Weakness

Her main weakness is not incompetence.

It is that **chaos often works well enough to reward itself**.

She may:

- procrastinate;
- underestimate cleanup;
- trust future-Mia too much;
- leave temporary solutions in place;
- misjudge how long something will take;
- become overconfident because she usually pulls things off.

This should occasionally cost her.

---

## Social instinct

Mia is less interested than Nora in fully resolving a discussion.

Her instinct is often:

> Is there a workable solution?

If yes, she may be ready to move on.

She can notice emotional tension and deliberately choose not to interrogate it.

That is a useful distinction from more socially analytical characters.

---

## Distinguish from others

**Nora:** Nora wants the reasoning resolved. Mia wants the problem functioning.

**Leo:** both can be pragmatic and energetic, but Leo is much more socially expansive and people-focused.

**Taiga:** Taiga tends toward structure and abstraction. Mia is far more willing to tolerate ugly but functional solutions.

**Rachel:** Rachel verifies. Mia improvises.

---

## Stable traits

Keep Mia:

- competent;
- chaotic;
- casually funny;
- fandom-heavy without becoming a parody;
- socially relaxed;
- technically pragmatic.

The joke must never become:

> weeb girl = incompetent chaos.

She is good at what she does.

Her system is simply terrifying to other people.

---

# 4. Sofia Navarro

## Core role

**Practical, athletic, confident, and fast at puncturing bullshit.**

Sofia is already distinct enough in the Draft.

The final rewrite should sharpen rather than redesign her.

---

## Social instinct

**State the practical reality and move on.**

Sofia tends to:

- establish a boundary;
- call out the obvious;
- puncture somebody's performance;
- identify what matters physically or practically;
- refuse to spend unnecessary time proving she won the exchange.

Her confidence comes partly from not needing the last word.

---

## Sentence shape

Use:

- compact statements;
- quick responses;
- practical vocabulary;
- little rhetorical decoration.

She should not debate like Nora.

She should not riff chaotically like Mia.

---

## Athletic / physiotherapy lens

Her background should naturally affect what she notices:

- movement;
- fatigue;
- soreness;
- recovery;
- bad training habits;
- injury risk;
- what a body is actually doing.

Do not turn her into a free medical encyclopedia.

---

## Humor mechanism

Sofia's humor is usually:

- quick;
- physical;
- practical;
- preemptive.

She can shut down Aaron before he finishes the setup and then continue with her day.

---

## Independence

Sofia must exist outside Aaron.

Use lightly:

- women's-team issues;
- physiotherapy coursework;
- her own training;
- friendships;
- plans that have nothing to do with the men's team.

Do not invent a major hidden insecurity unless the rewrite later creates a genuine need for one.

---

# 5. Rachel

## Core role

**The verifier.**

Rachel has little screen time.

That is not a problem.

She does not need a large personality architecture.

She needs one consistent axis strong enough that readers recognize her when she appears.

---

## Academic instinct

Rachel checks before she trusts.

Her natural response to:

> "It works."

is:

> "On what input?"

She is:

- methodical;
- testing-oriented;
- patient with debugging;
- quietly stubborn about edge cases;
- less interested in cleverness than correctness.

---

## Sentence shape

Use:

- clear;
- calm;
- fairly concise;
- question-driven when debugging;
- little unnecessary performance.

She should not become another Caleb.

Caleb's precision is a broad personality trait.

Rachel's precision is primarily **methodological**.

---

## Humor mechanism

Rachel does not need a strong comic gimmick.

Any humor can come from:

- quietly finding the edge case everyone else ignored;
- asking the one question that destroys a premature celebration;
- being unsurprised when untested code fails.

Do not force more personality onto her than her screen time supports.

---

## Distinguish from CS cast

**Taiga:** architecture and structure.

**Leo:** pragmatic implementation and social energy.

**Mia:** chaotic pragmatism.

**Rachel:** verification and testing.

That is enough.

---

# 6. Rewrite Audit

When revising scenes involving this group, check:

- Does Nora challenge ideas rather than merely deliver generic sarcasm?
- Is Nora occasionally too pushy because she thinks the reasoning is obvious?
- Does Mia's chaos have a recognizable fandom/CS flavor?
- Is Mia still visibly competent?
- Are anime/manga/game references occasional texture rather than every line?
- Does Sofia cut through performance without becoming emotionally flat?
- Does Sofia exist outside Aaron?
- Is Rachel recognizable through method rather than through an unnecessary gimmick?
- Could Nora and Mia exchange dialogue without the lines obviously belonging to the wrong person?
