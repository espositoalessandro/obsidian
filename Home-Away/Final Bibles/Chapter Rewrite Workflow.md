# Home/Away — Chapter Rewrite Workflow

> **Status: LOCKED WORKFLOW BIBLE**
>
> This file defines the default working process for rewriting a chapter of *Home/Away*.
>
> It governs **how the rewrite is performed**, not prose style, continuity facts, characterization or formatting. Those remain governed by their respective Bibles.

---

# 1. Core Principle

A chapter rewrite is not an isolated line-editing task.

Before changing a chapter, reconstruct the state of the novel around it:

> **whole-story context → current canon → chapter job → drafting → review → approved Final edits → closing audit → freeze**

Do not begin rewriting from the target Draft chapter alone.

The goal is to preserve accumulated character development, continuity, setup and payoff across the novel rather than producing a locally stronger chapter that damages the larger arc.

---

# 2. Required Reading Before Starting a Chapter

Before drafting substantial new material, inspect enough of the repository to understand the chapter in the context of the completed novel.

## 2.1 Existing Final manuscript

Read the existing Final manuscript, with particular attention to:

- the finalized chapters immediately before the target chapter;
- the current section / act progression;
- character and relationship state at the exact point where the chapter begins;
- promises, running jokes, objects, assignments, injuries, plans and unresolved conversations that carry into the chapter.

Do not reset characters to the state they had in the completed Draft if the Final rewrite has already changed their progression.

When starting a new act or section, review the completed Final material broadly enough to understand the whole rewritten trajectory that precedes it.

## 2.2 Completed Draft

Read the completed Draft as global story evidence, not merely the target chapter.

At minimum, inspect:

- the original target chapter or equivalent material;
- scenes before and after it that determine chronology;
- later scenes that depend on setup from this chapter;
- later reveals that must **not** be moved forward accidentally;
- callbacks, promises, running jokes or objects introduced here and used later.

For major restructuring, inspect the wider act / section and, when necessary, the entire completed Draft arc.

The completed Draft tells us what the story became. It is not automatically the wording or structure we must preserve.

## 2.3 Bible files

Before rewriting, read the relevant files from both:

- `Final Bibles/`;
- the older development `Bibles/` folder.

Final Bibles are authoritative when they conflict with older development material.

Older Bibles remain useful for:

- continuity details;
- abandoned alternatives that explain existing material;
- source notes;
- historical decisions not yet migrated into a Final Bible.

Do not mechanically force a chapter to satisfy an old planning note when the story now works better organically.

## 2.4 Source material when relevant

If a chapter materially uses Camp Buddy / Scoutmaster Season characters, lore or dialogue voice, consult the relevant source-script material when useful.

Examples:

- `CampBuddyScript.csv`;
- `Bibles/ScoutmasterSeasonScript.csv`.

Use source dialogue to recover character-specific rhythm and behavior, not to imitate visual-novel dialogue mechanically.

---

# 3. Establish the Chapter Before Writing It

Before substantial drafting, identify:

- the chapter's **dramatic job**;
- what is different at the end compared with the beginning;
- the chapter's place in the current act / section;
- the relationship progression it is allowed to make;
- any reveal that must remain deferred;
- any setup later chapters depend on;
- the rough scene ladder.

Think organically.

The chapter map and Bibles are continuity tools, not requirements to insert beats merely because a planning file once listed them.

If the existing structure already works better than the planned structure, preserve the better story and update planning material later if appropriate.

---

# 4. Write-Permission Rule

## Default rule

Unless Alessandro explicitly authorizes otherwise:

> **`Home-Away/Draft.md` is the only manuscript file that may be written to.**

Reading Final chapters is always allowed.

Editing them is not.

This applies even when a proposed correction is obvious.

Examples that **do not** authorize Final-manuscript edits:

- "check this";
- "what do you think?";
- "review the scene";
- "fix this in the draft";
- "let's rewrite this";
- "how would you phrase it?";
- discussing a possible change.

Examples that **do** authorize the specific Final edit being discussed:

- "okay, fix the English";
- "apply those fixes";
- "change that line yourself";
- "put this in the final chapter";
- "fix the formatting";
- another equally explicit instruction to modify the Final manuscript.

Approval applies to the requested changes, not as permanent permission to edit Final files freely afterward.

---

# 5. Draft.md Is the Staging Area

Use `Home-Away/Draft.md` for:

- new scene drafts;
- rewritten passages;
- alternative structures being tested;
- bridge scenes;
- dialogue experiments;
- material that has not yet been approved for the Final manuscript.

Do not copy a Draft.md passage into Final merely because the user likes the general direction.

Wait until the user explicitly approves moving or applying it.

When the staging material becomes obsolete, it may be replaced or cleared only when that is part of the current instruction.

---

# 6. Discussion Before Editing Final

During development, separate these actions clearly:

1. **diagnose** the issue;
2. **propose** the solution;
3. **discuss / revise** it;
4. **apply** it only after explicit approval.

If Alessandro asks for a review, report the issue first rather than silently editing the Final chapter.

If he then says to fix it, apply the agreed change directly.

Do not turn a review request into an unrequested rewrite.

---

# 7. Preserve Global Continuity

Every rewrite should check both directions.

## Backward continuity

Confirm the chapter inherits correctly from finalized earlier chapters:

- dates and weekdays;
- class / football schedules;
- roster status;
- friendships and romantic state;
- who knows what;
- who has met whom;
- locations and travel;
- injuries;
- assignments;
- promises;
- ongoing jokes and conversations.

## Forward continuity

Check later Draft material before deleting, moving or strengthening a beat.

Ask:

- Does a later scene rely on this setup?
- Would this reveal become redundant if moved here?
- Does a later chapter assume the relationship is less advanced?
- Is an object, promise or running joke paid off later?
- Does changing this character interaction alter a later conflict?

Prefer solving continuity **forward** rather than repeatedly reopening frozen chapters.

---

# 8. Character and Romance Progression

Do not evaluate a chapter's characterization in isolation.

For Taiga, Leo and recurring characters, ask what the Final manuscript has already earned.

In particular:

- preserve Taiga's accumulated social and emotional development;
- do not reset his defenses merely to recreate Draft-era tension;
- distinguish sexual attraction, romantic awareness, friendship, jealousy and acknowledged love according to the current stage;
- do not make Leo more emotionally diagnostic or therapist-like than his established voice;
- let relationship progression happen through behavior whenever possible;
- do not insert romantic beats merely because a chapter otherwise has little romance.

A chapter may primarily serve friendship, football, Camp Buddy, academics, family or another subplot.

Romance should remain present only to the degree appropriate for that point in the arc.

---

# 9. Ensemble and Source Voices

When a scene contains a large ensemble, avoid giving everyone the same humor mechanism.

Check:

- sentence shape;
- social instinct;
- humor mechanism;
- failure mode;
- source-game behavior where relevant.

Do not manufacture equal screentime mechanically.

Give characters the amount of space the scene naturally requires while making sure recurring characters do not disappear accidentally across the broader arc.

---

# 10. Rewrite Rather Than Preserve Draft Wording

The Draft is story evidence, not sacred prose.

Freely change:

- sentence construction;
- paragraphing;
- dialogue wording;
- staging;
- exposition placement;
- scene entrances / exits;
- comedic timing;
- POV filtering;
- compression / expansion.

Preserve:

- story facts;
- character progression;
- required setup and payoff;
- intentional continuity;

unless we have deliberately agreed to change them.

Do not keep a weak scene merely because it already exists.

Do not remove a scene merely because the Final version is becoming long.

Scene function matters more than inherited word count.

---

# 11. Avoid Over-Engineering

During drafting and revision, watch especially for:

- every exchange becoming setup → punchline → tag;
- every paragraph ending with a neat conclusion;
- excessive "Not X. Y." constructions;
- artificial reversals;
- "so X that Y happened" used repeatedly;
- generic LLM-like aphorisms;
- explaining a joke after it lands;
- explaining an emotion already visible in behavior;
- dialogue conveniently stating the chapter theme.

Do not remove good prose merely because it is polished.

The target is lived-in, character-specific writing rather than deliberate roughness.

---

# 12. English and Formatting Passes

Content decisions come before sentence polishing.

Do not spend time perfecting language in a scene likely to be cut or moved.

Once the structure is stable:

1. perform a natural-English pass;
2. check Taiga voice before "correcting" deliberate fragments;
3. apply the locked rules in `Formatting & Dialogue.md`;
4. verify the file mechanically when useful.

Formatting corrections to a Final chapter still require explicit approval under the write-permission rule.

---

# 13. Chapter-Wrapping Workflow

When the chapter is structurally complete, use:

`Final Bibles/Chapter Closing Checklist.md`

Run all **30 points in three batches**:

- **1–10**
- **11–20**
- **21–30**

Do not collapse the checklist into a vague "looks good" review.

For each batch:

- reread the current Final chapter;
- consult the relevant Bibles;
- identify genuine must-fix issues;
- distinguish them from taste / optional alternatives;
- report the findings before making unapproved Final edits.

If fixes are approved, apply them and continue.

After the final batch, perform a fresh verification pass if any changes were made.

---

# 14. Must-Fix vs Taste

Before reopening a passage, classify the problem.

## Must fix

- continuity error;
- factual contradiction;
- POV inconsistency;
- character inconsistency;
- unclear staging;
- broken emotional causality;
- grammar / natural-English error;
- formatting violation;
- accidental repetition that genuinely damages the reading experience.

## Usually taste

- another possible joke;
- an equally valid line;
- a prettier synonym;
- a rhythm preference;
- a title alternative that is not clearly better;
- a sentence that could theoretically be polished forever.

Fix the first category.

Be suspicious of continuing to polish the second.

---

# 15. Final Verification and Freeze

After approved closing fixes:

- reread the changed passages in context;
- verify Markdown and dialogue formatting;
- verify dates / continuity touched by the edits;
- make sure a fix did not create a new problem nearby.

Then ask the actual freeze question:

> **Would we be comfortable publishing this chapter and forcing later chapters to live with its choices?**

If yes, freeze it.

A frozen chapter should only be reopened for:

- typo;
- formatting issue;
- genuine continuity error;
- a later change that absolutely requires backward repair.

Do not repeatedly reread frozen chapters looking for sentences that could be marginally prettier.

---

# 16. Bible Updates

Do not update Bibles automatically after every chapter.

A Bible update is warranted when:

- a genuinely global rule has emerged;
- continuity important to later chapters has materially changed;
- an act / section architecture has changed;
- a recurring character rule has changed;
- an existing Bible is now factually wrong in a way that will mislead future work.

Do **not** add every local logistical detail.

Examples of things that usually do not belong in a Final Bible:

- one character returned to campus on January 4;
- one game used a particular prompt;
- one scene happened at a slightly different hour.

And under the write-permission rule, Bible edits also require explicit instruction or approval.

---

# 17. Revision Ledger

Use `Final Bibles/Revision Ledger.md` for genuine novel-wide issues that should be remembered but are not necessarily rules.

Do not use it as a dumping ground for every minor chapter note.

---

# 18. Working With Uncertainty

When a small ambiguity can be resolved safely from existing canon, use the repository and make the best continuity-preserving choice.

Do not interrupt the workflow with unnecessary clarification questions.

When two choices would materially change:

- characterization;
- romance progression;
- canon;
- chapter architecture;

present the tradeoff and let Alessandro choose before committing it to Final.

---

# 19. Default Workflow Summary

For a normal chapter rewrite:

1. Read the existing Final manuscript context.
2. Read the relevant completed Draft material, including later dependencies.
3. Read relevant Final Bibles.
4. Read relevant development Bibles.
5. Check source scripts when source-character voice / canon matters.
6. Define chapter job and progression.
7. Build or revise the scene ladder.
8. Draft new material in `Home-Away/Draft.md` only.
9. Review and iterate there.
10. Apply anything to Final only after explicit approval.
11. Once the Final chapter is structurally complete, run the 30-point closing checklist in batches of 10.
12. Apply approved must-fixes.
13. Verify formatting / continuity.
14. Freeze the chapter.
15. Update Bibles or Revision Ledger only when genuinely necessary and explicitly approved.
16. Move on.

---

# 20. Final Rule

The workflow exists to prevent two opposite problems:

- rewriting locally without understanding the novel;
- endlessly polishing a chapter that is already finished.

Always preserve the larger story first.

And when the chapter passes the closing audit:

> **stop editing it and continue the novel.**
