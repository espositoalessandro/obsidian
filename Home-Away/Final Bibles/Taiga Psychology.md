# Home/Away — Taiga Psychology Bible

**Discussion consolidated:** 8 November 2026.

**Status:** Agreed characterization model and development reference following the Final Act II ending. Act III–IV applications remain planning directions until implemented in the manuscript.

This bible records the psychological model discussed while replanning Act III: Taiga's fears, interpretations, protective behavior, existing progress, and the difference between resolving his past and participating in a present relationship.

## 1. Authority and use

- The Final manuscript remains the source of truth. This bible explains and guides characterization; it does not override coherent prose or require a scene merely to demonstrate a principle.
- Distinguish **established evidence**, **agreed interpretation**, and **possible application**. A plausible reaction or suggested scene is not an event that has already happened.
- The revised Act II ending establishes mutual romantic acknowledgment. Older development material that postpones this to the Camp Buddy pier conversation is superseded on that point.
- References to Draft Chapters 22–38 use the existing draft numbering, not a finalized rewrite ladder.
- The model is an author-facing formulation, not a clinical diagnosis. PTSD, a dissociative condition, or an attachment-style classification has not been assigned to Taiga.
- Do not organize the romance around a generic anxious-partner/avoidant-partner template. Fear of losing independence is not the agreed explanation for Taiga's resistance.
- Psychological explanation must remain beneath the prose. Taiga and Leo should not acquire the language or insight of this document simply because the author understands the mechanism.

## 2. Taiga's baseline must remain visible

Taiga is competitive, profane, practical, observant, and capable of humor, pleasure, competence, loyalty, affection, and initiative. He often expresses care through attention and action. Vulnerability can disrupt his fluency.

His history does not explain every preference, insult, disagreement, or quiet moment. He can be annoyed because somebody is annoying. He can want privacy without being in crisis. He can enjoy someone's company without monitoring himself throughout it.

The objective is to understand why particular situations become emotionally charged while preserving the person who exists between those situations.

## 3. The experiences beneath the current model

### Kieran and the first Camp Buddy term

Established history, as disclosed in Final Chapter 20:

- Kieran was Taiga's boyfriend and an important person in his life before their first camp term together.
- Taiga enjoyed finding friends and belonging at camp. He increasingly prioritized that new social life over time with Kieran.
- Kieran felt neglected and became jealous. They argued and blamed each other rather than addressing the problem effectively.
- Kieran arranged for Taiga to be framed during SportsFest. The resulting crisis damaged Taiga's reputation and relationships at camp.
- People believed the accusation. Taiga experienced rejection, avoidance, bullying, and the expectation that he would not be believed.
- A later confrontation with Kieran escalated physically. Kieran punched him, causing the scar across his nose.
- Taiga ended the relationship. Kieran left; Taiga stayed.
- Taiga did not publicly expose Kieran and did not want him subjected to the same social punishment.

Taiga's neglect was a real relationship failure. Kieran's framing and violence were Kieran's choices. Preserve both facts without turning the betrayal into something Taiga caused or deserved.

### Keitaro and Taiga's own harmful behavior

- Taiga returned wanting to recover belonging and repair his reputation.
- Keitaro's easy acceptance provoked jealousy and resentment.
- Taiga tried to discredit and hurt him, including the compromising photograph incident and burning his journal.
- Keitaro offered another chance. Taiga eventually participated in repair and rebuilt relationships through action over time.
- Camp Buddy subsequently became a place where Taiga was useful, trusted, and loved.

This history gives Taiga evidence of both being harmed by someone close and harming someone who had offered him care.

### Olympus and Pinetree

- Act I establishes substantial progress in entering a new environment, developing friendships, and accepting a place in a group.
- At Pinetree, Aaron's public comment exposes a closeness Taiga has been able to experience without naming it fully.
- Taiga leaves before physically attacking Aaron, but subsequently hurts Leo by pushing him away.
- In the aftermath, Leo's hurt becomes connected in Taiga's mind with Keitaro's hurt. He interprets his behavior as evidence of a recurring destructive self.
- In Final Chapter 20, Taiga seeks help, initiates a difficult conversation, apologizes, explains his history, and accepts Leo's continued closeness.

His restraint and repair are evidence too. His inner critic tends to give the failures more weight.

## 4. Four linked fears

These are working formulations of Taiga's expectations, not lines he must consciously think or speak.

| Area | What Taiga may fear | Relevant experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trust in someone close | Someone he loves could turn against him. | Kieran's betrayal. |
| Belonging and being believed | An accusation or mistake could make other people accept the worst account of him. | Rejection and bullying after SportsFest. |
| Trust in his judgment | He may fail to recognize danger in a person or situation until it is too late. | Having trusted Kieran and lost control of the public account. |
| Trust in himself | Feeling threatened could bring out behavior that hurts people he cares about. | His treatment of Keitaro and his rejection of Leo at Pinetree. |

He need not hold all four fears equally strongly or experience them in every relationship. Act I has already challenged broad mistrust of people and environments. Romantic conflict can remain more sensitive.

## 5. The inner critic and the "Mr. Hyde" interpretation

**Agreed model:** Taiga has a harsh inner critic that can convert a particular failure into a verdict about his character.

There is a difference between recognizing that he hurt Leo and concluding that he is fundamentally dangerous to anyone who gets close. His shame can collapse that distinction.

The "Mr. Hyde" metaphor describes how he may experience his worst behavior: an ugly version of himself that appears under pressure and cannot be trusted. It is an author-facing metaphor. Do not establish a separate personality, an inevitable violent transformation, or literal absence of agency.

His feared explanation is not the narrative's objective diagnosis. He has already restrained himself, reconsidered, sought advice, apologized, and returned to difficult conversations.

### Uneven self-monitoring

Self-monitoring should intensify around cues such as:

- noticing his own anger;
- somebody important looking disappointed or behaving differently;
- an accusation about his motives, loyalty, or character;
- jealousy or a familiar relationship conflict;
- realizing that something he said has hurt somebody.

It should recede when he is absorbed, comfortable, amused, or enjoying ordinary company. Constant vigilance in every scene would erase his existing development and flatten the emotional range of the novel.

## 6. Defensiveness and self-condemnation can alternate

An accusation can touch two different vulnerabilities:

1. Taiga has been falsely accused and disbelieved. He may experience an accusation as unjust or threatening.
2. Taiga knows he has genuinely hurt people. He may fear that part of the accusation is true.

This creates a plausible sequence:

**Perceived accusation → defense against unfairness → sharpness or withdrawal → recognition of the other person's hurt → shame and self-condemnation.**

He can move from defending himself to believing the other person would be better off without him. Neither position, on its own, requires him to remain present and address a specific mistake.

One useful behavioral expression is arguing against an unfair phrase while failing to answer the legitimate hurt underneath it. The unfair phrase gives him something concrete to contest; acknowledging the fair part feels more exposing.

This is a possible pattern, not an obligatory sequence. He can interrupt it at several points, and his ability to do so is part of the progression.

## 7. What makes a disagreement difficult

Taiga can handle ordinary disagreement about code, tactics, food, equipment, or a practical decision. "He reacts intensely to fights" is too broad to guide individual scenes.

Conflict becomes especially charged when he experiences it as a judgment about:

- what kind of person he is;
- whether he is loyal or trustworthy;
- whether he has a legitimate place in someone's life;
- whether he is repeating earlier harm;
- whether his explanation will be heard or dismissed.

Audience matters. Public exposure can threaten belonging and control over how others interpret him. A private request from Leo may carry a very different meaning.

The same words can land differently depending on existing tension, recent behavior, fatigue, privacy, and how safe the interaction feels. Establish those conditions in the scene rather than selecting a reaction mechanically.

## 9. The cycle that can make the past feel present

Taiga's statement that the past keeps returning contains both real circumstances and a possible reinforcing pattern.

An actual encounter with Kieran is unresolved history arriving in the present. Taiga did not create that encounter through pessimistic thinking.

However, a protective response can contribute to an outcome he fears:

**Anticipates rejection → becomes defensive or distant → hurts someone → interprets the hurt as proof that he ruins relationships.**

This is the specific sense in which a self-fulfilling pattern can operate. Avoid summarizing the whole problem as Taiga simply refusing to let go. It would make the harm and his subsequent difficulty sound more voluntary than the story establishes.

His development involves noticing where he has choices now. It does not require denying what happened or claiming that the past affects him only because he permits it.

## 10. Friendship, belonging, and persistent attachment

Act I's progress is real. Taiga can make friends, enjoy them, accept responsibility, and feel at home in a group. Do not restart him as somebody who trusts nobody.

Ed and Lee's continuity in his life is part of the discussion's understanding of how he has experienced durable friendship. Avoid turning the shorthand that they "never abandoned him" into a new absolute lore claim without checking the relevant source if precise events matter.

Friendship has become more practiced and familiar. Calling it easy or universally less demanding would oversimplify the bonds and risks already shown in the story.

Taiga may limit interactions, conceal vulnerable material, or resist depending on someone. He need not consciously decide that no one will be allowed to matter. He can discover that he already cares before he intended to admit it.

### Caring does not require unlimited access

"Once he cares, he does not let go" is most useful as a description of persistent attachment. He may continue caring while withdrawing, ending a relationship, or choosing a boundary.

He can care about Kieran and refuse romantic reunion. Ending a relationship does not prove that his earlier love was false or that he has become indifferent.

### People pleasing remains an open interpretation

Prioritizing new friendships over Kieran does not, by itself, prove people pleasing. It may reflect excitement, the reward of belonging, inattention to an existing relationship, or other motives.

To establish approval-seeking as a specific mechanism, the prose would need evidence that Taiga suppresses his preferences, struggles to disappoint others, or acts to secure approval. Do not invent that explanation simply to create symmetry with Leo.

## 11. What makes romance different for him

Taiga already tolerates many forms of commitment. General fear of responsibility or loss of independence is not the agreed foundation.

Romance reintroduces a situation he has little successful experience navigating since Kieran: another person reasonably expects particular consideration and priority, and Taiga's choices can disappoint that person.

Two fears can coexist:

- Someone he loves could hurt him.
- He could fail or hurt someone he loves.

He may believe that his first relationship proved he cannot provide enough. That belief has a basis in a real failure—neglecting Kieran—but can become distorted when he treats everything that followed as the consequence of his inadequacy.

The narrative must keep responsibility specific. Taiga can acknowledge neglect without accepting responsibility for framing, bullying, or violence committed by others.

Ordinary romantic needs are not automatically threats. Taiga can like being wanted and enjoy Leo choosing him. The difficulty develops when he interprets disappointment as condemnation, jealousy as the start of an old sequence, or a mistake as proof that he should not be in a relationship.

## 12. Leo's position in this model

The revised Leo has no established ex-boyfriend history. The older development bible's push-pull relationship backstory must not be imported to explain his Pinetree behavior. His current characterization is less perfectly self-aware and more inclined to accommodate or please people.

Leo can:

- sincerely want to help Taiga;
- offer space before realizing what that generosity costs him;
- feel jealous and struggle to admit it;
- say that he is fine while becoming quieter or sharper;
- want consideration without immediately knowing how to ask;
- make mistakes, become tired, and need support himself.

He is not Taiga's therapist, diagnostic narrator, or endlessly patient proof that love is safe. Taiga's progress cannot depend on Leo always responding perfectly.

Leo's proposed progression is complementary: in Act III he becomes able to admit hurt and ask for consideration; in Act IV he can increasingly express wants before suppressing them creates resentment. Exact scenes remain open.

## 13. Kieran's return: several meanings at once

Kieran is an encounter with an unresolved relationship and with the beginning of Taiga's damaged understanding of himself. He should have his own reasons to return, work, and reconnect with camp.

Taiga's response may include anger, resentment, residual affection, familiarity, curiosity, relief, or discomfort. The combination and timing remain choices for the rewrite. Intellectual forgiveness does not automatically determine comfort in Kieran's actual presence.

Old habits and ease can be real without indicating renewed romantic desire. Do not use every familiar interaction as evidence of a triangle Taiga secretly wants.

### The temptation to repair the original disaster

One agreed direction is that Taiga may see Kieran's return as a chance to handle the past correctly this time: listen, give enough attention, explain himself, avoid hurting someone, and demonstrate that he has changed.

The underlying temptation is to prove that he has escaped the person he fears he is by making this encounter turn out perfectly.

A possible mechanism—not yet a fixed scene—is overinvestment in Kieran's discomfort. Reassuring or helping gives Taiga something concrete to do. Leo seems capable and understanding, so Taiga assumes that he needs less consideration. Generosity becomes something Taiga takes for granted.

This would allow actual neglect to emerge without Taiga wanting Kieran back.

### Separate debts and choices

- Wanting redemption for harming Keitaro is different from wanting to resolve the relationship with Kieran.
- Taiga does not owe Kieran romantic access as compensation for neglecting him years ago.
- Understanding Kieran's jealousy does not justify his retaliation.
- Forgiveness, reconciliation, renewed friendship, and romantic reunion are distinct choices.
- Kieran may be disappointed by Taiga's decision. Taiga must be allowed to let that disappointment exist.
- Kieran should not become a villain solely to simplify the choice, or a matchmaker who explains Leo's feelings for Taiga.

The old draft has Taiga say that he forgave Kieran earlier. Whether that remains the precise timing in the rewrite is not settled by this bible. The agreed principle is that forgiveness does not require restoring the relationship.

## 14. Leo's jealousy and the repeated pattern

The proposed Act III conflict echoes the first camp term: Taiga becomes absorbed in something important to him, while the person beside him feels increasingly peripheral.

For the new version, Leo should have some concrete reason to feel neglected. Possible forms discussed include a missed shared plan, Taiga staying in a conversation longer than intended, or repeatedly checking Kieran's comfort while assuming Leo is fine. No specific incident has been selected.

Leo may initially encourage the reconciliation. His later hurt can therefore coexist with sincere support. He does not have to understand that contradiction immediately.

Taiga may initially feel pleased by evidence that Leo wants a special place beside him. The difficult moment comes when that desire reaches him as disappointment or accusation. He may respond to the feared old pattern instead of the particular need Leo is expressing.

The task for both is specific:

- Taiga hears Leo's actual complaint and owns his contribution without accepting every implied judgment.
- Leo expresses jealousy and hurt without treating the feeling as ownership or entitlement to all of Taiga's time.

The outcome should show that a familiar kind of conflict can unfold differently. It cannot guarantee that either will never hurt the other.

## 15. The starting point after Final Act II

Chapter 20 leaves them with meaningful gains:

- Taiga has admitted his feelings and stopped treating his own desire as an unsolved mystery.
- Leo knows that the closeness mattered and that he was not imagining the trajectory.
- Taiga has explained the important history behind his reaction.
- Leo has asked not to be excluded from decisions about whether the relationship is worth pursuing.
- Leo has also asked that other people's presence not make them retreat from how they treat each other.
- Taiga accepts deliberate affection and continues toward Leo despite discomfort.

These gains must survive the transition into Act III. They do not automatically create ease, a label, or readiness for every next step.

The immediate awkwardness belongs to having been exposed, hurt, forgiven, and seen differently. It must not become a new round of wondering whether either boy likes the other.

## 16. Act III: resolve the relationship with the past

**Agreed direction:** Act III becomes Taiga's past-resolution arc, with romantic progress emerging through his choices.

The central movement is away from believing that he must repair the past perfectly before he is allowed to move forward.

By the end of the act, the intended gains are:

1. Taiga has decided what place Kieran has in his present.
2. He distinguishes responsibility for his own neglect from responsibility for Kieran's betrayal.
3. He has experienced a meaningful disagreement with Leo that permits accountability and repair rather than confirming inevitable rejection or destruction.
4. He has practiced letting another person be disappointed without treating that disappointment as something he must eliminate at any cost.
5. He makes an active romantic choice toward Leo. Reciprocated feelings alone cannot be the act's new discovery.

Leo should leave the act with a clearer understanding of what Taiga is choosing to pursue with him. The precise agreement, use of a dating label, and placement of physical milestones remain to be decided organically.

Act III need not cure Taiga's inner critic, erase memories, eliminate defensiveness, or make him fully secure.

## 17. Act IV: participate in an imperfect present relationship

**Agreed direction:** Act IV tests the relationship they are choosing to build, especially when Taiga cannot prevent pain, perform well, or make himself useful.

The central problem remaining is accepting that a relationship can include failure, helplessness, receiving care, and uncertainty.

Useful unresolved pressures include:

- shame after genuinely making a mistake;
- a need to be competent, dependable, or useful;
- difficulty tolerating someone he loves being hurt when he cannot fix it;
- accepting comfort when he feels he has failed;
- continuing to express wants and make commitments in ordinary life.

The drafted Chapter 36, *Fault*, offers relevant material: Leo's injury leaves Taiga helpless; he tries to compensate through decisive action in the match, makes a consequential error, and struggles with having failed the team's trust. This is a candidate application, not an instruction to preserve every event or reaction unchanged.

Act III's progress must remain visible inside any later crisis. In *Fault*, asking for space and then choosing to go to Leo is a useful example. Leo can acknowledge an actual mistake while Taiga gradually accepts care.

A public kiss may remain a significant act of initiative and joy. It cannot be the first time Taiga respects the Chapter 20 request to acknowledge their closeness around other people.

## 19. Act III must not resolve through one gigantic fight

The user explicitly wants to avoid rebuilding Act II's architecture: mounting pressure, a large rupture, a comprehensive explanation, and a resolution that settles the whole arc.

One significant fight is currently expected, but its job must remain limited. It can address a concrete failure of consideration or communication. It should not settle Taiga's entire history, Kieran's future, Leo's expectations, and the relationship's meaning at once.

Distribute change through separate actions:

- meaningful romantic choices before camp;
- Taiga and Kieran's own developing conversations;
- some understanding or boundary before the fight;
- an argument about the actual present problem;
- repair and practical follow-through over time;
- a Kieran decision that has its own momentum;
- an affirmative choice toward Leo arising from desire, not merely compensation.

Placing the fight early enough in the camp section to show substantial life afterward is a strong working proposal. Exact placement remains open.

Repair can precede full understanding. Taiga may acknowledge leaving Leo waiting before he understands why he became absorbed in Kieran. Leo may admit jealousy before he can explain every expectation. Neither needs a definitive interpretation of the relationship before they regain some ease.

Ordinary camp life must retain value: work, jokes, food, independent friendships, boredom, affection, and enjoyment. Do not make every maintenance job a symbol of emotional repair or every exchange a psychological test.

## 20. The month before camp: readiness must develop

**Agreed planning constraint:** Approximately a month lies between the post-Pinetree conversation and the Camp Buddy trip. Exact calendar dates are not established here.

**Starting state:** Taiga is not ready to invite Leo immediately after Chapter 20. He can become ready over the following weeks. The invitation should be a consequence of that development.

The old pre-camp structure relied heavily on physical awareness, avoidance, and ambiguous touch. Much of that work has moved into Final Act II. It cannot be replayed as new progression or replaced by borrowing the camp conflict or Act IV's major tests.

### Working pre-camp progression

| Phase | Relationship movement | Psychological purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approximately the first week: rediscovering ease | Careful interaction gradually permits spontaneous humor and wanted company. | Both experience that they can still enjoy each other after hurt, disclosure, and acknowledgment. |
| Following week or two: making room | Taiga increasingly initiates contact and includes Leo in time their schedules have not already arranged. | Welcoming Leo becomes an experience Taiga actively finds rewarding. |
| Final stretch: extending the invitation | Spring Break creates the prospect of time apart; Taiga recognizes wanting Leo to share this particular week. | The camp invitation extends smaller choices he has already made. |

Roughly three chapter functions were proposed—rediscovering ease, making room, invitation/preparation—but no chapter count or ladder is locked.

### The first week belongs to both boys

Taiga may worry about saying something wrong or being viewed through what he disclosed. Leo may try to provide space, hesitate to assume closeness, or need time after being hurt. Neither has to stop wanting the other for the interaction to become overly careful.

Spontaneous enjoyment can break through: an ordinary disagreement, an involuntary laugh, a conversation neither wants to end. These moments restore ease while preserving the acknowledged feelings.

### The middle provides the bridge

Classes and soccer place them together automatically. Camp requires Taiga to create the opportunity himself.

The existing Room 317 dinner is a useful candidate: a modest invitation with an ordinary reason, followed by Taiga enjoying Leo in his space and with Caleb and Maya. Its value is inclusion, not another discovery that he is attracted to Leo. It remains a candidate until the new ladder is decided.

Separate obligations create variation. Leo has Enzo, projects, and plans; Taiga has coursework, soccer, Caleb, Nora, and his own time. Asking for company or arranging another opportunity can supply progression without a new physical milestone or manufactured crisis.

Once the initial difficulty is established, selected scenes and time jumps can cover the rest. Do not assign a new psychological obstacle to every week.

### The invitation's specific meaning

Taiga need not spend the month consciously preparing to bring Leo to Camp Buddy. He should enjoy making room for Leo enough that, when holiday plans arise, sharing that week becomes something he wants.

Readiness to invite him is a bounded development. Taiga can remain nervous, uncertain about labels, and vulnerable to later conflict. He wants this particular risk enough to take it.

## 22. Observable growth

Progress must occur within difficult moments as well as after them. Possible signs include:

- noticing defensiveness before becoming cruel;
- asking for a pause while indicating that he intends to return;
- hearing a legitimate complaint while disagreeing with part of its wording;
- apologizing for a specific action without declaring himself fundamentally harmful;
- approaching Leo while ashamed rather than waiting until he feels worthy;
- letting Kieran be disappointed without changing an honest answer;
- initiating wanted company without hiding entirely behind usefulness;
- accepting that someone can care for him while acknowledging his mistake.

These are options for varying scenes, not a checklist every interaction must satisfy. A later emotional collapse does not erase growth if his choices within and after it have changed.

## 23. Scene-level questions

Before building a significant reaction, ask:

1. What actually happened, independently of Taiga's interpretation?
2. What specific meaning does he attach to it?
3. Which fear is active: being betrayed, being disbelieved, misjudging someone, or causing harm?
4. Is this a normal disagreement, a reasonable need, an unfair accusation, or a mixture?
5. What protection does he reach for, and what does it accomplish immediately?
6. What does that protection cost him or the other person?
7. What choices are available at his current stage of development?
8. Does the scene preserve the other person's perspective and agency?
9. What can remain unresolved without making the interaction meaningless?
10. Is the behavior sufficiently clear that the narration can stop explaining it?

Do not put these questions into dialogue. Preserve Taiga's close POV and incomplete understanding; let the reader see patterns he cannot yet name.
