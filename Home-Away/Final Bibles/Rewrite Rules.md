# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Rules

This file defines what the final rewrite is allowed to change and what remains authoritative.

---

# 1. Source of Authority

For the final rewrite:

**Draft → Final Bibles → Final manuscript**

Meaning:

- the completed Draft defines what actually happened;
- Final Bibles define how that completed story should be rewritten;
- the Final manuscript is the polished implementation.

The existing `Bibles/` folder remains development and reference material.

It should not be retroactively rewritten to pretend it describes the finished draft perfectly.

---

# 2. Story Preservation

Default rule:

**Preserve the story and continuity unless a specific problem has been identified.**

The final rewrite is not a new outline pass.

Do not invent:

- new arcs;
- new conflicts;
- new relationship milestones;
- new backstory;
- new themes;

merely because a chapter could be made more dramatic.

---

# 3. What May Be Rewritten Freely

The final pass may substantially change:

- sentence construction;
- paragraphing;
- rhythm;
- dialogue wording;
- scene entrances and exits;
- staging;
- description;
- exposition placement;
- POV filtering;
- comedic timing;
- characterization clarity.

A scene does not need to preserve draft wording.

It needs to preserve the story unless an intentional story-level revision is logged.

---

# 4. Characterization Revisions

The rewrite may strengthen characterization already supported by the completed novel.

This includes:

- making voices more distinct;
- restoring established traits that faded later;
- making behavior more consistent;
- sharpening recurring habits;
- improving social dynamics.

Do not create a new personality merely to differentiate a weak character.

Strengthen what the novel already supports first.

---

# 5. POV Revision

Taiga-filtered close third is a final-text requirement.

Later-draft neutral narration should be rewritten where necessary.

Do not solve weak POV by mechanically inserting sarcastic fragments.

Restore:

- attention;
- judgment;
- priorities;
- interpretation;
- selective noticing.

---

# 6. Expansion and Compression

Expand when necessary for:

- staging clarity;
- emotional causality;
- character distinction;
- missing reaction;
- continuity;
- scenes that currently feel summarized but deserve dramatization.

Compress when necessary for:

- repeated information;
- redundant emotional explanation;
- repeated joke templates;
- duplicated beats;
- scenes that continue after their useful work is done.

Word count is not a target by itself.

The final novel may remain near the draft length, grow, or shrink.

---

# 7. No Automatic Beautification

Do not make prose more ornate merely because this is the final version.

Prefer stronger:

- precision;
- rhythm;
- POV;
- verbs;
- detail;
- character specificity.

Not more decoration.

---

# 8. Rewrite Workflow

For each chapter:

1. Read the full draft chapter.
2. Identify scene purpose and continuity requirements.
3. Check relevant old Bibles only where needed.
4. Check Final Bibles.
5. Rewrite rather than line-polish when the draft structure itself is weak.
6. Log novel-wide issues in `Revision Ledger.md`.
7. Update Final Bibles when a rule becomes genuinely global.
