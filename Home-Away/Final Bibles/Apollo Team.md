# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Apollo Team Bible

This file defines the **final-rewrite characterization, voice hierarchy and ensemble function of the recurring Apollo men's football cast**.

It is intentionally separate from the development-era `Bibles/Characters.md`.

The completed Draft remains evidence for relationships, continuity and scenes that already work, but this file is authoritative when the final rewrite deliberately sharpens or changes a team member's characterization.

---

# 1. Ensemble Principle

Apollo should eventually feel to Taiga like a second ensemble with the same **narrative solidity** as the Camp Buddy group.

This does **not** mean creating one-to-one copies of Camp Buddy characters.

The recurring Apollo cast should instead have:

- comparable recognizability;
- recurring social roles;
- distinct comic mechanisms;
- different failure modes;
- relationships with one another that exist independently of Taiga;
- enough repeated presence that the reader learns how each person changes the energy of a scene.

The primary recurring Apollo group is:

- **Vincenzo “Enzo” Delos Reyes**
- **Scott Stirling**
- **Aaron Bennett**
- **Evan Cole**
- **Gavin “Gav” Foster**

Leo belongs to the team but is defined separately as co-lead.

Other squad members recur to create team density without requiring equal subplot weight.

## Locked squad numbers and positions

Use this as the **Final-rewrite roster reference** whenever shirts, lineup boards, substitutions, match prose or positional relationships require a number.

### Numbering principle

The traditional **1–11 range belongs to returning Apollo players**. A freshman can already be a regular first-team player without inheriting the classic number associated with his position.

Freshmen use higher squad numbers because they have only just entered the program. Their shirt number should therefore communicate **seniority / roster history**, not simply tactical position.

This is especially important for Leo: he often occupies the role traditionally called the **No. 10**, but as a newly arrived freshman he wears **#24**, not #10.

| No. | Player | Position | Year / status |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Scott Stirling** | Goalkeeper (GK) | Returning player; first-choice goalkeeper and captain |
| **2** | **Jonah Ellis** | Right-back (RB) | Returning player |
| **4** | **Theo Walsh** | Centre-back (CB) | Returning / experienced player |
| **5** | **Malik Carter** | Centre-back (CB) | Second-year player |
| **6** | **Gav Foster** | Defensive / holding midfielder (DM) | Returning player |
| **8** | **Isaac Romero** | Central midfielder (CM) | Returning player |
| **9** | **Jordan Price** | Striker (ST) | Returning player; starting striker / primary 9 |
| **11** | **Evan Cole** | Left winger (LW) | Senior; final year; graduates at the end of the year; Taiga's main positional competition |
| **12** | **Nate Kim** | Goalkeeper (GK) | Returning reserve goalkeeper |
| **23** | **Aaron Bennett** | Left-back (LB) | Freshman; joins through the freshman tryout |
| **24** | **Leo Mendez** | Attacking midfielder / central attacking player (AM) | Freshman; newly joined Apollo |
| **27** | **Vincenzo “Enzo” Delos Reyes** | Right winger (RW) | Freshman; same incoming class as Leo |
| **41** | **Taiga Akatora** | Left winger (LW) | Freshman development player; number established in Final Chapter 2 |

### Numbering continuity rules

- Treat these as **player squad numbers**, not positional labels.
- **Leo is #24.** He may function tactically as Apollo's “10,” but prose should distinguish the role from the shirt number whenever confusion is possible.
- **All freshmen use numbers above 11.**
- Numbers **1–11 are reserved for players who were already in the Apollo program before the current freshman class arrived**.
- It is fine for some classic 1–11 numbers to be absent from the named cast. Do not force #3, #7 or #10 onto a freshman merely to complete a traditional XI.
- **Taiga remains #41** through the current first-year rewrite.
- **At the end of the year, Evan graduates and Taiga inherits #11.** This is Taiga's earned senior number and a payoff to his progression as Apollo's left winger.
- Do not give another Apollo player one of these locked numbers before the planned number transitions.

# 2. Vincenzo “Enzo” Delos Reyes

## Canon identity and visual anchor

Official Blits concept material identifies him as **Vincenzo “Enzo” Delos Reyes**.

Use:
- **Enzo** in ordinary narration, dialogue and teammate interactions;
- **Reyes** when Mercer refers to him by surname;
- **Delos Reyes** in full-name, roster, or other formal contexts;
- **Vincenzo** only where a full/legal given name is naturally required.

Visual anchor from the official concept sketch:
- dark brown skin;
- short, spiky black hair;
- athletic footballer's build;
- highly expressive face and posture.

These visual traits are canon anchors, not instructions to repeat a full physical description every time he appears.

---

## Core role

**The performer who knows when to stop performing.**

Enzo overlaps superficially with Hiro because both are loud, funny and socially energetic.

The distinction is fundamental.

### Hiro

Hiro tends toward:

- genuine chaos;
- friendliness;
- impulsiveness;
- enthusiasm;
- naivety;
- pettiness;
- getting swept into the bit himself.

### Enzo

Enzo tends toward:

- theatricality;
- deliberate exaggeration;
- filling silence;
- turning ordinary moments into performances;
- reading the room while he performs;
- knowing when the joke is no longer useful.

Hiro often creates chaos because he is caught in it.

Enzo often creates comedy because he has decided the room needs something.

---

## Social instinct

**Fill the gap.**

Enzo dislikes dead social air.

When conversation stalls, tension appears, or a group needs energy, his instinct is often to:

- make a declaration;
- invent a principle;
- exaggerate the stakes;
- mock-protest;
- turn the moment into a story;
- provoke a response.

This does not mean he is afraid of silence in a literal sense.

It means humor is one of the tools he uses to keep social momentum alive.

---

## Social perception

Enzo is more perceptive than his behavior initially suggests.

He should be particularly good at reading **Leo**, because of their long shared history.

He may notice:

- when Leo's enthusiasm is forced;
- when Leo is overcommitting;
- when Leo is hurt but still talking;
- when Leo needs the joke dropped;
- when something between Taiga and Leo is genuinely wrong.

He should not become omniscient.

But when the situation is serious, Enzo is often capable of realizing it before louder or more naive characters would.

---

## Serious mode

When something genuinely matters, the performance can disappear almost instantly.

Serious Enzo should be:

- direct;
- emotionally intelligent;
- mature;
- protective without becoming paternal;
- capable of listening;
- capable of saying something uncomfortable without hiding behind a joke.

The contrast should be strong enough that the reader feels the absence of his normal performance.

A serious Enzo scene should not sound like a slightly quieter version of ordinary Enzo.

---

## Sentence shape

Normal Enzo:

- expansive;
- declarative;
- theatrical;
- hyperbolic;
- comfortable with long comic escalation;
- willing to commit past the reasonable stopping point.

Serious Enzo:

- simpler;
- shorter;
- less ornamented;
- more precise.

---

## Humor mechanism

Enzo's comedy comes from **performance**.

Use:

- mock outrage;
- dramatic declarations;
- invented rules;
- exaggerated stakes;
- fake philosophical principles;
- overcommitment to a premise;
- capitalization in chats;
- food metaphors when natural.

Avoid making him random.

The joke should usually have a social target or purpose.

---

## Stable traits

Enzo remains:

- competitive;
- loyal;
- ridiculous;
- socially fearless;
- tactically aware;
- capable of maturity;
- close to Leo in a way that predates Olympus.

Do not make him stupid in order to make him funny.

---

# 3. Scott Stirling

## Canon identity and visual anchor

Official Blits concept material identifies him as **Scott Stirling**.

Use:
- **Scott** in ordinary narration and teammate interactions;
- **Stirling** when Mercer/coaches or formal football contexts use surnames.

Visual anchor from the official concept sketch:
- pale skin;
- long dark blue-gray hair that falls over and obscures his eyes;
- a comparatively narrow, withdrawn-looking silhouette off the pitch;
- goalkeeper presentation in the concept art.

The hidden-eyes silhouette works especially well with the final-rewrite contrast between off-pitch shyness and on-pitch authority. Keep that contrast behavioral rather than repeatedly explaining what his appearance “means.”

---

## Core role

**The shy captain who becomes completely different when the game gives him a role.**

Scott is Apollo's captain.

This should initially confuse Taiga.

Off the pitch, Scott appears like one of the least obvious people to lead anyone.

On the pitch, the reason becomes immediately clear.

---

## Off-pitch Scott

Scott is genuinely shy.

He:

- gets nervous easily;
- dislikes being the center of attention;
- can stutter;
- struggles with unfamiliar social situations;
- is bad at spontaneous conversation;
- may fail to know what response is socially expected;
- can become visibly uncomfortable when directly addressed by several people at once.

His quietness should no longer read primarily as cool detachment or effortless confidence.

Some of the current Draft's dry lines can remain, but their mechanism should change.

They may come from:

- brevity;
- nervous literalness;
- saying the one thing he can think of;
- accidentally sounding deadpan because he does not know how to decorate the answer.

---

## Social incompetence

Scott can resemble Hunter in broad shyness, but the effect should be more extreme and differently expressed.

Hunter can be anxious while still socially gentle and readable.

Scott may simply fail.

Examples:

- somebody compliments him and he has no usable response;
- he rehearses a sentence and still stumbles through it;
- he gets asked to give a captain's speech and visibly wants to disappear;
- a stranger starts small talk and Scott answers the literal question, then cannot restart the conversation;
- teammates intentionally make him order for the table because they think it is funny.

Do not make his shyness infantilizing.

He is a competent adult with a very uneven comfort profile.

---

## The goalkeeper transformation

When Scott puts on the gloves and enters the goal, he changes dramatically.

This is not magic.

The change comes from **role clarity**.

Social situations contain infinite possible responses.

Goalkeeping gives Scott:

- a defined job;
- clear information;
- known responsibilities;
- permission to be loud;
- authority everyone expects him to use;
- immediate consequences he understands.

On the pitch he becomes:

- confident;
- commanding;
- loud;
- decisive;
- constantly communicative;
- comfortable correcting people;
- capable of shouting without hesitation;
- willing to point, organize and demand.

His speech should become radically cleaner.

Off pitch:

> "I-I mean, uh... if you want, I guess—"

On pitch:

> "CARTER, STEP!"
>
> "AARON, INSIDE!"
>
> "HOLD!"
>
> "SECOND BALL!"
>
> "TAIGA, TRACK!"

No stutter.

No uncertainty.

---

## Captaincy

Scott is captain because the team trusts the version of him that appears when responsibility becomes concrete.

He:

- sees the whole pitch;
- organizes the defense;
- remains tactically aware under pressure;
- communicates constantly during play;
- takes responsibility for the team's shape;
- becomes reliable when everyone else is reacting.

Captaincy should create useful contrast off the pitch.

He may be excellent at:

- leading a defensive line;
- calling the team to attention in a match;
- confronting a tactical problem.

He may be terrible at:

- speeches;
- welcoming newcomers;
- formal social leadership;
- public praise;
- mediating a casual interpersonal disagreement.

Other teammates can naturally compensate for the parts of captaincy Scott is bad at.

---

## Taiga's first impression

A useful early progression:

Taiga meets Scott socially and wonders:

> *Why the fuck is this guy captain?*

Then he sees Scott in goal.

The question answers itself.

Do not over-explain the contrast afterward.

Let Taiga understand it through behavior.

---

## Humor mechanism

Scott's comedy comes from the mismatch between:

- official status and social incompetence;
- terrifying goalkeeper voice and awkward ordinary voice;
- people expecting Captain Scott and getting Scott-without-gloves.

Do not make every scene about his stutter.

The broader mechanism is **role-dependent confidence**.

---

## Failure mode

In ordinary social pressure:

- speech worsens;
- he may retreat;
- he may overthink simple interactions;
- he becomes more visibly nervous.

In football pressure:

- he becomes more focused rather than less;
- volume and certainty increase;
- he may become too controlling or harsh if the defensive structure repeatedly fails.

His competence should have its own failure mode.

---

# 4. Aaron Bennett

## Core role

**The confident guy who takes criticism far more personally than everyone realizes.**

Aaron is genuinely social and genuinely confident.

The confidence is not entirely fake.

But his sense of competence is surprisingly fragile.

This should be seeded from early in the novel so the Pinetree outburst no longer feels like a new personality appearing for one chapter.

---

## Normal Aaron

Normally he projects almost excessive self-assurance.

He is:

- casual;
- social;
- flirtatious;
- playful;
- quick to join a conversation;
- comfortable acting like he knows what he is doing;
- unnervingly certain of himself.

He can make mistakes and still behave as though the mistake is a temporary inconvenience rather than evidence of anything important.

---

## Criticism sensitivity

Aaron has one major weakness:

**he takes correction personally before he can process it technically.**

This should be visible in small, harmless ways long before Pinetree.

A normal pattern:

Someone:
> "You were late on that overlap."

Aaron:
> "Oh, fuck off."

Then he fixes it.

Or:

> "That pass was terrible."

> "Your face is terrible."

Then play continues.

This is usually:

- quick;
- low-stakes;
- familiar;
- not a real conflict.

The team knows Aaron does this.

They are not afraid to correct him.

He normally resets within seconds.

---

## Comic mechanism

Aaron's sensitivity itself can be funny when the stakes are low.

He may:

- reject the criticism;
- insult the messenger;
- immediately perform the correction anyway;
- pretend the correction was his idea;
- act offended because somebody noticed.

The joke is not that Aaron cannot take criticism at all.

The joke is that he has to **fight it for several seconds before accepting it**.

---

## Pinetree warning sign

This makes Pinetree much clearer.

During the joint weekend, Aaron is:

- repeatedly repositioned;
- repeatedly corrected;
- publicly evaluated;
- asked to perform unfamiliar jobs;
- unable to reset between corrections.

Normally someone criticizes him, he says something rude, fixes it, and moves on.

At Pinetree, he stops moving on.

The team should be able to sense the difference.

Possible warning sign:

- the usual comeback does not release the tension;
- he remains angry after the next possession;
- he starts defending every decision;
- even harmless teasing lands badly;
- somebody notices that this time Aaron is actually pissed.

His eventual outburst then becomes accumulation rather than surprise.

---

## Emotional weakness

Aaron is especially vulnerable to the feeling:

> everybody can see that I do not know what I am doing.

That is why public evaluation affects him so strongly.

Under enough pressure he can:

- become defensive;
- interpret correction as humiliation;
- overthink;
- make the next decision worse;
- lash out socially;
- weaponize information he knows will hurt.

The Pinetree incident remains genuinely wrong.

The rewrite should explain its context without excusing it.

---

## Repair

Aaron's apology remains an important part of his characterization.

Once the defensive phase burns out, he can:

- admit exactly what he did;
- admit why he did it;
- accept that Taiga may remain angry;
- apologize without turning the apology into charm.

His frailty does not remove his capacity for accountability.

---

## Sofia

Aaron's dynamic with Sofia remains useful because romantic stakes make his normal confidence less stable.

He can flirt easily.

Actually caring about Sofia's opinion is harder.

Do not let this become his only source of vulnerability.

---

## Sentence shape

Normal:

- casual;
- quick;
- conversational;
- slang-heavy;
- easy teasing.

Defensive:

- shorter;
- reactive;
- more explanations;
- more "I know";
- more immediate rejection.

Serious:

- capable of surprisingly direct, complete sentences once he stops protecting himself.

---

## Distinguish from Taiga

Taiga and Aaron may both react badly to pressure, but the trigger is different.

Taiga resents feeling controlled, exposed or emotionally threatened.

Aaron is especially sensitive to **being seen as incompetent**.

---

# 5. Evan Cole

## Core role

**The senpai.**

Evan is the oldest recurring Apollo player in the main cast.

He is a **senior in his final year at Olympus** and will graduate at the end of the academic year.

He has already experienced many of the things the younger players are currently panicking about.

That gives him a natural confidence.

His departure also has a concrete football consequence: Evan currently owns Apollo's **#11**, the traditional left-wing shirt. After Evan graduates, **Taiga inherits #11** as the symbolic payoff to his first-year progression from development player #41 into the senior left-wing role.

---

## Social function

Evan is the teammate who can say:

> "You'll survive."

and actually mean it because he already did.

He offers:

- advice;
- perspective;
- team-culture translation;
- senior-player teasing;
- occasional reality checks.

Unlike Mercer, he is still one of the players.

Unlike Gav, his authority comes from experience rather than obsessive knowledge.

Unlike Scott, he is socially comfortable.

---

## Taiga mentorship

Evan becomes Taiga's **informal senior mentor** within Apollo.

This is not an assigned role and should not turn Evan into a substitute coach. The relationship grows because:

- Evan and Taiga play the same primary position on the **left wing**;
- Evan is the senior player whose minutes and role Taiga is gradually growing toward;
- Evan is experienced enough to recognize which of Taiga's problems are normal freshman problems;
- Taiga is much more likely to accept practical advice from a teammate who has actually done the job than from someone trying to manage his emotions.

Evan can teach Taiga things Mercer cannot teach in the same way:

- how to survive a bad training session without treating it as a verdict;
- how to interpret criticism from coaches and teammates;
- how to recover after making a mistake during a match;
- how to understand winger-specific movement, timing and defensive responsibility;
- how Apollo's locker-room culture actually works;
- when to compete for a place and when to learn from the player currently ahead of him.

The mentorship should remain **informal, teasing and behavior-based**. Evan does not announce that he is mentoring Taiga. He corrects him, gives him context, makes fun of him, occasionally checks on him, and keeps treating him like somebody who is expected to improve.

Taiga should not immediately recognize Evan as a mentor either. Their early friction is useful: Taiga initially experiences Evan's advice as interference. Over time, the same kind of advice becomes one of the few forms of correction he can receive without automatically bristling.

Because Evan is graduating, the relationship has a natural endpoint. Taiga inheriting **#11** is therefore not simply a vacant-number reassignment. It represents the role Evan has been preparing him to occupy, whether either of them ever says that explicitly.

---

## Sentence shape

Use:

- direct complete sentences;
- confident delivery;
- concrete examples;
- little hesitation;
- enough explanation to make the advice useful;
- no need to prove he is right.

His confidence should feel lived-in.

---

## Humor mechanism

Evan's humor often comes from:

- senior-player teasing;
- treating freshman catastrophes as ordinary;
- giving advice nobody requested;
- being far too confident about topics outside football.

This last point becomes important in his romantic quirk.

---

## Women

Evan is enthusiastically obsessed with women in a **classic anime senpai** way.

Keep it:

- enthusiastic;
- harmless;
- non-predatory;
- occasionally embarrassing;
- sincere.

He may have:

- strong opinions about types;
- dating stories;
- terrible romantic theories delivered with total confidence;
- an assumption that other men naturally want to discuss attractive women.

This creates obvious opportunities with Taiga.

For example:

> "Blonde or brunette?"

> "What?"

Evan may initially assume he means women because that is the conversation Evan is having.

Once he knows Taiga is gay, the adjustment should be immediate rather than dramatic.

Something like:

> "Fine. Blonde or brunette guys?"

Same seriousness.

The joke is Evan's framework, not Taiga's sexuality.

---

## Relationship advice

Evan may consider himself unusually qualified to give relationship advice.

Evidence may suggest otherwise.

That contradiction is useful.

He can become Taiga's source of **confident but questionable senpai wisdom**.

Sometimes the advice can even be good accidentally.

---

## Failure mode

Evan should generally be one of the more stable Apollo personalities.

He does not need a dramatic hidden wound merely because others have one.

Possible pressure behavior:

- overconfidence;
- assuming experience means he already understands the new problem;
- giving advice too early;
- underestimating how different somebody else's situation is.

His age and experience should not make him infallible.

---

# 6. Gavin “Gav” Foster

## Canon identity and visual anchor

Official Blits concept material identifies him as **Gavin “Gav” Foster**.

Use:
- **Gav** in ordinary narration, dialogue and teammate interactions;
- **Foster** when Mercer/coaches or formal football contexts use surnames;
- **Gavin** only when the full given name is natural.

Visual anchor from the official concept sketch:
- light, tousled curly hair;
- rectangular dark-framed glasses;
- visibly muscular, athletic build.

The glasses/nerd presentation should not make him physically slight: he is still very obviously a serious college footballer.

---

## Core role

**The nerd.**

Gav should be one of the most visibly enthusiastic nerds in the Apollo cast.

He can share some broad energy with Lee from Camp Buddy:

- detailed explanations;
- deep niche interests;
- the ability to keep talking when a subject activates him;
- unexpectedly specific knowledge.

But Gav is significantly more independent.

He does not need another person to organize his social life.

He actively maintains multiple communities of his own.

---

## Social life

Gav participates in several campus groups outside football.

Locked:

- **Chess Club**
- **D&D / tabletop roleplaying club**
- **Birdwatching club**

These should not all appear as exposition in one scene.

Let them emerge naturally.

The point is that Gav has a full university life that exists parallel to Apollo.

---

## Chess

Chess appeals to:

- competition;
- pattern recognition;
- analysis;
- long-term planning.

Taiga is competitive enough that Gav can plausibly lure him into trying it.

This may become dangerous for everyone involved.

Gav should enjoy the possibility of discovering that Taiga has become invested despite claiming not to care.

---

## D&D

Gav is the kind of person who can invite Taiga to a session and then accidentally spend twenty minutes explaining why Taiga would enjoy it.

Example energy:

> "We need another player Thursday."

> "For football?"

> "D&D."

> "No."

> "You haven't heard the campaign premise."

The comedy comes from Gav treating the missing information as the only reason Taiga said no.

---

## Birdwatching

Birdwatching gives Gav a very different nerd mode from chess and D&D.

It connects naturally to:

- biology;
- field observation;
- patience;
- pattern recognition;
- campus/outdoor life.

Gav may suddenly identify a bird during an unrelated scene because he actually noticed it.

Avoid turning this into a catchphrase.

The point is that his attention naturally contains information other characters do not have.

This also prevents all of his interests from being stereotypically indoor/geek-coded.

---

## Academic direction

Retain:

- biology;
- possible interest in sports medicine.

His curiosity should feel broad rather than like a list of hobbies assigned to make him quirky.

---

## Sentence shape

When uninterested or talking normally:

- ordinary;
- complete;
- reasonably compact.

When a subject activates him:

- longer;
- increasingly specific;
- explanatory;
- enthusiastic;
- capable of giving context nobody requested;
- may fail to notice when the listener stopped needing information three sentences ago.

This contrast is important.

Do not make him permanently verbose.

---

## Social instinct

**Share the thing.**

Gav's instinct when he likes something is often to:

- explain it;
- recommend it;
- invite someone;
- show them why it is interesting;
- connect their existing interests to his.

He recruits.

This is very different from the previous development-bible version of Gav as the squad's primary stabilizer.

He may still be:

- reliable;
- tactically intelligent;
- sensible when needed.

But **"Team Reasonable Person" is no longer his defining social role.**

That function is distributed across:

- Scott's on-field authority;
- Evan's senior perspective;
- Enzo's social perception;
- the general competence of the group.

---

## Humor mechanism

Gav's comedy comes from:

- disproportionate expertise;
- sincere over-explanation;
- nerd enthusiasm colliding with Taiga's impatience;
- treating an invitation as a solvable information problem;
- casually knowing something absurdly specific.

Unlike Enzo, he is not performing for the room.

He genuinely cares about the subject.

---

## Football

Gav remains tactically smart.

His nerdiness should strengthen that rather than replace it.

He may naturally enjoy:

- structures;
- patterns;
- positional relationships;
- systems;
- tactical analysis.

But do not make every football observation sound like a lecture.

He is still an experienced player operating at speed.

---

## Failure mode

Gav may:

- overfocus;
- overexplain;
- get lost in the interesting part of a problem;
- assume more information will help when the actual issue is emotional;
- become stubborn when he believes the system is correct;
- fail to notice that other people do not share his level of interest.

He is socially independent, not socially perfect.

---

# 7. Core Ensemble Contrast

The primary Apollo group should be recognizable by what each person instinctively contributes.

| Character | Default social engine | Main comic mechanism | Pressure behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Enzo** | Fill the gap | Performance and escalation | Performance drops; becomes serious and perceptive |
| **Scott** | Avoid uncontrolled social exposure | Captain/social mismatch | Socially freezes; on-field certainty increases |
| **Aaron** | Project confidence and participate | Defensive banter and exaggerated self-assurance | Criticism becomes personal and accumulates |
| **Evan** | Advise from experience | Senpai confidence and questionable wisdom | Can become overconfident in his own perspective |
| **Gav** | Share interests and information | Sincere nerd overinvestment | Overfocuses and overexplains |

None of these characters should require generic dry sarcasm to function.

---

# 8. Supporting Apollo Squad

The remaining named players exist to give Apollo density.

They should recur naturally without receiving artificial arcs.

## Malik Carter

- center-back;
- calm locker-room bluntness;
- understated snark;
- physically confident;
- can drop one unhelpful truth into a conversation and move on.

Do not make him Scott 2.0.

## Jordan Price

- starting striker / 9;
- direct;
- competitive;
- knows exactly what service he wants;
- on-field communication is request-based: earlier, feet, near post, again.

Off field, he does not need to become another loud social engine.

## Theo Walsh

- experienced center-back;
- calm;
- contextual;
- useful for senior-player explanations;
- quieter and less teasing than Evan.

## Jonah Ellis

- right-back;
- highly vocal and task-oriented during play;
- constant information about width, marks and movement;
- much more ordinary socially off the pitch.

## Isaac Romero

- central midfielder;
- tactically economical;
- often communicates through short calls, signals and positioning;
- should remain a light recurring presence.

## Nate Kim

- reserve goalkeeper;
- gives the goalkeeper group density;
- quieter than Scott;
- familiar with keeper routines;
- does not need a subplot unless the manuscript later gives him one.

---

# 9. Squad Density Rule

Not every player needs a secret wound, an arc or a spotlight scene.

Apollo should feel complete because:

- the same people recur;
- players speak to one another without Taiga;
- positional partnerships repeat;
- jokes belong to identifiable people;
- off-field interests occasionally intrude on football scenes;
- somebody can enter a scene, contribute two recognizable lines and leave.

Repeated light presence is more valuable than artificial depth.

---

# 10. Rewrite Audit

When revising Apollo scenes, check:

- Could the five core players be recognized without dialogue tags?
- Is Enzo performing because the room gives him something to perform with?
- Does serious Enzo become meaningfully different?
- Is Scott's off-field shyness visible without turning every line into a stutter joke?
- Does on-field Scott feel radically more authoritative?
- Is Aaron's criticism sensitivity seeded before Pinetree?
- Does Aaron normally recover quickly enough that the Pinetree failure feels exceptional?
- Does Evan sound older and more experienced without becoming another coach?
- Does Gav have interests and social communities outside football?
- Is Gav's nerdiness enthusiasm rather than generic awkwardness?
- Are supporting players recurring naturally rather than appearing only when the lineup needs a body?
