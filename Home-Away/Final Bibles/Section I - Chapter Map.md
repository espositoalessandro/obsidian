# Section I — Forty-One: Flexible Chapter Map

> **Status: FLEXIBLE PLANNING BIBLE**
>
> This file defines the current dramatic role of each chapter in Section I. It is **not locked continuity** and it does **not override the completed Draft**.
>
> Chapter boundaries, scene placement, titles, timing, and individual beats may change during the Final rewrite when a stronger structure emerges.
>
> Use this map to avoid adjacent chapters doing the same dramatic job, to identify duplicated material, and to decide where existing Draft scenes serve the overall progression best.

## Section-level progression

The current intended movement is:

**resist → adapt → experience friction → discover the limits of neat summaries → commit → earn a place → participate voluntarily → recognize a direction and belong socially → return to the old home → let old and new coexist → achieve → normalize the achievement → destabilize the new baseline**

This should remain compatible with the Section I calibration in `Prose & Voice.md`: Taiga begins as a guarded newcomer who is already capable of growth, and ends the section with Olympus no longer feeling provisional. The final disturbance is not whether he belongs there, but what **Leo specifically** may now mean inside that life.

### Current structural revision

The former Draft Chapter 8 no longer needs to survive as a standalone chapter.

Its useful material about Taiga choosing a major is split across Chapters 7 and 8:

- Chapter 7 seeds the unresolved major problem without resolving it.
- Chapter 8 makes Taiga's emerging Computer Science direction increasingly obvious without requiring a formal declaration, while combining that thread with Arcadia and Apollo social belonging.
- The formal Computer Science declaration is reserved as a possible later-act payoff, after the reality of Taiga repeatedly choosing CS already exists.
- The former single "Coexistence" chapter has split into **Chapter 9 — Home** and **Chapter 10 — Away**. The split is structural rather than cosmetic: *Home* returns Taiga to the people and world that were already home, while *Away* brings him back to Olympus and lets the old and new worlds coexist inside the same social life.
- Former Draft Chapter 13 is pulled into Section I as the new **Chapter 13**.
- Section I therefore now contains **13 chapters**.
- Section II can begin directly at Camp Buddy.

## Chapter 1 — Resistance

### Dramatic role

Taiga arrives convinced Olympus is probably a mistake.

He evaluates the environment from outside and resists investing in it. The scholarship, the campus, the roommate, the social environment and soccer are initially things happening **to** him rather than parts of a life he has chosen.

### Movement

**I don't want this place → maybe Olympus deserves one point.**

### Guardrail

Do not turn this into an origin story for Taiga's emotional growth. Camp Buddy has already changed him. His first instinct may be rejection, but he is already capable of stopping himself before every irritation becomes a confrontation.

## Chapter 2 — Adaptation

### Dramatic role

The hostile mass starts becoming understandable.

Classes develop patterns. Caleb becomes manageable. Football gains roles and vocabulary. Apollo players begin becoming individual people rather than one noisy group. Taiga starts learning the physical and social routes through Olympus.

The important change is not that he loves the place. It is that he discovers he can function inside it.

### Movement

**This place is hostile → I know how to live in it.**

### Guardrail

Do not resolve Taiga's belonging here. Familiarity is not yet attachment.

## Chapter 3 — Friction

### Dramatic role

Participation starts having consequences because Taiga now cares.

Football criticism can hurt because he wants to be good. Caleb can violate a space Taiga has begun considering his. Taiga also notices Leo physically for the first time in a way he cannot quite dismiss as neutral observation, but Chapter 3 should stop there: he thinks Leo has a nice ass; he does not yet turn that into a larger realization about attraction.

Friction should arise from investment, not from Olympus simply becoming hostile again.

### Movement

**I can function here → shit, things here can actually affect me.**

### Current likely anchors

- bad practice / conflict and repair with Evan;
- first unmistakable physical noticing of Leo and the Zoe mention, without escalating it into a settled attraction;
- room-boundary argument with Caleb.

The Political Institutions 74/100 is currently **not preferred here**, because stacking every form of friction into one chapter risks making the structure feel engineered.

## Chapter 4 — Known unknowns / Exposure

### Dramatic role

By the fourth week, Olympus and the people in it are becoming easier for Taiga to summarize. He reduces familiar people to compact personality buckets because those summaries are useful.

The chapter does not need to prove those summaries wrong. Instead, it keeps adding information that the summaries did not contain. The 74/100 exposes a gap in Taiga's academic self-model. Chemistry and Git add new facets to people he already thinks he understands, especially Leo. Hall's office-hours explanation gives Taiga one concrete way to deal with a gap rather than simply being angry that it exists.

The chapter should puncture the feeling that familiarity means complete understanding.

### Movement

**I know enough to summarize this place → apparently the summaries are incomplete.**

### Current planning note

The **74/100 Political Institutions scene is now locked here** as the chapter's first major puncture.

The first General Chemistry lab remains an important anchor. The official table list also reveals **Julio Mendez** as Leo's legal name.

Do not place the masturbation scene here. Chapter 4 should deepen familiarity and complexity without confirming Taiga's attraction beyond the physical noticing already established in Chapter 3.

## Chapter 5 — Commitment

### Dramatic role

Football stops being something Taiga is merely testing and becomes something he is emotionally committed to.

His first actual reserve minutes create real performance pressure. He enters against Northbridge overwhelmed, simplifies his game long enough to settle, then begins recognizing space and making decisions inside the match rather than experiencing it as something happening to him. The match ends 0–0. Socially, he accepts the post-match team dinner instead of automatically leaving, another small step from proximity toward participation.

The romantic thread also advances one deliberate step. A second shower encounter with Leo confirms that Taiga's earlier physical noticing was not a one-off. At dinner, Zoe clarifies that she and Leo are not dating and says she does not think Leo is into girls. The information matters to Taiga, but he does not consciously classify why. Later that night, an involuntary dream about Leo becomes an awake choice: after resisting, Taiga consciously continues and develops the fantasy while masturbating. He recognizes afterward that the dream cannot explain what he chose to do once awake, but postpones confronting what that means.

### Movement

**I want to be good at this → I'm staying even when I'm not.**

### Guardrail

The chapter should not imply that commitment means confidence. Taiga can be deeply invested while still insecure about whether he is good enough.

For the romantic thread, preserve the order:

**second shower → Zoe says Leo may not be into girls → dream → conscious awake fantasy / masturbation → postponement of interpretation.**

Zoe's information must not create the attraction. The reader already knows the attraction exists before Taiga learns Leo may be available to men. The meaningful threshold is not the dream itself, which is involuntary, but Taiga choosing while awake to continue the fantasy and take it further.

This still does **not** begin the later nervous-crush phase. Chapter 5 establishes sexual attraction. Early Chapter 6 should let Taiga consciously file the result under a simple explanation — **Leo is hot; that explained enough** — and return to normal behavior around him.

The question shifts from whether Olympus will tolerate Taiga to whether he has actually earned a place within it.

The development review gives the football thread an institutional answer. Academic work can also begin showing improvement rather than only difficulty. Taiga increasingly encounters evidence that people expect him to remain.

### Movement

**I'm trying to belong here → people are expecting me to remain.**

### Current planning note

If the 74/100 academic thread begins in Chapter 4, this chapter is a natural place for meaningful payoff or visible improvement.

Do not make improvement total. Earning a place is not the same as "everything is easy now."

Caleb and Maya should receive meaningful domestic space here. Maya needs to become a person Taiga actually spends time with, not merely Caleb's girlfriend on a screen, while Caleb's roommate relationship with Taiga should remain active as the football plot grows.

### Romantic guardrail

Chapter 6 is a **step back into normality**, not an escalation.

Taiga may briefly acknowledge the obvious conclusion from Chapter 5: Leo is hot, and that explains the shower, dream and conscious fantasy well enough for now. After that, do not make Taiga nervous, fixated or unusually attentive around Leo. Leo can be present without automatically initiating a Taiga scene, and Taiga can react to his name or presence normally.

The point is that Taiga believes the question is settled.

Do not spend the later Chapter 13 turning point here.

## Chapter 7 — Voluntary Participation / Future Uncertainty

### Dramatic role

Taiga begins doing Olympus things that are not required.

House Apollo, lunch with Nora and the Acropolis hike matter because they are no longer scholarship obligations, roommate necessities, academic requirements or football sessions. He starts spending time with people simply because he wants to.

At the same time, the unresolved major question enters the background. Taiga is clearly capable in Computer Science and increasingly interested in it, but still resists treating that interest as a life decision.

The chapter also opens a new social channel with Leo: private texting begins for a legitimate practical reason, but Taiga does not treat the contact itself as meaningful.

### Movement

**I participate because I have reasons to → sometimes I participate because I want to.**

And, academically:

**I do not know what I am choosing → apparently I already have preferences.**

### Current anchors

- Monday academic advising with Elena Park seeds the major problem without resolving it. Taiga remains undeclared; the provisional next-semester route keeps both Computer Science and Chemistry open.
- Caleb and Maya invite Taiga to hike Acropolis; he says yes.
- Lunch with Nora moves their friendship outside class. Nora reframes Taiga's complaint that everyone is “making” him do things as people repeatedly inviting him places, and asks for a photo from the overlook.
- Thursday House Apollo film review is required; once it ends, Taiga could leave but stays voluntarily for food, conversation, chess and the team's ordinary social life.
- The Sunday Acropolis hike remains the chapter's main event. Taiga is comfortable and competent outdoors, talks naturally about Camp Buddy with Maya, helps navigate the detour, sends Nora the requested viewpoint photo and later saves Maya's candid photo from the day.
- Leo texts Taiga privately for the first time after the hike. The youth clinic provides a legitimate reason for contact: he invites Taiga to the next clinic on **Thursday, October 29**, and Taiga agrees to go. The clinic itself is deliberately reserved for Chapter 9 rather than shown here. Once the logistics are finished, Leo keeps the conversation going by asking about the hike and later messages again. Taiga saves his number but files the whole thing under the consequences of his Apollo “initiation” / Leo being annoying rather than examining it.

### Guardrail

Taiga does not need to become socially enthusiastic. Voluntary participation can remain selective, reluctant and very Taiga-shaped.

Do not let the major material take over the chapter. Chapter 7 is still fundamentally about **choosing to participate**, not choosing a degree.

The first Leo DM is a small relationship escalation, not romantic destabilization. The reader may notice that Leo continues the conversation beyond its practical purpose, but the contact must remain fully deniable as ordinary Leo sociability. Taiga does not become nervous, fixated or unusually attentive, and the physical-attraction compartment remains stable.

## Chapter 8 — Direction + Social Belonging

### Dramatic role

The former "Choice" and "Social Belonging" chapters are now one chapter.

The major question becomes harder for Taiga to avoid, but it does **not** resolve through a formal declaration. The next classes he actually wants are already largely Computer Science classes, and other people can see the direction before he is willing to name it. At the same time, Apollo stops functioning primarily as "the soccer team" and becomes a group of people Taiga actively wants to spend time with.

The chapter should let both developments emerge through ordinary choices rather than force either into a declaration scene.

### Movement

Academically:

**I do not know what I am choosing → apparently I am already choosing a direction.**

Socially:

**These are people I know → these are people I actively want in my life.**

### Current structure

- Open with football to show how much more Taiga now understands about Apollo both tactically and personally. He can recognize Leo's space creation rather than merely the final pass, while Aaron, Enzo, Gav and Scott read as familiar people rather than roster functions.
- Enzo organizes the Arcadia outing in the locker room. Taiga initially refuses, but the chapter does **not** need a separate deliberation scene.
- Nora and spring registration make the academic direction visible. Park's provisional schedule is now **Data Structures, Discrete Structures, Calculus II and General Chemistry II**; Nora points out that this is already very close to a CS major. Taiga remains undeclared, with the declaration page open but untouched.
- Taiga goes to Arcadia voluntarily and minimizes the choice in character: he was free anyway.
- Arcadia provides the chapter's main body. The games should be allowed enough space to show Taiga simply having fun, his low-stakes competitive side, and Apollo functioning as a social group outside football.
- Dinner broadens the group's personal context. Zoe and Leo discover that Taiga is already taking Foundations of Computer Science, and Leo learns that he and Taiga are likely to share Data Structures next semester. Santo Domingo establishes Leo and Enzo's shared background without turning the dinner into biography delivery.
- The quieter train ride home gives Taiga and Leo their first substantial personal conversation. Halloween and the Camp Buddy photograph let Leo see part of Taiga's previous life; Taiga learns more about Leo's arrival at Olympus and the insecurity beneath his apparently easy social competence.
- The walk back deliberately becomes lighter again. Taiga and Leo argue about the arcade shooter rather than continuing to exchange important information. The fact that they keep talking after the group has already gone inside is itself the relationship progression.
- End with the understated goodbye. Leo using "Taiga" rather than "AG" may register to the reader, but Taiga's final response keeps the beat firmly inside his existing filter.

### Academic guardrail

Do **not** add a formal CS declaration simply to close the chapter's academic thread.

The important development is that Taiga's behavior is beginning to precede his labels: he keeps selecting CS courses and treating them as the subjects he actually wants, while still resisting the idea that this has to define his future.

The formal declaration can become a later-act payoff once Taiga has spent much longer repeatedly choosing Computer Science in practice. Likewise, the more personal **"Why did you choose CS?"** conversation with Leo should be saved for later, when questions of direction and future carry more weight.

### Leo / romance note

Chapter 7 plants only a fully deniable hint: Leo has a legitimate reason to text, keeps the conversation going after that reason is exhausted, and messages again later. Taiga treats this as normal / annoying and does not interrogate it.

Arcadia is the first point where the reader may reasonably infer that **Leo has started seeing Taiga differently**.

The progression should come through small, deniable choices rather than explicit flirting: Leo is openly pleased Taiga came, easily pulls him into games, works naturally with him at the shooter, pays attention to the Camp Buddy photograph, tells him he looked great, listens closely when Taiga talks about camp, and continues talking with him after the rest of the group has effectively peeled away.

Taiga does **not** interpret any of this romantically. He still considers Leo an attractive teammate / friend whose company he enjoys. No nervousness, fixation, "why did he say that?" spiral, stomach-drop reaction or special narrative gravity.

The brief shoulder contact at the shooter can register physically because attraction is already established, but another game target immediately breaks the moment. The chapter must not spend Chapter 13's destabilization.

## Chapter 9 — Home

### Dramatic role

Taiga returns to the people and world that were already home, discovers that they have continued growing without becoming less familiar, and returns to Olympus carrying that world with him rather than leaving it behind.

The youth clinic opens the chapter by letting Taiga use something Camp Buddy taught him inside Olympus. Hunter's exhibition then becomes the emotional center of the chapter: Taiga sees that Hunter has built an independent life at art school, while the Camp Buddy group still fits together naturally when reunited. The old world has moved forward without disappearing.

The chapter ends after Taiga returns to Olympus and opens Yuri's bag. The old Camp Buddy Halloween costume becomes the literal object he can carry from one life into the other.

### Movement

**I still belong there → home can keep changing without becoming less mine.**

### Current structure

- **Oct 29 — Olympus youth clinic.**
  - Pay off Leo's Chapter 7 invitation.
  - Taiga stays voluntarily and handles the kids naturally because of his Camp Buddy experience.
  - Matt remembers Forty-one.
  - Leo is openly pleased that Taiga followed through, but Taiga reads the interaction as ordinary teammate / friend familiarity.
- **Oct 30 — Hunter's exhibition.**
  - Taiga takes the long train trip and reunites with Keitaro, Hiro, Natsumi, Yoichi, Lee and Eduard.
  - Yoichi arrives carrying the unexplained bag Yuri sent for Taiga.
  - Hunter remains recognizably shy and nervous, but the exhibit also proves that he has built a life at school that exists independently of Camp Buddy.
  - Elena and the other art-school connections matter because they show Hunter has not been waiting in place for the old group to return.
  - The five-piece exhibit should keep the Camp Buddy shoreline / *Greatest Memories* piece and the hallway that strangers overinterpret as "fear of adulthood."
  - Dinner lets everyone exchange ordinary updates rather than turning the reunion into nostalgia alone.
  - Taiga can talk about Olympus, Apollo and Leo without feeling that doing so threatens his place in the old group.
  - Leo's message about the exhibition keeps Olympus present at the edge of the chapter without taking over it.
- **Oct 31 — return to Olympus.**
  - Taiga gets back around noon.
  - Yuri's bag is finally opened.
  - It contains the original Camp Buddy Halloween costume.
  - End with Taiga realizing there is enough time before the party, grabbing his keys and leaving to adapt it.

### Romance guardrail

Leo may be present through the clinic and through texting, but Chapter 9 is not a romantic escalation chapter.

Taiga can enjoy Leo's attention and answer him normally without becoming nervous, fixated or suspicious of romantic interest. The chapter's emotional center belongs to Taiga's relationship with his old home and the realization that distance has not erased it.

### Guardrail

Hunter's exhibition should remain the chapter's emotional center.

Do not treat the Camp Buddy reunion as proof that Olympus matters less. The point is not to choose one home over the other. The chapter should establish that the old one remains real even as everyone inside it continues changing.

## Chapter 10 — Away

### Dramatic role

Taiga is physically back at Olympus, but his old and new lives no longer exist in separate compartments. Olympus increasingly functions as another home.

Halloween brings together several parts of his current life at once: Maya and Caleb, Apollo, Nora, Zoe and Sofia. The Camp Buddy video call then places the old group directly inside that Olympus social space. Taiga does not need to stop one life in order to enter the other.

Aaron's attempt to ask Sofia out gives the chapter its immediate plot spine. Around that plot, Taiga moves through Olympus socially by choice, spends time alone with Leo without treating it as remarkable, and eventually gives Aaron advice drawn from his own difficult relationship with bluntness and other people's judgment.

### Movement

**Olympus is where I moved → Olympus is becoming another place I simply live and belong.**

Together, Chapters 9 and 10 form the section's clearest **Home / Away** mirror:

**I can go home without losing Olympus → I can return to Olympus without leaving home behind.**

### Current structure

- **Oct 31 — Olympus Halloween party.**
  - Open with the completed adaptation of the Camp Buddy costume. Maya and Caleb's reaction reveals to the reader what Taiga did after Chapter 9 cut away.
  - The Hunter hallway joke may return only as a compressed callback, not a replay of the exhibition scene.
  - Leo recognizes the costume immediately because he saw the original Camp Buddy photograph in Chapter 8.
  - Apollo's main comic spine is Aaron finally trying to ask Sofia out before she leaves for the girls' dorm party.
  - Nora, Zoe and Sofia join the same social space naturally; the chapter should feel like Taiga's Olympus circles overlapping rather than a cast roll call.
  - The wingman operation deliberately thins the group until Taiga and Leo are left together.
  - Their mystery-box, tarot and photo-booth sequence can take the shape of an accidental date without Taiga interpreting it romantically.
  - The tarot question is ostensibly about Aaron and Sofia, while the cards carry delayed resonance for Taiga and Leo: offer, release from self-imposed restriction, then conflict.
  - The Camp Buddy video call is the first direct crossover between Taiga's old group and the Olympus people currently around him. Enzo and Leo entering the call should make the two worlds collide comically rather than sentimentally.
  - Yoichi's "Dynamite" nickname becomes new ammunition for Leo and Enzo.
  - Aaron's repeated failures lead to the quieter Taiga / Aaron conversation. Taiga's advice should remain messy and character-specific rather than turning him into a source of polished wisdom.
  - Aaron finally runs after Sofia and gets the date: **Next Saturday.**
  - After the social climax, return to Taiga and Leo. Leo is unusually tired; Taiga notices without interrogating it. End on the "Dynamite" tease and Taiga shoving him into the elevator.

### Costume continuity note

The costume appears several times because each beat has a different job:

- Chapter 9 reveals Yuri returned the original pieces.

- Leo's reaction pays off the Chapter 8 photograph.
- Zoe's comment triggers the old-photo / revealing-costume joke that gets Taiga out of the group with Leo.

Do not add further explanations or admiration beats unless they serve a new function.

### Romance guardrail

Halloween comes **after Arcadia and after Hunter's exhibition**, so the reader is already allowed to suspect that Leo has started seeing Taiga differently.

Leo may seek Taiga out, remember details from the Camp Buddy photograph, follow him away from the group, wander booths with him, enter the photo and finish the night walking back beside him. Those choices can accumulate for the reader.

Taiga still does **not** interpret them romantically.

He already knows Leo is physically attractive and has consciously fantasized about him. That fact does not make ordinary interactions romantic in Taiga's mind. Do not introduce nervousness, jealousy, fixation, unusual body-awareness caused by Leo's attention, or any sense that Taiga is beginning to consciously suspect romantic interest.

Halloween should strengthen the reader's inference without spending Chapter 13's destabilization.

### Guardrail

The chapter's immediate story remains Aaron asking Sofia out. Taiga and Leo gain substantial relationship material around that spine, but they should not swallow the ensemble or turn the party into an undeclared romance scene.

The Home / Away split should remain visible in function: Chapter 9 preserves the reality of the old home; Chapter 10 demonstrates that Olympus is becoming home too.

## Chapter 11 — First Reserve Start

### Dramatic role

The concrete football payoff of Section I is not first-team selection. It is Taiga earning his **first start at any organized level**.

He began the semester without organized football experience, entered the developmental roster, earned reserve substitute minutes, survived the review and gradually became useful. Starting a reserve match is the believable culmination of that progression: Mercer now trusts him enough to include him in the initial plan rather than introducing him later once the match has already taken shape.

### Movement

**I'm useful enough to get minutes → I'm trusted enough to start.**

### Current likely anchors

- Mercer tells Taiga he will start for the reserves.
- The news matters because Taiga has never started an organized match before.
- The week can retain some anticipation, but it should not repeat the catastrophic review anxiety from Chapter 6.
- A Northbridge rematch is a strong candidate for the match because it directly contrasts with Taiga's first reserve appearance in Chapter 5.
- The first Northbridge match overwhelmed him; this time he recognizes the same kinds of spaces and problems earlier.
- He does not need a goal or heroic performance. A solid 60–70+ minutes, one useful attacking contribution and several correct ordinary decisions are enough.
- Mercer removes him because the match / his legs require it, not because he has failed.

### Guardrail

Do **not** give Taiga first-team minutes in Section I.

Three months of progress should make him a legitimate developmental / reserve player, not yet a first-team player. The next selection threshold remains available for Section II.

The football payoff should emphasize tactical growth, trust and repeatability rather than spectacle.

## Chapter 12 — New Baseline

### Dramatic role

The things that once felt new have become ordinary life.

Reserve football, Apollo, Nora, Caleb, Computer Science and Leo are no longer isolated novelties. Taiga no longer needs every training session or squad decision to prove that he belongs. After his first reserve start, he is simply one of the players Mercer can use there.

Olympus has become the baseline from which the next chapter can disturb something more personal.

### Movement

**Belonging here was the goal → belonging here is now the baseline.**

### Current likely anchors

- Taiga's first reserve start no longer needs to remain an event for the whole chapter.
- He may receive further reserve minutes or selection without treating each one as an existential judgment.
- Training continues with the same Apollo squad structure already established; do not invent separate "senior training sessions."
- House Apollo functions as somewhere he can simply study and exist around other people.
- CS, Nora, Caleb, football and Apollo coexist without every scene needing to prove progress.
- The first-team threshold remains visible only as a future possibility, not as a milestone that must be teased or reached before break.

### Romantic guardrail

Do **not** use Leo's "sometimes I forget there was a version of Olympus where you weren't here yet" line here.

Chapter 11 may show that Leo is part of Taiga's ordinary life, but it should not yet break Taiga's compartment. Avoid obvious nervousness, intrusive thoughts or repeated tracking of Leo.

The purpose is to establish a stable baseline immediately before Chapter 13 disrupts it.

## Chapter 13 — Disruption / Act I Ending

### Dramatic role

Taiga has finally built an ordinary life at Olympus. The final chapter of Section I disturbs exactly one part of that stability: **Leo**.

Finals end. Campus empties. Taiga is leaving for Camp Buddy the next morning. Leo specifically asks him to spend the evening at the Acropolis winter rink.

The outing begins as another voluntary social choice and ends as the first experience Taiga cannot comfortably reduce to "Leo is hot; that explains enough."

### Movement

**Leo is an attractive friend inside my normal life → something about this no longer fits the explanation I was using.**

### Current likely structure

- Finals finish and campus begins emptying for break.
- Caleb and the others leave; Taiga is due to travel to Camp Buddy the next morning.
- Leo privately invites Taiga to the Acropolis rink.
- The evening is one-on-one and playful rather than explicitly romantic.
- On the ice, Leo nearly falls; Taiga catches him and they go down together. The physical closeness affects **both** of them.
- Taiga may initially explain his own reaction through already-established attraction.
- Hot chocolate afterward shifts the interaction into more personal territory.
- Taiga hears Leo speaking Spanish with his mother and learns more about his family / home life.
- Taiga talks more openly about Camp Buddy, why he stayed, why he eventually left, and the difference between belonging somewhere and being trapped there.
- Only **after the rink and the deeper conversation**, Leo says some version of:

> "Sometimes I forget there was a version of Olympus where you weren't here yet."

- Leo may become embarrassed or call the line corny, but should not retract it so completely that Taiga can dismiss the sincerity.
- They separate.
- Taiga returns to the mostly empty dorm, packs for Camp Buddy, and the sentence returns despite his attempts to file it away.

### Romantic guardrail

This is the **first true destabilization of Taiga's compartment**, not the moment he solves his feelings.

Do not have him conclude "I have a crush on Leo." The important result is loss of explanatory certainty.

The reader has already been allowed to suspect Leo's interest since Arcadia. The rink gives the reader stronger evidence that Leo is affected physically and personally too. Taiga remains behind the reader.

### Section-ending target

By the end of Chapter 13:

- Olympus is no longer provisional.
- Taiga's Computer Science direction is established through repeated choices, even though he remains formally undeclared.
- Apollo is a social group, not merely a team.
- Camp Buddy and Olympus can coexist.
- Taiga has progressed from developmental newcomer to a legitimate reserve starter without being prematurely pushed into first-team football.
- Caleb, Nora, Maya and the Apollo cast are part of ordinary life.
- Leo is the first element of that ordinary life that has become difficult to categorize.
- Taiga leaves for Camp Buddy with the question unresolved.

**Section II should open directly at Camp Buddy.**

# How to use this file during rewriting

Before restructuring a chapter, ask:

1. What is this chapter's current dramatic job?
2. Does the proposed scene advance that job, complicate it, or merely repeat the previous chapter?
3. Would the scene become more useful if moved earlier or later?
4. Does moving it preserve Draft continuity and the Section I progression?
5. Is the chapter still producing a distinct change in Taiga by its end?

If a stronger structure emerges during the rewrite, **change this map**. The map exists to support the novel, not to force the novel to obey an outdated plan.
