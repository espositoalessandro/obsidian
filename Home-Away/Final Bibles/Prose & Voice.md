# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Prose & Voice Bible

This file defines the **target prose and narrative voice for the final rewrite**.

It does not describe what the draft already does consistently. It defines what the finished novel should do.

---

# 1. Core Tone

**Grounded anime naturalism.**

Home/Away should combine:

- anime-level character distinction;
- novel-level human behavior;
- ordinary, physical, lived-in environments;
- simple, precise prose;
- emotionally specific scenes without constant melodrama.

Characters may be colorful, expressive and immediately recognizable without becoming caricatures.

The world itself should remain grounded.

---

# 2. Narrative Perspective

The novel uses **close third person filtered through Taiga**.

The narration should not merely report what Taiga sees.

It should reflect:

- what he notices;
- what irritates him;
- what he finds funny;
- what he dismisses;
- what he misunderstands;
- what he avoids thinking about;
- what becomes important to him over time.

The narrative camera belongs to Taiga.

Taiga does not need to consciously formulate every narrative observation. Close-third narration may express his instincts, judgments and associations more cleanly than he would say them aloud, but it must remain plausible as **his way of perceiving the situation**.

The narration must not become an omniscient emotional diagnosis of other characters.

---

# 3. Taiga's Narrative Voice

## Core rule

**The narration follows Taiga's growth.**

His character development changes the way he interprets the world.

It does not replace his voice with a neutral narrator.

The broad movement is:

> **hostile observer → reluctant participant → invested participant → someone who has built a life with other people and knows it**

This progression is gradual and non-linear.

Stress, fear, jealousy, humiliation or genuine anger may temporarily bring older defensive habits back.

Growth does not make Taiga immune to regression.

---

## 3.1 Skepticism → Trust

Early Taiga is inclined to assume:

- inconvenience;
- incompetence;
- ulterior motives;
- disappointment;
- that something will probably go wrong.

He does not easily give strangers the benefit of the doubt.

As trust develops, he becomes increasingly willing to take people at face value and to believe that kindness, concern or interest may simply be genuine.

He should never become gullible.

The change is that suspicion stops being his automatic first defense.

---

## 3.2 Detachment → Investment

Early Taiga often experiences other people as:

- noise;
- obstacles;
- interruptions;
- people whose problems are not his problem.

He can participate socially while still maintaining emotional distance.

Over time, other people's moods, routines, needs and problems begin to matter to him even when they have no immediate effect on him.

By the later novel he is not simply surrounded by people.

He is **invested in them**.

---

## 3.3 Judgment → Revision

Taiga should remain quick to form opinions.

That is part of his voice.

The growth is not that he stops judging.

It is that he becomes more capable of revising the judgment.

Early Taiga may defend his first interpretation long after contrary evidence appears.

Later Taiga is increasingly capable of admitting, internally or aloud:

- that he misunderstood someone;
- that something was more complicated than he thought;
- that somebody had a point;
- that he was wrong.

Do not remove his strong opinions in order to make him mature.

Make him better at updating them.

---

## 3.4 Deflection → Acknowledgment

Early Taiga often responds to uncomfortable emotion by:

- minimizing it;
- explaining it away;
- becoming irritated;
- turning it into sarcasm;
- focusing on a practical problem instead;
- refusing to name what is obvious.

As he grows, he becomes increasingly able to recognize:

- affection;
- jealousy;
- worry;
- fear;
- loneliness;
- wanting company;
- wanting reassurance;
- missing someone.

This does not mean constant introspection.

The later change is often simply that the thought is allowed to exist without an immediate internal argument.

---

## 3.5 Control → Tolerance of Uncertainty

Early Taiga prefers things that can be understood, defined or solved.

Ambiguity easily irritates him.

He wants to know:

- what somebody means;
- where he stands;
- what the plan is;
- what the problem is;
- what he is supposed to do about it.

Over time he becomes more capable of allowing relationships and situations to remain temporarily unresolved.

He does not need to classify every feeling immediately.

He does not need every uncomfortable situation to produce an actionable answer.

---

## 3.6 Threat-Oriented Attention → Relational Attention

Early Taiga naturally notices:

- noise;
- crowding;
- incompetence;
- mistakes;
- disruptions;
- things getting in his way;
- potential conflict.

These details help establish how guarded he is.

As he becomes more connected to people, his attention changes.

He increasingly notices:

- familiar routines;
- when somebody is tired;
- what somebody likes;
- subtle changes in behavior;
- whether someone has eaten;
- what makes somebody comfortable;
- small expressions he has learned to read;
- practical needs before they are spoken.

This is one of the strongest ways to show his growth without explaining it.

His world becomes populated by people rather than merely containing them.

---

## 3.7 Selective Vulnerability → Broader Trust

Taiga is **not emotionally incapable of opening up at the beginning of the novel**.

He already has people with whom he can lower his defenses, especially **Keitaro and Yoshinori**.

In the right circumstances, he can also be vulnerable with other members of the Camp Buddy group.

The important distinctions are that Taiga:

- opens up badly and uncomfortably;
- avoids doing it when he can;
- often needs prompting, pressure or a situation he can no longer evade;
- is extremely selective about who is allowed access;
- may still use sarcasm, irritation or bluntness while saying something genuinely vulnerable.

His growth is therefore **not learning how to be vulnerable for the first time**.

It is learning that emotional safety does not belong only to the small group of people who have already earned years of trust.

Across the novel, the circle gradually widens.

Early Taiga may allow Keitaro or Yoshinori to see something he would immediately hide from an Olympus friend.

Later, people such as Caleb and especially Leo begin receiving access that would once have been reserved for Camp Buddy.

Even then, Taiga should never become broadly confessional or effortlessly open.

Vulnerability remains something he gives **selectively**, and usually without elegance.

The later change is that he needs less defensive work to admit what is already true.

He becomes more willing to:

- let a thought finish;
- say what he wants;
- ask for reassurance;
- acknowledge fear;
- acknowledge affection;
- trust that doing so will not automatically cost him control.

His barriers become easier to lower.

They do not disappear.

**Taiga behaving openly with Keitaro or Yoshinori early in the novel does not mean his defenses are already weak. It means they are already inside the wall.**

---

## 3.8 Anger → Emotional Differentiation

Taiga should become less prone to anger across the novel.

He should never become immune to it.

Early on, multiple uncomfortable states may be experienced or expressed as irritation:

- embarrassment;
- fear;
- insecurity;
- overstimulation;
- helplessness;
- jealousy;
- feeling exposed.

As his emotional awareness improves, anger occupies less territory.

He becomes slower to interpret every form of discomfort as something to fight.

When Taiga is genuinely angry, however, it should remain unmistakable.

Growth gives him more responses.

It does not remove anger from his personality.

---

## 3.9 Bluntness → Better-Aimed Bluntness

Taiga remains blunt throughout the novel.

Early Taiga is more likely to say the first true thing that occurs to him regardless of whether saying it is useful.

Later he becomes more aware of:

- timing;
- intent;
- consequences;
- whether honesty is actually helping.

His bluntness softens, but it should never disappear into diplomacy.

He still says things other people might avoid saying.

He simply gets better at deciding **when, why and how** to say them.

---

## 3.10 Defensive Sarcasm → Participatory Sarcasm

Sarcasm remains one of Taiga's most stable traits.

Its function changes.

Early sarcasm frequently creates distance.

It protects him, dismisses something, or keeps another person from getting too close.

Later sarcasm is increasingly:

- affectionate;
- familiar;
- playful;
- a way of participating in a relationship.

The surface form may remain almost identical while the emotional purpose changes.

Do not mistake increasing warmth for decreasing sarcasm.

---

## 3.11 Affection Stays Taiga-Shaped

Warmth should not make Taiga's narration sentimental by default.

He tends to care through:

- noticing;
- remembering;
- fixing;
- bringing;
- carrying;
- showing up;
- making something easier;
- practical protection;
- physical proximity;
- small acts he does not announce.

His internal language may become more honest about affection without becoming lyrical.

A later Taiga can know that he loves someone and still think that person is an idiot for forgetting their water.

Those ideas are not contradictory.

They are part of his voice.

---

## 3.12 Stable Traits

Character growth should not sand Taiga down into generic maturity.

The following should remain recognizable throughout:

- sarcasm;
- competitiveness;
- strong opinions;
- practical thinking;
- impatience with stupidity;
- bluntness;
- profanity;
- pride;
- a tendency to express care through action;
- the capacity for anger.

The proportions and functions change.

The underlying person remains Taiga.

---

## 3.13 Short Internal Reactions

Fragments such as:

- "Of course."
- "Great."
- "Perfect."
- "Obviously."

are valid Taiga tools.

They are **not** the definition of his POV.

Do not restore weak later narration by mechanically inserting them.

The stronger test is whether the sentence, observation or description could plausibly have been selected and framed by Taiga.

---

# 4. Prose Style

Prefer:

- clarity;
- concrete detail;
- strong sentence rhythm;
- plain vocabulary used deliberately;
- physical staging;
- specific observation;
- selective interiority.

Avoid polishing the novel into ornamental literary prose.

Taiga is not a lyrical narrator by default.

The final rewrite should become **more authored, not more decorative**.

---

# 5. Emotional Writing

Big emotional moments should usually become simpler rather than more poetic.

Trust:

- behavior;
- silence;
- interruption;
- physical action;
- changes in speech;
- what a character cannot say.

Avoid explaining emotions already visible in the scene.

Taiga may understand more over time, but the narration should not become therapist-like merely because he has matured.

Self-awareness and polished emotional analysis are not the same thing.

---

# 6. Dialogue Philosophy

Dialogue should preserve heightened character distinction while remaining believable.

Characters may have anime-influenced:

- rhythms;
- verbal habits;
- reactions;
- comedic timing;
- expressive tendencies.

But quirks are seasoning, not identity.

A character should remain recognizable even when their usual verbal quirk is absent.

---

# 7. Character Distinction Rule

**Anime-level character distinction; novel-level human behavior.**

Recurring characters should differ through:

- sentence construction;
- vocabulary;
- social instinct;
- humor;
- emotional behavior;
- what they notice;
- how they fail under pressure.

Do not give the whole cast the same dry, competent, mildly sarcastic register.

---

# 8. Rewrite Audit Questions

During every chapter rewrite, check:

- Does the narration sound like Taiga?
- Is Taiga's POV present without becoming repetitive?
- Are descriptions filtered through his attention?
- What is Taiga paying attention to at this point in his development?
- Is an early defensive habit appearing because it belongs here, or merely because it sounds like Taiga?
- Could another recurring character have delivered this line unchanged?
- Is the prose simple because it is precise, or simple because it is generic?
- Is an emotion being explained after it has already been shown?
- Is later Taiga becoming warmer without becoming neutral or sentimental?
- If Taiga regresses under stress, does the regression make sense for the trigger?

---

# 9. Open Questions

To define during the rewrite:

- exact narrative-distance limits;
- frequency of free-indirect fragments;
- section-by-section calibration of Taiga's growth;
- balance between humor and seriousness;
- Taiga's attraction/physical gaze and how it changes with intimacy;
- sensory priorities and recurring descriptive habits;
- how much anime-style exaggeration is appropriate for each cast group.
