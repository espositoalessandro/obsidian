# Home/Away — Final Rewrite Prose & Voice Bible

This file defines the **target prose and narrative voice for the final rewrite**.

It does not describe what the draft already does consistently. It defines what the finished novel should do.

---

# 1. Core Tone

**Grounded anime naturalism.**

Home/Away should combine:

- anime-level character distinction;
- novel-level human behavior;
- ordinary, physical, lived-in environments;
- simple, precise prose;
- emotionally specific scenes without constant melodrama.

Characters may be colorful, expressive and immediately recognizable without becoming caricatures.

The world itself should remain grounded.

---

# 2. Narrative Perspective

The novel uses **close third person filtered through Taiga**.

The narration should not merely report what Taiga sees.

It should reflect:

- what he notices;
- what irritates him;
- what he finds funny;
- what he dismisses;
- what he misunderstands;
- what he avoids thinking about;
- what becomes important to him over time.

The narrative camera belongs to Taiga.

Taiga does not need to consciously formulate every narrative observation. Close-third narration may express his instincts, judgments and associations more cleanly than he would say them aloud, but it must remain plausible as **his way of perceiving the situation**.

The narration should use a **mix of direct thought and biased external narration**.

Direct thought may appear as:

- *Why the fuck did they have to do it this way?*
- *Of course.*
- *Great.*

Biased external narration may instead frame the world through Taiga without quoting a thought:

- The room was way too crowded for his tastes.
- The explanation took three times longer than it needed to.
- Leo had apparently decided that one bag was not enough.

Both modes are valid.

Do not force every subjective judgment into an italicized thought, and do not make external narration neutral when Taiga's bias would naturally color it.

The narration must not become an omniscient emotional diagnosis of other characters.

## 2.1 Free indirect style and grammatical elasticity

Because the narration is tightly filtered through Taiga, **free indirect style may deliberately inherit the shortcuts, fragments, omissions and roughness of his internal language**.

This means that a sentence does not always need to be grammatically complete if the imperfection makes the perception feel more immediate, natural or recognizably Taiga.

Examples of acceptable effects include:

- omitted verbs or subjects when the meaning remains obvious;
- compressed or fragmentary description;
- thought-shaped syntax that would be awkward in neutral narration;
- abrupt changes in sentence structure under stress, irritation or sensory overload;
- colloquial constructions that sound like Taiga mentally sizing something up rather than a narrator formally describing it.

Do **not** automatically "correct" these during grammar passes merely because a neutral narrator would phrase them differently.

The test is:

> **Does the imperfection strengthen Taiga's lived perception without making the sentence genuinely confusing?**

If yes, preserve it.

Grammar remains important for ordinary narration, dialogue clarity and accidental errors. This rule is not permission for random mistakes; it is permission to let **voice outrank formal correctness when the effect is clearly intentional and readable**.

---

# 3. Taiga's Narrative Voice

## Core rule

**The narration follows Taiga's growth.**

His character development changes the way he interprets the world.

It does not replace his voice with a neutral narrator.

The broad movement is:

> **hostile observer → reluctant participant → invested participant → someone who has built a life with other people and knows it**

This progression is gradual and non-linear.

Stress, fear, jealousy, humiliation or genuine anger may temporarily bring older defensive habits back.

Growth does not make Taiga immune to regression.

---

## 3.1 Skepticism → Trust

Early Taiga is inclined to assume:

- inconvenience;
- incompetence;
- ulterior motives;
- disappointment;
- that something will probably go wrong.

He does not easily give strangers the benefit of the doubt.

As trust develops, he becomes increasingly willing to take people at face value and to believe that kindness, concern or interest may simply be genuine.

He should never become gullible.

The change is that suspicion stops being his automatic first defense.

---

## 3.2 Detachment → Investment

Early Taiga often experiences other people as:

- noise;
- obstacles;
- interruptions;
- people whose problems are not his problem.

He can participate socially while still maintaining emotional distance.

Over time, other people's moods, routines, needs and problems begin to matter to him even when they have no immediate effect on him.

By the later novel he is not simply surrounded by people.

He is **invested in them**.

---

## 3.3 Judgment → Revision

Taiga should remain quick to form opinions.

That is part of his voice.

The growth is not that he stops judging.

It is that he becomes more capable of revising the judgment.

Early Taiga may defend his first interpretation long after contrary evidence appears.

Later Taiga is increasingly capable of admitting, internally or aloud:

- that he misunderstood someone;
- that something was more complicated than he thought;
- that somebody had a point;
- that he was wrong.

Do not remove his strong opinions in order to make him mature.

Make him better at updating them.

---

## 3.4 Deflection → Acknowledgment

Early Taiga often responds to uncomfortable emotion by:

- minimizing it;
- explaining it away;
- becoming irritated;
- turning it into sarcasm;
- focusing on a practical problem instead;
- refusing to name what is obvious.

As he grows, he becomes increasingly able to recognize:

- affection;
- jealousy;
- worry;
- fear;
- loneliness;
- wanting company;
- wanting reassurance;
- missing someone.

This does not mean constant introspection.

The later change is often simply that the thought is allowed to exist without an immediate internal argument.

---

## 3.5 Control → Tolerance of Uncertainty

Early Taiga prefers things that can be understood, defined or solved.

Ambiguity easily irritates him.

He wants to know:

- what somebody means;
- where he stands;
- what the plan is;
- what the problem is;
- what he is supposed to do about it.

Over time he becomes more capable of allowing relationships and situations to remain temporarily unresolved.

He does not need to classify every feeling immediately.

He does not need every uncomfortable situation to produce an actionable answer.

---

## 3.6 Threat-Oriented Attention → Relational Attention

Early Taiga naturally notices:

- noise;
- crowding;
- incompetence;
- mistakes;
- disruptions;
- things getting in his way;
- potential conflict.

These details help establish how guarded he is.

As he becomes more connected to people, his attention changes.

He increasingly notices:

- familiar routines;
- when somebody is tired;
- what somebody likes;
- subtle changes in behavior;
- whether someone has eaten;
- what makes somebody comfortable;
- small expressions he has learned to read;
- practical needs before they are spoken.

This is one of the strongest ways to show his growth without explaining it.

His world becomes populated by people rather than merely containing them.

---

## 3.7 Selective Vulnerability → Broader Trust

Taiga is **not emotionally incapable of opening up at the beginning of the novel**.

He already has people with whom he can lower his defenses, especially **Keitaro and Yoshinori**.

In the right circumstances, he can also be vulnerable with other members of the Camp Buddy group.

The important distinctions are that Taiga:

- opens up badly and uncomfortably;
- avoids doing it when he can;
- often needs prompting, pressure or a situation he can no longer evade;
- is extremely selective about who is allowed access;
- may still use sarcasm, irritation or bluntness while saying something genuinely vulnerable.

His growth is therefore **not learning how to be vulnerable for the first time**.

It is learning that emotional safety does not belong only to the small group of people who have already earned years of trust.

Across the novel, the circle gradually widens.

Early Taiga may allow Keitaro or Yoshinori to see something he would immediately hide from an Olympus friend.

Later, people such as Caleb and especially Leo begin receiving access that would once have been reserved for Camp Buddy.

Even then, Taiga should never become broadly confessional or effortlessly open.

Vulnerability remains something he gives **selectively**, and usually without elegance.

The later change is that he needs less defensive work to admit what is already true.

He becomes more willing to:

- let a thought finish;
- say what he wants;
- ask for reassurance;
- acknowledge fear;
- acknowledge affection;
- trust that doing so will not automatically cost him control.

His barriers become easier to lower.

They do not disappear.

**Taiga behaving openly with Keitaro or Yoshinori early in the novel does not mean his defenses are already weak. It means they are already inside the wall.**

---

## 3.8 Anger → Emotional Differentiation

Taiga should become less prone to anger across the novel.

He should never become immune to it.

Early on, multiple uncomfortable states may be experienced or expressed as irritation:

- embarrassment;
- fear;
- insecurity;
- overstimulation;
- helplessness;
- jealousy;
- feeling exposed.

As his emotional awareness improves, anger occupies less territory.

He becomes slower to interpret every form of discomfort as something to fight.

When Taiga is genuinely angry, however, it should remain unmistakable.

Growth gives him more responses.

It does not remove anger from his personality.

---

## 3.9 Bluntness → Better-Aimed Bluntness

Taiga remains blunt throughout the novel.

Early Taiga is more likely to say the first true thing that occurs to him regardless of whether saying it is useful.

Later he becomes more aware of:

- timing;
- intent;
- consequences;
- whether honesty is actually helping.

His bluntness softens, but it should never disappear into diplomacy.

He still says things other people might avoid saying.

He simply gets better at deciding **when, why and how** to say them.

---

## 3.10 Defensive Sarcasm → Participatory Sarcasm

Sarcasm remains one of Taiga's most stable traits.

Its function changes.

Early sarcasm frequently creates distance.

It protects him, dismisses something, or keeps another person from getting too close.

Later sarcasm is increasingly:

- affectionate;
- familiar;
- playful;
- a way of participating in a relationship.

The surface form may remain almost identical while the emotional purpose changes.

Do not mistake increasing warmth for decreasing sarcasm.

---

## 3.11 Affection Stays Taiga-Shaped

Warmth should not make Taiga's narration sentimental by default.

He tends to care through:

- noticing;
- remembering;
- fixing;
- bringing;
- carrying;
- showing up;
- making something easier;
- practical protection;
- physical proximity;
- small acts he does not announce.

His internal language may become more honest about affection without becoming lyrical.

A later Taiga can know that he loves someone and still think that person is an idiot for forgetting their water.

Those ideas are not contradictory.

They are part of his voice.

---

## 3.12 Stable Traits

Character growth should not sand Taiga down into generic maturity.

The following should remain recognizable throughout:

- sarcasm;
- competitiveness;
- strong opinions;
- practical thinking;
- impatience with stupidity;
- bluntness;
- profanity;
- pride;
- a tendency to express care through action;
- the capacity for anger.

The proportions and functions change.

The underlying person remains Taiga.

---

## 3.13 Attraction and Physical Attention

Taiga may notice physical attraction directly.

Early in the novel, he is more likely to:

- notice a body, face, voice, gesture or physical detail;
- react to it;
- then dismiss, minimize or redirect the thought.

For Leo, preserve a strict distinction between **sexual attraction** and **personal romantic charge**.

The Section I progression is:

- **Chapter 3:** first unmistakable physical noticing. Taiga registers that Leo has a nice body; this does not yet become a larger question.
- **Chapter 5:** the attraction becomes undeniable. The second shower, Zoe's comment, the dream and Taiga's conscious awake fantasy establish that he is sexually attracted to Leo.
- **Early Chapter 6:** Taiga settles on a deliberately simple interpretation. In substance: **Leo is hot. That explained enough.** The text may state this once, briefly and Taiga-shaped, so the reader understands that Chapter 5 has not been forgotten.
- **Chapters 6–10:** do **not** turn that admission into nervousness, fixation, intrusive thoughts or special narrative gravity. Taiga can know Leo is hot and still behave normally around him. He does not start seeking Leo out with his attention, getting flustered by his presence, or wondering what every interaction means. The attraction is compartmentalized and, from Taiga's perspective, adequately explained.
- **Chapter 11:** Halloween has improved the friendship, and Leo may by now have a genuine crush on Taiga. The reader may see that through a few small choices, but the relationship should not suddenly become constant. Leo does not orbit Taiga or become his automatic companion; that stronger social-default pattern belongs to Act II. Taiga reads the increased warmth as friendship and remains oblivious.
- **Chapter 12:** the compartment breaks through a **progression**, not one magical touch. Leo takes both of Taiga's hands to teach him to skate; the contact begins as practical and goes unnoticed while Taiga is busy balancing. Once Taiga has enough spare attention to realize they are still holding hands, he becomes genuinely nervous and ends the contact himself. Minutes later, Taiga cannot stop quickly enough after warning Leo about another skater and collides into him; Leo catches him in a standing embrace. Their faces remain centimeters apart, both flush, and Taiga abruptly notices details of Leo's face he has no practical reason to catalogue. He files the moment as awkward rather than diagnosing a crush.
- **After the Chapter 12 turn:** the narration may begin snagging on Leo in a more personal way. Physical details, presence, absence and interaction can now carry emotional weight. The final separation before break adds a second kind of evidence: three weeks without Leo feels longer than Taiga expects.

Do not spend the Chapter 12 token early.

The reader should be able to distinguish:

> **Leo is hot.**

from:

> **Leo matters to me in a way I have to think about.**

The first is established by Chapter 5 and consciously filed away in Chapter 6. The second begins later.

As Taiga's attraction eventually becomes personally acknowledged, the **dismissal should gradually disappear**. Later Taiga does not need to argue with himself every time he notices Leo's body, mouth, hands, the way clothes fit him, sweat after training, physical closeness or other concrete details.

This should remain:

- physical;
- specific;
- matter-of-fact;
- recognizably Taiga.

It should not become purple, reverent or voyeuristic by default.

The development is not simply that Taiga notices more.

It is that the **meaning attached to the noticing changes**.

---

## 3.14 Short Internal Reactions

Fragments such as:

- "Of course."
- "Great."
- "Perfect."
- "Obviously."

are valid Taiga tools.

They are **not** the definition of his POV.

Do not restore weak later narration by mechanically inserting them.

The stronger test is whether the sentence, observation or description could plausibly have been selected and framed by Taiga.

---

# 4. Taiga Voice Progression by Section

This map is calibrated from the completed Draft.

It is not a rigid slider. Individual scenes may move backward or forward depending on stress, familiarity and who Taiga is with.

The purpose is to prevent the final rewrite from applying one static version of Taiga across the entire novel.

---

## Section I — Forty-One

### Narrative state
**Guarded newcomer who is already trying to be better.**

Taiga does not arrive at Olympus as the same person he was before Camp Buddy.

He already has:

- some self-control;
- established trust with the Camp Buddy cast;
- awareness that his anger can hurt people;
- the ability to stop himself before every irritation becomes a confrontation.

The opening voice should therefore not read as an origin story for emotional growth.

It is the voice of someone who has already changed once and does not entirely trust that change yet.

### Dominant narration

Strongest traits:

- skeptical;
- blunt;
- defensive;
- judgmental;
- threat-oriented;
- easily irritated by noise, crowds, inefficiency and social intrusion;
- strongly biased external description;
- frequent short direct thoughts;
- quick sarcasm.

Olympus is initially evaluated from the outside.

Taiga notices what is:

- loud;
- badly organized;
- inconvenient;
- unnecessary;
- expensive;
- socially exhausting.

His first instinct is often rejection.

### Social position

Camp Buddy is already inside the wall.

Olympus is not.

With Keitaro, Yoshinori and sometimes the rest of the Camp Buddy group, Taiga may already show vulnerability that he would never offer a new Olympus acquaintance.

This contrast is important.

The question in Section I is not whether Taiga can care about people.

It is whether **new people can become important without him deciding that this is dangerous**.

### Emerging change

Across the section:

- Caleb becomes easy to exist around;
- Nora becomes someone Taiga actively engages with;
- football stops being something provisional and becomes something he wants;
- Leo becomes physically attractive to Taiga before that attraction becomes personally meaningful.

The narration should begin allowing small positive observations without immediately turning every one into sentiment.

Taiga may still dismiss them.

But the world is already becoming less hostile.

### Attraction

Attraction to Leo appears before Taiga accepts it as personally meaningful.

Physical reactions may be:

- noticed;
- isolated;
- rationalized;
- dismissed as situational.

By the end of Chapter 5, Taiga can no longer honestly treat the attraction itself as accidental. Chapter 6 should therefore give him one brief conscious conclusion — effectively **Leo is hot; that explains enough** — and then let him move on.

From Chapters 6–10, compartmentalization is stable rather than anxious. Taiga should not become newly nervous around Leo merely because he has admitted the physical attraction. Their ordinary banter and teammate/friend dynamic can continue without romantic self-surveillance.

Chapter 11 can make the friendship visibly warmer after Halloween. Leo may have a real crush by then, and the reader may catch it through a few deniable choices, but do not accelerate frequency so far that Leo becomes Taiga's constant companion. The stronger default-pair dynamic belongs to Act II. Taiga still reads the relationship as friendship.

The key early mechanism is not ignorance.

It is **compartmentalization**.

Chapter 12 is the break in that compartment. The rink should use **two escalating physical beats rather than a meaningful line**. First, Leo takes both of Taiga's hands to teach him to skate. The contact begins as neutral and useful; Taiga only becomes nervous once he is competent enough to notice that they are still holding hands, and he ends it himself. Later, Taiga cannot stop quickly enough and runs into Leo after Leo avoids another skater. Leo catches him in a standing embrace; both boys flush at the close distance, and Taiga notices unusually specific details of Leo's face before pulling away. He can call that second beat awkward. What he cannot fully explain is why harmless hand-holding made him nervous, why the embrace registered so intensely, or why the prospect of three weeks without Leo bothers him at the end of the night. The point is not to reveal a crush to Taiga. It is to make his old explanation — **Leo is hot; that explains enough** — stop explaining everything.

### Anger and regression

Anger remains close to the surface.

However, even in Chapter 1, Taiga can interrupt the old reflex before acting on it.

That self-interruption is part of the baseline.

### Final-section calibration

By the end of Section I, Taiga should feel:

- less like Olympus is temporary;
- more invested in football;
- more accustomed to Caleb and Nora;
- increasingly aware that other people know and notice him;
- uncomfortable with how much that matters.

He is not warm yet.

He is **participating**.

## Section II — Past Mistakes

### Narrative state
**Awareness turns into recognition.**

Chapter 12 has already shown Taiga that Leo no longer fits cleanly inside "hot friend."

Section II is where Taiga spends months trying to manage that knowledge before finally admitting the obvious.

The central contradiction is:

> **when Taiga notices the pattern, he pulls back; when he acts on what he actually wants, he moves closer.**

### Dominant narration

Compared with Section I:

- less blanket hostility toward Olympus;
- more relational attention;
- more awareness of Leo's presence and absence;
- more sensitivity to touch, eye contact and one-on-one situations;
- more attention to whether Leo is expected somewhere;
- stronger discomfort when other people can see the same pattern Taiga has begun seeing privately.

Taiga's new nervousness is **situational**, not a replacement personality.

He can still joke, train, argue, study and text with Leo normally.

The nervousness activates when something resembles the unresolved Chapter 12 question:
- prolonged touch;
- close physical proximity;
- private / intimate conversation;
- consciously choosing Leo;
- realizing he has changed plans because Leo is involved;
- somebody else naming their closeness.

### Push / pull

Taiga's behavior should repeatedly produce the contradiction before he can resolve it.

He may:
- create distance after a charged moment;
- then notice Leo's absence immediately;
- decide not to make things weird;
- then check whether Leo has messaged;
- tell himself an outing is optional;
- then become interested once Leo says yes;
- resent how visible the pattern feels;
- then seek Leo out when he actually needs someone.

Do not make this a repetitive cycle in every chapter.

The kind of evidence should change.

### Longing before admission

Section II should make absence meaningful before Taiga is ready to call the feeling romantic.

Rooms can feel different when Leo is not there.

Plans can become more attractive because Leo is attending.

Taiga can want to tell Leo something before noticing he has selected Leo as the person to tell.

This is **longing as behavior**, not poetic yearning.

### Pinetree rupture

By Pinetree, other people are naming a pattern Taiga has already privately noticed.

That is why outside attention is threatening.

The fear is not:

> "What? That's ridiculous."

At a deeper level it is:

> **"Fuck. You can see it too."**

Taiga tries to restore the old category by creating distance.

That choice hurts Leo and exposes the cost of using rejection as self-protection.

Leo's boundary remains essential: Taiga may be afraid or confused, but he cannot turn Leo's care into something shameful and then expect Leo to keep absorbing the push / pull.

### Repair and realization

The repair must not reset them to "friends who still have no idea what is happening."

By the end of Section II, Taiga should understand his own desire.

A line in the working spirit of Leo saying:

> **"I don't want to change wherever we were going."**

forces the issue away from labels and toward direction.

Taiga does not need to be ready to call Leo his boyfriend.

He does need to recognize:

- he likes Leo romantically;
- he wants more than ordinary friendship;
- the movement between them was real;
- he wants that movement to continue.

The key Section II result is:

> **I want to keep moving toward there.**

### Final-section calibration

By the end of Section II, Taiga should feel:

- consciously aware that he likes Leo as more than a friend;
- aware that nervousness, longing and preference were all part of the same pattern;
- more cautious about what defensive anger can damage;
- willing to let the relationship keep moving forward;
- still frightened by definition and commitment;
- not yet ready to make a formal relationship claim.

His defenses remain.

What disappears is plausible deniability about **his own desire**.

## Section III — Moving On

### Narrative state
**Known desire becomes action.**

Section III is not a second recognition act.

Taiga already knows by the end of Section II that he likes Leo and wants more.

The section begins from:

> **I know I want him. Now we keep moving toward each other.**

The remaining uncertainty concerns:
- intent;
- boundaries;
- expectations;
- what kinds of intimacy they are willing to make deliberate;
- how much meaning can exist before either boy wants a formal label.

### Dominant narration

Compared with Section II:

- less denial about why Leo matters;
- less surprise at preference;
- more direct physical and emotional attention;
- more awareness of intentional choices;
- less need to explain away every moment of longing;
- stronger sensitivity to whether Leo means a touch / invitation / reaction the same way Taiga does.

Taiga can still become embarrassed or defensive.

What he should not do is reset to:

> "Why am I noticing Leo?"

He knows why.

### Attraction

Physical attraction has been consciously known since Chapter 5.

Section III changes its **context**, not its existence.

Now that Taiga has admitted he likes Leo, ordinary body awareness becomes harder to quarantine from romantic meaning.

The useful question is no longer:

> "Am I attracted to him?"

It becomes:

> **"Did he mean to do that?"**

Taiga may look away, get flustered or avoid escalating a moment, but the narration should not pretend his own attraction is mysterious.

### Deliberate choice

Taiga's invitation to Camp Buddy must be conscious.

He is not accidentally choosing Leo and understanding it later.

He knows:
- he wants Leo there;
- inviting him into Camp Buddy is personally meaningful;
- Leo is not interchangeable with another friend.

When Taiga later says, in substance:

> **"I knew what I was doing when I invited you."**

the statement should be literally true.

### Camp Buddy and expectations

Bringing Leo into Camp Buddy overlaps Taiga's old and new emotional worlds.

Because Taiga already knows he likes Leo, the Kieran material does not function as a device for discovering romance.

It exposes:
- expectations that have already formed;
- jealousy;
- incomplete disclosure;
- the difference between privacy and strategic omission;
- the fact that both boys have begun assigning special meaning to their place beside each other.

Taiga's old relationship history matters because he is moving toward something new, not because he still needs to discover that he wants Leo.

### Intimacy

Section III lets emotional and physical movement become increasingly intentional.

The Camp Buddy climax can explicitly establish:
- mutual romantic interest;
- mutual sexual attraction;
- the special meaning of the invitation;
- deliberate physical intimacy.

The boys still do not need to become boyfriends immediately.

The uncertainty is now about **shape and definition**, not whether desire exists.

### Final-section calibration

By the end of Section III, Taiga should feel:

- fully conscious that he wants Leo;
- confident that Leo reciprocates romantic and sexual interest;
- capable of acting intentionally on that knowledge;
- able to bring Leo into emotionally loaded parts of his life by choice;
- still uncomfortable with formal labels / relationship definition;
- increasingly able to remain present after vulnerability instead of fleeing from it.

He is no longer asking **what do I want from him?**

He is living the answer while avoiding, for now, the question of what to call it.

## Section IV — New Threshold

### Narrative state
**The existing relationship crystallizes into chosen definition.**

Section IV begins after attraction, romantic interest and deliberate intimacy are already mutual facts.

Taiga and Leo are not waiting to discover whether they want each other.

They are already behaving like two people in an emerging relationship.

The remaining threshold is:

> **Will they choose to define it and accept the commitment that comes with the label?**

### Dominant narration

Compared with Section III:

- less self-surveillance;
- less dismissal of attraction;
- more participatory sarcasm;
- more physical ease;
- more practical care;
- more confident use of "normal" for routines involving Leo;
- stronger attention to shared goals rather than only private emotional uncertainty.

The narration should be warmer without announcing that it is warmer.

### Attraction and intimacy

Physical attention is straightforward.

Taiga does not need to:
- justify looking;
- interrogate every touch;
- treat desire as evidence requiring analysis.

He can simply notice Leo and continue.

The relational question has moved from:

> "Do I want this?"

to:

> **"Am I willing to call this a relationship and behave accordingly?"**

### Definition

Act / Section IV should **crystallize** the trajectory into an actual boyfriend relationship.

The first kiss / explicit relationship threshold should matter because it formalizes something already lived rather than creating romance from nothing.

Do not make the label feel like a surprise reward after prolonged ignorance.

It is a choice.

### Football and control

Much of Taiga's remaining rigidity can surface through football rather than romance.

He may still:
- overanalyze mistakes;
- replay decisions;
- try to solve matches before they happen;
- become frustrated when performance cannot be controlled.

This keeps his control-oriented personality active even while relational defenses soften.

### Social belonging

Olympus should feel familiar in the narration.

Caleb, Nora, teammates and campus routines no longer need introductory interpretation.

Taiga's attention can assume knowledge.

That assumption itself communicates belonging.

### Vulnerability

Taiga increasingly permits other people to see:
- pride;
- disappointment;
- need;
- affection;
- embarrassment.

He may still react badly to being teased or exposed, but the response is more often comic embarrassment than true defensive threat.

### Final-section calibration

By the end of Section IV, Taiga should feel:

- comfortable being wanted;
- comfortable wanting Leo;
- capable of chosen romantic commitment;
- able to call Leo his boyfriend without the label itself feeling like loss of control;
- strongly invested in his Olympus relationships;
- still sarcastic, competitive, blunt and proud;
- significantly less likely to turn emotional exposure into hostility.

The important shift is that closeness has stopped feeling inherently dangerous enough to require ambiguity as protection.

## Section V — The Other Side

### Narrative state
**Warm, trusting Taiga in unfamiliar territory.**

Section V reverses the environmental setup of Section I.

Taiga is once again the outsider.

But he is no longer the same kind of outsider.

He enters Leo's world with:

- an established relationship;
- broader trust;
- more emotional vocabulary;
- greater willingness to participate;
- much less need to protect himself from every new person.

This contrast should be one of the clearest demonstrations of his growth.

### Dominant narration

The final rewrite should strengthen Taiga's POV here because the Draft often becomes too neutral.

The intended Section V voice is:

- curious;
- observant;
- affectionate;
- practical;
- socially cautious without being hostile;
- willing to be amused;
- willing to like people quickly;
- still sarcastic;
- still capable of irritation.

The narration should remain strongly Taiga-shaped even when he is happy.

### Attention

Threat-oriented attention should be at its weakest here.

Taiga increasingly notices:

- family routines;
- who calls Leo what;
- who needs help;
- who is tired;
- who occupies which role in the household;
- how Leo changes around family;
- when Leo is stretching himself too thin;
- the difference between Leo enjoying people and Leo automatically serving them.

This is mature **relational attention**.

### Social trust

Taiga can allow Leo's family to become meaningful without requiring years of defensive testing first.

That does not mean instant intimacy.

He may remain:

- quieter with unfamiliar adults;
- careful with language;
- embarrassed by attention;
- selective about disclosure.

The difference is that he no longer assumes closeness is a threat.

### Care

Taiga's affection should be most visible through action here.

He:

- waits;
- notices;
- prepares food;
- learns Spanish;
- participates in Leo's interests;
- tracks Leo's exhaustion;
- tries to create space for him;
- becomes upset when repeated obligations consume their time together.

This care should not be converted into sentimental narration.

It remains Taiga-shaped.

### Anger

Section V should demonstrate the mature version of Taiga's anger.

When conflict arrives, the trigger is no longer primarily:

> I feel exposed, therefore I need distance.

It is more often:

> Something important is being ignored, and I do not know how long I can tolerate it.

He may still become sharp.

He may still say the wrong thing.

But anger is more differentiated and more connected to identifiable needs.

The rewrite should allow him to understand, sooner than early Taiga would, that beneath the anger are:

- worry about Leo;
- frustration at being deprioritized;
- disappointment;
- wanting time with him;
- fear that Leo is exhausting himself.

### Participation

The Las Terrenas material shows an important final-stage Taiga:

- willing to join strangers in a game;
- willing to dance badly in public;
- willing to laugh at himself;
- willing to be pulled into Leo's social world without treating participation as defeat.

He remains competitive and sarcastic.

But participation itself is no longer threatening.

### Final-section calibration

By the end of Section V, Taiga should feel:

- warm without becoming soft-focus;
- trusting without becoming gullible;
- emotionally perceptive without becoming a therapist;
- openly invested without becoming broadly confessional;
- less prone to anger without losing it;
- comfortable with attraction and affection;
- capable of enjoying unfamiliar people and situations;
- still unmistakably Taiga.

The final voice should not feel like a different narrator from Section I.

It should feel like **the same narrator after the novel happened to him**.

---

## 4.1 Regression Rule

The progression above is not monotonic.

Older versions of Taiga may reappear under pressure.

Likely regression triggers include:

- humiliation;
- public exposure;
- jealousy;
- fear of abandonment;
- feeling manipulated;
- loss of control;
- serious football failure;
- old Camp Buddy wounds;
- believing that someone important is being harmed.

Regression should affect:

- what he notices;
- how quickly he judges;
- how hostile his direct thoughts become;
- how much he trusts another person's intent;
- how easily anger substitutes for a more precise emotion.

However, later Taiga should usually recover faster.

That is part of the growth.

The question is not whether an old reaction ever returns.

The question is **how long he remains trapped inside it and what he can do afterward**.

---

# 5. Prose Style

Prefer:

- clarity;
- concrete detail;
- strong sentence rhythm;
- plain vocabulary used deliberately;
- physical staging;
- specific observation;
- selective interiority.

Avoid polishing the novel into ornamental literary prose.

Taiga is not a lyrical narrator by default.

The final rewrite should become **more authored, not more decorative**.

---

# 6. Emotional Writing

Big emotional moments should usually become simpler rather than more poetic.

Trust:

- behavior;
- silence;
- interruption;
- physical action;
- changes in speech;
- what a character cannot say.

Avoid explaining emotions already visible in the scene.

Taiga may understand more over time, but the narration should not become therapist-like merely because he has matured.

Self-awareness and polished emotional analysis are not the same thing.

---

## 6.1 Rendering Taiga's Emotions

When Taiga feels something important, prefer the most immediate form that fits his current level of self-awareness.

Use this hierarchy:

1. **Direct thought, judgment or reaction**
   - *Great.*
   - *Annoying.*
   - *Fuck.*
   - *Why did that matter?*

   This is usually the strongest Taiga-filtered option when he can consciously frame the reaction.

2. **Concrete bodily or sensory response**
   - stomach dropping or tightening;
   - heat in his face, neck or ears;
   - dry mouth;
   - shaking hands;
   - jaw or fingers tightening;
   - looking away, going still, losing his breath.

   This is especially useful when Taiga is feeling something he does not yet want or know how to name.

3. **Behavior that reveals the emotion**
   - deleting a message;
   - staying or leaving;
   - changing the subject;
   - snapping;
   - checking something again;
   - avoiding eye contact;
   - doing something helpful instead of verbalizing care.

4. **Explicit emotional naming**
   - *Taiga felt uncomfortable.*
   - *He felt satisfied.*
   - *He was embarrassed.*
   - *He felt relieved.*

   Use this only when clarity genuinely requires it.

The goal is **not** to ban emotion words or the verb *felt*. Sensory constructions such as *his stomach dropped*, *he felt sick*, or *Taiga felt heat in his ears* remain close POV because the reader is experiencing the body with him.

The risky construction is narration that diagnoses the emotion for him when his thought, body or behavior could carry it instead.

Prefer:

> "Thank you."  
> "You don't need to make it a thing."

over:

> "Thank you."  
> Taiga felt uncomfortable.

Likewise, do not explain an emotion after behavior has already shown it.

### Vague bodily metaphors

Use constructions such as:

- *something tightened;*
- *something loosened;*
- *something shifted;*
- *something warm settled in his chest*

sparingly.

Concrete sensation is usually stronger than a vague internal metaphor.

### Development across the novel

This rule should support Taiga's character progression.

Early Taiga often stops at:

> body → reaction → deflection

Later Taiga may increasingly be able to name the underlying emotion directly.

For example, early vulnerability may appear as:

> His stomach dropped.  
> Fuck.

where later Taiga may be able to think or say:

> I was scared.

Greater emotional clarity is part of his development. It should not turn the narration into polished psychological analysis.

### Mental-state verbs that remain valid

Direct judgments such as:

- *Taiga hated the question.*
- *He appreciated that.*
- *He regretted mentioning it.*
- *He disliked the noise.*

are still strongly filtered through Taiga and do not count as detached emotional diagnosis.

The test is:

> **Does the sentence sound like Taiga experiencing or judging something, or like an outside narrator reporting Taiga's emotional condition?**

Prefer the former.

---

# 7. Dialogue Philosophy

Dialogue should preserve heightened character distinction while remaining believable.

Characters may have anime-influenced:

- rhythms;
- verbal habits;
- reactions;
- comedic timing;
- expressive tendencies.

But quirks are seasoning, not identity.

A character should remain recognizable even when their usual verbal quirk is absent.

---

# 8. Character Distinction Rule

**Anime-level character distinction; novel-level human behavior.**

Recurring characters should differ through:

- sentence construction;
- vocabulary;
- social instinct;
- humor;
- emotional behavior;
- what they notice;
- how they fail under pressure.

Do not give the whole cast the same dry, competent, mildly sarcastic register.

---

# 9. Rewrite Audit Questions

During every chapter rewrite, check:

- Does the narration sound like Taiga?
- Is Taiga's POV present without becoming repetitive?
- Are descriptions filtered through his attention?
- What is Taiga paying attention to at this point in his development?
- Is an early defensive habit appearing because it belongs here, or merely because it sounds like Taiga?
- Could another recurring character have delivered this line unchanged?
- Is the prose simple because it is precise, or simple because it is generic?
- Is an emotion being explained after it has already been shown?
- Is later Taiga becoming warmer without becoming neutral or sentimental?
- If Taiga regresses under stress, does the regression make sense for the trigger?

---

# 10. Open Questions

To define during the rewrite:

- section-by-section calibration of Taiga's growth;
- balance between humor and seriousness;
- sensory priorities and recurring descriptive habits;
- how much anime-style exaggeration is appropriate for each cast group.
