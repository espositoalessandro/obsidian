
Enzo's sign ended up saying:

**YUUTO PLEASE DO THE VOLLEYBALL GOOD**

No amount of persuasion was enough to make him change that. They only managed to making him keep Roberto the shark in Apollo. A small victory.

Most of the glitter didn't stick properly to the sign and fell to his sleeves.

"Do not touch me," Taiga said when he came closer.

The arena was already noisy. Shoes squeaked on the court, balls struck the floor and somebody behind the home bench was testing a whistle.

Enzo spotted someone near the aisle and headed toward him.

"Avan!"

The guy looked up from his program. Dark hair, glasses, a pale blue jacket folded neatly beside him. He felt familiar.
He glanced past Enzo at the rest of them.

"How many of you are there?"

"Six."

Avan checked the row. "We can sit on this side. Just keep your bags out of the aisle; people still need to get past."

"He's Yuuto's roommate," Enzo said.

They sat on the row. Avan lead the way, sitting on the far end. 

On court, a guy with white dyed hair was bouncing a ball between his hands. Number four. He looked up when Enzo raised the sign.

His whole face lit up.

"YOOO! YOU BROUGHT THE WHOLE SQUAD!"

Several players turned. Yuuto pointed at the sign, laughed and gave them two thumbs up. His coach called his name. He dropped one hand, keeping the other thumb raised while jogging back to his teammates.

"Looks like he likes it," Leo said.

Avan was still looking at the sign.

"He's going to want that in our room."

Enzo turned it toward him. "There's space for your name."

"No, thanks."

Taiga took his coat off. The place was warm, which made a pleasant change from spending Saturday freezing beside a soccer pitch.

Olympus lost the first set. Yuuto kept hitting into the block, and Enzo lowered his sign onto his knees as if the wording might be at fault.

"He needs to stop trying to hit through them," Leo said. "There's room beside their hands."

Taiga turned toward him. "How do you know that?"

"My sister played for two years. Then, she retired at fourteen because was too hard and everyone screamed."

"Reasonable."

"She does theater now."

Taiga looked at him.

"Yeah, I've pointed that out. She says it's different when she's the one screaming."

Olympus drew level in the second set. Yuuto chased one ball almost into the advertising boards, and Enzo shouted loudly enough to make him look up.

"He's supposed to be watching the ball, not you," Avan told him.

Enzo waited until Yuuto turned away before raising the sign again.

By the third set, Taiga was leaning forward beside Leo, who had started telling the setter where to put the ball.

"He can't hear you."

"Who knows? He might."

An Olympus player dived for a ball Taiga had already given up on. It came back up, a teammate reached it, and Yuuto jumped.

"Come on—"

Leo caught Taiga's forearm as they both stood. Yuuto dropped the ball just behind the block.

Their row erupted. Leo let go to clap, and Taiga had to push Enzo's sign away to see.

Olympus took the set, then the fourth. By the end, Avan had folded the program in half and was clapping with the rest of them.

"That last ball," Gav said, still watching the court. "I thought it was down."

"So did I," Avan admitted.

He was smiling when Yuuto looked up at them.
