By Monday morning, Central Park looked like a park again.

The lanterns were gone. The temporary stage had disappeared. Somebody had removed the pumpkins before students could start kicking them around campus, which was probably wise.

Aaron and Sofia’s upcoming date became a recurring subject in the Apollo group chat, until Aaron threatened to leave it. Enzo responded by changing the group name to AARON’S SUPPORT NETWORK, which lasted almost ten minutes before Gav changed it back.

Then November started doing what November apparently did at university: everything became due.

Professor Hall assigned their final paper and spent ten minutes explaining that three citations couldn’t magically turn six pages of opinions into an academic paper. Chemistry added another lab report. Rao put the last Foundations assignment online and casually mentioned the final as if that wasn’t the kind of sentence that ruined people’s week.

Taiga started spending more time in the library. When the library got too crowded, he worked in his room instead.
Caleb had developed an entire system involving two timers, a spreadsheet and scheduled breaks. Taiga refused to participate on principle and then slowly started using the breaks anyway.

Life outside classes and training kept happening too.
He ate with the team after practice sometimes and stayed at House Apollo once after film because Gav had ordered too much food. Nora kept stealing the chair beside him in Political Institutions.
Enzo slowly forgot the whole Dynamite story while Leo did not, obviously. At least he never brought it back in front of Enzo.
Caleb and Maya dragged him to Starbuffs one Sunday morning and then spent half of breakfast discussing whether a trail near Acropolis would still be usable after the first snow.

For almost three weeks, Olympus simply continued.

Soccer didn’t slow down either. If anything, Mercer got worse.

Taiga played twenty-six minutes in one friendly, then thirty in the next. Nothing particularly interesting happened in either.

Training was another story.
The first weeks had been full of pauses. Stop. Reset. Explain where everyone was supposed to be. Explain why. Repeat.

Now Mercer mostly expected them to know. A bad press got a whistle and a surname. A bad run got a whistle and a stare.
Sometimes that was worse.

“Again!”

Taiga jogged back toward the touchline. They were working on a reduced-size pitch, eight against eight, with the space compressed enough that every bad touch immediately became somebody else’s problem.

Scott rolled the ball to Gav. Taiga checked over his shoulder. Aaron was deeper, between him and goal, while Leo was moving inside.

Gav passed forward and Leo came toward the ball.

A month earlier, Taiga would’ve seen Leo moving and gone toward him too. Or passed straight to him. Or done something equally useful for the defender following him.

Now he saw the defender move with Leo. Space opened behind him and Taiga ran.

Leo received with his back to goal, touched the ball once and sent it around the defender into the space Taiga was already attacking.
Taiga reached it before Aaron could close him down and drove a low ball across the middle. Price got a foot to it, but Scott saved.

“Fuck.”

“Again!” Mercer shouted.

They played another fifteen minutes before Mercer finally blew twice.

“Enough.”

Several people immediately bent over. Enzo dropped onto his back.

“I have died.”

“You say that every Thursday,” Aaron said.

“Because every Thursday he kills me.”

Mercer ignored them.

“Saturday we got Briarwood. Same time as usual. I’ll post the squad list tonight.”

Players started toward the bottles. Taiga had barely taken a sip before Mercer called him.

“Akatora.”

Taiga stopped and approached him.

“You’re starting. Left wing.”

Starting? For a second, he thought he’d misunderstood.

“Starting?” he repeated.

“Yeah.”

Mercer waited, but nothing useful came out.

“Uh… Alright,” he just said.

“Good.”

Mercer nodded and left.

Taiga went to the locker room, still metabolizing the thought of him actually starting.

“Aaron! You didn’t tell us how it went with Sofia,” Enzo said.

Aaron shrugged. “Pretty normal, I’d say?”

“Did you two kiss?” Evan said with a grin.

A very red Aaron turned immediately toward his locker. “That’s not your fucking business.”

Taiga was barely listening. He lifted his shirt, shoved it into his bag and sat down to untie his boots.
Starting. Actual fucking starting. Not first team, sure, but… that should mean something. Right?

“You alright?”

Taiga snapped out of it and looked up. Leo was standing in front of him, a towel around his waist and still damp from the shower.

“Mh? Yeah.”

“You were staring at the floor like it had personally offended you or something.”

“Maybe it had.”

Leo smiled. “The nerve!”

He grabbed his stuff from his locker and sat beside him, drying his hair with a towel.

“Did Mercer say something requiring that much computation?”

“Not really, I’m just—” Taiga gestured vaguely. “Starting against Briarwood.”

Leo stopped and looked at him in surprise. “Seriously?”

“I hope?”

“Shit, that’s a big milestone!”

Taiga pulled out his flip-flops and a towel from the bag.

“Yeah, big deal. It’s a friendly, I’ve already played a couple of them.”

“Dude, it’s your first start. You can celebrate a little.”

He stopped for a second. “Maybe.”

Leo frowned. “Okay, that was unexpectedly fast.”

“You sound disappointed,” said Taiga while finishing undressing. “Did you want me to say I’ll probably fuck up?”

“N-No! Obviously, I just—” He stopped for half a second. “You know, I expected it.”

“Well, I’ll probably fuck it up anyway. What’s the point in worrying about it now?”

Leo laughed. “Look at you.”

Taiga shook his head and opened his bag to retrieve his shampoo and shower gel.
When he turned, Leo was still looking at him and smiling.

“What?”

“Nothing, nothing.”

Taiga raised an eyebrow and turned to go to the showers.

When he went outside the locker room, he found Evan talking to Mercer. Mercer said something Taiga couldn’t hear, Evan nodded, and then the conversation ended.

Mercer walked away, while Evan spotted Taiga. A bright smile appeared on his face.

“There he is.”

“No. Don’t.”

“I just wanted to congratulate you.”

“Don’t do that.”

Evan laughed and fell into step beside him. They headed toward the main campus. For a while they didn’t talk about football at all. Evan complained about professors moving deadlines, other seniors having regular panic attacks and, in general, feeling like Olympus was trying its best to get rid of him.

“You never told me what you’re studying,” Taiga said at some point.

Evan shrugged. “Well, you never asked!”

Taiga blinked. Yeah, Caleb-virus was definitely airborne.

“I’m a Modern Languages major. Mostly Spanish and a bit of French here and there.”

“Oh. Cool.”

“It is! Besides, most people don’t know, but Spanish is one of the most widely spoken languages in the world. So I hope it’ll serve me well.”

“Is it?”

“Well, if you think about it, just on our team, there are two native Spanish speakers. Plus me.”

Taiga frowned. “Who?”

“Who? Leo and Enzo, of course. Didn’t you know they were from the Dominican Republic?”

Right. He didn’t think about the implications of that.

“Yes, I just didn’t think about it. I’ve only ever heard them speak English, though. And they speak it pretty good.”

“Yeah, they must have studied it since they were children. I’ve never actually asked them.”

“Probably.”

“What about you though? I only know you’re in the same Chemistry class as Leo.”

“I’m…” Taiga twisted his mouth. “Actually, I’m still undeclared.”

Evan shrugged. “That’s fine. I didn’t declare my major until almost May of freshman year.”

“I’m considering Computer Science.”

“Then you’re already ahead of me. If I’m not mistaken, Leo’s in CS too, right?”

“Unfortunately.”

“Heh. Well, you’ll have company at least.”

“Unfortunately.”

Evan smiled. They continued for a couple of minutes without speaking. Then Taiga asked:

“What are you doing after graduation?”

“I haven’t got the faintest freaking idea.”

Taiga stared at him.

“Dunno,” Evan continued, “I’ll probably end up working for some company. Or maybe I’ll be a teacher. Or even a tour guide, that’d be nice. Free travel!”

“Wh— Seriously, you don’t know?”

“Well, it’s not like I’m graduating tomorrow, right?”

“Still—”

“I think I’ll try out a thing or two once I’m outta here. See what clicks. And what allows me to pay for an actual house.”

Taiga stayed silent for a few seconds.

“Right. Fair enough.”

Evan turned toward him. “How about you?”

“I’ve not even chosen a major.”

“No, no. I mean, what are you doing once I’m out?”

What? …Ah. Right.

“If you’re talking about the team, I don’t know. Besides, it’s not up to me to decide.”

“Well, it kinda is, Taiga,” Evan said.

“What do you mean?”

“Look, I think you know that you’re the only one who’s training specifically for that position. Right?”

“Mercer never said it was decided.”

“And that’s true. I don’t think you’re ready for it. Yet.”

They stopped outside the dorm building.

“I think you know that during the tryouts there were way better players than you,” Evan continued.

Taiga frowned. “Yeah. I know.”

“Some of them were actual wingers. Yet, here you are.”

He paused a second, then pointed at the dorm building.

“And there they are.”

Taiga didn’t answer.

“Look, what I’m trying to say is that I think Mercer is betting on you. He could have picked another winger and kept you in development for the whole year or as a reserve.”

Taiga gulped and, after a couple of seconds, said, “That’s a… risky gamble.”

Evan smiled. “Yeah. But Mercer is a good coach. He knows what he’s doing. I had my doubts at the beginning, but… I’m starting to see why he did that.”

Then he put a hand on Taiga’s shoulder.

“I’d really like it if you were the one wearing my shirt next year.”

Taiga felt his stomach tighten. Evan gently patted his shoulder and removed his hand.

“N-No pressure, huh?” Taiga said.

“Oh no. Pressure. I know it sucks, but I want you to know what’s at stake. Just in case you thought you didn’t matter that much.”

“That’s… fucked up.”

Evan laughed hard. “Yeah.”

Taiga smiled as well.

“…Thanks,” he said after a while.

“Anything for my little kohai.”

Taiga blinked.

“Don’t ever say that again.”

Evan smiled. “Anyway, don’t make that face. I’m not donating you my job because Mercer gave you one start in November.”

“Yeah, I know.”

“Good. Because I’m planning to remain significantly better than you until graduation.”

Taiga laughed. “Go fuck yourself.”

Evan started towards the door. “Eheh, not until May! Good luck, forty-one.”

Taiga stood there for a minute, before going back to his room.

Somehow Nora was getting more unbearable than usual. As weeks passed, she spent more and more time in the library. Her army of notes, Post-its, books and colored tabs had grown enough to require half a table every time.
The good thing about this was that she was so focused on her studies that annoying Taiga had become secondary.

The bad thing, however, was that whoever tried to talk to her while she was deep in a paragraph basically risked their life.

Taiga discovered this once when he asked about Oliver while she was clearly struggling to write an assignment for Hall.

“He’s better.”

“Is he still in the ridiculous cone?”

“No.”

“So, the infection has passed?”

“I don’t know.”

Taiga waited.

“Is that what I usually sound like?”

She pointed a pen at him with a murderous look.

“Taiga. Not now.”

He raised his hands in surrender. “Alright, sorry.”

Geez.

It was the day after his talk with Evan. Besides all the soccer stuff that was still sitting like a concrete block in his stomach, he was still thinking about how chill Evan was about his future.

I’ll try out a couple of things and see what sticks.

That was a stupid way of making decisions.

Maybe, though, having an always-open tab of the school program sitting in the background with the “Computer Science” option already selected in the dropdown was even more stupid.

Taiga glanced at Nora. She was resting her head in both hands, staring intently at the screen of her laptop.

Pointless. He didn’t need another fucking conversation about how he’d already picked two CS classes for the next semester.

His phone buzzed.

Taiga just gave him an “I told you” look when he turned to him after that. Eventually, he pulled out his laptop.

“This assignment is pointlessly cruel,” he said with a frown, looking at the screen with intense disgust.

Taiga leaned over to look at his screen.

“What’s your problem?”

“The fucking partition.”

He scanned his code for a minute. The overall structure was right, except…

“Why did—”

Leo put a hand over Taiga’s mouth. Taiga was slightly taken aback by the sudden, uncalled-for physical contact.

“No. Don’t. I’ll figure it out.”

He removed his hand. Taiga just shrugged.

“Suit yourself.”

“Stick to emotional support.”

Leo turned toward him and raised an eyebrow.

“Okay, that definitely needs work.”

Across the table, Nora snorted. Taiga glared at her.

“Mind your assignment.”

She didn’t say anything, but Taiga saw her exchanging a knowing glance with Leo.
Suddenly, he missed Caleb and his room.

After fourteen minutes, Leo tried another run and the test suite was still red. His forehead dropped straight onto the laptop keyboard.
Taiga glanced at that pathetic scene. The cursor was leaving a trail of “bbbbbbbb” in the file.
He still hadn’t caught the error.

“Need more emotional support?”

Leo raised his middle finger without lifting his head.

“This is fucking pointless, why can’t I just use other arrays?” he said.

“I guess it’s part of the exercise.”

Leo slowly turned his head, still on the keyboard.

“Whose side are you on?”

Taiga smiled. “I’ll be there if you need me.”

“Not until you wipe that smug look off your face.”

His smile widened in response, and he turned to his own algorithm.

After a minute, Leo finally lifted his head. He cleared the “bbbb” mess and stared at the code for a while.

“Fine. Alright, just tell me,” he eventually said.

Taiga leaned over. “It’s the partition function.”

“You’re assigning the index to the starting position of the array.”

“Oh, Jesus fucking Christ.”

What the fuck was wrong with everyone?

Jesus. Why was everybody so obsessed with him being undeclared?

“I didn’t mean it like that. I know that it’s perfectly fine. I’m just surprised because the other people I know who are still undeclared haven’t actually picked a subject they like.”

Taiga stared at him for a couple of seconds. The fuck?

He opened the Olympus courses tab again and stared at it.
What was wrong with him?
Almost everyone he’d talked to was unsure about their future too. Yet, none of them were being such dramatic pussies over a simple major declaration.
Instead, each time he opened the portal and looked at the submit button, he ended up staring at that fucking page for minutes, until eventually deciding he was still not sure enough.

…Which was stupid. He had basically already decided to give CS a try.

Just like that idiot Aaron. He had already decided he wanted to ask Sofia, yet every time he chickened out.
And Aaron asked him for advice? When he couldn’t even bring himself to press a button that would change basically nothing?

Taiga rubbed his face, trying to snap out of the spiral. God, how he wished he had a shutdown button for his stupid brain.

Shutdown?

“Shut my brain off until I’ve gone far enough that I can’t chicken out anymore.”

He laughed into his hands. Nora looked up.

“You finally lost it?”

Taiga looked back, still smiling.

“That was long ago.”

Then he opened the portal, quickly selected “Computer Science” from the major dropdown, and pressed submit before thinking too hard about it.

A loading spinner appeared over the page, followed shortly after by a green checkmark.

TAIGA AKATORA
Current program: Computer Science

He closed the tab before his brain could catch up. Then he leaned back in the chair and took a deep breath.

Absolutely nothing changed. That was the worst part of it.

Leo looked at him and then at Nora. “Should we worry?”

“Both of you can go fuck yourselves.”

Saturday arrived gray, cold and windy.

Mercer had just left the locker room after the usual briefing.
Taiga looked at the magnetic board.

Seeing his name in the starting lineup still felt a bit uncomfortable.
Around him, on the other hand, things were still normal. Enzo was shouting nonsense, Leo was arguing back from across the room, Scott was silently taping his fingers, and Gav was explaining stuff to another freshman.

Briarwood arrived in dark red shirts. Taiga knew almost nothing about them beyond what Mercer had said during film: compact midfield, aggressive fullbacks, annoying press.

They warmed up, lined up, shook hands, and the whistle went.

The match itself was mostly ordinary.
Briarwood pressed hard for the first fifteen minutes, then slowed down. Taiga got the ball, lost it, won it back once, sent one cross too long and another into the box where nobody reached it. He made a few runs that went nowhere and a couple that pulled the fullback away from Aaron.

By the second half, Taiga had stopped thinking about the fact that he had started at all.

Olympus scored in the fifty-eighth minute through Price. Taiga had contributed by pulling the fullback wide, opening space for Leo to move in and make the final pass.
After celebrating with Price, Leo turned to him and gave him a thumbs-up with a grin.

Taiga smiled. It wasn’t the first time he’d done something like that, but it still felt fucking good.

In the sixty-eighth minute, he saw Evan jogging along the sideline. A few minutes later, the board went up.

41 OUT
11 IN

Taiga went toward the sideline. Evan waited beside the fourth official.

They slapped hands as they crossed.

“Not terrible,” Evan said.

“Fuck you.”

Evan grinned and ran on.

Taiga dropped onto the bench, grabbed a bottle and drank until his breathing settled. Somebody handed him a jacket without asking. He pulled it on and watched the rest of the match.

The final whistle came with the score still 1-0.

Taiga stood with everyone else, shook a few hands and headed back inside.

By the time he reached the locker room, Enzo had apparently already made a decision for the entire team.

“Tonight we celebrate the victory and our Forty-one’s first start!”

Taiga sat down and started pulling off his boots.

“That’s not really a reason.”

Enzo shrugged. “No freaking idea.”

“What’s happening?”

“Akatora’s first-start dinner,” Enzo said.

“Five seconds ago.”
