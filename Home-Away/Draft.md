# Chapter 12 — Working Plan

## Working title: **The Bubble Pops**

Inspired by **"The Girl in the Bubble"** from *Wicked 2*.

The title is currently thematic / working, not locked.

---

# Chapter 12 — Core Dramatic Job

This is the **ending chapter of Section / Act I**.

It has a difficult double job:

1. **End the first semester / school arc cleanly.**
2. **Begin the actual romantic arc.**

Do **not** spend half the chapter re-proving that life at Olympus keeps moving or that Olympus has become Taiga's life. Chapter 11 already did that.

Chapter 12 should close school efficiently and then give the rink / one-on-one outing the majority of the chapter.

### Approximate weight

- **25–30%:** finals / football wrap / people leaving
- **70–75%:** Leo + Taiga outing, rink, hot drinks / conversation, separation

The chapter should eventually **become the rink chapter**, not feel like a finals chapter with a romantic scene attached at the end.

---

# What Act I Is Actually Ending On

Taiga already has a stable model for Leo:

> Leo is hot.  
> Leo is my friend.  
> I like spending time with him.  
> Those facts do not contradict each other.

That model has worked since Chapter 6.

Chapter 12 does **not** need Taiga to realize he is in love, or even cleanly conclude that he has a crush.

Its job is smaller and more important:

> **Taiga realizes that "friend" is no longer enough to explain how he behaves around Leo.**

He enters the outing with the sincere / deluded assumption:

> He's my friend.

He exits with something closer to:

> ...is he just that?

The bubble is not ignorance of sexual attraction. Taiga already knows Leo is attractive.

The bubble is the belief that **sexual attraction + friendship fully explains the relationship**.

Chapter 12 pops that bubble.

# Revised Romance Architecture

## Act I — Awareness

Core movement:

> **Something about Leo has stopped fitting inside "friend."**

Chapter 12 is the ignition point.

Taiga does not solve the feeling. He simply becomes aware that there is now a question.

After the rink, he knows something is wrong / different in the way he reacts to Leo.

---

## Act II — Recognition / Realization

Act II should no longer end vaguely on:

> I still don't know what this is.

That is too evasive for two twenty-year-olds who both already have relationship history.

Act II is where Taiga:

- becomes genuinely nervous around Leo in specific situations;
- sometimes pulls back because he notices the pattern;
- denies / minimizes what is happening;
- tries to restore "normal friendship";
- misses Leo when he is absent;
- notices when Leo is not in a room;
- checks for messages;
- becomes more interested in plans because Leo said yes;
- seeks Leo out even while telling himself he should create distance;
- increasingly chooses Leo anyway.

The key contradiction is:

> **Taiga keeps pulling back whenever he notices the pattern, while his actual choices keep moving him closer.**

Longing happens before full admission.

By the end of Act II, after the rupture / repair, Taiga should **know what he wants**.

Leo's eventual idea:

> **"I don't want to change wherever we were going."**

should force the issue into a simple choice.

Taiga does not need to define the relationship yet.

He does need to realize:

> I like Leo.  
> I want more from him than friendship.  
> I want to keep moving toward whatever this is.

Act II is the **realization act**.

---

## Act III — Enactment / Going There

Act III is **not**:

> What do I want from Leo?

Taiga already knows.

It is:

> **I know I want him. Now we naturally keep moving toward each other.**

This makes later material stronger:

- Taiga invites Leo to Camp Buddy knowingly.
- When he eventually says **"I knew what I was doing when I invited you"**, he really did.
- He knows the invitation is personally meaningful.
- He wants Leo in that part of his life.
- Jealousy, Kieran, mutual expectation and sexual intimacy happen because both boys are already moving somewhere together.

The unresolved issue is not attraction / desire.

It is the **shape, boundaries and definition** of what they are becoming.

---

## Act IV — Crystallization / Definition

Act IV crystallizes the already-existing trajectory into:

> **actual boyfriends**

The question is no longer:

> Do I want Leo?

or:

> Are we moving toward something?

Those are settled.

The remaining threshold is:

> **Are we ready to define this as a relationship and accept the commitment / label that comes with it?**

Working macro progression:

> **Act I:** Something is happening.  
> **Act II:** I want it to happen.  
> **Act III:** We let it happen.  
> **Act IV:** We choose what it is.

Keep this efficient.

We mainly need:

## Finals

- Finals pressure / studying.
- Enough academic texture to make the semester feel like it is actually ending.
- CS declaration is already settled from Chapter 11. No further academic decision.
- We do **not** need a farewell tour through every course.

## Football

Give Mercer the **last football word of the semester**.

No new milestone.

Possible function:

- concise evaluation;
- reminder that the break does not reset Taiga's progress;
- expectation that he comes back ready.

The exact line / scene is still open.

Mercer's function is closure, not another threshold.

## Departure transition

People leave / obligations disappear only as much as needed to create the circumstance for the outing.

Do not spend pages philosophizing about life moving on.

The useful causal chain is:

> finals end → football pauses → people leave → Taiga is still on campus → Leo is still on campus → Leo asks him to go skating

The removal of the ensemble should feel logistical, not engineered.

# Engineering the "Date"

The outing must feel date-like to the **reader** before Taiga is willing to interpret it that way.

Taiga enters with:

> We're friends. Everyone else left. We're hanging out.

And technically, that remains a plausible interpretation.

The outing naturally takes a date shape:

> travel together → winter rink → physical learning / play → drinks → increasingly personal conversation → walk / train home → separation before break

Nobody should call it a date.

Leo does not need to behave explicitly romantically.

The point is that **Taiga himself is already treating Leo differently**, without having admitted what that means.

# Rink Progression — Current Locked Working Choreography

## 1. Enter rink

- Taiga and Leo arrive.
- Taiga has never skated.
- Taiga immediately struggles with balance.
- He is focused on not falling, not on romance.

## 2. Leo takes Taiga's hands

Important:

> **Leo takes Taiga's hands. He does not offer them and wait.**

If Leo simply held his hands out and asked, Taiga would absolutely refuse.

The contact must arise naturally from Leo deciding:

> okay, you're useless, give me these

Leo skates backward while holding both of Taiga's hands, like a ski / skating instructor teaching a child.

For the first minute or two, **Taiga does not register the intimacy**.

He is too busy:
- balancing;
- listening to instructions;
- managing his feet;
- trying not to die.

This is important because the contact itself does not begin as romantically charged.

## 3. Taiga becomes competent enough to notice

After a minute or two, Taiga gains enough confidence that skating no longer consumes all of his attention.

Only then does his awareness shift.

He realizes:

> We're still holding hands.

The pressure / contact starts becoming unusually salient.

He becomes genuinely **nervous**.

This is new.

Sexual attraction to Leo has never made Taiga chronically nervous around him before.

That is why this reaction matters.

Taiga retracts first, under a perfectly reasonable excuse.

Working energy:

> "Right. I think I got it. Let me go."

Exact prose TBD.

Leo lets go without making a thing of it.

He keeps skating backward.

Taiga can tell himself the contact ended because he no longer needed help.

But on some level, he knows he pulled away because he became too conscious of it.

This is the **first crack**.

---

# 4. Second physical beat — collision / embrace

Give them a few minutes of normal skating after the hand-holding.

Enough time for Taiga to settle and file the nervousness away.

Leo continues skating backward.

Then:

- someone approaches / almost bumps Leo from behind;
- Taiga sees it first and warns him;
- Leo reacts immediately and stops;
- Leo is not hit;
- Taiga is now competent enough to move, but **not competent enough to stop that quickly**;
- Taiga goes straight into Leo.

Leo catches him instinctively.

This becomes effectively a hug / embrace to keep Taiga from falling.

Important distinction from the old Chapter 13 fall:

> **They do not crash onto the ice.**

They remain standing.

That removes the easy adrenaline / impact excuse.

---

# 5. Faces centimeters apart

Leo is holding Taiga.

Taiga is in Leo's arms.

They are stable.

Their faces are only centimeters apart.

Both freeze.

Both get visibly red / flustered.

This is where Taiga's attention suddenly records concrete details of Leo's face at close range.

Possible details, to choose sparingly in prose:
- eyes;
- darker ring around iris;
- snow / water in hair;
- cheeks flushed from cold;
- breath;
- mouth;
- expression.

Do **not** turn it into a full body inventory.

The meaningful fact is:

> Taiga notices details he normally would not have any reason to catalogue.

Leo being flustered matters because now the evidence is not only:

> I'm acting weird.

Taiga can see that Leo is affected too.

Taiga pulls away.

They continue skating.

No confession.

No explanation.

---

# 6. Taiga's immediate interpretation

Taiga does **not** conclude:

> I have a crush on Leo.

He uses the cheapest available explanation:

> **That was awkward.**

He files it away.

This is denial, but not cartoonishly stupid denial.

The moment genuinely was awkward.

The point is that the explanation does not account for everything.

Later he may think, explicitly or implicitly:

> Why the fuck was I noticing every detail on his face?

That question matters more than an immediate romantic label.

---

# After the Physical Trigger

The outing continues.

This is essential.

Do **not** make:

> charged moment → immediately leave rink → chapter ends

That would make the trigger feel too mechanically like THE ROMANCE BEAT.

They keep skating.

They continue joking.

The friendship still works.

But Taiga's perception has been altered.

Small ordinary physical details may now register more than they did before:
- proximity;
- an arm touch;
- Leo moving beside him;
- eye contact;
- Leo's face.

Do not overdo this into constant hyperawareness.

The important development is simply:

> physical neutrality has been disturbed.

---

# Post-Rink Conversation — Emotional Inflation

The physical scenes do not finish the chapter's work.

Afterward, the "date" should become increasingly intimate **through conversation**.

This is where the bubble keeps inflating / the consequences of the physical trigger become harder to dismiss.

## Hot drinks

Hot chocolate / coffee remains a strong transition.

It slows the pace and gives them a believable reason to sit together after skating.

## Leo's Spanish / Moma phone call

Strong candidate to preserve from old Chapter 13.

Functions:
- Taiga hears Leo speak Spanish naturally.
- Leo's rhythm / voice changes.
- Taiga gets access to a side of Leo he has not experienced directly.
- Family becomes concrete.
- Sister / scarf / home can enter naturally.
- Taiga becomes interested in more of Leo as a person.

This matters because the romantic shift is **not merely stronger horniness**.

After the physical destabilization, Taiga's attention increasingly attaches to:

> Leo himself.

## Camp Buddy / Taiga's gap year

Also useful, but keep it compressed.

Possible functions:
- Leo asks why Taiga stayed at Camp Buddy.
- Taiga explains enough to show why he eventually left for Olympus.
- This quietly closes the Act I belonging theme:
  - Camp Buddy remains home.
  - Leaving it did not destroy that.
  - Olympus can become another home.
- Taiga gives Leo more access to himself than he might have earlier in the semester.

Do not turn this into a full Scoutmaster Season exposition dump.

The conversation should feel like two people who keep choosing to talk.

# What the "Date" Is Showing Without Taiga Naming It

Across the outing, Taiga should repeatedly make choices that are individually friendship-compatible but cumulatively meaningful.

Examples:

- he goes when Leo asks;
- he lets Leo teach him physically;
- he gets nervous when the hand-holding becomes conscious;
- he is strongly affected by the accidental embrace;
- he notices Leo's reaction too;
- he continues the outing instead of retreating;
- he accepts drinks / continues spending time together;
- he asks questions about Leo's home / family / language;
- he volunteers more of his own past;
- he keeps extending the interaction;
- he enjoys being alone with Leo specifically.

The reader should gradually think:

> Dude. This is a date.

Taiga should continue thinking:

> I'm spending time with my friend.

Until that category starts failing.

---

# Ending / Separation Beat

Strong current direction:

At the end of the evening, they separate because both are leaving for break.

Taiga realizes he will not see Leo for roughly **three weeks**.

That suddenly does not sit right.

Important:

Do **not** immediately turn this into:

> I don't want to be away from him because I have feelings for him.

Taiga has not processed enough yet.

The cleaner final data point is simply:

> Three weeks suddenly felt like a long time.

or equivalent Taiga-shaped prose.

Potential emotional function:

- hand-holding showed nervousness;
- embrace showed physical charge + reciprocal fluster;
- conversation showed increasing personal intimacy;
- separation shows **pre-emptive absence / longing**.

This completes the chapter's progression without solving it.

Taiga now has enough evidence to know:

> something about how I act with Leo is different.

He still has to process what that means.

That processing belongs to Act II.

He does **not** conclude:
- "I'm in love";
- "I have a crush";

He leaves Act I with:

> **"Friend" is no longer a complete explanation.**

# Act II Consequence

Chapter 12 gives Act II permission to make Taiga **actually nervous** around Leo for the first time.

But this nervousness should be situational, not constant.

Taiga can still:
- joke normally;
- train normally;
- argue;
- study;
- text;
- coexist.

The nervousness appears when the Chapter 12 question is activated:
- prolonged touch;
- close eye contact;
- ending up alone;
- unusually personal conversation;
- consciously choosing Leo;
- realizing he is looking for Leo;
- somebody else noticing their closeness.

His likely responses:
- pull back;
- minimize;
- deny;
- restore distance;
- then miss Leo;
- seek him again;
- notice absence;
- choose plans because Leo is there.

Act II is the pressure created by those contradictory behaviors.

---

# Guardrails

- Do not spend half the chapter on "life keeps moving."
- Do not add another school / football milestone after Chapter 11.
- Do not let Leo dominate before the chapter naturally narrows to him.
- Do not make Leo explicitly flirt in order to create the rink tension.
- Do not have Leo offer his hands and wait; **he takes Taiga's hands**.
- Do not make them fall to the ice in the second beat.
- Do not make the embrace create a full romantic revelation.
- Do not make Taiga suddenly shy around Leo in every interaction.
- Do not confuse nervousness with sexual arousal.
- Do not have Taiga diagnose Leo's feelings from one blush.
- Do not call the outing a date in dialogue.
- Do not end on a generic "Olympus is home now" summary.
- Do not solve the romance in Act I.

By finals week, nobody at Olympus asked how anyone was doing anymore.

They asked what exam came next.

Political Institutions was Thursday morning. Chemistry was Friday. Calculus waited until Monday because Professor Chen apparently believed dread improved with storage. Foundations came last, which Taiga considered rude considering it was the only one he actually wanted to get to.

House Apollo had adapted to the situation by becoming a badly managed library.

Every table in the common room had at least one laptop on it. Charging cables crossed the floor in ways that would eventually kill somebody. Someone from track had fallen asleep on one of the couches with a textbook open on his chest. Scott was sitting on the floor beside the coffee table, working through flashcards without reacting to anything around him.

Enzo had a spreadsheet.

This was going badly.

"Why is eleven a date?"

"What?"

"I typed eleven. It became November."

"That's not possible."

Enzo turned the screen around.

"Yes."

"Then use a formula."

"I did."

"No. You typed a date."

"I typed numbers."

"Numbers can still be a date."

Enzo looked offended by the concept.

Taiga kept reading Hall's review sheet.

At the other end of the table, Gav said, "He's trying to calculate his participation grade."

Leo looked back at Enzo.

"It has components."

"Four."

"Because the classes overlap visually."

"Conditional formatting," Enzo repeated, as if that settled anything.

Leo looked between them.

"You're both terrible."

"Community service."

"Against your will?"

"At this point, yes."

Enzo pushed the laptop toward him.

"Fix November."

Leo sighed and moved his chair.

Taiga went back to Hall.

The review packet was mostly broad prompts: executive power, institutional legitimacy, federalism, emergency authority. The kind of questions where knowing the material was only half the problem. "Then he has vision."

"Never tell him that."

"Obviously."

The train left the university district behind.

Taiga watched the buildings flatten into shops and apartment blocks.

Leo talked.

Nothing unusual about that.

His flight was at eleven-twenty tomorrow. Enzo had somehow packed four pairs of shoes for three weeks. Leo had agreed to help his sister with something two days after getting home and had also promised an old friend he'd go out that night.

"Same day?" Taiga asked.

"Technically."

"What does technically mean?"

"It means one doesn't have a time yet."

"That's worse."

"It means flexible."

"It means you haven't discovered the conflict yet."

Leo pointed at him.

"Do not use the Caleb voice."

"That is my voice."

"No. There's a specific version where you become aggressively reasonable."

"You agreed to two things at the same time."

"Yes, and I regret telling you."

"You should."

Leo laughed and looked back out the window.

Taiga smiled.

This was normal.

They were friends.

Friends did things together.

That was the entire explanation required.

***

Acropolis Park had been turned into something aggressively seasonal.

Lights wrapped every tree along the main path. Temporary stalls lined the lawn. Music came from somewhere near the fountain, which had been turned off and covered in enough lights to be visible from space.

A giant illuminated arch announced **WINTER WEEK**.

Leo stopped beneath it.

"This is perfect."

"It is crowded."

"That's what perfect looks like sometimes."

"Disgusting sentence."

Leo laughed and headed toward the rink.

Taiga followed.

The rental skates looked untrustworthy.

He turned one over in his hands while Leo tied his laces.

"You've done this before, right?" Leo asked.

"No."

Leo stopped.

"Never?"

"No."

"You were at a camp."

"In summer."

"Right."

Taiga stared at him.

Leo nodded seriously.

"My brain made a connection that wasn't there."

"Your brain is damaged."

"Probably."

He went back to his laces.

"Okay. I've done it like five times."

"That's your qualification?"

"Five more than you."

"Terrifying."

"Soccer balance transfers."

"Does it?"

Leo smiled.

"No idea."

"Excellent."

They stepped onto the ice.

Taiga immediately discovered that every instinct he possessed about balance had been designed for surfaces that did not move under him.

His right skate slid.

He caught the barrier.

Leo turned around.

"Okay."

"Shut up."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were going to."

"I was going to tell you to bend your knees."

"I am bending them."

"No. You're threatening to bend them."

Taiga pushed away from the wall.

One skate moved.

Then the other.

His body objected to all of it.

A child passed him at a speed that should have required licensing.

Taiga watched her disappear around the curve.

"She's six."

"She's probably been doing this forever."

"She has not been alive forever."

"You don't know her life."

Leo skated backward in front of him.

"Stop doing that."

"Why?"

"Because you're going to fall."

"I'm not."

Leo moved closer.

Taiga's left skate drifted sideways.

He swore.

Leo reached forward and took both of his hands.

Not offered.

Took.

"Here."

Taiga barely had time to object before Leo started moving backward, pulling him away from the barrier.

"What the fuck are you doing?"

"Teaching you."

"I didn't ask."

"You were one second away from developing a long-term relationship with the wall."

"I was fine."

"You were holding it with both hands."

"That doesn't mean anything."

"Push out. Not back."

Taiga did.

Wrong.

Leo's grip tightened.

"No. Sideways."

"I know what sideways means."

"Your feet don't."

"Fuck off."

Leo laughed.

"Again."

Taiga pushed.

This time he moved properly.

"There."

"Don't sound proud."

"I am proud. My son can walk."

"I will kill you."

"Not until you learn how to stop."

For the first minute, Taiga had no room in his brain for anything except his feet.

Push.

Glide.

Do not lock the knees.

Do not lean backward.

Do not let the skates cross.

Leo kept moving in front of him, holding both hands and talking continuously.

"You're looking down again."

"I'm looking at my feet."

"They're still there."

"I need to know what they're doing."

"They're trying to betray you. Looking won't help."

Taiga looked up.

Leo smiled.

"Better."

They kept moving.

Slowly.

Then less slowly.

The panic around every small slide started disappearing.

Taiga stopped trying to correct every centimeter.

His body adjusted.

Push. Glide.

Leo said something about turning.

Taiga tried it.

It worked.

Barely.

"See?" Leo said. "You're fine."

"Obviously."

"You almost died three minutes ago."

"That is not what happened."

"No, you're right. The wall saved you."

Taiga laughed.

Then, somewhere in the middle of it, his brain had enough space to notice something else.

They were holding hands.

Still.

Both of them.

Leo was skating backward with his arms extended between them, gloves wrapped around Taiga's hands like this had been the most normal solution in the world.

Which it was.

He was teaching him.

That was all.

Taiga looked down at their hands.

Then immediately wished he hadn't.

The contact became impossible not to feel once he had noticed it.

Leo's grip shifted every time Taiga drifted.

His thumbs pressed over Taiga's knuckles through the gloves.

Their arms moved together with every push.

Taiga looked back at Leo.

Too long.

Leo was talking about something.

Taiga had no idea what.

His stomach tightened.

Not in the familiar way.

Not the shower. Not the dream. Not the very simple fact that Leo was hot.

This was different.

Nervous.

Why the fuck was he nervous?

"Right."

Leo stopped talking.

"What?"

Taiga let one hand loosen.

"I think I got it. Let me go."

The words came out faster than he meant them to.

Leo looked at him for half a second.

Then released both hands immediately.

"Okay."

That was it.

No joke.

No question.

Leo simply kept skating backward.

Taiga moved on his own.

Push.

Glide.

Fine.

See?

He had only needed help.

Now he didn't.

That was why he'd let go.

Obviously.

Leo smiled.

"Look at you."

"Don't."

"Independent."

"Keep skating."

"You're welcome."

Taiga raised a middle finger.

His balance survived.

Good.

Problem solved.

***

Five minutes later, Taiga could turn without feeling like he had signed a waiver.

Stopping was still more theoretical.

Leo had apparently decided this justified becoming annoying again.

He skated backward several meters ahead, talking while Taiga followed.

"Okay, now try to push harder on the left because you're still favoring the right."

"I'm not."

"You are."

"How would you know?"

"Because I'm looking at you."

That shouldn't have landed anywhere.

It did.

Taiga frowned and pushed harder with the left.

Leo grinned.

"See?"

"Shut up."

Someone came onto the rink behind Leo.

Fast.

A guy in a dark coat, looking sideways at whoever was skating beside him.

Leo couldn't see him.

"Behind you."

Leo looked over one shoulder and stopped immediately.

The guy swerved past.

Nothing happened.

To Leo.

Taiga tried to stop too.

His skates disagreed.

"Oh, fuck—"

He went straight into Leo.

Leo's arms came around him before either of them fell.

One around Taiga's back.

The other catching his side.

Taiga grabbed Leo's jacket with both hands on instinct.

Their skates slid half a meter.

Then stopped.

Stable.

Completely stable.

Taiga realized three things at once.

Leo was holding him.

Taiga was still holding Leo.

Their faces were very, very close.

Neither moved.

For one second, maybe two, Taiga forgot the rink existed.

He could see individual snowflakes caught in Leo's hair.

The pink across his cheeks from the cold.

The small crease between his eyebrows that appeared when he was concentrating.

The way his lips had parted when Taiga crashed into him.

Leo's eyes were fixed on his.

Then they dropped.

Not far.

Back up.

Color moved higher across Leo's face.

Taiga felt heat hit his own ears so fast it was embarrassing.

What the fuck?

Leo swallowed.

Taiga became abruptly aware of Leo's arm still around his back.

He pulled away.

"Fuck. Sorry."

Leo let him go just as quickly.

"No. You're fine. I stopped too fast."

"You stopped."

"Yeah."

"I didn't."

"That's usually how stopping works."

The joke arrived late.

Both of them knew it.

Taiga looked away.

"Whatever."

Leo rubbed the back of his neck.

"Nobody died."

"Great standard."

"Successful evening."

Taiga pushed forward again.

Leo followed beside him this time.

They skated.

That was all.

A stupid awkward collision.

Two people almost falling.

Nothing.

Taiga concentrated on the ice.

For almost thirty seconds.

Then his brain replayed the exact shape of Leo's mouth from six inches away.

Taiga nearly caught his skate on the turn.

"Careful," Leo said.

"I'm fine."

"Didn't say you weren't."

Taiga kept moving.

Awkward.

That was the word.

Awkward explained it.

Probably.

***

They stayed on the ice another half hour.

Taiga got better.

Not good.

Better.

Enough to pass the six-year-old from earlier once, which he considered a meaningful competitive achievement even if she had stopped to talk to her mother.

Leo disagreed.

"She was stationary."

"Still passed her."

"That's not a race."

"Sounds like loser talk."

"You're competing with a child."

"And winning."

Leo laughed hard enough to drift sideways.

Taiga noticed the laugh.

Then noticed that he had noticed it.

Annoying.

A lap later, Leo brushed his elbow against Taiga's while cutting inside.

Nothing.

Taiga still felt it.

He told himself this was because twenty minutes earlier he had collided with him.

That was a perfectly reasonable explanation.

Then Leo suggested another lap.

Taiga said yes.

After that one, Leo suggested one more because the lights had come on fully around the park.

Taiga said yes again.

Eventually his ankles started hurting enough to win.

They left the rink.

***

Leo wanted hot chocolate.

Taiga wanted coffee.

The nearest stall sold both and charged enough to qualify as organized theft.

They paid anyway.

Most of the benches under the lights were occupied, so they found one near the edge of the market.

The snow had thickened. Not enough to become a storm. Enough to collect on roofs, tables and the shoulders of people's coats.

Leo took a drink immediately.

"Fuck."

Taiga looked at him.

"You watched them pour it."

"I believed in thermodynamics."

"You waited six seconds."

"I was optimistic."

"That's your problem."

"One of them."

Leo blew over the cup.

Taiga took a drink of coffee.

Too hot.

He waited.

For a while they watched people move between the stalls.

Their shoulders touched once because the bench was narrow.

Taiga registered it.

He hated that he registered it.

Leo's phone rang.

He looked at the screen.

"Oh, shit."

"What?"

"My mother."

"Why is that shit?"

"Because I told her I was packing."

Taiga looked around at Acropolis Park.

Leo pointed at him.

"You are now legally involved in the lie."

"I am absolutely not."

Leo answered.

"Hola, mami."

The change was immediate.

Not personality.

Rhythm.

Leo spoke faster in Spanish, the words running together more easily than they ever did in English.

"Sí, mañana. Ya revisé."

Taiga understood tomorrow.

That was about it.

"No, el vuelo no está cancelado."

Leo closed his eyes.

"Mami, ya te mandé el boleto."

The voice on the other end continued.

Leo looked down at the yellow scarf.

"Sí, tengo la bufanda."

Pause.

"Sí, esa."

Taiga smiled into his coffee.

Leo kicked the side of Taiga's shoe.

"Sí, comí."

Another pause.

His expression softened.

"Yo también te quiero."

Taiga looked over.

Leo was staring at the snow.

"Sí. Chao. Chao, mami."

He waited.

"Mami."

Another second.

"Mami."

The call finally ended.

Leo lowered the phone.

"This is my future."

Taiga laughed.

"You sound different."

Leo glanced over.

"In Spanish?"

"Yeah."

"That's usually what happens when you change languages."

"Not that, idiot."

Leo smiled.

"You talk faster."

"My mother talks faster. It's survival."

"I understood tomorrow."

"Excellent."

"And scarf."

"Practically fluent."

Taiga took another drink.

"She called you Julio."

Leo looked at him.

"You noticed that?"

"I know what your name sounds like."

"Fair."

He wrapped both hands around the cup.

"Mom and Abuela mostly call me Julio. Everybody else usually says Leo."

"Why?"

Leo shrugged.

"No dramatic reason. Leo started when I was little and it stuck. Julio is just... family voice, I guess."

Taiga looked at him.

"Family voice."

"You know what I mean."

"I do."

Leo smiled.

There was nothing especially important about the information.

Taiga still filed it away immediately.

That was becoming a problem.

"Your English is annoyingly good," Taiga said.

Leo blinked.

"Annoyingly?"

"I thought you were from here when I met you."

"Seriously?"

"Yeah."

Leo looked pleased.

More pleased than the compliment deserved.

"Okay. I'm taking that."

"You went to a bilingual school, right?"

"Yeah. Me and Enzo. English since we were kids. School, games, internet, movies." He took a cautious drink. "By the time we got here, the hard part wasn't really speaking it. It was speaking it all day."

"What happens?"

"Sometimes around six my brain decides it has fulfilled its contractual obligations."

Taiga smiled.

"You still talk."

"I always talk."

"That's the problem."

"Exactly."

Leo nudged his shoe against Taiga's.

Taiga looked down.

Then back up.

Leo hadn't noticed anything.

Good.

"My house is loud," Leo continued. "Like, really loud. First night here, my roommate went to sleep at ten-thirty and I sat there wondering if everybody had died."

"You would've hated Room 317."

"Caleb would've had me assassinated."

"He would've made you a schedule first."

"Worse."

Taiga laughed.

Leo looked at him over the rim of the cup.

"Your family quiet?"

"Yeah."

"Going there after camp?"

"Yeah."

"Camp first for a week."

"Correct."

"You stayed there for the whole year before Olympus, right?"

"Pretty much."

"What did you actually do?"

Taiga looked at him.

"Work."

"No."

"That's a job with worse branding."

Taiga smiled despite himself.

"The camp was rebuilding."

Fair.

There was no reason not to.

So he did.

Not everything.

He told Leo about the camp being broke after the summer.

About the fundraiser.

About Keitaro's journal somehow becoming a published book.

About the sponsor who came after that and the expansion that followed.

Actually listened.

"Why'd you stay?"

Taiga rolled the cup between his palms.

"At first?"

"Yeah."

"I thought I owed them."

Leo didn't immediately ask who.

Good.

Taiga continued.

"Then after a while I just wanted to."

"That's different."

"Yeah."

Snow landed on Leo's scarf and disappeared into the yellow fabric.

Taiga watched one melt.

Leo said, "So why leave?"

The question should have been easy.

It was, actually.

"Because staying wasn't the only way to keep it."

Leo frowned slightly.

"What?"

Taiga looked toward the rink.

"Camp Buddy was home before I came here."

"Okay."

"For a while I treated that like it meant I had to stay there. If I left, then I was leaving it. Like those were the same thing."

"Then everyone else kept leaving and coming back anyway. Keitaro. Natsumi. Hunter. People who'd been scouts there years ago came back for the expansion." Taiga shrugged. "Eventually I figured out the camp wasn't going to stop being mine because I lived somewhere else."

"Eventually."

"Voluntarily?"

"Debatable."

Leo smiled.

About ignoring it.

About Keitaro threatening to fill out the application himself.

"Eleven days."

"That's not even that long."

"It was sitting on my desk."

"Ah. Active ignoring."

"Exactly."

Leo laughed.

The conversation moved after that.

Not because either of them deliberately changed it.

It just did.

Leo told him his sister had once hidden that yellow scarf for two weeks because she wanted to see whether he'd notice. He had.

One involved her stealing his headphones.

He didn't.

Then Leo asked about Yoichi.

"Fuck."

Leo looked over.

"What?"

"It's eight-twelve."

Leo checked his own phone.

"Oh."

Neither of them moved immediately.

Taiga noticed that too.

Leo finished the last of his hot chocolate.

"Train?"

"Yeah."

They stood.

Leo argued that weather forecasts were just educated pessimism.

Taiga told him meteorologists had done nothing to deserve that.

The train jolted once around a turn.

Leo caught Taiga's forearm.

Normal.

He let go immediately.

Taiga still felt the place where his hand had been.

Not normal.

That was possible.

People got awkward.

People got too physically aware after crashing into each other.

Problem solved by geography.

"Yeah."

"Yeah."

"I fly at eleven-twenty."

"I know."

"When are you back?"

The question came out before he decided to ask it.

Leo thought.

Camp Buddy for a week.

Then home.

Leo in Santo Domingo.

They would not be in the same place again for almost three weeks.

Three weeks.

"Oh," Taiga said.

Leo looked at him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

There it was.

A stupid answer.

"Text me when you get to camp."

"Why?"

"So I know the angry dog didn't eat you."

"Yuki likes me."

"Exactly what people say before dogs eat them."

"You've never met her."

For a second, neither of them moved.

Not like the rink.

Nothing weird.

Just the end of the evening.

"Night."

Taiga started toward Room 317.

After a few steps, he looked back.

Leo was already halfway to the next path.

Three weeks wasn't long.

He had spent longer than that away from people he cared about before.

It still sat wrong in his chest as he walked back through the snow, and unlike the rink, there wasn't even anything awkward he could blame it on.
