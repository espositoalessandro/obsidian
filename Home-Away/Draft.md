### Chapter 23 - Northbound

Taiga had typed *you awake?* when Leo came through the station doors. The clock over the ticket machines said 9:38. He deleted the text.

Leo had a duffel over one shoulder, a backpack over the other and the yellow scarf wound so high that only half his face showed. He spotted Taiga and raised an arm. The duffel slid down and hit him in the elbow.

"Ow. Hey!"

"You're early."

"Yeah, well, I woke up at seven and couldn't get back to sleep." Leo wrestled the strap back onto his shoulder. "Is that for me?"

Taiga handed him the coffee with milk. "Figured the line would be bad."

It was now. The station café had been all but empty when he'd gone in. Leo took a sip and made a little noise of approval.

"Aw, you got it right."

"You put an obscene amount of milk in it. It isn't hard to remember."

Leo smiled at him over the cup. Taiga had almost got through the morning without thinking about that stupid kiss on the cheek. Which was a lie. He'd thought about it getting dressed, on the walk here and again while the woman at the café asked if he wanted a lid.

"Come on," he said. "Platform seven."

At the stairs, Leo reached for Taiga's canvas bag. Taiga opened his mouth to object, then gave him the handle. His duffel was heavy enough, and Leo had already tucked his coffee into the outside pocket of his backpack so he could take it.

"What have you got in here?" Leo asked.

"Food. There's a rain jacket for you on top."

"I have one."

"You sent me a picture. That thing has holes under the arms."

"Those are vents."

"I could see your desk through them."

"Okay, but was your first thought really that I'd get cold?"

Taiga started up the stairs. "My first thought was that I'd have to listen to you complain about getting cold."

"Yeah. You'd probably have to do that either way."

The wind ran straight along the platform. They waited beneath the canopy, among families and a few people Taiga recognized from the mess hall. A woman was trying to get her kid to say goodbye to Olympus through a video call. The kid waved at a pigeon.

Leo shifted his bag to the other shoulder. "Do you usually go up on your own?"

"Since I started here, yeah. The others came in from all over at New Year. We met at camp."

"Huh." Leo pushed his scarf down with his chin. "I keep thinking about how for you this is just the train home, and I don't even know what direction we're going."

"North." Taiga took a drink. "You can have the window."

***

They got their duffels up onto the rack and pushed the smaller bags under the seats in front. Leo sat by the window. The train had barely cleared the platform when he pulled out his phone to take a picture.

"Of what?" Taiga asked. They were passing a wall with a drainpipe every ten feet.

"My mom asked me to let her know when we'd left."

"You could tell her in words."

"Yeah, but then how's she gonna know I got the window?"

Leo took another one when the wall gave way to the backs of houses. This time he leaned back so he could get Taiga in it. Taiga saw himself on the screen just in time to frown.

"Perfect. You wanna see?"

He did, actually. The window was bright behind Leo, and the bottom of the frame cut off half of Taiga's chin. Somehow it was still obvious which one of them had been caught off guard.

Leo lowered his phone a little. "You mind if I send that one?"

Taiga took another look. "You want your mom to think I always look like that?"

"She already knows you always look like that."

"What the fuck have you been telling her?"

Leo laughed. "Mostly that I beat you and you had to buy me a pizza."

"Send it, then."

Leo did. A little while later, when a reply made him laugh, he told Taiga his mom wanted them to bring their jackets when they went out. Even if it looked sunny. Especially if it looked sunny.

"Two rain jackets," Taiga said. "We're doing great."

"See? I knew this would work out."

Leo put his phone away and leaned against the glass. They'd been going for almost an hour. The houses were farther apart now, with fields between them and bare trees along the fence lines. Taiga could see the next stretch of track when they went around a curve. Usually by then he'd have his headphones on and his jacket bunched up against the window.

"Hey, when we get there," Leo said, "can you show me where you took that photo? The one you sent at New Year, with the lake."

Taiga knew the one. He'd taken it from the shore, with everybody standing on the pier. He could remember the cold getting through his gloves while he tried to keep the phone still.

"Yeah. It's just down from the cabins."

Leo nodded and looked out again.

Taiga had been trying to work out how to get Leo from the car to their cabin without Yuri getting to him first. He'd forgotten he could take him down to the water whenever he wanted. He could show him the rest of the place, too. Christ, he could show him the kitchen door that wouldn't shut until he and Yoichi had finally taken the whole damn thing off its hinges. Leo would probably want to know why they'd taken so long.

He rested his arm on the armrest. After a minute, Leo's sleeve came up against his. Taiga turned his hand over on his knee. Leo took it without turning away from the window.

***

They ate lunch between the train and the bus. By the time the road narrowed and the pines started coming right up to the shoulder, there were only four passengers left. Leo had his feet in the aisle and his backpack on the empty seat across from them.

Taiga leaned past Leo to look out. A row of mailboxes went by, then the bend where the road dipped sharply enough that his stomach always went with it. He reached across and pressed the stop button.

"Already?" Leo pulled his feet in.

"Yeah. Someone's picking us up here. It's another fifteen minutes by car."

Leo twisted round to get his backpack off the seat. Taiga usually had his bag on his lap well before the bend. He let the bus go on for a few more seconds before he reached for it.

"You can see the lake from the road after this," he said. "I'll tell you when to look."

The bus left them beside a gas station with two pumps and a bait shop with plywood over the windows. Taiga put his bags down by the bench. The last time he'd been there, the snow had reached the seat.

Leo looked back at Taiga. "This is it?"

"No."

"Good, because I was about to have questions."

He turned to watch the bus go, then sat down. He'd just got his phone out when a gray utility vehicle came around the bend, rattling a little over the broken asphalt. Taiga recognized the camp sticker on the windshield.

"Oh, good," he said. "It's Aiden."

Aiden pulled up beside the bench and leaned over to wave at them. Then he got out and hugged Taiga before Taiga could get his hand all the way up.

"Welcome back, buddy!" He gave Taiga's shoulder a squeeze as he let go. "You eat yet?"

"At the station."

"Oh, good. Did they feed you enough?"

Taiga caught Leo smiling. Jesus, he'd been here for about thirty seconds.

"Aiden, this is Leo." He hooked his thumb toward him. "Leo, Aiden."

"Yeah. Hi, I'm Leo."

"Aiden Flynn. Yoshinori sent me before he decided the schedule needed another clipboard."

Taiga snorted. "That sounds like him."

Aiden shook Leo's hand. "Thanks for coming all the way out here, buddy."

"I was promised manual labor."

Aiden's smile widened. "Oh, you'll get plenty of that."

He lifted Leo's duffel into the back. "Hop in wherever you like."

They put the rest of the bags in the back, and Taiga got in up front out of habit. There was a pile of cut branches where the plow had pushed the snow against a fence. From the back seat came the occasional rustle of Leo's jacket as he leaned from one window toward the other. After a while Taiga reached up to tap the glass beside him.

"Here. Through the trees."

Leo was quiet long enough that Taiga turned in his seat. Then the road came out along the water, and Leo went, "Oh," so softly Taiga almost missed it. Taiga remembered that sound from the overlook at Pinetree.

The lake was gray and wider than it usually looked in Taiga's photos. There was a pale strip on the far shore where the trees thinned. Taiga had been looking forward to seeing it all week. Now that it was in front of him, he kept turning to see how much of it Leo could make out from the back.

"First time this far out?" Aiden asked.

"More of a sea boy."

"Ahh. That explains the face."

"What face?"

"The lake face."

"That is not a thing."

"It absolutely is. Wait till you get down by the water. Gets even bigger from there."

The camp sign came up on the next bend. Someone had replaced the bracket on the bottom corner. The shine on the new bolts made the rest of it look a little worse, which was probably why Goro would make somebody paint it before summer. Then the turn opened up and Taiga could see the lodge, the empty path to the mess hall and the newer cabins farther back. Leo leaned forward between the seats.

"Are those the ones you helped with?"

"Some of them. See that one at the end with the steps?"

"Yeah."

"I spent half a morning under there looking for a screw I dropped."

Aiden pulled in beside the lodge. "And then he dropped another one."

"I found both."

"That's true. We never said he was fast."

Taiga opened his door. Yuki hit him from the side before he'd straightened up. He caught himself on the car and got a hand under her collar.

"Hi. Yeah, hi. Hang on, my foot's still in the car."

She whined and put both paws on his leg. There was already mud on his jeans and he hadn't even made it off the gravel.

"Tch. Look what crawled back."

Yoichi was leaning against the maintenance shed with his hands in his pockets. Taiga rubbed both sides of Yuki's head and waited for her to get down.

"Missed you too, dog."

"Didn't say that." He pushed himself off the wall and came closer anyway.

Leo came around the back of the car with Taiga's canvas bag in one hand. Yoichi gave him a slow look. "Who's this?"

"Leo." He put the bag down and offered his hand. Yoichi glanced at it, then at Leo's hiking boots.

"Those real, or you one of those idiots who buys boots to wear to coffee?"

"I asked the same thing," Taiga said.

Leo looked between them. "This feels coordinated."

"It isn't," Taiga and Yoichi said together.

Leo's smile widened. Yoichi clicked his tongue. "Psh. Great. He's annoying too."

Yuki finally noticed Leo. She lifted her head, and he crouched down with his hand low and waited for her to come to him.

"Hey."

"Don't baby her," Yoichi said.

"I haven't touched her."

"Your voice is annoying."

"That's genetic."

Yuki sniffed his hand, then his jeans. By the time he'd sat back on his heels she had her chin on his wrist.

"Traitor," Yoichi muttered.

"Should I apologize?"

"Yeah."

"Sorry."

"Fuck you."

Leo started laughing. Yuki immediately tried to climb into his lap. Taiga took the bag from the gravel before she got any ideas about the food inside it.

Yoshinori came out of the lodge with a clipboard under his arm. He smiled when he saw Taiga and put a warm hand on his shoulder.

"Good to have you back. How was the trip?"

"Good. Aiden was there when we got off."

"Barely had time to sit down," Leo added.

"And you must be Julio."

Leo's head swung toward Taiga. "Why did you do that?"

"What? It's your name."

"Only my mom calls me that!" Leo sighed and straightened up to shake Yoshinori's hand. "Yes, sir. Just Leo."

"I'm Scoutmaster Yoshinori. You do not need to call me sir, though."

"Give him twenty minutes," Taiga said.

Leo ignored him. "Nice to meet you, Yoshinori."

"And you, Leo. Thank you for coming to help."

Yuri pushed the lodge door open with her elbow. She had a basket full of towels and a smear of white paint on her wrist.

"KYAAA! You really came! Come here!"

Taiga took one step back. "Yuri."

She set the basket down on the step and hugged him anyway. "Aww~ look at you! You look wonderful! College agrees with you."

"It doesn't."

"It absolutely does. Your shoulders—"

"No."

Leo turned away. Taiga pointed at him from inside the hug. "I can see you."

That only made him laugh harder. Yuri finally released Taiga and caught sight of Leo beside the car.

"Oh! You must be Julio!"

Leo glanced at Taiga. "Yeah. Just Leo. Hi."

"I'm Yuri," she said, taking his hand with both of hers. "Welcome to Camp Buddy! Yoshinori told us Taiga was bringing a friend from Olympus."

Taiga looked at Yoshinori.

"I had to give everyone an accurate head count," Yoshinori said.

"That doesn't explain her."

"Nothing explains her." Yoichi shut the back of the car and picked up both duffels.

Yuri ignored him. "So you're on the soccer team with Taiga?"

Leo nodded. "And Computer Science."

Her smile sharpened. Taiga knew that look. "I know what that face means."

"What face?"

"The one you make before everybody at the table gets embarrassed."

Leo looked between them. "That sounds promising."

"It isn't."

Yuri laughed. "Oh, I like him."

"Crazy Lady, quit scaring Sunball before dinner."

Yoichi was already halfway across the yard by the time Leo said, "Sunball?"

"Yeah. That's you now."

"Means he likes you," Taiga said.

"I CAN HEAR YOU."

Yuri covered her mouth. "Come on, boys. Aiden's making dinner, Dad is pretending he isn't waiting to inspect the new volunteer, and I promise to behave."

"No one believes you," Taiga said.

"Hihi~"

Yoshinori pointed out the path to Cabin Two. The beds were made, dinner was at seven, and if Taiga wanted a look at the maintenance list he could see it in the morning. Taiga had only glanced at the clipboard once.

"If you say anything, I'm leaving," he told Yoshinori.

"I was only going to say that it is good to see you bring a friend here."

That was worse. Taiga took the basket off the step and followed Yoichi, who was telling Leo to keep his boots clear of the puddle by the porch. He'd gone straight through it himself.

***

Cabin Two smelled of cold wood and floor cleaner. Leo left his boots on the mat without being asked. Taiga gave him the lower bunk by the window and put his own duffel down across from it.

"Did you ever sleep here?" Leo asked.

"Yeah. Up there, last summer."

Taiga put a hand on the post of the bunk above him. The three marks he'd made with his pocketknife were still there. One was longer than the others because he'd slipped.

Leo came over to see what he was looking at. "Oh, did you do those?"

"Maybe."

"Did somebody keep stealing your bed?"

"No. I was bored."

"That's worse. I came up with a whole reason for you."

Taiga grinned into his bag as he pulled out a towel. He tossed it onto his bunk and showed Leo the bathroom at the back. There were low wooden partitions between the shower stations, two stalls with doors and a tile floor that would take forever to warm up.

"Every cabin has one of these?"

"Yeah."

Leo stuck his head back into the room. "I had a whole speech ready about being brave in an outhouse."

"I can find you one if you're disappointed."

"No, no. I think I can live with the bathroom."

He went back to his bunk and began emptying his backpack onto the mattress. The gloves Taiga had told him to bring came out first, then an extra pair of socks and a whole tangled handful of cables. Taiga got his own charger out. They shifted the little dresser between the bunks so they could both reach their phones while they charged. On his own, Taiga usually left his on the floor.

***

Dinner was stew. Aiden raised his ladle as they came in.

"This is preseason food. Hot, filling, and there's enough of it that nobody gets to complain they're hungry later."

Yoichi, already eating, looked into his bowl. "It's stew."

"Correct. Good job."

"Psh."

"Keep talking and I'll assume you don't want seconds."

Yoichi pulled his bowl closer. "Didn't say that."

Most of the tables were pushed against the walls for the off-season. At the long one nearest the kitchen, Goro had a folder open beside his bowl. He closed it when Taiga came over.

"Taiga, welcome back. It is good to see you here again."

"Good to see you too," Taiga said.

Goro gave him a small nod and turned to Leo. "Mendez, I presume."

Leo stood a little straighter. "Yes, sir."

"Ehem. Yoshinori informed me that you chose to spend your university recess assisting us with preseason preparations. Your help is appreciated. If you intend to help, I expect you to take the work seriously."

"Dad," Yuri said, pulling out a chair.

"What? It is a reasonable expectation."

"It is," Leo said. "I can do serious."

Taiga looked at him. Leo caught it. "Sometimes."

Aiden set a bowl down in front of Leo. "Eat first. Goro can turn anything into an orientation speech if you give him enough time."

"Flynn."

"What? I'm helping."

Yuri sat beside her father. "Dad has been in preseason mode since February."

"I have been planning since February."

"That's exactly what I said."

Leo took a bite, then reached for his water. "Oh, wow. This is really good."

"That's what I like to hear, buddy!" Aiden got him another piece of bread. Taiga had forgotten how quickly it happened. At Olympus he had to get seconds himself.

Lloyd and Darius came in while Taiga was wiping stew off his thumb. Lloyd had a roll of drawings under his arm.

"TAIGA! There he is! You came back during actual work season. I knew college hadn't ruined you."

"Hey, Taiga," Darius said.

"Hey, Dar."

Darius looked him over once. "You still have all your fingers."

"Barely."

"Good."

Taiga nodded toward Leo. "This is Leo."

"Oh! New volunteer." Lloyd put the roll down and shook Leo's hand with enough enthusiasm for both of them. "Lloyd Sirius. Architect, scoutmaster, occasional victim of unrealistic deadlines."

Darius held out his own hand. "Darius Najjar. Foreman. I build things. I'll be with you on the cabin row tomorrow."

"Nice to meet you," Leo said.

"Same."

Lloyd pointed at Taiga. "We taught this one most of what he knows about construction."

"Some of it," Darius said.

"Okay, rude," Taiga said.

Lloyd had already turned back to Leo. "Important question. Birthday?"

Leo blinked. "What?"

"Birthday."

"May twenty-fourth."

"Gemini! I knew it."

"You asked after meeting him," Darius said.

"Confirmation is still a valid scientific method."

Darius slid the drawings clear of the bowls and sat down beside Lloyd. Leo leaned forward to ask what he'd done that was Gemini, and Taiga kept eating while Lloyd told him.

Yuri lasted about three minutes before she began the interrogation she'd promised was innocent.

"So, Leo, what exactly is House Apollo? Taiga's explanations are usually about six words long."

Leo laughed. "That's generous. It's one of the God Houses. A lot of athletes live there, and a bunch of the soccer team ends up there."

"And you?"

"Yeah."

Yuri's eyes moved between them. "So you two train together, study together, and live in the same House?"

Taiga stopped eating. "Not the same room."

"I didn't say the same room."

"You were thinking it."

"I was thinking what a convenient arrangement that is."

"For soccer."

"Of course." Leo's ears had started turning pink. Yuri leaned toward him. "Does he act this defensive at Olympus too?"

Leo glanced at Taiga. "All the time."

"Traitor."

Yuri laughed. "Oh, this is going to be fun."

Goro put down his spoon. "Yuri."

"Yes, Dad?"

"Allow our guest to eat without interrogating him."

"I am getting to know him."

"You are embarrassing Taiga."

"That's part of getting to know him."

Yoichi snorted into his bowl. "Dynamite's gonna fucking explode before dessert."

"Shut up."

"Dessert," Aiden said, as if that were the only important part of the exchange.

Goro asked Leo how he was doing in his classes. While he answered, Yoichi tore his bread into pieces and kept looking over at him as if he were waiting for his turn. As soon as Leo paused, Yoichi pointed his spoon at him.

"So, Sunball. You actually good at soccer, or do you just run around smiling until people give you the ball?"

Leo swallowed. "I guess both?"

"Better answer."

"Better than what?" Taiga asked.

"Your dumbass answer when I asked about Olympus. 'Team's fine.' Psh. You make everything sound lame."

"That was months ago."

"Still lame."

Leo looked between them. "Okay, yes. He absolutely does that. He'll get an assist and describe the match like he went to the post office."

Yoichi slapped the table once. "Exactly!"

"Fuck both of you."

"See?" Yoichi said to Leo. "Now he's emotional."

Taiga reached for his water while Aiden laughed. By the time the bowls were empty, the sun had gone behind the ridge. There was still light out on the water.

"Leo, has Taiga shown you the dock yet?" Yuri asked.

"Not yet."

"You should go before the week is over. Sunset over the lake is beautiful." She smiled. "Very romantic, too."

Leo looked down at his bowl. Taiga looked at Yuri. "Why."

"What? It's true."

"You know what you're doing."

"Yes."

Yoichi snorted. "Dumbass walked straight into that one."

Taiga picked up his bowl and Leo's, but Aiden met him halfway to the kitchen and took them.

"Nope. You two traveled all afternoon. Go."

"That's not a rule," Yoichi said. He left his bowl by the sink and took a step toward the door.

"It is tonight."

"You make shit up constantly, Buttcheeks."

"That's one of the privileges of being a scoutmaster." Aiden tossed him a towel. "Dry."

"Fuck."

"Tomorrow you can work until you regret volunteering," Aiden told Taiga. "Tonight, show Leo around before it gets dark."

Yuri passed with a stack of bowls. "Yes, Taiga. Show Leo somewhere quiet and scenic."

"Yuri."

"What? He came all this way!"

Leo covered his mouth. Taiga hated spring break.

"Come on," he said, reaching for his jacket. "There's still enough light."

Leo stood immediately.

***

The path to the lake was soft enough to make them take the long way around the field. Taiga showed Leo the craft lodge on the way past, then pointed to the maintenance shed, which Leo said he'd be able to find by following the noise anyway.

"Is this where you had SportsFest?" Leo asked as they came alongside the field.

"Yeah. The far end gets muddy before this one does. We used to lose a ball in the trees every other day."

He'd answered sooner than he'd expected to. Leo stopped to knock a lump of dirt off his boot against a tree root, and when he caught up he asked how far they were from the lake.

"Right there, past those two cabins."

Part of the dock was roped off. Two planks near the shore had orange tape on them, and one looked soft enough that Taiga could see the edge lifting from the path. He went down toward the water and stopped where the mud turned to little flat stones.

"Here. This is where I took it."

Leo came to stand beside him and got his phone out. Taiga saw him open an album called *Camp*. He had enough time to take that in before the photo came up. The water on the screen looked a darker blue than it did now. Hunter and the others stood near the end of the pier in their winter coats, facing the sunset.

"You made a folder?"

"Yeah." Leo swiped once and there was the bonfire. "Otherwise I have to scroll past like four hundred pictures from practice to find anything."

"How many photos of camp do you even have?"

Leo tilted the phone away. "It's a very efficient folder."

Taiga reached for it. Leo took a step back and nearly got a shoe full of mud, so Taiga caught his sleeve and pulled him back onto the stones.

"Okay, I see why you've got planks," Leo said, putting his phone away.

"Those two with the tape need to come out. Stay off them."

"Can you tell just by looking?"

Taiga pointed to the corner that had lifted. Leo bent down to see where he meant, his sleeve still bunched in Taiga's hand. Then he straightened and turned back toward the far shore. For a moment the water caught what was left of the light right in front of them. Taiga let go of the sleeve, and their arms stayed together where they stood.

"It's bigger when you're down here," Leo said.

"That's what Aiden told you."

"Yeah, well. Now I'm down here."

Taiga stayed until he couldn't see the orange tape clearly anymore. When they went back to the cabin, Leo found the right path on his own.

***

Yoichi banged on the door at six forty-five on Sunday morning. Taiga knew it was him because nobody else would hit a door that hard and then shout through it anyway.

"Wake up, dorks!"

Taiga checked his phone. "It's six forty-five."

"Yeah!"

"Die!"

"Breakfast in fifteen!"

Leo drew a breath from under the blanket. "I'm dead!"

"Good! More eggs!"

His footsteps went down the porch. Taiga got up and put both feet on the cold floor. Across the room a hand came out from under Leo's blanket and groped for the phone until it knocked against the bedpost.

"Does he do this every morning?"

"Only when there's something he wants you to do."

"Oh. Cool. That's so much worse."

They got to breakfast in time for the eggs. Leo's hair was still flat on one side and sticking up all over on the other. Yuri saw him before he could sit down.

"Oh my."

Leo closed his eyes. "I know. They'll start behaving in a couple of hours."

"I love it."

"Thank you."

Taiga laughed. Yuri turned to him. "Taiga, yours is hardly better."

Taiga stopped laughing. Leo did not.

Yoshinori waited until Aiden had sat down with his toast before opening the clipboard. "Good morning, everyone."

Yoichi groaned into his coffee.

"Today we will focus on the west cabin row and lower trail. The weather should remain dry, so I would like the exterior work completed first."

Aiden raised a hand. "Kitchen inventory?"

"After lunch."

"Then I have time to start the soup before lunch."

Goro looked up. "We had stew last night."

"Different soup!"

"That is not my concern."

"Yoichi, the tree's still across the lower trail?" Yoshinori asked.

"Yeah. Saw it when I took Yuki out yesterday."

"And then?"

"Then I took her the other way."

Yoshinori waited.

"What? She wanted to keep walking."

"Please clear it before lunch. Come find me if you need a hand. Darius, take Taiga and Leo through cabins six to ten. Window seals, moisture check, mattresses, shutters. If you finish early, report to Yuri for equipment inventory."

"Got it," Darius said.

Lloyd lifted one hand. "I'll finish the expansion-side drainage notes and join the lower trail after that. I want another look at the runoff before the next rain."

"Good," Yoshinori said.

"Perfect," Yuri said. Taiga knew that tone. "You two can have the cabins all to yourselves."

Leo lowered his coffee very carefully.

"For inspections," Taiga said.

"I know."

"Then stop smiling."

"I am allowed to be happy that my volunteers get along." Yuri laughed. "Alright, alright. When you're done, come find me. I have the inventory labels ready."

***

Darius handed Leo a moisture meter in the first cabin. "Green is good. Yellow, write it down. Red, come get one of us."

Leo checked the display. "Simple."

"Usually."

Darius left him to try it on the wall below the window. The latch on Taiga's side gave after he pressed the frame in with his thumb. He remembered having to do the same thing last summer. He'd meant to see to it then. He tightened the latch and tried the window again. Darius checked it on his way out.

"It holds."

"That was praise," Taiga told Leo.

Leo laughed.

By the third cabin they'd got into a rhythm. Leo checked the walls and turned the mattresses while Taiga took care of the windows and shutters. They swapped when Taiga got sick of reaching above his head. For a while there was nothing much to say. He could tell by the sound what Leo was doing on the other side of the room. When the window finally slid shut without sticking, he opened it once more just to hear it go.

"Taiga, can you look at this?"

Leo was crouched by the next window. He pushed a finger into the very bottom corner of the frame. The wood gave a little.

"Just this corner?"

"Yeah. The number went straight up when I got close."

Taiga crouched beside him. A hairline gap in the seal ran along the bottom of the glass. He touched it with a fingernail, and Leo leaned in far enough for their sleeves to brush.

"That's probably where it got in," Taiga said.

"Should I get the sealant?"

"Mark it first. Don't seal damp wood." Darius came over and pressed his thumb to the frame.

"Because we'd trap the moisture?" Leo asked.

"Yeah. We'll see how much dries with the window open."

Leo wrote it down, then moved the curtain aside so it wouldn't blow over the wet wood. By then the sun had got across the floor and lit up a trail of dust behind the dresser. Taiga caught the side and pulled it out so he could sweep.

There was a dead mouse underneath.

"Oh. That's why it smelled in here," Leo said.

Taiga held out the dustpan.

"Wait, me? What did I do?"

"You wanted the full experience."

"I wanted to carry lumber."

"You can carry that with one hand."

Leo made a face, but he went for a bag and held the dustpan himself. By the time they sat down on the steps for a drink he was telling Taiga he'd have preferred to find two. At least then they could've taken one each.

Yuki showed up and pushed her nose against Leo's bottle until he moved it out of the way. She set a paw on his boot. Leo bent forward to scratch under her chin.

"You're good at this stuff," he said.

"Checking windows?"

"You took that latch apart and put it back together while I was trying to figure out which way to hold the meter." He leaned back on his palms. "Have you missed doing it?"

"Some of it." Taiga rolled the bottle over his knee. He'd have been happy to go another year without finding that mouse. "I like having something I can finish by lunch."

"Yeah, I get that. We finish something for Rao and he puts part two up before I can close my laptop."

Taiga snorted. Yuki leaned harder into Leo's leg. He slid down a step so she could get both front paws across his knees.

"DYNAMITE!"

Taiga closed his eyes. There it was.

Yoichi came up the path with a bow saw in one hand. He looked at them on the step. Then at Yuki.

"Lazy assholes."

"What do you want?" Taiga said.

"Break ended."

"You're not my boss."

"No shit. I'd fire you."

Leo looked down at the saw. "What's that for?"

"Tree came down over the drainage cut. Need another set of hands."

"How much have you done?" Taiga asked.

"I got the saw out."

"Very demanding work."

"Yeah. I nearly had to take my jacket off."

"You told Yoshinori?" Taiga asked.

"Yeah, he told me to fetch you."

"So ask normally."

"I did."

"You yelled."

"So what?" Yoichi turned to Leo. "You know how to use a bow saw?"

"Yes."

"Really?"

"My dad has a yard."

"That is not the same."

"Show me."

Yoichi stared for another second. "Fine. Don't cut your fingers off. Crazy Lady hates paperwork."

Leo scrambled back for his gloves. Taiga stood there with Yuki long enough for him to catch up.

***

The lower trail was still wet from the thaw. The pine lay across the drainage channel with all its branches still on it. Yoichi handed Leo the saw.

"Hang on. Which one do you want me to start with?" Leo asked.

"That one over the ditch." Yoichi pointed. He stayed right where he was until Taiga dropped the first armful of loose brush at his feet.

"What?"

"You can start carrying these up while he cuts."

"Psh. All right, Dynamite. I heard you."

"Psh. At least Sunball knows how to listen," Yoichi said.

"That was actually nice."

"Wasn't nice. Most dumbasses don't."

"I'll still take it."

A little later Taiga heard them arguing about what counted as a small branch. They'd apparently got as far as measuring one with their hands.

Taiga hauled brush up to the path while Leo dealt with the branches. Yoichi carried two loads up, then found a dry spot to sit until Leo called him over to help shift a branch that was stuck down in the ditch. By the time Lloyd came down to look at the ditch, there was room for water to run underneath what was left of the trunk. Then Yoichi put one foot on the bank to come up, slipped and sent a fan of dirty water up the back of Leo's jeans.

Leo looked down at himself. "You did that while I was helping you."

Yoichi planted his other foot and hauled himself up. "You gonna tell your jeans?"

"They were warned." Leo brushed at the mud once and gave up. "Can we say I got it doing something impressive?"

"That stick you carried over was pretty big."

"Dude. Thanks."

Yoichi snorted. He handed Leo his bottle from the dry side of the ditch and took the saw back. They walked up the path ahead of Taiga, still arguing about the size of the branch.

Lunch was sandwiches. Aiden had started the soup, but it needed a few more hours and he wouldn't let anybody take a spoon to it. Leo ate two sandwiches and went back for another. Aiden intercepted him at the counter with a whole plate to take to the table.

"There we go. You boys work all morning, you eat."

Yoichi took the top one off the plate before Leo sat down.

"You're on your third sandwich," Aiden told him.

"Different."

"Of course."

Leo took a bite, then looked toward the kitchen. "So you're the one who does most of the cooking?"

"Me and whoever I can drag in to help. During summer, everybody gets involved sooner or later."

"Don't recruit him yet," Taiga said.

"Hey, I wasn't going to."

"You looked at him like free labor."

Aiden laughed. "Well, he *is* free labor."

Leo pointed his sandwich at Taiga. "I was warned."

"Not enough."

"Nothing's enough for Sunball," Yoichi muttered. "He volunteered for this shit."

Leo smiled. "And yet you keep giving me jobs."

"Yeah, because you actually do them."

Leo dropped his eyes to his plate. Taiga could still see the smile from the other side of the table. He pulled his feet back when Leo stretched one muddy leg under it.

Yuri joined them a minute later, with the inventory sheets already under her arm.

***

Equipment inventory took most of the afternoon. Yuri had cleared a whole bench in the shed and labeled the shelves by color. Leo leaned in to get a better look.

"Blue means inspected?"

"Exactly!"

"Yellow?"

"Needs cleaning."

"Red?"

"Repair or replace."

Leo looked at the labeled bins. "This is beautiful."

Taiga groaned. "Do not encourage her."

"Thank you!" Yuri looked personally vindicated. "If I have to stare at storage shelves for three hours, they may as well look nice."

Taiga pointed at Yoshinori's clipboard across the room. "He organizes."

"Boringly."

Yoshinori looked up. "I heard that."

"You were supposed to."

Taiga counted paddles while Leo put batteries in the lanterns and switched them on one by one. Yuri inspected fabrics and pulled things out of cupboards Taiga wouldn't have thought to check if he'd been given a week. At one point Leo opened a tin and lifted a handful of old friendship bracelet thread.

"This feels dangerous."

"Put it back," Taiga said.

Yuri saw the colors in his hand. Her eyes went from Taiga's hair to Leo's. "Ooooh!"

"No."

"I haven't said anything!"

"You don't have to."

Leo looked down at the thread. His ears turned pink. Yuri held a length of yellow and a length of red together. "Come on. You have to admit they'd make a cute pair."

"They're thread."

"Yes. Matching thread."

"Yuri."

She laughed. "Fine! No bracelets."

Leo sat down on an equipment crate, laughing. Yuri put the lengths back in the tin.

By late afternoon, Leo had a streak of dust down his sleeve and one cuff on his tribute jeans was still wet. The last lantern wouldn't turn on. He took it over to Yuri's side of the bench and came back with a red label on his thumb and a cloth to see if cleaning the contacts would help. Taiga waited by the door for him. He'd been expecting to spend some part of the day looking over to see if Leo was bored or wanted a break. Apart from that first minute at breakfast, he'd barely had a chance.

Yoshinori came by to tell them they could leave the rest until morning. There was rain in the forecast, so they'd start indoors and see how the dock looked once it cleared. Yuri collected the sheets from the bench. Leo peeled the label off his thumb and stuck it to the lantern on his way out.

"You coming?" he asked.

Taiga held the door for him. "What, you don't need one more job?"

"I need to get out of these jeans before they dry that way."

"They look better now."

"They were fine before!"

"Except for the pocket with the hole in it. You had your phone in there on the train."

Leo slapped a hand to his thigh. Then he found his phone in his jacket and brought it out to show Taiga. It made both of them laugh more than it deserved.

Back in the cabin they left their boots on the mat and took the two showers with an empty station between them. Taiga was rinsing sawdust out of his hair when Leo called across the partition.

"Why are half the hooks outside the wet area?"

"So your clothes stay dry."

"That makes sense." The water kept running next door. Then Leo said, "I was hoping it was something stranger."

"Sorry to disappoint you."

"I'll recover."

At dinner Aiden's soup finally made it out of the kitchen. Nobody had much to say for the first few minutes. Taiga went up for more bread and heard Yoichi ask Leo if he'd come out and have a look at Yuki's favorite stick, which Aiden was threatening to move from the steps. Leo took his bowl with him. Through the mess hall window Taiga saw them sit down on either side of the stick, the two of them talking while Yuki tried to get her nose into Leo's soup. He left them to it and took the bread back to the table.

***

Leo got one bar of Wi-Fi in Cabin Two, sitting on the floor with his back against the bunk. He held his phone up until a photo went through to Enzo. Taiga sat on the opposite bed, rubbing the mark his boot had left above his sock.

"Enzo wants to know where the hot counselors are," Leo said.

"What did you send him?"

"Yuki."

"Helpful."

"He says she has leadership energy." Leo turned the screen around. Yuki filled nearly all of it. You could just make out the heel of Yoichi's boot behind her.

"He's right about that."

Leo sent something back and set his phone down to charge. For a while the only sound in the cabin was an intermittent knock from the window. Taiga knew he could stop it by folding a piece of paper into the gap. He'd fixed enough windows for one day.

"I thought Yoichi was gonna tell me to piss off at dinner," Leo said.

"Why?"

"Yuki had her whole face in my bowl." Leo brought his knee up and rested an arm on it. "He went in and got me another one. Came back with it and told me the stick was still under my foot."

"Sounds like him."

"I know." Leo looked toward the window. "I kinda get why you miss it here."

Taiga put his foot down. "Even after this morning?"

"I think tomorrow I can be dead louder. Get his attention before he gets to the door."

Taiga threw his pillow at him. Leo caught it against his chest and scrambled up onto his bed when Taiga stood.

"Thanks. I wanted this one."

"Give it back."

"I need emotional support."

"You have your own."

"This one has history."

Taiga got up and took one corner. Leo held the other for as long as it took Taiga to get a knee onto the mattress, then let go without warning. Taiga caught himself with one hand on the bed and the other against Leo's side. Leo had leaned back against the wall. His elbow had slipped into the gap beside the mattress.

"You stuck?"

Leo pulled it free. "I'm good."

He looked up at Taiga. His hair was drying badly around his ears. Taiga started to lift his hand, but Leo put his own over it, just for a second. The room went very quiet. Then Leo glanced down at the pillow still trapped under Taiga's knee.

"You did all that and you're gonna squash it?"

Taiga looked down too. He could feel the heat spreading over his face.

"Better than letting you have it."

He pulled it out and went back to his own bunk. Leo got hold of his blanket with one foot and dragged it up from the end of the bed. Taiga switched off the lamp on the dresser before he could see whether Leo's face had gone as red as his.

Some time later, after he'd almost started to drift off, Leo said, "Hey, Taiga?"

"Yeah?"

"Thanks for bringing me."

Taiga rolled onto his back. He could just make out the edge of the other mattress where a bit of light reached it from the window.

"Yeah," he said. Then, before Leo could go quiet again, "I'm glad you came."

Leo's blanket rustled. "Me too."

Somewhere out by the lodge, Yuki barked once. Taiga waited for Yoichi to tell her to shut up. When he did, it sounded like he was already half asleep. Taiga smiled before he could help it and pulled his pillow closer.
