# Chapter 12 — Working Plan

## Working title: **The Bubble Pops**

Inspired by **"The Girl in the Bubble"** from *Wicked 2*.

The title is currently thematic / working, not locked.

---

# Chapter 12 — Core Dramatic Job

This is the **ending chapter of Section / Act I**.

It has a difficult double job:

1. **End the first semester / school arc cleanly.**
2. **Begin the actual romantic arc.**

Do **not** spend half the chapter re-proving that life at Olympus keeps moving or that Olympus has become Taiga's life. Chapter 11 already did that.

Chapter 12 should close school efficiently and then give the rink / one-on-one outing the majority of the chapter.

### Approximate weight

- **25–30%:** finals / football wrap / people leaving
- **70–75%:** Leo + Taiga outing, rink, hot drinks / conversation, separation

The chapter should eventually **become the rink chapter**, not feel like a finals chapter with a romantic scene attached at the end.

---

# What Act I Is Actually Ending On

Taiga already has a stable model for Leo:

> Leo is hot.  
> Leo is my friend.  
> I like spending time with him.  
> Those facts do not contradict each other.

That model has worked since Chapter 6.

Chapter 12 does **not** need Taiga to realize he is in love, or even cleanly conclude that he has a crush.

Its job is smaller and more important:

> **Taiga realizes that "friend" is no longer enough to explain how he behaves around Leo.**

He enters the outing with the sincere / deluded assumption:

> He's my friend.

He exits with something closer to:

> ...is he just that?

The bubble is not ignorance of sexual attraction. Taiga already knows Leo is attractive.

The bubble is the belief that **sexual attraction + friendship fully explains the relationship**.

Chapter 12 pops that bubble.

---

# Revised Romance Architecture

## Act I — Awareness

Core movement:

> **Something about Leo has stopped fitting inside "friend."**

Chapter 12 is the ignition point.

Taiga does not solve the feeling. He simply becomes aware that there is now a question.

After the rink, he knows something is wrong / different in the way he reacts to Leo.

---

## Act II — Recognition / Realization

Act II should no longer end vaguely on:

> I still don't know what this is.

That is too evasive for two twenty-year-olds who both already have relationship history.

Act II is where Taiga:

- becomes genuinely nervous around Leo in specific situations;
- sometimes pulls back because he notices the pattern;
- denies / minimizes what is happening;
- tries to restore "normal friendship";
- misses Leo when he is absent;
- notices when Leo is not in a room;
- checks for messages;
- becomes more interested in plans because Leo said yes;
- seeks Leo out even while telling himself he should create distance;
- increasingly chooses Leo anyway.

The key contradiction is:

> **Taiga keeps pulling back whenever he notices the pattern, while his actual choices keep moving him closer.**

Longing happens before full admission.

By the end of Act II, after the rupture / repair, Taiga should **know what he wants**.

Leo's eventual idea:

> **"I don't want to change wherever we were going."**

should force the issue into a simple choice.

Taiga does not need to define the relationship yet.

He does need to realize:

> I like Leo.  
> I want more from him than friendship.  
> I want to keep moving toward whatever this is.

Act II is the **realization act**.

---

## Act III — Enactment / Going There

Act III is **not**:

> What do I want from Leo?

Taiga already knows.

It is:

> **I know I want him. Now we naturally keep moving toward each other.**

This makes later material stronger:

- Taiga invites Leo to Camp Buddy knowingly.
- When he eventually says **"I knew what I was doing when I invited you"**, he really did.
- He knows the invitation is personally meaningful.
- He wants Leo in that part of his life.
- Jealousy, Kieran, mutual expectation and sexual intimacy happen because both boys are already moving somewhere together.

The unresolved issue is not attraction / desire.

It is the **shape, boundaries and definition** of what they are becoming.

---

## Act IV — Crystallization / Definition

Act IV crystallizes the already-existing trajectory into:

> **actual boyfriends**

The question is no longer:

> Do I want Leo?

or:

> Are we moving toward something?

Those are settled.

The remaining threshold is:

> **Are we ready to define this as a relationship and accept the commitment / label that comes with it?**

Working macro progression:

> **Act I:** Something is happening.  
> **Act II:** I want it to happen.  
> **Act III:** We let it happen.  
> **Act IV:** We choose what it is.

# School / Semester Closure

Keep this efficient.

We mainly need:

## Finals

- Finals pressure / studying.
- Enough academic texture to make the semester feel like it is actually ending.
- CS declaration is already settled from Chapter 11. No further academic decision.
- We do **not** need a farewell tour through every course.

## Football

Give Mercer the **last football word of the semester**.

No new milestone.

Possible function:
- break program;
- concise evaluation;
- reminder that the break does not reset Taiga's progress;
- expectation that he comes back ready.

The exact line / scene is still open.

Mercer's function is closure, not another threshold.

## Departure transition

People leave / obligations disappear only as much as needed to create the circumstance for the outing.

Do not spend pages philosophizing about life moving on.

The useful causal chain is:

> finals end → football pauses → people leave → Taiga is still on campus → Leo is still on campus → Leo asks him to go skating

The removal of the ensemble should feel logistical, not engineered.

# Engineering the "Date"

The outing must feel date-like to the **reader** before Taiga is willing to interpret it that way.

Taiga enters with:

> We're friends. Everyone else left. We're hanging out.

And technically, that remains a plausible interpretation.

The outing naturally takes a date shape:

> travel together → winter rink → physical learning / play → drinks → increasingly personal conversation → walk / train home → separation before break

Nobody should call it a date.

Leo does not need to behave explicitly romantically.

The point is that **Taiga himself is already treating Leo differently**, without having admitted what that means.

# Rink Progression — Current Locked Working Choreography

## 1. Enter rink

- Taiga and Leo arrive.
- Taiga has never skated.
- Taiga immediately struggles with balance.
- He is focused on not falling, not on romance.

## 2. Leo takes Taiga's hands

Important:

> **Leo takes Taiga's hands. He does not offer them and wait.**

If Leo simply held his hands out and asked, Taiga would absolutely refuse.

The contact must arise naturally from Leo deciding:

> okay, you're useless, give me these

Leo skates backward while holding both of Taiga's hands, like a ski / skating instructor teaching a child.

For the first minute or two, **Taiga does not register the intimacy**.

He is too busy:
- balancing;
- listening to instructions;
- managing his feet;
- trying not to die.

This is important because the contact itself does not begin as romantically charged.

## 3. Taiga becomes competent enough to notice

After a minute or two, Taiga gains enough confidence that skating no longer consumes all of his attention.

Only then does his awareness shift.

He realizes:

> We're still holding hands.

The pressure / contact starts becoming unusually salient.

He becomes genuinely **nervous**.

This is new.

Sexual attraction to Leo has never made Taiga chronically nervous around him before.

That is why this reaction matters.

Taiga retracts first, under a perfectly reasonable excuse.

Working energy:

> "Right. I think I got it. Let me go."

Exact prose TBD.

Leo lets go without making a thing of it.

He keeps skating backward.

Taiga can tell himself the contact ended because he no longer needed help.

But on some level, he knows he pulled away because he became too conscious of it.

This is the **first crack**.

---

# 4. Second physical beat — collision / embrace

Give them a few minutes of normal skating after the hand-holding.

Enough time for Taiga to settle and file the nervousness away.

Leo continues skating backward.

Then:

- someone approaches / almost bumps Leo from behind;
- Taiga sees it first and warns him;
- Leo reacts immediately and stops;
- Leo is not hit;
- Taiga is now competent enough to move, but **not competent enough to stop that quickly**;
- Taiga goes straight into Leo.

Leo catches him instinctively.

This becomes effectively a hug / embrace to keep Taiga from falling.

Important distinction from the old Chapter 13 fall:

> **They do not crash onto the ice.**

They remain standing.

That removes the easy adrenaline / impact excuse.

---

# 5. Faces centimeters apart

Leo is holding Taiga.

Taiga is in Leo's arms.

They are stable.

Their faces are only centimeters apart.

Both freeze.

Both get visibly red / flustered.

This is where Taiga's attention suddenly records concrete details of Leo's face at close range.

Possible details, to choose sparingly in prose:
- eyes;
- darker ring around iris;
- snow / water in hair;
- cheeks flushed from cold;
- breath;
- mouth;
- expression.

Do **not** turn it into a full body inventory.

The meaningful fact is:

> Taiga notices details he normally would not have any reason to catalogue.

Leo being flustered matters because now the evidence is not only:

> I'm acting weird.

Taiga can see that Leo is affected too.

Taiga pulls away.

They continue skating.

No confession.

No explanation.

---

# 6. Taiga's immediate interpretation

Taiga does **not** conclude:

> I have a crush on Leo.

He uses the cheapest available explanation:

> **That was awkward.**

He files it away.

This is denial, but not cartoonishly stupid denial.

The moment genuinely was awkward.

The point is that the explanation does not account for everything.

Later he may think, explicitly or implicitly:

> Why the fuck was I noticing every detail on his face?

That question matters more than an immediate romantic label.

---

# After the Physical Trigger

The outing continues.

This is essential.

Do **not** make:

> charged moment → immediately leave rink → chapter ends

That would make the trigger feel too mechanically like THE ROMANCE BEAT.

They keep skating.

They continue joking.

The friendship still works.

But Taiga's perception has been altered.

Small ordinary physical details may now register more than they did before:
- proximity;
- an arm touch;
- Leo moving beside him;
- eye contact;
- Leo's face.

Do not overdo this into constant hyperawareness.

The important development is simply:

> physical neutrality has been disturbed.

---

# Post-Rink Conversation — Emotional Inflation

The physical scenes do not finish the chapter's work.

Afterward, the "date" should become increasingly intimate **through conversation**.

This is where the bubble keeps inflating / the consequences of the physical trigger become harder to dismiss.

## Hot drinks

Hot chocolate / coffee remains a strong transition.

It slows the pace and gives them a believable reason to sit together after skating.

## Leo's Spanish / Moma phone call

Strong candidate to preserve from old Chapter 13.

Functions:
- Taiga hears Leo speak Spanish naturally.
- Leo's rhythm / voice changes.
- Taiga gets access to a side of Leo he has not experienced directly.
- Family becomes concrete.
- Sister / scarf / home can enter naturally.
- Taiga becomes interested in more of Leo as a person.

This matters because the romantic shift is **not merely stronger horniness**.

After the physical destabilization, Taiga's attention increasingly attaches to:

> Leo himself.

## Camp Buddy / Taiga's gap year

Also useful, but keep it compressed.

Possible functions:
- Leo asks why Taiga stayed at Camp Buddy.
- Taiga explains enough to show why he eventually left for Olympus.
- This quietly closes the Act I belonging theme:
  - Camp Buddy remains home.
  - Leaving it did not destroy that.
  - Olympus can become another home.
- Taiga gives Leo more access to himself than he might have earlier in the semester.

Do not turn this into a full Scoutmaster Season exposition dump.

The conversation should feel like two people who keep choosing to talk.

---

# What the "Date" Is Showing Without Taiga Naming It

Across the outing, Taiga should repeatedly make choices that are individually friendship-compatible but cumulatively meaningful.

Examples:

- he goes when Leo asks;
- he lets Leo teach him physically;
- he gets nervous when the hand-holding becomes conscious;
- he is strongly affected by the accidental embrace;
- he notices Leo's reaction too;
- he continues the outing instead of retreating;
- he accepts drinks / continues spending time together;
- he asks questions about Leo's home / family / language;
- he volunteers more of his own past;
- he keeps extending the interaction;
- he enjoys being alone with Leo specifically.

The reader should gradually think:

> Dude. This is a date.

Taiga should continue thinking:

> I'm spending time with my friend.

Until that category starts failing.

---

# Ending / Separation Beat

Strong current direction:

At the end of the evening, they separate because both are leaving for break.

Taiga realizes he will not see Leo for roughly **three weeks**.

That suddenly does not sit right.

Important:

Do **not** immediately turn this into:

> I don't want to be away from him because I have feelings for him.

Taiga has not processed enough yet.

The cleaner final data point is simply:

> Three weeks suddenly felt like a long time.

or equivalent Taiga-shaped prose.

Potential emotional function:

- hand-holding showed nervousness;
- embrace showed physical charge + reciprocal fluster;
- conversation showed increasing personal intimacy;
- separation shows **pre-emptive absence / longing**.

This completes the chapter's progression without solving it.

Taiga now has enough evidence to know:

> something about how I act with Leo is different.

He still has to process what that means.

That processing belongs to Act II.

# Chapter 12 Exit State

By the end of **The Bubble Pops**:

## School / Olympus
- fall semester is complete;
- finals are over;
- football is paused for break;
- Taiga's CS direction is settled;
- Olympus / Apollo / Room 317 / Nora / Caleb are already established as ordinary life;
- Taiga is leaving for Camp Buddy and fully expects to return.

## Leo / romance
Taiga already knew:
- Leo is physically attractive;
- Leo is his friend;
- he enjoys Leo's company.

Now he additionally knows, even if he cannot yet explain it:
- prolonged contact with Leo makes him **nervous**;
- he pulled away because of that nervousness;
- being held close to Leo affected him beyond simple horniness;
- he noticed absurdly specific details of Leo's face;
- Leo got flustered too;
- he wanted to keep spending time with Leo afterward;
- the prospect of not seeing Leo for three weeks bothers him.

He does **not** conclude:
- "I'm in love";
- "I have a crush";
- "Leo likes me";
- "we should date."

He leaves Act I with:

> **"Friend" is no longer a complete explanation.**

# Act II Consequence

Chapter 12 gives Act II permission to make Taiga **actually nervous** around Leo for the first time.

But this nervousness should be situational, not constant.

Taiga can still:
- joke normally;
- train normally;
- argue;
- study;
- text;
- coexist.

The nervousness appears when the Chapter 12 question is activated:
- prolonged touch;
- close eye contact;
- ending up alone;
- unusually personal conversation;
- consciously choosing Leo;
- realizing he is looking for Leo;
- somebody else noticing their closeness.

His likely responses:
- pull back;
- minimize;
- deny;
- restore distance;
- then miss Leo;
- seek him again;
- notice absence;
- choose plans because Leo is there.

Act II is the pressure created by those contradictory behaviors.

# Guardrails

- Do not spend half the chapter on "life keeps moving."
- Do not add another school / football milestone after Chapter 11.
- Do not let Leo dominate before the chapter naturally narrows to him.
- Do not make Leo explicitly flirt in order to create the rink tension.
- Do not have Leo offer his hands and wait; **he takes Taiga's hands**.
- Do not make them fall to the ice in the second beat.
- Do not make the embrace create a full romantic revelation.
- Do not make Taiga suddenly shy around Leo in every interaction.
- Do not confuse nervousness with sexual arousal.
- Do not have Taiga diagnose Leo's feelings from one blush.
- Do not call the outing a date in dialogue.
- Do not end on a generic "Olympus is home now" summary.
- Do not solve the romance in Act I.

### Chapter 12 - Unscheduled

By the first week of December, House Apollo had stopped functioning as a soccer house and become a badly managed library.

Every table in the common room had at least one laptop on it. Charging cables crossed the floor in ways that would eventually kill somebody. A stack of flashcards occupied half the coffee table. Someone from track had fallen asleep on the couch with a textbook open on his chest, and nobody had bothered waking him because finals had apparently suspended ordinary standards of dignity.

Taiga had taken the corner of the long table nearest the wall.

Not because it was his spot.

It just happened to be the spot he used most often.

Different thing.

He had Calculus open on the laptop, Chemistry notes spread beside it and Professor Hall's final review sitting underneath both because Political Institutions could wait until numbers stopped trying to ruin his life.

Across the room, Enzo stared at a spreadsheet with the expression of somebody who had just discovered betrayal.

"Why is eleven a date?"

Leo did not look up from his laptop.

"What?"

"I typed eleven. It became November."

"That's not a date."

"It says November eleventh."

"Then you typed something besides eleven."

"I typed eleven slash eleven."

Leo slowly raised his head.

Taiga already knew where this was going.

"Enzo."

"What?"

"Eleven slash eleven is a date."

"I wanted eleven divided by eleven."

"Use a formula."

"I did."

"No, you didn't."

"I typed the numbers."

"That is not a formula."

Enzo turned the laptop around as if the evidence might vindicate him. Leo looked at the screen for two seconds, closed his eyes and rubbed them.

"Okay. First, put an equals sign in front of it. Second, why are you dividing eleven by eleven in a spreadsheet?"

"Statistics."

"That answered less than nothing."

At the other end of the table, Gav looked up from a biology textbook.

"He's calculating his participation grade."

Leo stared at Enzo.

"You built a spreadsheet for one grade?"

"It has components."

"How many?"

"Four."

Taiga snorted into his hand.

Enzo pointed at him. "You have no right to judge me, Forty-one. I saw your course planner."

"That was a schedule."

"It had conditional formatting."

"Because the classes overlap visually."

"Conditional formatting," Enzo repeated, like he'd won.

Leo looked between them.

"Wow. You're both terrible."

"You're helping him," Taiga said.

"Community service."

"Against your will?"

Leo looked at Enzo's screen again.

"At this point, yes."

Enzo shoved the laptop toward him. "Fix November."

Leo got up with a sigh dramatic enough to suggest he had been conscripted.

Taiga went back to Calculus.

For almost three minutes.

Then Enzo said, "Why is it one?"

"Because eleven divided by eleven is one."

"That seems wrong."

"Mathematically?"

"Emotionally."

Taiga laughed.

Nobody reacted. Not because they hadn't heard him. Because people laughed in House Apollo all the time now. Somebody complained, somebody answered, another person shouted from the kitchen, and the room kept moving.

A month ago, Taiga probably would've noticed that.

Now he mostly wanted Enzo to stop asking questions about division.

His phone buzzed beside the laptop.

**Nora:** library tomorrow after hall?

**Taiga:** yes

**Nora:** 2nd floor or 4th

Taiga looked at the room around him. Someone had started the coffee machine in the kitchen. Scott was on the floor near the couch, silently working through flashcards. Leo had apparently taken full control of Enzo's spreadsheet and was now explaining why percentages needed to be formatted as percentages. Gav had headphones on.

**Taiga:** 4th

**Nora:** obviously

He put the phone down.

"You're smiling at your phone," Leo said.

Taiga looked up.

Leo was back at his own laptop.

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"You were looking at Enzo's spreadsheet."

"I can multitask."

"Poorly."

Leo smiled. "Nora?"

Taiga narrowed his eyes.

"How the fuck—"

"Who else schedules library time like a hostage negotiation?"

"Caleb."

"Okay, fair."

Enzo turned around. "Are we studying together tomorrow?"

"No," Taiga said.

"I did not ask you."

"You were going to."

"I was asking Leo."

Leo looked at him. "I have a Chemistry review thing."

Enzo's face fell.

"Everybody has abandoned me."

"You are currently surrounded by twelve people."

"Emotionally."

Scott spoke without looking up from his cards.

"Do your work."

Enzo stared at him.

Then he turned back to the spreadsheet.

"Captain has spoken."

Taiga smiled and returned to Calculus.

Outside, somebody was training under the lights on the far field. He could hear the occasional whistle through the windows.

Apollo training had become shorter since finals started. Mercer still expected them to show up, but ninety minutes had become sixty, then fifty-five on Wednesday when half the squad had walked in carrying textbooks.

There had been no new milestone after Briarwood.

No new start. No announcement. No dramatic proof that anything had changed.

Taiga trained.

He held width when he needed to. Checked behind him before receiving more often than he forgot. Made runs when Leo dragged somebody inside. Got corrected when he moved too early. Got yelled at when he stopped tracking after losing the ball. Yelled back exactly once before remembering that Mercer did not consider volume a tactical argument.

Normal.

His first start had not transformed anything.

That was probably why it had started feeling real.

"Akatora."

Taiga looked up.

Gav pointed at the clock.

"Your room booking starts in ten."

Taiga checked the time.

"Fuck."

He had booked one of the small study rooms in the Academic Zone because Caleb had a remote Econometrics session at eight and had informed Taiga, with fourteen hours of notice, that speaking during it would be "suboptimal."

Taiga closed the laptop.

Leo looked over. "Leaving?"

"Apparently."

"Good luck."

"With Calculus?"

"With whatever violent relationship you have with Calculus."

Taiga put his notes into the bag.

"You're taking it too."

"Yes, but Professor Chen doesn't look at me like I've personally disappointed mathematics."

"That's because you sit in the back."

"Strategy."

Taiga stood.

He didn't say goodbye to the room.

Nobody expected him to.

He just left.

Finals arrived without any respect for the fact that everyone was already tired.

Political Institutions came first.

Professor Hall handed out the exam face down and waited until every paper was distributed before saying, "If your argument requires me to invent three missing premises on your behalf, I will not be feeling charitable."

Several people groaned.

Taiga looked at Nora.

She looked back.

Neither smiled.

Hall continued.

"Show me the bridge."

Taiga did.

At least, he hoped he did.

Calculus came two days later and contained an optimization problem Taiga distrusted on sight. Chemistry required enough equilibrium calculations to make him briefly reconsider whether atoms deserved rights. Foundations of Computer Science ended with recursion, aliasing, Quicksort and one design question Professor Rao had almost certainly written for the specific purpose of punishing people who believed working code automatically meant good code.

Taiga finished that one first.

Which was probably a bad sign.

The days around the exams blurred together anyway.

He studied with Nora twice. Once productively, once for three hours during which they spent at least forty minutes arguing about whether Hall's sample answer actually supported its own conclusion.

Caleb printed a finals calendar and taped it beside his desk.

Taiga objected until he noticed his own exams were already on it.

Then he asked Caleb to send him the file.

"This changes nothing," Taiga said.

"I didn't say it did."

Maya appeared on video call long enough to laugh at both of them.

Camp Buddy kept existing at the edge of everything too.

Natsumi sent a photo from a swim meet and complained that arriving early had given him time to imagine every possible disaster.

Hiro replied:

**Hiro:** THEN STOP IMAGINING AND SWIM FAST

**Natsumi:** Thank you. I had not considered swimming quickly at the swim meet.

Yoichi contributed:

**Yoichi:** don't drown mr perfect

Three hours later, Natsumi sent a season-best time.

Taiga sent congratulations while walking from Chemistry to training.

Keitaro sent pictures of the first snow at Camp Buddy.

Hunter posted a sketch of Yuki with her nose buried in it.

Yuri asked whether Taiga was bringing enough warm clothes for the break.

Taiga did not answer that one on principle.

Football continued between exams, reduced to the necessary parts.

One afternoon, Mercer ended training after fifty minutes and handed everybody a break program.

"You're not going to lose all your fitness in three weeks," he said. "You can absolutely come back feeling like shit if you spend those three weeks doing nothing."

Enzo raised a hand.

"What qualifies as nothing?"

"Whatever you're planning."

The team laughed.

Mercer looked at the developmental group.

"Same for you. Break doesn't reset the board. Come back ready to train."

Taiga folded the sheet once and put it in his bag.

No speech. No promise.

Still enough.

By Thursday, the library had become unusable.

By Friday morning, people had started leaving.

Nora finished her last exam before Taiga did. She found him outside the Academic Zone with a suitcase because apparently she had decided dragging luggage through campus was preferable to returning to the dorm first.

Oliver was waiting in her mother's car wearing a red sweater and the expression of a dog who had been personally betrayed by winter.

Taiga looked through the open door.

"That's ridiculous."

Nora put a hand over Oliver's ears.

"He can hear you."

"Good."

Oliver stared at him.

Taiga crouched and scratched behind his ears.

Nora watched.

"Interesting."

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

Taiga looked up.

"You're about to."

"I was going to say he likes you."

"That's worse."

Nora smiled.

Her mother called something from the driver's seat.

"I have to go."

"Yeah."

Nora adjusted the strap of her bag.

"Train tomorrow?"

"Nine-twelve."

"Camp Buddy for a week?"

"Yeah."

"Tell them I said hi."

"No."

"Perfect."

She opened the car door, then paused.

"Have a good break, Taiga."

He looked at her.

It should not have sounded strange. People said that constantly.

Still.

"You too."

Nora got in.

Oliver turned once in the back seat before the car pulled away.

Taiga stood there for a second longer than necessary, then went to his last final.

The first snow of the semester arrived on the same afternoon everybody finished leaving.

Taiga noticed because, for the first time in nearly four months, he had nowhere he was supposed to be.

That felt suspicious.

He came out of Foundations at two-forty with his backpack over one shoulder and stopped under the Academic Zone awning.

Tiny white flakes drifted between the buildings and disappeared as soon as they touched the pavement.

People reacted anyway.

Someone near the library entrance shouted. Two students stopped in the middle of the path to take pictures. A guy in a red coat held both hands out as if the weather had personally delivered him a gift.

Taiga stepped into it.

One flake landed on his cheek and melted.

"Great."

His phone buzzed.

**Nora:** IT'S SNOWING

Taiga looked at the snow currently falling directly onto him.

**Taiga:** thanks i hadn't noticed

**Nora:** you looked like you needed confirmation

He smiled and put the phone away.

Campus already looked wrong.

Suitcases rolled over the paths. Cars waited outside Residential Hall. Parents stood in lobbies. People hugged classmates they were going to see again in less than a month with an intensity Taiga found objectively excessive.

He had made fun of them yesterday.

Then Caleb left.

Maya arrived around ten with two reusable bags and an argument already in progress about whether Caleb needed three pairs of shoes for the break.

Caleb said yes.

Maya said no.

Taiga stayed out of it until Maya tried to recruit him.

"Don't involve me."

"See?" Caleb said. "He understands."

"I understand that I don't care."

Five minutes before leaving, Caleb checked the window latch, unplugged the extra power strip and stood beside the door looking around Room 317.

Taiga sat at his desk.

"Did you lose something?"

"No."

"Then why are you staring?"

"I am checking whether I forgot anything."

"You've checked four times."

Maya leaned around the doorframe.

"Five."

Caleb ignored her.

Then he looked at Taiga.

"I will be back January sixth."

"I know."

A pause.

"Have a good break."

There it was again.

Taiga had looked at him for half a second.

"You too."

"Bye, Taiga."

"Bye."

The door closed.

For the first ten minutes, the room had felt wrong.

Then Taiga discovered the advantages of having it entirely to himself and stopped being dramatic.

Mostly.

Now he crossed Central Park while snow started sticking to the grass.

His train to Camp Buddy left tomorrow at 9:12. From there, regional bus. Yoshinori had offered to have somebody pick him up from the stop.

Yoichi had responded to this information with:

**Yoichi:** walk

Keitaro:

**Keitaro:** It's fourteen kilometers, Yoichi.

**Yoichi:** good

Taiga had not dignified that with an answer.

Acropolis Park had been converted into something aggressively seasonal.

Lights wrapped every tree along the main path. Temporary wooden stalls lined the lawn. Music played from somewhere near the fountain, which had been shut off and covered in enough lights to be visible from space.

A giant illuminated arch announced WINTER WEEK.

Leo looked delighted.

"Oh, this is perfect."

"It is crowded."

"That's what perfect looks like sometimes."

"Disgusting sentence."

Leo laughed and headed toward the rink.

Taiga followed.

The rental skates looked untrustworthy.

He sat on the bench and turned one over in his hands.

Leo noticed.

"You've done this before, right?"

"No."

Leo stopped tying his lace.

"Never?"

"No."

"You were at a camp."

"In summer."

"Probably."

"Terrifying."

"Soccer balance transfers."

"Does it?"

Leo smiled.

"No idea."

Taiga stared at him.

"Excellent."

The first thirty seconds were humiliating.

Not because Taiga fell.

Falling would have at least been a clear event.

Instead, every reflex his body had developed for not sliding suddenly became useless. His ankles felt unstable. Stopping did not seem physically supported by the universe. The barrier immediately became the most trustworthy thing in Olympus City.

Leo pushed away beside him with much more confidence than five previous skating experiences justified.

"Bend your knees."

"I am."

"No, you're standing like somebody put a weapon against your spine."

"I'm trying not to fall."

Taiga tried.

"Beautiful."

"Fuck off."

"You moved."

"I walk every day."

"Different surface."

"You don't know her life."

"Because if you fall while teaching me, I'm leaving you here."

"I'm not going to fall."

Leo nearly fell immediately.

His arms shot out.

Taiga laughed.

"Controlled demonstration," Leo said.

"Of failure?"

"Recovery."

The first lap took forever.

The second took less.

By the third, Taiga's body had stopped treating the ice as an active emergency. He could push properly. Turn, mostly. Slow down without grabbing the barrier.

Stopping remained theoretical.

Leo became overconfident in direct proportion to Taiga's improvement.

"See?" he said, skating backward again. "I told you the balance transfers. Your hips already understand not to lock up, which is the hard part. Most people do this—"

He demonstrated a stiff-legged panic shuffle.

Taiga laughed.

"That's not skating."

"Exactly. Panic with blades."

"You looked like that ten minutes ago."

"Lies."

Leo glanced sideways.

The kid kept going.

Leo turned back too quickly, caught the outside edge of one skate and slipped.

"Shit—"

Taiga reached him on instinct.

One hand caught Leo at the side of his ribs. The other grabbed his arm.

Leo's hands closed around Taiga's shoulders.

For one ugly second, both of them slid.

Then Taiga found his edge.

Leo found his.

They stopped.

Neither fell.

"Got you," Taiga said.

Leo laughed once, breathless.

"Yeah."

That should have been it.

They were stable.

Taiga knew they were stable.

Leo knew it too.

Taiga's hand was still pressed against his side through the thick coat.

Leo was still holding both shoulders.

They were close enough that Taiga could see snow melting in Leo's hair.

Leo looked at him.

The laughter disappeared first.

Not dramatically.

It just stopped.

For a second, neither of them moved.

Leo's fingers tightened once against Taiga's jacket.

Stupid.

They were wearing coats. Gloves. Scarves. Half the fucking textile industry between them.

Then back up.

Normal.

Normal.
