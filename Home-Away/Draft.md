### Chapter 11 - Good

By Monday morning, Central Park looked like a park again.

The lanterns were gone. The temporary stage had disappeared. Somebody had removed the pumpkins before students could start kicking them around campus, which was probably wise.

Halloween survived mostly through photos.

Aaron and Sofia's date became a recurring subject in the Apollo group chat for approximately forty-eight hours, until Aaron threatened to leave it. Enzo responded by changing the group name to **AARON'S SUPPORT NETWORK**, which lasted another six hours before Scott changed it back.

Dynamite lasted longer.

Taiga muted the chat.

Then November started doing what November apparently did at university.

Everything became due.

Professor Hall assigned their final paper and spent ten minutes explaining that no, six pages did not mean six pages of opinions with three citations attached afterward. Chemistry added another lab report. Rao put the last Foundations assignment online and casually mentioned the final as if that wasn't the kind of sentence that ruined people's week.

Taiga started spending more time in the library.

When the library got too crowded, he worked in Room 317 instead. Caleb had developed an entire system involving two timers, a spreadsheet and scheduled breaks. Taiga refused to participate on principle and then slowly started using the breaks anyway.

Football didn't slow down either.

If anything, Mercer got worse.

The first weeks had been full of pauses. Stop. Reset. Explain where everyone was supposed to be. Explain why. Repeat.

Now Mercer mostly expected them to know.

A bad press got a whistle and a surname.

A bad run got a whistle and a stare.

Sometimes that was worse.

Taiga played twenty-six minutes in one friendly, then thirty-eight in the next. Nothing particularly interesting happened in either. He lost the ball a few times. Won it back a few times. Put one cross straight into a defender and another somewhere vaguely close to Price.

He also stopped feeling like every touch required a committee meeting inside his head.

That was useful.

Life outside classes and training kept happening too, just less dramatically.

He ate with the team after practice sometimes. Stayed at House Apollo once after film because Gav had ordered too much food. Nora kept stealing the chair beside him in Political Institutions. Camp Buddy remained permanently active on his phone. Caleb and Maya dragged him to the Oyster one Sunday morning and then spent half of breakfast discussing whether a trail near Acropolis would still be usable after the first snow.

Nothing exploded.

Nobody held an exhibition.

Nobody organized a giant night out.

For almost three weeks, Olympus simply continued.

Taiga found himself appreciating that more than he wanted to admit.

***

The air had gotten cold enough that standing still during training was a punishment.

Luckily, Mercer had apparently declared standing still illegal.

"Again!"

Taiga jogged back toward the touchline.

They were working in a reduced field, eight against eight, with the space compressed enough that every bad touch immediately became somebody else's problem.

Scott rolled the ball to Gav.

Taiga checked over his shoulder.

Number four on his side. Aaron deeper. Leo moving inside.

Gav passed forward.

Leo came toward the ball.

A month earlier, Taiga would've seen Leo moving and gone toward him too. Or passed straight to him. Or done something equally useful for the defender following him.

Now he saw the defender move with Leo.

Space opened behind him.

Taiga ran.

Leo received with his back to goal, touched the ball once and sent it around the defender into the space Taiga was already attacking.

There.

Taiga reached it before Aaron could close him down and drove a low ball across the middle.

Price got a foot to it.

Scott saved.

"Fuck."

"Again!" Mercer shouted.

Nobody stopped to explain what had happened.

Taiga jogged back into position.

Good enough.

The next play went worse.

He tried to turn around Aaron, got the ball taken off him and immediately started after it.

Two steps.

Then he stopped.

Aaron had already passed inside. Chasing him would only drag Taiga away from the left side.

He recovered his position instead.

Scott shouted something at Gav. Leo dropped. The ball moved backward.

Nothing happened.

Which, six weeks earlier, would've felt like failure.

Now it just meant nothing happened.

They played another fifteen minutes before Mercer finally blew twice.

"Enough."

Several people immediately bent over.

Enzo dropped onto his back.

"I have died."

"You say that every Thursday," Aaron said.

"Because every Thursday he kills me."

Mercer ignored them.

"Saturday. Northbridge. Same time as usual. I'll send the group tonight."

People started toward the bottles.

"Akatora."

Taiga stopped.

Mercer pointed at him.

"You're starting left."

For a second, Taiga thought he'd misheard.

"Starting?"

"Yes."

"Oh."

Mercer waited.

Taiga realized that probably wasn't a useful response.

"For how long?"

"Depends on you."

Of course.

Mercer glanced toward the equipment bag.

"Put the bibs away before Reyes decides he's too dead to help."

From the ground, Enzo lifted one arm.

"I heard that."

"Good."

Taiga grabbed the mesh bag.

Apparently, he was starting Saturday.

Fine.

***

The locker room was louder than usual because Enzo had recovered from death in under four minutes.

Taiga changed his shirt, shoved it into his bag and sat down to untie his boots.

"First start."

He looked up.

Leo was sitting on the bench opposite him with one sock halfway off.

"Apparently."

Leo smiled.

"Nice."

"Yeah."

Taiga went back to his laces.

When he looked up again, Leo was still looking at him.

Not doing anything. Just looking.

"What?"

Leo blinked.

"Nothing."

He pulled the sock off and reached for the other one.

Taiga stood and grabbed his bag.

Behind him, Enzo was arguing with Gav about whether cereal counted as soup.

It did not.

Taiga left before the conversation got worse.

Outside, Mercer was still beside the pitch talking to Evan.

Evan had changed already. Jacket zipped up, training bag hanging from one shoulder. Mercer said something Taiga couldn't hear, Evan nodded, and then the conversation ended.

Mercer walked toward the offices.

Evan spotted Taiga.

"There he is."

"No."

"I haven't said anything."

"You were going to."

"I was going to congratulate you."

"That's worse."

Evan laughed and fell into step beside him.

They headed toward the main campus.

For a while they didn't talk about football at all.

Evan complained about one of his professors moving a deadline forward. Taiga told him that sounded illegal. Evan said it should be.

Then they crossed the road outside the athletic complex.

"You nervous?"

Taiga shrugged.

"A little."

"Good."

Taiga looked at him.

"Why does everyone keep saying that like it's useful?"

"Because if you weren't nervous, you'd probably try to dribble through number four on your first touch."

"I'm not stupid."

Evan gave him a look.

Taiga sighed.

"Fine. Not anymore."

"Progress."

They kept walking.

The campus had gotten quieter with the cold. People moved quickly between buildings instead of sitting outside. Most of the benches were empty.

Evan shoved his hands into his jacket pockets.

"You know I'm gone after spring, right?"

Taiga glanced at him.

"I know what senior means."

"Just checking. You miss obvious things sometimes."

"Fuck you."

Evan smiled.

Then he looked ahead again.

"That side's going to need somebody."

Taiga didn't answer immediately.

Evan continued.

"I'm not saying it's yours."

"Very generous."

"I'm serious."

That changed the tone enough that Taiga looked at him again.

Evan shrugged.

"You've got the physical part. You've had that since tryouts. You're fast, you're strong, and you really enjoy running directly at problems."

"Sounds like a compliment."

"It's fifty-fifty."

"Of course."

"But you're learning when not to."

Taiga looked ahead.

The statement landed harder than he expected.

Evan kicked a pebble off the pavement.

"When I leave, I want somebody there who actually understands the position. Not somebody who thinks left wing means sprint forward until the field ends."

"You're describing me."

"I'm describing you in September."

Taiga snorted.

"Still kind of describing me."

"Less."

They reached the fork where Evan had to turn toward the senior dorms.

Taiga stopped.

"So you think I can take your place?"

Evan adjusted the strap of his bag.

"Eventually?"

"Yeah."

He considered it for a second.

"I think you can compete for it."

Not the same thing.

Better, actually.

Evan pointed at him.

"Don't make that face. I'm not donating you my job because Mercer gave you one start in November."

"I wasn't asking you to."

"Good. Because I'm planning to remain significantly better than you until graduation."

"Go fuck yourself."

"There he is."

Evan started walking backward.

"And don't wrestle number four."

"I know."

"You said that last time."

"I learned."

"We'll see."

He turned and left.

Taiga watched him go for a second, then headed toward the Residential Hall.

One start.

Apparently that was enough for people to start discussing next year.

Ridiculous.

Still.

He didn't hate it.

***

Friday afternoon, Nora had taken over half a library table.

Two books. Three printed articles. A notebook. Her laptop. A second notebook for reasons Taiga had stopped trying to understand.

Taiga had one laptop and a coffee.

He considered his setup superior.

"You're doing it again."

He looked up.

"Doing what?"

"Staring at the same page instead of making a decision."

"I'm reading."

"You haven't scrolled in eight minutes."

Taiga looked at the screen.

The Foundations assignment was minimized.

Behind it sat the page he'd opened twenty minutes earlier.

**Major Declaration**

Nora leaned slightly sideways.

"Oh my God."

"What?"

"You still haven't done that?"

Taiga frowned.

"You knew I hadn't."

"I knew you hadn't when we registered. That was weeks ago."

"And?"

"And you're taking Data Structures, Discrete Structures and Calculus II next semester."

"And Chemistry."

"Yes. One chemistry course."

"Two this year."

"Taiga."

He leaned back.

"I'm just saying."

"No, you're constructing an argument whose conclusion you already know is weak."

"You're enjoying this."

"A little."

Of course she was.

Taiga looked at the declaration page again.

Computer Science was sitting in the dropdown.

He'd already selected it.

He just hadn't pressed submit.

"It's four years," he said.

Nora frowned. "What is?"

"The degree."

"I know how degrees work."

"So choosing it because I like one class is stupid."

"Good thing you're not choosing it because you like one class."

Taiga looked at her.

Nora started counting on her fingers.

"You spent half the semester complaining about Political Institutions. You complain about chemistry labs every week. You voluntarily fixed Zoe's Git disaster. You already picked two more Computer Science courses. You spent forty minutes last Tuesday explaining to me why Rao's example was inefficient."

"It was."

"That's not the point."

"It should be."

She ignored him.

"You keep acting like declaring the major creates the preference. It doesn't. You're just writing down the preference you already have."

Taiga stared at the form.

Annoying.

Reasonable, but annoying.

He started thinking again.

Maybe he should wait until finals. Maybe Data Structures would be different. Maybe he'd hate it. Maybe there was something else he hadn't tried yet. Maybe—

Aaron.

Taiga closed his eyes.

Three weeks earlier, he'd stood in Central Park telling Aaron that eventually thinking stopped being useful. That sometimes he had to shut his brain off for five fucking minutes and do the thing before he invented another reason not to.

Great advice.

Apparently.

Taiga opened his eyes.

Nora was watching him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're doing the face."

"What face?"

"The one where you're angry because somebody else was right."

"Nobody was right."

"Okay."

He hated that tone.

Taiga moved the cursor.

**Computer Science.**

Submit.

A confirmation page loaded.

**Your major declaration has been received.**

That was it.

No dramatic music. No sudden certainty about the next four years.

Just a white page and a green checkmark.

Nora smiled.

"Congratulations."

"Don't."

"You declared a major."

"I clicked a button."

"After refusing to click it for approximately a month."

"Still a button."

"Would you like me to clap?"

"I'd like you to shut up."

Nora laughed and went back to her article.

Taiga looked at the confirmation for another second.

Then he closed the tab and reopened Rao's assignment.

There.

Done.

***

Saturday was gray, cold and windy.

Northbridge looked exactly as annoying as Taiga remembered.

Number four especially.

He stood near the halfway line while both teams warmed up, somehow still looking larger than everyone around him.

Taiga stared at him.

Evan appeared beside him.

"Romantic reunion."

"Go away."

"He remembers you."

"How the fuck would you know?"

Number four looked over.

Then directly at Taiga.

He smiled.

Evan laughed.

"See?"

"Fuck off."

Mercer called everyone in.

The lineup was already on the board.

Taiga had seen his name there when he'd arrived.

**41 - AKATORA - LW**

He'd looked at it for maybe five seconds too long.

Now Mercer went through the same instructions he always did.

Keep the first pass simple. Don't force the center. Northbridge pressed hard and recovered quickly. If Olympus let the game become a fight, Northbridge would happily spend ninety minutes fighting.

Nothing Taiga didn't already know.

That helped.

They went outside.

This time, when Taiga stepped onto the field, nobody had to hold up a board for him.

He was already supposed to be there.

That was new.

The whistle went.

For the first thirty seconds, his body tried to betray him anyway.

Heart too fast. Legs too light. Too much awareness of everything at once.

Then Aaron received behind him.

Taiga checked his shoulder.

Number four.

Fine.

He stayed wide.

Aaron played inside instead.

Nothing happened.

Next possession, Gav switched the ball left.

Taiga checked again before it arrived.

Number four was already closing.

Taiga touched the ball back to Aaron and moved.

Simple.

No disaster.

No sudden disappearance of the entire field.

The next time the ball came toward him, he already knew where number four was.

That was the difference.

Not that the defender had gotten smaller.

He definitely had not.

Taiga just knew where the bastard was before turning.

At eight minutes, Taiga tried to cut inside too early and ran straight into traffic.

The ball popped loose.

Northbridge broke.

Taiga spun and chased.

He caught their midfielder near the halfway line and poked the ball away.

Mercer shouted from the sideline.

"GOOD RECOVERY!"

Taiga barely had time to feel satisfied.

"WOULDN'T NEED IT IF YOU STOPPED DOING STUPID SHIT FIRST!"

There it was.

Taiga jogged back into position.

Fair.

At fourteen minutes, number four leaned into him near the line.

Taiga gave up the ball before the contact fully arrived, passed inside and ran behind him.

The return pass didn't come.

Fine.

At nineteen, same thing.

This time the pass came late and number four recovered.

Taiga sent the ball backward.

Also fine.

By twenty-five minutes, the match had settled into something readable.

Northbridge still pressed. Still hit hard. Still tried to turn every loose ball into a sprint.

But Taiga could see the shapes now.

When their winger dropped, Aaron moved.

When Leo drifted toward the right, Gav adjusted behind him.

When Price came short, somebody else had to attack the space he left.

Not every movement required Taiga to do something.

That remained one of football's more offensive concepts.

Northbridge nearly scored from a corner before halftime.

Scott punched it away.

The whistle came a few minutes later.

Still 0-0.

Mercer waited until everyone had water.

Then he pointed at Taiga.

"You're coming inside too early."

Taiga frowned.

"Four keeps following."

"No. You're deciding for him."

Taiga looked at the board.

Mercer drew one short line toward the center.

"You move first, he gets to react. Stay wide. Make him choose."

Right.

Taiga nodded.

"Okay."

"Good."

Second half.

Taiga stayed wide.

The first time, nothing happened.

The second time, nothing happened again.

The third time, number four got impatient.

He stepped toward Aaron.

Taiga went behind him.

The pass arrived.

For once, number four wasn't close enough to throw him into another postal code.

Taiga drove forward.

Price moved toward the near post.

Taiga saw him.

Then he saw Romero arriving behind.

He cut the ball back.

Romero hit it first time.

The goalkeeper saved.

Price reached the rebound half a second too late and Northbridge cleared.

Taiga stopped near the edge of the box.

Fuck.

Mercer clapped once from the sideline.

Taiga turned and ran back.

Good enough.

A few minutes later, the same space opened again.

This time the ball never reached him.

It didn't need to.

Number four stayed wide with Taiga. Aaron carried inside through the space he'd left. Apollo got a shot from it.

Wide.

Still.

Taiga looked at number four jogging back beside him.

The defender hadn't touched him.

Taiga hadn't touched the ball.

And something useful had happened because of where he stood.

Ridiculous sport.

Olympus scored in the sixty-third minute.

Taiga had very little to do with it.

Gav won the ball. Leo carried it through the middle. Aaron overlapped on the left and crossed low. Price got there first.

1-0.

Taiga ran over with everyone else anyway.

Nobody asked whether he'd contributed enough to celebrate.

At sixty-nine minutes, his left calf started tightening.

At seventy-two, the board went up.

**41 OUT**
**11 IN**

Taiga jogged toward the touchline.

Evan waited for him.

For half a second, the image felt backwards.

Last time against Northbridge, Evan had come off and Taiga had gone on.

Now Evan slapped his hand and stepped past him.

"Not terrible."

"Fuck you."

Evan grinned and ran onto the field.

Taiga sat down.

His lungs were working hard. His legs were heavy. Mud had dried along one sock. Number four had managed to hit him in at least four separate places.

He felt good.

Not amazing.

Not transformed.

Good.

Olympus kept the lead.

When the final whistle went, everyone started toward the sideline.

Taiga stood and picked up his bottle.

Mercer was walking past when he stopped.

"Akatora."

Taiga looked at him.

Mercer nodded once.

"Good."
