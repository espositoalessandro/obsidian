Taiga balanced his plate on one knee and reached for his drink. Beside him, Leo was still smiling as he tried the curry.

"You staying tonight?" Taiga asked Maya.

"No, I've got a class in the morning." She took a spring roll from the box Caleb held out. "He's coming to mine for a few days over break, though."

"Your parents' place?"

"Mhm."

Taiga looked up at Caleb. "Have you met them?"

"Yes. In October."

"Oh." He hadn't known that.

Leo swallowed. "How did it go?"

"Well."

Maya smiled at Caleb over her spring roll. "My dad's already asked if you're coming early enough for breakfast."

"I'd like to."

"See? I'm going home to sleep. They're going out at seven."

Leo laughed. "Wait, you just leave her there?"

"She's asleep," Caleb said.

"The first time, I woke up and couldn't find either of them," Maya added. "Mom told me they'd gone out. I thought she meant to the bakery around the corner, but apparently they'd walked all the way into town."

"Your father wanted to show me the old station."

"After breakfast."

"Yes."

Taiga reached for another spring roll. Caleb had mentioned that station before. Taiga had assumed he'd found pictures of it somewhere.

"What's special about it?" Leo asked.

"They closed the line years ago. Most of the platform is still there, but they've converted the station building into shops." Caleb set the box down within reach of the three of them. "Her father remembers using it."

"He used to take me past it on the way to school," Maya said. "I didn't know half the things he told Caleb."

"Did you go back with them?"

"Eventually. When they agreed to leave after I'd woken up."

Caleb turned his chair away from the desk. "Your mother came too."

"Yeah. And then we all had to wait while she went into every shop."

Maya picked a loose piece of pastry off her plate, smiling. Caleb was looking at her rather than his food.

"Are they far from town?" Taiga asked.

"About half an hour on foot," she said. "There's a shorter way along the road, but it's horrible. I always take the path by the river."

"Longer?" Leo asked.

"A little. It's nicer, though."

"It's much nicer," Caleb said.

Maya looked pleased. She reached up to touch his knee, then took the container he offered her.

Taiga finished his spring roll and wiped his fingers on a napkin. Leo was reaching up for the rice, and Caleb passed it down before he could ask.

"Have you met his parents too?" Leo asked Maya.

"Not yet. I've talked to his mom a couple of times."

"On the phone," Caleb said.

"She caught me here once when he called her. We ended up talking for ages."

"What about?"

"Mostly you."

Caleb lowered his fork.

"Nothing bad," Maya added, laughing. "She asked how we met, what I was studying. Then she wanted to know if I was staying for dinner."

"You were."

"I know. She was very relieved."

Leo smiled. "My mother does that. I'll say I'm at somebody's house and she wants to know if they've fed me. Then I have to explain that I've been there for ten minutes."

"Does she know your friends?" Maya asked.

"Most of them. Enzo's basically another child. She asks me where he is, as if we come to university together every morning."

"Do your parents get up that early too?" Maya asked.

"My mother does. She works at a school. My dad depends on his shift, but if he's home, he makes breakfast." Leo reached for his drink. "He starts cooking before he asks if anyone's hungry. By the time I get downstairs, it's too late to say I just want coffee."

"What does he make?"

"Eggs, plantains. Whatever's there. Sometimes he starts doing one thing and sees something else in the fridge, and then we have both." He smiled into his cup. "He's good at it, though."

"Does he make breakfast for everyone?" Taiga asked.

"Whoever's there. My grandmother will come out with her coffee, and then my sister shows up, and somebody else smells food." Leo smiled. "He usually has to make more."

"What do you miss most?" Maya asked.

Leo thought about it while he scraped the last of the rice onto his plate.

"Breakfast, probably. When I don't have to go anywhere afterward. I can sit with my coffee for ages."

"And I miss sitting outside," he added. "I keep forgetting I can't just do that here. I go out with my drink, get cold and come straight back in."

"You could wear a coat," Taiga said.

"It's not the same if I need equipment."

Maya nodded. "I'm so ready for it to warm up again. I miss being able to go out barefoot."

"Exactly!"

"Your feet would freeze to the pavement," Taiga said.

"We know. That's what we're complaining about."

Leo nudged his knee, then returned to his food. Taiga shifted to make room for his own plate. Their legs settled against each other.

"You sit in the garden?" Maya asked.

"Courtyard. We all use it. My grandmother's got plants everywhere."

"Oh, what does she grow?"

"Flowers, mostly. And there's aloe. I know that one because I'm allowed to cut it."

Maya smiled. "The others are protected?"

"Very. You can sit next to them. That's about it."

"Sounds like my mother," Maya said. "She's got this huge basil plant in the kitchen window. Every time I go home she offers me a cutting, and every time I have to remind her I don't have anywhere to put it."

"Your windowsill?" Taiga asked.

"Faces a wall. I tried. It lasted about a month."

"Could you leave it here?" Leo asked.

Maya looked up at Caleb, then toward the window.

"You can," Caleb said. "You'll have to tell me what it needs."

"I'd have to ask Mom. She just tells me to water it when it looks sad."

Caleb glanced at Taiga. "Is that all right?"

Taiga shrugged. "It's a plant."

"Okay." Maya smiled and tucked her feet under her. "I'll ask her to keep one for me."

Leo finished the rice on his plate, then sat back against the bed. Taiga reached across him to put his own plate on the desk.

"Give me yours."

"Thanks."

He stacked them together. Maya was still eating, so he left the food where it was and settled back onto his pillow. Leo had shifted while he was leaning over; their shoulders touched when Taiga sat down again.

"My sister's got plants in her room," Leo said. "She's much better at it than I am."

"How old is she?" Maya asked.

"Sixteen. She told me not to come into her room without knocking last time I went home. Very firmly."

"Fair," Maya said.

"I know! I was only going to say hi."

"You could've knocked," Taiga said.

"I did after that."

Maya laughed and set her plate aside. Caleb held out the spring-roll box.

"There's one left."

She broke it in half and gave one piece back to him. "You have some."

Leo had started telling Maya about his sister again. Taiga listened until he mentioned a game they played together.

"She beat you?"

"Once."

"How many times did you play?"

Leo turned toward him. "Can I finish the story?"

Taiga grinned and reached for his drink. "Go on, then."
