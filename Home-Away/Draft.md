### Chapter 21 - Uncharted paths

Things got boring before pizzas arrive



Pizza arrives





On Sunday, Zoe arrived at House Apollo carrying a drink and a dice bag large enough to contain a small animal.

Gav was clearing the low table in front of the couch. Scott had taken the armchair, his character sheet balanced on a book. Taiga sat on the rug with his back against the couch, beside Leo, who was reading the same spell for the third time.

"Don't spill anything on the map," Gav said as Zoe leaned across it.

"I've done this before."

"You're holding a drink over it."

She put the cup on the side table and tipped dice into a tray instead. Several rolled into Gav's pile. Taiga picked up a green twenty-sided one and asked whose it was.

"The dark green ones are his. Mine have gold numbers."

"They're all green."

"Different greens."

Leo inspected the pile seriously, returned one of Zoe's dice and began separating the others. She pulled a blue set from her bag and slid it toward Taiga.

"Use those. They're easier to read."

He chose the twenty-sided one and left it beside the fighter sheet. Gav had already asked them to introduce their characters. Zoe's rogue had a coat full of pockets and a reason to leave town quickly; Leo's halfling bard apparently owed money to several people. Scott had chosen a dwarf cleric and had underlined the spells that restored health.

Taiga checked the name he'd written.

"Brann. Human. Sword."

Gav waited.

"And shield," Leo supplied.

"What else do you want?"

"Why is he here?" Gav asked.

"For the money."

Zoe nodded approvingly. "He's ready."

Gav explained the basic idea. They said what they wanted to do; he described what happened, sometimes asking them to roll. The numbers on the sheet helped determine whether they managed it. Taiga checked where he'd written the bonuses while Gav arranged a few figures behind the folding screen.

Then Gav lowered his voice.

"You're in a tavern near the eastern docks. It's late afternoon. Rain's coming in off the river, and somebody's left the door open."

He described wet coats, a smoky hearth and a woman waiting at a table with an empty wooden case in front of her. Customs had taken her brass astrolabe when she entered town. She had paid the fee, but now the collector claimed he had no record of it. The instrument would be sold that evening with the unclaimed cargo.

Leo wanted to know what an astrolabe was, then how big it was, then whether it would fit in his bag.

"An instrument for measuring the position of the stars," Gav explained. He held his hands apart. "About this big."

"Okay, that's fine. I was worried we were stealing something the size of a telescope."

"Recovering," Scott said quietly.

"That too."

The woman had a receipt and an inventory number. She also had money, which interested Zoe considerably more. By the time they left the imaginary tavern, the rogue had negotiated half their payment in advance and given their employer a name Taiga was fairly certain wasn't on her sheet.

Gav unfolded the plan of the customs house. The main stairs led from the entrance to the collector's office upstairs. Behind the storage floor, a separate staircase connected the loading area to the back door of the same office.

Taiga traced the rear wall with his pencil.

"Can we get in from there?"

"There's a small dock. Two men are unloading crates from a boat."

"What about windows?"

Gav marked one beside the storage floor. Zoe immediately moved her figure around to it.

"I'm having a look. You three can keep discussing the front door."

Her first roll was good. Through the unlatched window, her rogue could see sacks, cupboards and a guard sitting on a stool. He was peeling an orange.

"Excellent. I like him already."

Taiga considered how far the window was from the cupboards. He was still working out whether they could all get through when Leo proposed asking to view the auction items.

"Like buyers. They want money, right? We've got the money Zoe just bullied out of the astronomer. We can at least find out where they're keeping the thing."

He turned toward Taiga.

"Unless you want to try the back way."

"Front's easier if it works."

"Okay. I'll ask."

Leo sat up and gave Gav a smile.

"We heard there's an auction this evening. My associate here is interested in instruments."

"What sort of instruments?" Gav asked, in a voice considerably less welcoming than his own.

"Scientific ones," Taiga said.

Leo glanced at his sheet. "Brann looks scientific?"

"He didn't ask for my fucking qualifications."

Zoe laughed into her sleeve. Her rogue was still outside the window; she hadn't moved the figure, apparently trusting them to provide a distraction.

Gav asked Leo to roll. The die bounced out of the tray and stopped against Taiga's knee.

"Seventeen."

"Plus five." Leo checked the sheet. "Twenty-two. That's good, right?"

The front guard took the three prospective buyers upstairs. Taiga moved Brann's figure after him while Leo was still looking pleased with himself.

"You're doing the talking upstairs too."

"Gladly. We've established I'm good at it."

The collector had rings on three fingers and very little interest in allowing strangers to examine anything for free. Gav held out his hand for a viewing deposit.

Five silver. Refundable only if they bought something.

"That's not a deposit," Taiga objected.

"Tell him that," Zoe said.

Taiga repeated it to Gav, who lifted his chin and suggested they wait outside for the public auction. Leo paid before Taiga could argue further. The front guard stayed beside the office's main door while the collector asked which item they wanted to see.

Downstairs, Zoe's rogue slipped through the window when the other guard turned away. Gav switched between the two groups while Taiga took notes around the edge of the map. He had drawn a small orange beside the warehouse guard. It was easier than remembering his name.

Zoe found the inventory number on a cupboard and got the lock open. When Gav described six identical wooden boxes inside, she rubbed both hands over her face.

Upstairs, the collector had begun asking what Taiga knew about astronomy.

"I know what the thing looks like," Taiga said.

"Tell him that too."

"Why don't you tell him something?"

"Because I just told him you're the expert."

Taiga turned toward Leo.

"You volunteered me!"

"You said you were interested in instruments!"

"I wasn't speaking!"

Scott covered his mouth. Zoe put her head down over her character sheet while Leo tried to explain that he had only needed somebody to sound convincing.

"You could've said you collected them," he protested. "People collect things they don't understand all the time. Enzo has three binders of cards."

Taiga was laughing now too, which made it harder to explain how little that helped. Gav waited behind his screen until they settled down.

"Fine. I ask whether the mounting's complete. And whether he has the case."

Gav checked a note.

"The collector says the case is available separately."

"Bullshit."

"The collector looks offended."

"Good."

Leo lost it again. Taiga reached for his drink, discovered it was empty and put it down without really noticing.

The collector eventually went to fetch the instrument himself, leaving the front guard with them. Downstairs, Zoe had opened one box and found glass bottles. She was reaching for another when Gav described footsteps on the main stairs.

She moved her rogue behind the cupboard.

While the collector stopped to berate the warehouse guard about the open lock, Leo turned his spell sheet toward Gav.

"Can I make his voice come from outside? Calling that guy down to the dock. I've heard him now."

Gav measured the distance on the plan and checked the spell.

"The loading door's in range."

"Great. I want him to sound really impatient. Like he's already called twice."

"Which guard are you calling?"

Leo hesitated. Taiga tapped the orange.

"That one."

"Yes. Him. He's had enough time with his fruit."

The warehouse guard put the orange down and went to the loading door. Zoe followed the collector between the cupboards instead, waiting to see which one he opened.

It was the one beside the cupboard she'd searched.

"Why are there two?" she demanded.

"Different lots."

"You put the same number on both."

Gav checked the labels. For several seconds he didn't say anything.

Leo leaned forward. "Administrative error?"

Gav picked up his pencil and changed one.

"The first label was damaged."

Zoe threw a spare die at his screen.

Taiga retrieved it from beside his foot, still grinning. He had started the afternoon wondering how much of this he'd have to learn before he could do anything. Now Gav kept describing things while he was trying to decide what to do with the last piece of information.

Beside him, Leo had drawn an arrow between the office and the loading door. He added the guard's direction, then tapped Taiga's sheet.

"Hang on. Before we do anything else, I've got something I can give you."

Zoe helped him find the right section. Leo read it aloud, then passed Taiga a smaller die for Bardic Inspiration. Gav explained that he could use it if a roll went badly.

Taiga set it beside the blue one.

"What's it supposed to do?"

"I'm being encouraging. You should appreciate this. I've spent most of the afternoon funding your scientific interests."

Somewhere during the next argument, Enzo arrived with fries. He stayed long enough to offer several investment recommendations, then settled at the far end of the couch with headphones on. His food remained within reach of the table.

Taiga took a fry while Gav brought the collector back upstairs with the astrolabe. The warehouse guard was still searching the dock for whoever had called him. Zoe's rogue followed the collector up the rear stairs and waited outside the office's back door.

Taiga considered the figures. The instrument was on the desk. The main door had a guard. Zoe could get in behind the collector, but they still had to leave the building afterward.

"We need him away from the desk."

"I could ask him to show me how it works," Leo suggested.

"You've been saying you know how it works."

"That was you. I'm financially involved."

Scott shifted in his chair.

"C-could I look at the receipt? That the woman gave us. Does it have his signature?"

Taiga stopped reaching for another fry. Gav searched his notes, found the receipt, and confirmed that it did.

"Oh, he's fucked," Zoe said.

They presented it. The collector denied signing it.

Taiga pointed to the ledger Gav had drawn on the desk.

"Compare it with that."

The collector shut the book. When Taiga reached for his figure, Leo put a hand on his arm.

"Hold on. Let me try something before you rob the tax man."

"He's already robbing us."

"I know. I'm trying to make that profitable."

Leo began describing an investigation into the missing fees. All the people who would need to come and examine the books. All the other customers who might remember paying.

He knocked Taiga's pencil away while he talked, caught it and put it back without interrupting himself. Taiga watched Gav's expression, trying to tell how much of the argument was getting through.

The collector was listening.

Then somebody called from the dock.

"There's nobody out here!"

Gav moved the warehouse guard toward the rear stairs. At the office's back door, Zoe was about to have company.

"Can I hear him coming?" she asked.

"You hear him call. Then the outside door opens."

She pushed her figure into the office.

"The door I'm coming through—does it open inward?"

Gav nodded. Zoe shut it behind her and wedged the stool beneath its handle, leaving the returning guard on the stairs outside. Taiga checked the other exit. The front guard had straightened up beside it.

"Is the astrolabe still on the desk?"

It was. Taiga reached for it.

The collector caught Brann's wrist, and Gav asked for a roll. Taiga read the result, added the bonus and discovered it was still terrible.

"The instrument slips—"

"Your other die," Leo said, tapping the smaller one. "Use that."

Taiga rolled it. This time, the total was enough. Brann caught the astrolabe against his chest before it hit the floor.

"Yes!" Leo slapped his shoulder.

Zoe passed a folded note across the table. Gav read it, asked for a roll, and nodded while Leo was retrieving the die from beside Taiga's sheet.

Then the front guard drew his sword.

They rolled initiative. Taiga found the right die without help this time, then read his sheet while Gav worked around the table. He could hear the warehouse guard trying the barricaded door. The main exit was still blocked, and the collector was shouting something about thieves.

As if he could fucking complain.

Scott's cleric got between the front guard and Leo. Taiga kept the astrolabe away from the collector while Zoe dragged a chest against the back door to reinforce the stool. Leo asked about the office window.

It opened onto a narrow roof above the loading area.

"Then that's where I'm going."

"We're upstairs," Taiga said.

"Yes, and I'm trying to remain upstairs until we've got a way down. You can keep arguing with the man who has a sword, but I'm going to look."

He moved the bard onto the roof. Taiga passed him the astrolabe through the window, then tried to follow and took a hit from the front guard. Gav gave him the damage; Taiga crossed out his remaining health, wrote the new number and sat straighter.

That was a lot.

Scott checked his underlined spells. On his turn, the cleric came alongside Brann and restored enough health for Taiga to breathe again.

Zoe was still beside the chest.

"Before I leave, can I see what's in here?"

Gav described a small lock. She got it open, lifted the lid, and found several years of customs records.

For a moment, she just stared at him.

"No money?"

"Receipts. Account books."

"Why is every box in this building disappointing?"

Leo bent forward laughing, his shoulder pressing against Taiga's. Taiga caught his upper arm before he knocked the dice tray over and kept his hand there while Zoe tried to establish whether any of the records had resale value.

Gav described somebody kicking the door behind the chest.

"Fine! I leave the tax paperwork. I'm going out the window."

Scott started laughing too, quietly at first, then enough that he had to put his pencil down. Taiga could feel Leo still shaking beside him.

He let go when his turn came and checked the distance between Brann and the guard.

"I'll keep him here. You two go after Leo."

Zoe and Scott reached the window. The collector had retreated behind his desk, leaving the front guard between Taiga and the way out.

"Can I knock him out instead of killing him?"

"Yes. Tell me if you get him down."

Taiga rolled to attack. Nineteen, before he even added the bonus.

Gav asked for damage, checked the guard's remaining health, and described Brann catching him with the flat of the blade. The guard dropped beside the desk. Taiga moved his figure through the window before Gav could introduce any more fucking customs officers.

Leo hit the table with both hands.

"There he is!"

Enzo pulled one headphone away from his ear.

"Are you winning?"

"Shut up," four people said at once.

They still needed to get off the roof. Taiga checked his equipment and found rope.

Fifty feet of it. More than enough.

Zoe tied it around the chimney while Leo put the astrolabe in his bag. Scott climbed down first and took the bag when Leo lowered it. Taiga kept checking the back door on the plan while Gav described the chest scraping across the office floor.

"The boat," he said. "The one they were unloading. Is it still there?"

Gav nodded. The men who had been unloading it were occupied with the noise upstairs.

"So it's not our boat."

"No."

Zoe moved her rogue toward the dock.

"It is now."

They pushed off before the warehouse guard got through the office and reached the window. A crossbow bolt struck the water behind them. Leo asked whether he could make the collector's voice shout from the other side of the dock again, but they had rowed too far out for the spell.

He sighed and kept rowing.

Gav took them around the river bend, through another argument about where to land, and back to the tavern. Taiga asked for the rest of the money before the astronomer had finished thanking them.

Zoe raised her fist toward him. He bumped it.

"Okay," Gav said, sitting back. "That's where we'll stop."

Taiga put his pencil down. There were notes all around the map now, several of them crossed out. He had drawn a route through the warehouse that they hadn't used and written guard? beside a cupboard. The orange had acquired a face.

Scott began erasing the damage from his sheet.

"You can leave that," Gav told him. "We'll reset if we play again."

"Oh. Okay."

Leo stretched his legs beneath the table and leaned back against the couch.

"My back's killing me."

"You've been leaning forward for an hour."

"Because every time I sit back, somebody decides to pick up something expensive. I was the only person treating that astrolabe like it could break."

"You climbed out of a window with it."

"And then lowered it very carefully."

Gav went to put the kettle on. Zoe began collecting dice, pausing to argue that the window downstairs would still have been a better way in. Scott listened with his character sheet in his lap, occasionally pointing out where her rogue would have met the guard.

Taiga reached for the last fry at the same time as Leo. They caught opposite ends of it and stopped.

Taiga let go.

"You've touched it now."

Leo ate it, smiling. Taiga wiped his hands on a napkin and picked his sheet up off the floor. One corner had a grease mark. He flattened it against the table while Leo leaned over his shoulder.

"What?"

"Just checking how much money we've got."

"We?"

"I helped you keep it. I think that counts."

"Your viewing deposit's still upstairs."

Leo dropped his head against the couch.

"Oh, fuck. It is."

Taiga started laughing. From the kitchen, Gav asked what he'd missed.

"Five silver!" Leo called back. "We left five silver with that asshole!"

Zoe paused over her dice bag.

"Yeah. That's why I took his purse."

Scott stared at her. Gav came back carrying the first two mugs.

"I wondered when you'd bring that up."

Zoe reached for her character sheet and smoothed it against the table.

"So. How are we dividing this?"
