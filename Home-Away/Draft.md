### Chapter 21

By Tuesday afternoon, Taiga had managed to say good morning to Leo, ask whether he had finished the Discrete assignment, and spend the rest of their conversation talking about the assignment.

He was doing brilliantly.

Leo was already in the lab when he arrived, pulling a charger out of his bag. He moved the bag off the chair beside him.

"Hey."

"Hey."

Taiga sat down. Leo plugged his laptop in, then offered him the spare socket.

"You need this?"

"I'm at eighty percent."

"Right. Okay."

There had never been this much ceremony involved in a fucking power outlet.

Taiga opened his laptop. Beside him, Leo was scrolling through the course page, his thumb running along the edge of the trackpad.

They had eaten together after the park. Leo had complained about the soup, Taiga had managed half a plate, and neither had known what to do when they reached the dorm entrance. Eventually they had said good night. Taiga had lain awake afterward, remembering pieces of the conversation with horrifying clarity.

Now Leo knew all of it. The scar. Keitaro. The journal.

Taiga clicked into the starter repository before he could get any further.

"Did you look at this already?" he asked.

"A little. The deletion bit's annoying."

"Which case?"

"Two children. Mine keeps deleting more tree than I asked for."

Taiga glanced at him.

Leo's mouth curved, briefly. Then Owen called for attention.

The assignment was an ordinary binary search tree. Insert, search, remove. Owen wanted the structure intact afterward, which apparently needed specifying.

"You can discuss it with whoever you're sitting with. Individual submissions, please. And don't just test the example I gave you. I also know how to copy an example."

He went back to his desk.

Taiga read the requirements while Leo ran his code again. A red line appeared at the bottom of the screen.

"Still doing it," Leo said.

"Show me."

Leo turned the laptop toward him. Taiga leaned over, stopping with his hand beside the trackpad.

"Can I?"

"Yeah. Of course."

He scrolled through the function. Leo watched for a while, then pulled a notebook out from beneath his laptop and drew a small tree.

"Wait. Before you change anything."

"I wasn't changing anything."

"You had the look."

Taiga glanced up.

Leo looked down at the notebook. "I mean, I think I know where."

Taiga let go of the trackpad.

"Okay. Show me."

Leo added another branch to the drawing, crossed out the top node, then followed the arrows with his pen.

For a minute, Taiga watched him work. Leo had drawn the numbers too close together and was now trying to squeeze another one between two branches. Normally, Taiga would have said something about that.

"That one," Leo said.

"The replacement?"

"Yeah. It has a child too. I'm moving the value, then treating the old node like it's empty."

He changed two lines and ran the tests. The red line disappeared.

Taiga read the function again.

"You could have done that without the extra search."

Leo sat back.

"I could also have spent another twenty minutes writing it wrong. This version works."

"Until you call it on a tree with ten thousand nodes."

"Are we doing that?"

"That's not the point."

"It is a little bit the point."

Taiga opened his own implementation.

"Look. You've already got the reference here."

Leo leaned in. "Okay, but then the parent has to—oh. Wait."

He took the pen back and drew a line across the page.

"Like that?"

"Yeah."

"That is uglier."

"It's shorter."

"Both things can be true."

Taiga snorted before he could stop himself.

Leo smiled properly this time. He turned back to the screen and started making the change, talking through it while he typed.

A few minutes later, Taiga's last test failed.

Leo looked over immediately.

"What happened to your beautiful solution?"

"Shut up."

"I'm asking professionally."

Taiga ran it again. Same result.

Leo studied the input, then reached across and pointed.

"You're testing the empty one."

"I know."

"You're reading the root before you check whether it exists."

Taiga stopped.

For several seconds, Leo said nothing. Then he very carefully folded his hands.

Taiga looked at him.

"Go on."

"No, no. I think we should respect the deceased."

"Fuck off."

Leo laughed, ducking his head when Owen glanced their way.

Taiga fixed the line. All green.

They worked through the written questions afterward. Leo argued with one of his explanations, Taiga argued back, and for a while the room shrank to the two screens and Leo's terrible diagram.

When Owen dismissed them, Leo closed his laptop but stayed seated.

Taiga packed his charger.

"We've got time for coffee before training."

Leo looked up.

"Yeah?"

"Unless you've got something else."

"No. Coffee sounds good."

They left together. At the stairs, both stepped aside to let the other go first.

Leo stopped.

Taiga stared at him.

Then Leo made a small, helpless sound of laughter.

"Okay, you go. We'll die here otherwise."

Taiga went.

Nora had claimed a table near the mess hall windows on Wednesday. By the time Taiga reached it, she had spread a notebook, two articles and the remains of her lunch across most of the available space.

She moved an article off the empty chair.

"I need an opinion."

"That's unfortunate."

"On one sentence."

Taiga put his tray down. "You've never asked for an opinion on one sentence in your life."

She turned the notebook toward him.

The sentence ran over four lines.

He read it, then read the beginning again.

"Who does 'they' refer to?"

"The committee."

"You mentioned the students more recently."

"Yes, but the students can't approve the proposal."

"Then why make me work it out?"

Nora took the notebook back, frowning at the page.

"Fine. That's useful."

Taiga began eating. She crossed something out, rewrote it, then looked up again.

"Would you still object if the context made it clear?"

He swallowed.

"Depends on the context."

"Because the paragraph before this explains—"

"There it is."

She smiled and pushed the notebook aside.

They spent most of lunch talking about an essay she had due on Friday. Taiga disagreed with the argument, which Nora apparently found more useful than agreement. By the time he finished his food, she had taken the notebook back twice.

Eventually she closed it.

"What are you doing for spring break?"

"Not sure yet. Probably camp."

"Camp Buddy?"

"Yeah."

"That sounds nice."

"What about you?"

"Home for a few days. My sister wants us to go away for a weekend, but every place she's sent me costs more than the trip."

"Send her somewhere cheaper."

"I did. Apparently it lacked atmosphere."

"What does that mean?"

"I asked. She sent me a photograph of a bathtub."

Taiga laughed.

Nora shook her head and began gathering the articles. "Anyway, I need to book the train before she decides we're going somewhere by boat."

"Why a boat?"

"Because I made the mistake of saying that once, and now she thinks I'm interested."

She checked the time and swore softly.

"I've got to go."

Taiga stood with his tray. Outside, Nora continued explaining why her sister's definition of a reasonable budget was indefensible. They parted at the path toward the library, still disagreeing about whether a bathtub could justify a hotel.

He had Calculus work waiting in his room and an unread Chemistry brief in his inbox.

He opened the brief on the walk back.

Three weeks. Group project.

Fantastic.

On Thursday, Professor Hassan went through the kinetics assignment before the lecture ended.

There were approved reaction systems, a booking calendar for the instruments, and a requirement to explain what the results actually showed. Taiga had already read the brief. Several people around him apparently hadn't.

"No, the mentor does not become an extra member of your group," Hassan said, answering the same question for the second time. "You will collect your own data and submit your own work."

After lunch, Taiga found Priya, Marcus and Daniel at their usual lab bench. Priya was reading the schedule on her phone. Daniel had printed the brief.

Marcus looked up.

"Please tell me you've got Thursday evenings free."

"I don't."

"Excellent start."

A few minutes later, their mentor arrived.

Taiga recognized him before Priya said his name. It would have been difficult not to. Purple hair tied back, gold at his ears, nearly a head taller than everyone clustered around the bench.

Zayne Alexander set his bag on an empty stool.

"Sorry. My lab ran over."

"You're ours?" Priya asked.

"That's the plan."

Marcus grinned. "We're getting celebrity supervision."

"Only on Thursdays. The other days cost extra."

He took the spare copy of the brief Daniel offered him and asked which reaction they were considering.

Priya showed him the system she'd marked. Taiga had chosen the same one: a purple solution that lost its color as the reaction progressed, leaving them with changes they could measure rather than guess at.

They talked through the first week's work. Marcus wanted to know whether they really needed so many repeats. Daniel wanted to check how long they could book the instrument. Taiga opened the example data on his laptop.

"If we divide the runs between us, we can get the measurements done faster."

Zayne leaned over the screen.

"Using the same setup?"

"Same instrument."

"But not necessarily the same preparation."

Priya tapped the procedure.

"That's what I was saying."

Taiga looked from the sheet to the booking calendar.

They would spend half their first session proving they had all made the same solution.

"Fine. We do the first ones together."

"Good," Zayne said. "Then you can complain about each other's technique with evidence."

Marcus pulled the schedule toward him. They found a slot, argued about the backup slot, and eventually agreed on a plan that required nobody to skip a class.

Zayne checked the reservation on his phone.

"Okay. Send me the first set of results before you start deciding which graph you want to believe."

"Do people do that?" Daniel asked.

"Constantly."

Taiga closed the example plot.

Zayne glanced at him. "Computer Science?"

"Yeah."

"Thought so."

"Because of the laptop?"

"Because you looked offended when we couldn't split the experiment into four identical jobs."

Marcus laughed.

Taiga put the laptop away. "It would have been faster."

"If the jobs were identical."

"Yeah, I got that."

Zayne smiled, quite untroubled by his tone.

"Good. We'll get along."

As they started collecting their things, two students stopped to speak to Zayne from the next bench. He remembered one name immediately and needed prompting for the other. Priya was still asking Daniel about the booking when Zayne turned back.

"Taiga, right?"

He nodded.

"You're with Apollo."

"How do you know?"

"I pay attention."

Marcus made a small sound behind him.

Taiga ignored it.

"So do a lot of people."

"Fair. The red hair helps."

Zayne picked up his bag.

"Send me the plan when you've written it up, handsome."

Taiga looked at him.

"Is that part of the supervision?"

"No, that's complimentary."

Zayne left before Taiga decided whether the answer deserved a response.

Priya held out her phone.

"Group chat. Before everybody escapes."

Taiga scanned the code.

They still had almost three hours of lab ahead of them.

Gav caught him after Friday's training while Taiga was trying to get his shin guards back into their sleeves.

"Are you free Sunday afternoon?"

"Why?"

"I'm running D&D at Apollo. Just a short one."

From the next locker, Leo looked over.

"How short is short?"

"Three hours. Maybe four if you spend a long time planning."

"That sounds directed at somebody," Leo said.

Gav was looking at Taiga.

He pulled the sleeve over the second guard.

"I've never played."

"I know. I've got characters ready. You can pick one, change the name, and I'll explain the rest."

"That sounded suspiciously easy."

"It is easy to start. You don't need to learn all the rules before we play."

Scott came past carrying his towel.

"He's already sent me twelve pages."

"Most of that is your character sheet," Gav said.

Scott gave Taiga a brief, apologetic look and went to the showers.

Leo leaned back against his locker.

"I'll try it."

Gav nodded, pleased. "Good. Zoe's coming too. She's played before, so she can help."

"You asked Zoe?"

"I asked her yesterday. She sent me about twenty character ideas."

Leo looked at the sheets in Gav's hand.

"Did you print all of them?"

"No. She's narrowed it down."

Leo was grinning.

"To nineteen?"

"Three. Which is manageable."

Gav began explaining the adventure. There was a harbor town, a stolen object, and apparently several ways to get arrested. Taiga finished packing, sat through an explanation of why the premade fighter would give him plenty of things to do, and eventually took the sheet Gav offered him.

Leo leaned over to look.

"That one has a sword."

"I can see that."

"And a shield. You're very equipped."

"What are you playing?"

"I haven't picked yet."

Gav showed him the remaining sheets.

Leo read the first page of the bard and looked increasingly interested.

"Oh, I can work with this."

Taiga took a photograph of Sunday's time and put the fighter sheet in his bag.

***

Saturday took them to Easton, where the ball spent an unreasonable amount of time out of play.

Olympus won. Taiga got the last twenty minutes, collected mud inside both socks and spent most of the bus ride home listening to Gav answer Leo's questions about spells.

By the time they reached campus, he knew the bard could make noises appear where there were no noises.

Leo had already asked whether those noises could include Enzo.

Enzo, three rows behind them, demanded royalties.

***

On Sunday, Zoe arrived at House Apollo with a dice bag large enough to contain a small animal.

Gav was clearing the low table in front of the couch. Scott had already taken the armchair, his character sheet balanced on a book. Taiga sat on the rug with his back against the couch.

Leo was beside him, reading the same spell for the third time.

"Don't spill anything on the map," Gav said.

Zoe put her drink on the side table.

"I've done this before."

"You're holding it over the map."

"Because you're still putting the table down."

Gav finished arranging the sheets and sat behind a folding screen.

Zoe tipped dice into a tray. There were far more than she could possibly need.

Taiga picked up a twenty-sided one.

"This yours?"

"The green ones are Gav's. Those are mine."

"They're all green."

"Different greens."

Leo leaned over and inspected the pile seriously.

"I think I've got one of yours."

Zoe accepted the die he offered her and slid a dark blue set across the table to Taiga.

"Use those. They're easier to read."

He put the twenty-sided one down in front of him.

Gav asked them to introduce their characters.

Zoe had chosen a tiefling rogue with a coat full of pockets and a reason to leave town quickly. Leo was a halfling bard who apparently owed money to several people. Scott's dwarf was a cleric.

Taiga looked at the name he'd written on the fighter sheet.

"Brann. Human. Sword."

Gav waited.

"And shield," Leo supplied.

Taiga turned toward him.

"What else do you want?"

"Why is he here?" Gav asked.

"For the money."

Zoe nodded approvingly.

"He's ready."

Gav explained the basic idea. They said what they wanted to do; he described what happened, sometimes asking them to roll. The numbers on the sheet helped determine whether they managed it.

Taiga checked where he'd written the bonuses.

Across the table, Scott had lined up a pencil, an eraser and his dice. He caught Taiga looking and moved the eraser out of the way.

Gav lowered his voice.

"You're in a tavern near the eastern docks. It's late afternoon. Rain's coming in off the river, and somebody's left the door open."

He described wet coats, a smoky hearth and a woman waiting at a table with a wooden case in front of her.

The case was empty.

Customs had taken her brass astrolabe when she entered town. She had paid the fee, but now the collector claimed he had no record of it. The instrument would be sold that evening along with the unclaimed cargo.

"What's an astrolabe?" Leo asked.

"An instrument for measuring the position of the stars."

"How big?"

Gav held his hands apart.

"About this big."

"Can I put it in my bag?"

"If you find it."

"Important detail."

The woman offered them money to bring it back. Zoe immediately asked how much of the payment they could get now.

Gav, as the woman, objected.

Zoe negotiated anyway.

By the time they stood outside the imaginary customs house, they had half the money in advance and Taiga had learned that the rogue's name only needed to be true if somebody could prove otherwise.

Gav spread out a rough plan of the building.

Front entrance. Storage floor. Stairs to an office. A loading door at the river.

Taiga leaned forward.

"Can we get in from the back?"

"There's a small dock. Two men are unloading crates."

"Windows?"

"High ones along this wall. One downstairs window at the side."

Zoe pointed.

"I'm checking that one."

"You're going alone?" Leo asked.

"I'll just have a look. You can keep looking at the door."

She rolled, added a number from her sheet and moved her little figure to the side of the building.

The window was unlatched. Through it she could see barrels, sacks and a guard sitting on a stool.

"Is he watching the window?"

"He's peeling an orange."

"Excellent. I like him already."

Taiga studied the front entrance again.

"We could wait until—"

"I could ask to see the auction stuff," Leo said. "Like a buyer. They want money, right? I've got the money Zoe just bullied out of the astronomer."

Gav looked down at his notes.

"You can try."

Leo turned slightly toward Taiga.

"Unless you want to do the back way."

Taiga considered the map.

"Front's easier if it works."

"Okay. I'll ask."

The guard at the front wanted to know who they were.

Leo sat up and gave Gav a smile.

"We heard there was an auction this evening. My associate here is interested in instruments."

Taiga looked at him.

Gav followed the glance.

"What sort of instruments?"

"Scientific ones," Taiga said.

"Brann looks scientific?"

"He's not asking my fucking degree."

Leo pressed his lips together.

Zoe, still by the window, started laughing.

Gav asked Leo to roll. The die bounced out of the tray and stopped against Taiga's knee.

"Seventeen," Taiga said.

"Plus five," Leo added, checking the sheet. "Twenty-two."

The guard let the three of them inside.

Taiga moved his figure through the doorway while Leo was still looking pleased with himself.

"You're doing the talking upstairs too."

"Gladly. I have references now."

Zoe slipped through the window once the orange-peeling guard turned away. She waited behind a pile of sacks while Gav brought the others up to the office.

The collector had rings on three fingers and very little interest in allowing strangers to examine anything for free.

Gav held out a hand.

"Viewing deposit."

"How much?" Leo asked.

"Five silver."

Taiga frowned.

"For looking?"

"Refundable if you buy."

"That's not a deposit."

"Tell him that," Zoe said.

Taiga looked at Gav, who had somehow made his face more unpleasant by lifting his chin.

"That's not a deposit."

Gav spread his hands.

"Then I suggest you wait until the public auction."

Leo put the imaginary money on the desk before Taiga could begin arguing properly.

"We're very interested."

The collector took it and called for his guard.

Zoe lifted a finger.

"While they're upstairs, I'm looking for the cabinet."

There were several locked cupboards on the storage floor. She searched the labels, found the inventory number the astronomer had given them, and got her tools out.

Taiga watched her roll.

She added the bonus.

Gav nodded.

"The lock opens."

"Oh, beautiful. I take the astrolabe."

"There's a problem."

"Of course there is."

The cupboard contained six identical wooden boxes.

Zoe rubbed both hands over her face.

Upstairs, the collector had begun asking what Taiga knew about astronomy.

"I know what the thing looks like."

"Tell him that too," Leo said.

"Why don't you tell him something?"

"Because I just told him you're the expert."

Taiga stared.

Leo started laughing.

"You volunteered me!"

"You said you were interested in instruments!"

"I wasn't speaking!"

Scott covered his mouth. Zoe had put her head down over her character sheet.

Gav waited, smiling behind his screen, while Leo tried to explain what he had meant and made it worse.

Taiga shook his head.

"Fine. I ask whether the mounting's complete. And whether he has the case."

Gav's smile changed.

He checked a note.

"The collector says the case is available separately."

"Bullshit."

"The collector looks offended."

"Good."

Leo lost it again.

When they finally quieted down, Gav sent the collector downstairs to fetch the instrument himself. A guard stayed with the party.

Zoe froze with her hand over the map.

"How long do I have?"

"He's on the stairs."

"I open the nearest box."

"Glass bottles."

"Next."

Gav glanced at the plan.

"Footsteps reach the bottom."

Zoe stared at him.

Then she moved her rogue behind the cupboard and held very still.

The collector stopped beside the orange-peeling guard. Something was missing from the inventory, and apparently this was the guard's fault.

Upstairs, Leo was reading his spell sheet.

"Can I make his voice come from outside?"

Gav leaned forward.

"The collector's?"

"Yeah. Calling the guard down to the dock. I've heard him now."

"Where outside?"

Leo pointed to the loading door on the map.

Gav measured the distance, checked the spell and nodded.

"The sound can come from there."

"Great. I do that. I make him shout for the guard."

"Which guard?"

Leo hesitated.

Taiga looked at the names he'd scribbled beside the map.

"The one with the orange."

Leo pointed at him.

"That guy. I want that guy."

Gav described the shout. The guard downstairs put his orange down and went to the loading door, muttering.

Zoe immediately moved her figure.

"I follow the collector. I need to see which box he takes."

Taiga grinned.

"There you go."

She stayed behind the stacked cargo while Gav moved the collector to a second cupboard. It had its own lock.

Zoe looked at the cupboard she'd opened.

Then at the new one.

"Why are there two?"

"Different lots."

"You put the same number on both."

Gav checked.

A silence fell.

Leo leaned forward.

"Administrative error?"

Gav looked between the labels, picked up his pencil and changed one.

"The first label was damaged."

Zoe threw a spare die at his screen.

An hour later, the common room smelled of fries.

Enzo had appeared halfway through an argument about whether Leo could afford the astrolabe, discovered that their money was imaginary and stayed long enough to make several investment recommendations.

Now he was at the far end of the couch with headphones on, watching something on his phone. His food had somehow become communal.

Taiga reached for another fry without taking his eyes off the map.

The collector was showing them the instrument. Zoe had made it upstairs through the back passage and was waiting outside the office. The guard who had gone to the dock had found nobody there and was on his way back.

"We need him away from the desk," Taiga said.

"I could ask him to show me how it works," Leo suggested.

"You've been saying you know how it works."

"That was you. I'm financially involved."

Scott shifted in his chair.

"C-could I look at the receipt?"

Gav looked up.

"The receipt?"

"That the woman gave us. Does it have his signature?"

They all turned toward him.

Scott looked down at the sheet again.

"I just thought..."

Gav held up one finger while he found the note.

It did.

Zoe sat back.

"Oh, he's fucked."

They put the receipt on the table.

The collector denied signing it.

Taiga leaned closer to Gav.

"Compare it with the book on his desk."

Gav looked at Scott.

"Are you asking to see it?"

Scott nodded.

The collector shut the ledger.

"No."

"Then I pick it up," Taiga said.

Leo touched his arm.

"Hold on. Let me try something before you rob the tax man."

"He's already robbing us."

"I know. I'm trying to make that profitable."

Leo turned to Gav and began explaining how expensive an investigation into the missing fees would be. How disruptive. How many people would need to come and look at the books.

His hands moved while he talked. One knocked Taiga's pencil away; he caught it, put it back and kept going.

Taiga watched Gav's expression.

The collector was listening.

Then the guard returned.

"There was nobody at the dock."

Leo stopped.

Zoe looked toward the ceiling.

Gav folded his hands.

"What do you do?"

Taiga looked at the front stairs. Guard. The collector was behind his desk. Zoe was outside the other door.

"Is the instrument still on the table?"

Gav nodded.

"I take it."

The collector grabbed his wrist.

Taiga rolled when Gav told him to. His first result was terrible. He read the bonus, added it and discovered it was still terrible.

The instrument fell. Scott made a small noise.

Leo's hand landed on Taiga's forearm.

"Wait, I can help with that. I've got—where is it?"

Zoe reached over and turned his sheet around.

"That one."

Leo read it.

"Bardic Inspiration. Can I give him that?"

Gav nodded.

"Then I do."

Taiga rolled the smaller die Leo passed him.

This time, the total was enough.

He caught the instrument against his chest before it hit the floor.

"Yes!" Leo slapped his shoulder.

Zoe pushed her figure into the office. She passed Gav a folded note while Leo looked for a pencil. Gav read it, asked her for a roll and nodded.

Then she pointed to the back passage.

"I shut that door and put something under the handle."

"What something?"

"Whatever's there. Chair. Crate. Tax legislation."

Gav glanced at the map.

"There's a stool."

"Wonderful."

The guard by the front stairs reached for his sword.

Gav asked them to roll initiative.

Taiga found the right die without help this time.

The office became crowded very quickly.

Scott's cleric got between the guard and Leo. Zoe dragged a chest into the back doorway to reinforce the stool. The collector tried to get around the desk, and Taiga's fighter knocked him back into his chair with the shield.

Leo used his turn to move toward the window.

Taiga looked at him.

"Where are you going?"

"Out."

"We're upstairs."

"There's a roof under it."

Gav checked the drawing.

There was.

Taiga moved his figure toward the window.

The guard hit him before he reached it.

"How much?"

Gav told him.

Taiga crossed out the number, looked at what remained and sat straighter.

"Fuck."

Scott leaned forward and checked his spells.

"I can heal him."

"You're next," Gav said.

"Good. Because that's a lot."

Scott moved his cleric beside Brann and restored enough health for Taiga to breathe again.

Leo was already outside on the narrow roof over the loading area.

He leaned over the table, bringing his shoulder against Taiga's.

"Pass it through. I've got a bag."

"You've got approximately four hit points."

"Eleven, actually, and very good balance."

"That is not a bag."

Zoe was trying to get the back door open again.

"What happened to the stool?" Taiga asked.

"I need to come with you."

"Move the chest first."

"I am moving the chest."

"You're on the wrong side of it."

She looked at the little figure, then at the chest Gav had drawn.

"Oh, fuck."

Leo bent forward laughing. His shoulder pressed harder against Taiga's.

Taiga caught his upper arm as he nearly knocked the dice tray over and kept his hand there while Zoe tried to negotiate with the furniture.

Gav was describing somebody kicking the back door.

"Can I just drag it sideways?" she demanded.

"Yes."

"Then I do that! Why are we discussing it?"

Scott began laughing too, quietly at first, then enough that he had to put his pencil down.

Taiga let go of Leo and moved Brann back toward the office door.

"I hold this one. Let them go out."

Zoe reached the window. Scott followed.

The guard advanced on Taiga.

"Can I knock him out instead of killing him?"

"Yes. Tell me if you get him down."

Taiga rolled to attack.

The die landed on nineteen. He added the bonus.

"That hits," Gav said. "Damage?"

Taiga rolled again and gave him the total.

Gav checked the guard's remaining health.

"Brann catches him with the flat of the blade. He drops beside the desk."

Taiga climbed through the window and joined the others on the roof.

Leo hit the table with both hands.

"There he is!"

Enzo pulled one headphone away from his ear.

"Are you winning?"

"Shut up," four people said at once.

***

They still needed to get off the roof.

Taiga had rope.

For the first time all afternoon, this solved a problem without creating another one.

Zoe tied it around the chimney. Scott went down first, then took the astrolabe when Leo lowered it in the bag. A boat waited beside the loading dock.

Nobody had mentioned a boat before.

Gav pointed at his notes when Taiga objected.

"You saw the two men unloading it."

"So it's not our boat."

"No."

Zoe moved her figure into it.

"It is now."

The men had gone to find out why somebody was shouting upstairs. By the time the first guard reached the window, the party had pushed off into the river.

Gav described a crossbow bolt hitting the water.

Leo asked whether he could make the collector's voice shout from the other side of the dock.

Gav looked at the distance and shook his head.

"Too far."

Leo sighed and began rowing.

They made it around the bend with the instrument, most of their health and all of Zoe's advance payment.

The astronomer was delighted.

Taiga asked for the rest of the money before Gav could finish describing her gratitude.

Zoe raised her fist toward him. He bumped it.

Gav sat back at last.

"Okay. That's where we'll stop."

For a moment, nobody moved.

Then Scott let out a long breath and began erasing the damage from his sheet.

"You can leave that," Gav told him. "We'll reset if we play again."

"Oh."

Leo stretched his legs out beneath the table.

"My back's gone."

"You've been leaning forward for an hour," Taiga said.

"People kept putting me in situations."

"You climbed out of the window."

"And saved the operation. You're welcome."

Gav got up to put the kettle on. Zoe began collecting dice, stopping every few seconds to explain exactly where her plan had been better than theirs. Scott listened with his character sheet still in his lap.

Taiga reached for the last fry.

Leo took the other end of it.

They looked at each other.

Then Taiga let go.

"You've touched it now."

Leo ate it, smiling.

"Very generous."

Taiga wiped his hands on a napkin and picked his sheet up off the floor. One corner had a grease mark. He flattened it against the table.

Beside him, Leo was looking over his shoulder.

"What?"

"Nothing. Just checking how much money we've got."

"We?"

"I helped you keep it."

"Your viewing deposit's still upstairs."

Leo dropped his head against the couch.

"Oh, fuck. It is."

Taiga started laughing.

Gav called from the kitchen to ask what he had missed, and Leo sat up immediately.

"Five silver! We left five silver with that asshole!"

Zoe looked up from her dice bag.

"Yeah. That's why I took his purse."

Scott stared at her.

Gav came back carrying the first two mugs.

"I wondered when you'd bring that up."

Zoe reached for her character sheet and smoothed it against the table.

"So. How are we dividing this?"
