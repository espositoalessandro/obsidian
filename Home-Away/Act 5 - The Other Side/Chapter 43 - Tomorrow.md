Taiga was halfway through an argument with a sentence.

He had written it three different ways. All three looked wrong.

Leo stood at the wardrobe pulling a clean shirt from between two things Dani would probably claim if she saw them. His phone lay on the bed, playing a voice message from Enzo that seemed to contain more traffic than information.

Taiga waited until it ended.

“Does he ever call from inside a building?”

“Only if somebody in the building is louder than him.”

Leo sent a reply, then put the phone in his pocket.

“He’s going to see our old coach. I said I’d come. You want to?”

Taiga looked at the notebook, then at the laundry basket beside the door. He had meant to wash his clothes before lunch, and if he left the sentence now, it would still be waiting when he returned.

“I think I’m staying here.”

“Okay. We’ll probably eat somewhere after, so don’t wait for me.”

Leo leaned over the desk to see what he was writing.

“What are you trying to say?”

“That we went to the beach on Friday.”

“We did.”

“I’m aware of the event. I’m having trouble putting it in the correct fucking tense.”

Leo pointed at the middle version.

“That one.”

“Explain why.”

“It sounds right.”

Taiga turned to look at him.

Leo smiled.

“I can explain it badly if you want, but Enzo’s already outside.”

“Go before you make it worse.”

Leo kissed the top of his head on the way past.

“I’ll bring you something.”

“I can find food.”

“I know. I meant something I want you to try.”

Taiga considered that.

“Then bring two.”

Leo laughed and left.

The gate closed a minute later. Taiga heard Enzo’s voice through the open window, Leo answering, then both of them moving away down the street.

He went back to the sentence.

The version Leo had chosen was the one he had written first.

“Of course it fucking is.”

He underlined it, found the explanation in his notes and spent another twenty minutes untangling what had gone wrong. After that he carried the laundry downstairs.

Mercedes was sitting in the shade with a plant between her feet, clipping away dead leaves and dropping them into an old bowl. She looked up when he came through.

“*Julio?*”

“*With Enzo. Visit their coach.*”

She nodded, then said something that seemed to include an offer of food.

“*Later. Thank you.*”

Taiga lifted the basket a little.

Mercedes glanced at it, pointed toward the washing area and went back to her plant.

He had used the machine with Leo twice by now. The only part he distrusted was the amount of detergent, which everyone seemed to measure by looking at it and reaching a private agreement.

He chose less than he thought necessary, started the cycle and went back inside for water.

Mercedes called to him as he crossed the patio.

Taiga stopped.

She pointed at something beside the stairs.

“*The little one. There.*”

There were three pots.

He pointed at the smallest.

“*This?*”

“*The other little one.*”

Taiga looked at the two larger pots, then back at her.

She followed his expression and started laughing.

“*That one.*”

He carried it over.

Mercedes inspected it, turned it once and asked him another question. He missed nearly all of it. She repeated herself more slowly, holding up the plant, then pointing into the pot.

“*More big?*” Taiga tried.

“Más grande.”

He repeated the phrase. She nodded.

Together they decided that the plant needed the larger pot after all.

Taiga went back for it.

“*You could say before.*”

Mercedes answered cheerfully. He caught *now* and *know*, enough to suspect that she did not consider any of this her fault.

He sat on the low step and held the new pot steady while she worked. There was soil in an open bag beside her chair, and when she asked for it, he understood from the first word.

For the next fifteen minutes they managed with pointing, repetition and occasional sentences Taiga wished he knew how to finish. Mercedes did not turn the task into a lesson. If he failed to understand, she showed him what she wanted and continued.

He liked that.

When they finished, she brushed soil from her hands and asked him to put the plant near the wall.

This time she specified which part of the wall before he lifted it.

“*Better,*” he told her.

She smiled at him as if the improvement had been entirely his.

The washing machine finished while he was rinsing his hands. He hung his clothes, made himself something to eat from the leftovers Carmen had shown him, and took his notebook back outside.

For a while, Mercedes listened to her radio and Taiga studied. A woman stopped at the gate to speak to her. Taiga recognized her face from the birthday but could not recover her name, so he said good afternoon and trusted that the rest would not become an examination.

The woman asked where Leo was.

This time the answer came more easily.

She nodded, then asked something else.

Taiga looked at Mercedes.

Mercedes repeated the question with fewer words.

“*You like it here?*”

“*Yes. Very much.*”

The woman smiled and continued talking to Mercedes.

Taiga returned to his notebook.

He had expected being on his own in the house to feel different. Mostly it meant that when he got something wrong, nobody beside him laughed in English.

His phone buzzed.

Leo had sent a photograph of Enzo eating.

**Leo:** he says this is his good side

**Taiga:** does he have one

**Leo:** he can see my phone

**Taiga:** good

A second photograph arrived, this time of Enzo’s middle finger.

Taiga smiled, put the phone down and finished the exercise.

Leo came home after two with a paper bag and a story that took him halfway up the stairs to tell.

Their old coach had remembered a match differently from both of them. Enzo had found a photograph that proved one small part of their version and treated it as a complete victory. Somebody else had arrived just as they were leaving, so they had stayed to talk.

Leo handed him the bag.

“You did get two.”

“One is mine.”

“That wasn’t the agreement.”

“There wasn’t an agreement. There was an instruction I chose to reinterpret.”

They ate sitting on the bedroom floor because Taiga’s clean clothes were spread across the bed.

Leo looked at them.

“Those are dry already?”

“Most of them.”

“I have stuff to wash.”

“Then wash it.”

“I was going to do it this morning.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo took another bite.

“Tomorrow works.”

Taiga handed him a napkin and asked about the coach.

Leo told the rest of the story with both hands, nearly losing half his food in the process.

---

On Tuesday morning, Taiga bought bread without him.

That was the whole expedition. He walked to the bakery they had already visited, waited behind a woman buying significantly more than he was, asked for what Carmen had written down and understood the price without needing it repeated.

Then the man behind the counter asked him something.

Taiga caught *Julio*, frowned and tried to reconstruct the rest.

The man repeated it.

“*Home?*”

Taiga understood.

“*Yes. At home.*”

That satisfied him. He put the bread in a bag and moved on to the next customer.

Outside, Taiga stood for a second looking at the street before turning the right way. On the way back he passed the turn for Antonio’s school, which Leo had pointed out during one of their walks.

He had his phone. He knew the name of the bakery. He knew where he was staying. None of that stopped the first familiar corner from producing an unreasonable amount of satisfaction.

By the time he returned, Leo had his own laundry going and was sitting at the patio table with two mugs.

“You look pleased with yourself.”

Taiga put the bread down.

“I completed a commercial transaction.”

“Incredible. I’ve heard those are difficult.”

“You weren’t there to interfere.”

“I’m devastated.”

Leo reached for the bag, and Taiga sat beside him.

Carmen had asked for the bread before leaving for work. Taiga had offered to get it while Leo was finding his clothes. Nobody had held a discussion about whether he could survive the journey.

He broke off a piece.

“The guy asked if you were home.”

“Which guy?”

“The one selling bread.”

“There are two.”

“The one who asked if you were home.”

Leo considered that.

“Helpful. I’ll narrow it down.”

After breakfast, they hung Leo’s laundry together. One of his shirts had twisted around a towel, and Taiga watched him try to separate them without putting anything down.

“Give me the towel.”

“I’ve got it.”

“You’ve been fighting it for thirty seconds.”

“It started.”

Taiga took the towel.

With both hands free, Leo pulled the shirt loose and shook it out. Water flicked against Taiga’s forearm.

“Asshole.”

“Collateral damage.”

They worked along the line, leaving enough room between things that Carmen would not come home and rearrange all of it.

Leo looked up as he pegged the last shirt.

“We should go somewhere again.”

Taiga held the empty basket against his hip.

“Already bored?”

“No. I liked Friday. I want more of that.”

“More buses?”

“Preferably fewer buses. Or at least not all on the same day.”

Taiga thought about their journey home from the beach, the warm window beside his head and Leo trying to find somewhere to put a damp towel without soaking the bag.

“We could stay somewhere.”

Leo turned toward him.

“You mean overnight?”

“Generally what staying means.”

“I know what staying means. I’m making sure you’re suggesting what I think you’re suggesting.”

“Two nights, maybe. Depends what it costs.”

Leo’s smile arrived slowly enough that Taiga had time to regret how much he liked causing it.

“I’d like that.”

“Then we can look.”

“Beach again?”

“Maybe. You said there were places inland you liked.”

“There are.”

“Show me those too.”

They took the basket upstairs and spent part of the morning sitting on the bed with Leo’s phone between them. He had photographs from trips with family, some better at showing the location than others. In one, most of the view was obscured by Ramón pointing.

Taiga moved to the next picture.

Leo moved it back.

“That was a really good place.”

“Was it behind your uncle?”

“Mostly, yeah. There’s another picture somewhere.”

They looked at rooms, rejected one because the price changed when Leo selected two people, and another because the photographs showed everything except the room itself.

Taiga wanted somewhere they could walk around. Leo wanted decent food nearby. Both agreed that paying extra to spend most of the visit traveling defeated the point.

They did not choose a destination.

They did choose two nights.

“The weekend after this?” Leo asked.

Taiga checked the dates.

“That gives us time.”

Leo nodded and set the phone down.

Then he leaned into Taiga, kissed him and stayed close enough afterward that Taiga could feel him smiling.

“You’re very pleased about an unbooked room.”

“I’m pleased about the person going in it.”

“Terrible taste.”

“I’m committed now.”

Taiga pulled him closer by the back of his shirt.

The laundry remained outside until after lunch.

---

Wednesday afternoon found them at the court Leo had shown Taiga during his first walk around the neighborhood.

Enzo had suggested it. Luis came too, with a ball under one arm and a bottle of water that he set carefully in the shade before stretching. Two other people Leo knew were already there.

They sorted teams through a discussion Taiga understood only in pieces. He recognized numbers, names and several objections. Enzo switched sides twice before anyone had touched the ball.

“Is there a reason for any of this?” Taiga asked.

“Luis wants you because he thinks I’m going to be annoying,” Leo said.

“He makes a good point.”

“I’m standing here.”

“That’s how he knows.”

They played until the heat made conversation less ambitious.

Taiga had missed this: reacting before he had to find words, watching a shoulder turn and knowing where the ball would go, making a run and having somebody actually see it. The surface changed what he could attempt, and the boundaries were established partly by agreement and partly by where everyone stopped chasing.

Luis was patient on the ball. Enzo was not.

Leo intercepted a pass Enzo had announced with his entire body, looked directly at Taiga and grinned before sending it between his feet.

Taiga turned after him.

“You get that once.”

Leo kept running.

“That’s all I needed.”

Taiga got him back ten minutes later, although Leo argued that it counted less because the ball had glanced off someone else first.

Nobody supported this interpretation.

By the time they stopped, Taiga’s shirt was sticking to his back and Luis had used most of his carefully saved water to rinse his face.

They sat along the shaded edge while Enzo replayed a sequence of the game using a stone and three bottle caps.

Taiga listened until the story assigned him a run he had not made.

“I wasn’t there.”

Enzo shifted the cap.

“You were emotionally there.”

“You passed to an empty space.”

“A space with potential.”

Leo laughed, leaning forward with his elbows on his knees. He looked as pleased with the afternoon as Taiga felt.

Luis said something about getting food. Everybody agreed with the part Taiga understood.

They ate at a place nearby, still arguing about the game. Taiga managed to order for himself and Leo, then discovered that knowing how to order did not necessarily prepare him for being asked which sauce he wanted.

Leo pointed out the choices. Taiga picked one.

The meal lasted longer than they intended because another friend stopped to greet Enzo and stayed to discuss football. Taiga knew enough Spanish by now to identify when someone was blaming the referee, but not enough to determine whether the accusation was reasonable.

He asked Leo once.

Leo listened, then leaned closer.

“He’s blaming the referee.”

“I got that. Was he right?”

“No idea. I didn’t watch the match.”

They got home near dinner, neither particularly hungry.

Taiga showered first. When he came back into the room, Leo was sitting on the edge of the bed reading messages with his towel over one shoulder.

“Your turn.”

“Yeah, one second.”

He finished typing, stood and put the phone down.

“Mariela’s trying to organize something for Samuel. His birthday’s next month, but he works some nights, so she’s asking which weekend makes sense.”

“Not ours.”

Leo looked up, smiling.

“Already possessive of the unbooked room?”

“I’m protecting my investment.”

“You haven’t invested anything.”

“I spent forty minutes looking at your uncle’s arm.”

Leo laughed and went to shower.

Later they lay across the two mattresses with the fan turned toward them. Taiga was reading a message from Caleb when Leo’s foot nudged his leg.

“What are you doing?”

“Explaining something badly.”

“To Caleb?”

“He asked.”

“Then he deserves it.”

Taiga sent the reply and put the phone aside.

Leo was lying on his stomach with his face turned toward him, one arm tucked under the pillow. His hair was still damp at the ends.

“Good afternoon,” he said.

“You lost.”

“I had Enzo.”

“That’s fair.”

Leo closed his eyes, smiling.

For a while they stayed like that. Someone downstairs moved a chair; Carmen called to Dani; a motorbike passed beyond the wall. Taiga had stopped trying to distinguish every voice when Leo reached out and settled a hand against his hip.

He left it there until they had to move for dinner.

---

Thursday was a holiday, which Taiga discovered when he went downstairs and found Dani eating breakfast in the clothes she had slept in.

He checked the day on his phone.

She watched him do it.

“*No school today.*”

“*Why?*”

She named something he did not recognize.

Taiga looked toward Leo.

“Corpus Christi,” Leo said, reaching for a mug. “Holiday.”

“Your calendar continues to surprise me.”

“You could look at it.”

“I was relying on local expertise.”

“Local expertise was asleep.”

The household had settled into a slower version of morning. Manuel was at the stove, Ramón had come downstairs to see what was cooking and somehow acquired a plate, and Carmen was sorting a cupboard she had apparently intended to sort for months.

Nobody seemed interested in leaving.

Taiga ate beside Leo and listened to Dani explain something about school. She was speaking to her brother, not slowing down for him, but he caught enough to identify a teacher and some kind of assignment.

Leo interrupted with a question.

Dani answered, then turned toward Taiga.

“Can I ask you something in English later?”

“You’re doing it now.”

“I know. A different thing.”

“What thing?”

“We have to record ourselves talking about somewhere we want to visit. I want to know if mine sounds strange.”

“Ask him. He talks constantly.”

“He says everything sounds good.”

Leo looked up.

“Because it does.”

“You didn’t hear the end.”

“I heard the beginning. It was a promising beginning.”

Dani looked back at Taiga.

“You see?”

He agreed to listen after breakfast.

She returned with her phone and a paragraph written in a notebook. Taiga expected three sentences. There were considerably more, and two had arrows leading to additions in the margin.

Dani sat across from him and began.

Her English was good enough that he understood everything. Her main problem was that she had written sentences too long to say in one breath.

He stopped her halfway through one.

“Where are you going?”

She looked confused.

“I said London.”

“I mean in the sentence. You started with a museum. Now there’s a train.”

“I want to do both.”

“Then give them different sentences.”

She looked at the notebook, drew a line and started again.

Leo sat beside them for a few minutes, offering a word when Dani asked for it. Then Antonio came through carrying a cardboard box with an entire corner missing.

He showed it to Leo with an expression that suggested serious damage had occurred.

Leo took it, turned it over and asked a question.

Taiga lost the answer. Dani had begun reading again, so he listened to her instead.

By the time she was ready to record, Leo and Antonio had moved to the floor near the wall. The damaged box had become part of something larger. There were two other boxes, a flattened tube and several strips of tape attached to Leo’s knee.

Antonio explained where another piece should go.

Leo held it in position while he taped it.

“You’re breathing loudly,” Dani told Taiga.

He stared at her.

“I’m sitting here.”

“The phone can hear it.”

“Then move the phone.”

She did. Her second recording was better.

When she finished, she played it back and listened with a frown.

“That’s what my voice sounds like?”

“Unfortunately, yes.”

“I don’t like it.”

“Everyone says that.”

“Do you?”

“I don’t record myself describing train journeys.”

She looked at him for a moment, then saved the recording.

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

She went upstairs with the notebook under one arm.

Taiga carried his empty mug into the kitchen. When he came back, Antonio had decided the construction needed wheels.

Leo was trying to explain why bottle caps could not simply be taped flat to the bottom if he wanted them to turn.

Antonio disagreed.

Taiga crouched beside them.

“What are you making?”

“A bus,” Leo said. “Apparently a very large one.”

Taiga looked at the connected boxes.

“How many people live in it?”

“Three, so far.”

“Efficient use of space.”

Antonio offered him a cap. Taiga took it and looked around for something that could serve as an axle.

They worked on it until lunch, mostly because once Taiga had established that the wheels could turn, he objected to stopping before they actually did.

Leo was no help. He kept adding suggestions that required more cardboard.

At one point Carmen came to inspect what had happened to the floor. She listened to Antonio’s explanation, looked at the mess, then walked away shaking her head.

Leo waited until she was out of hearing.

“That means she likes it.”

“That means she’s deciding who cleans it.”

“Also possible.”

Antonio rolled the bus forward. One wheel came off, bounced against the wall and disappeared behind a pot.

All three of them watched it go.

Taiga got up.

“I’ll get it.”

By the time they finally carried the thing upstairs to show Isa, Leo had tape stuck to his elbow and a streak of pencil across his cheek. He seemed entirely happy.

Taiga rubbed at the mark with his thumb.

“You’re making it worse,” Leo said.

“Hold still.”

“That has never reassured me.”

Taiga used the corner of his damp towel instead.

“There. Hold still next time.”

Leo inspected it, then leaned in and kissed him.

“Payment.”

“Underpaid.”

“I’ll see what I can do.”

The afternoon brought visitors without becoming another event. A cousin came by with her daughter. Someone Carmen knew stopped for coffee. Ramón went out and returned with a man who had apparently come to ask him something and ended up staying to eat.

Taiga moved between the bedroom, the kitchen and the patio. He read for a while, helped Manuel carry food out and spent nearly twenty minutes trying to follow a conversation between Mercedes and the cousin.

He understood enough to ask about a child’s age. His next question came out wrong, and the woman answered as if he had asked when she was leaving.

Leo, passing with two cups, caught the confusion.

“*He means how old she is*.”

“*Oh!*”

The woman laughed and told Taiga.

He thanked her, then tried the question again.

Leo corrected one word and continued toward the other table.

It was easier now to let a conversation end when he ran out of language. Nobody looked abandoned if he got up. He could take his plate inside, find something else to do and return when he wanted.

By early evening, he was upstairs putting away the clothes he had washed on Monday. Leo had meant to do his too, so Taiga cleared a space on the bed and left the folded pile where it would be easy to reach.

Leo appeared a little later and sat beside it.

“Did you do all that?”

“Mine. Yours are there.”

“Right. Thanks.”

He picked up a shirt, looked at it and set it down again.

Taiga closed the drawer.

“You all right?”

“Yeah. I’m just sitting for a second.”

Leo leaned back on his hands. There were voices in the courtyard below, but he did not look toward the window.

Taiga took the desk chair and turned it around.

“The bus still alive?”

“Lost a wheel again. Tía fixed it better than we did.”

“That hurts.”

“I’m choosing to believe she had superior materials.”

They talked about the wheels for a minute, then about Dani’s recording. Leo asked whether she had sounded good.

“She did.”

“I told her that.”

“You told her before she finished.”

“She gets annoyed if I interrupt.”

“She also gets annoyed if you don’t listen.”

Leo smiled.

“Yeah. She’s very flexible.”

He rubbed both hands over his face, then reached for the shirt again.

“Long day?” Taiga asked.

“Just a lot of talking.”

Taiga glanced toward the window. That much was true.

Leo put two shirts into the wardrobe before his phone vibrated. He checked it, listened to a short message, then replied.

“Mariela?” Taiga asked.

“Luis. He can’t do the first Saturday.”

“Then tell Mariela.”

“I am.”

Leo sent another message, waited for the reply and stood the phone against the pillow while he put the rest of his clothes away.

---

Friday morning was quiet again.

Leo went out to pick up something Carmen had ordered, and Taiga stayed at the table with his notebook. After an hour he put it away, took a shower and called Caleb.

The call connected to a view of a ceiling.

Taiga waited.

A hand moved across the camera, followed by Caleb’s face.

“I put it down backward.”

“I noticed.”

Caleb adjusted the phone.

Behind him, Taiga could see a lamp and part of a shelf. Somewhere outside the frame, another person was moving things.

“Are you busy?”

“Not anymore. I was putting something together.”

“How did that go?”

“The instructions were wrong.”

“Were they?”

Caleb tilted the camera toward a small shelving unit. One shelf was visibly upside down.

Taiga considered it.

“You sure about the instructions?”

“The diagram for that part was mirrored.”

“A convenient explanation.”

“I can show you.”

“Please don’t.”

Caleb put the phone back.

“How’s Leo?”

“Good. Out getting something.”

“And you?”

Taiga looked across the patio. Mercedes was inside; he could hear her radio through the doorway.

“I’m fine. I know where the bread is now.”

“Was it missing?”

Taiga laughed.

“No. I mean I can go buy it without him.”

Caleb nodded.

“Your Spanish getting better?”

“A bit. People keep talking after I understand them, which makes it worse.”

“They tend to do that.”

“Very inconsiderate.”

They talked about the question Caleb had sent him the week before, then about Olympus. Taiga had expected the mention of Room 317 to make him miss it more sharply. Mostly he found himself wondering where he had packed one particular cable.

Caleb knew.

“Front pocket of the bag.”

“Why do you remember that?”

“You asked me the same thing before you left.”

“Of course I did.”

Leo came through the gate while they were still talking, carrying a small package. He saw the phone, mouthed *Caleb?*, and waited for Taiga’s nod before leaning into view.

“Hey! Did you fix whatever he was being unhelpful about?”

“I fixed part of it.”

“I was helpful,” Taiga said.

“You sent me a beach,” Caleb replied.

Leo laughed.

“He did send you a beach.”

“It was a good beach.”

Leo put a hand on Taiga’s shoulder, said a few more words to Caleb and went inside with the package.

When the call ended, Taiga stayed outside for a while, looking up something Caleb had mentioned. He was still there when Leo came back carrying lunch.

They ate together, then went upstairs to look at places for their trip again.

One room had a small balcony Taiga liked. Leo liked it too until they worked out where it was in relation to everything else.

“We’d need to get rides every time,” Leo said.

Taiga moved back to the map.

“That’s annoying.”

“There are other ones. We have time.”

They saved two possibilities and left the rest for later.

In the afternoon, Manuel returned from work and began repairing a cupboard door in Carmen’s kitchen.

Taiga was getting water when he noticed the loose hinge. He had heard the door scrape all week without paying much attention to it.

Manuel looked up and gestured for him to hold it steady.

Taiga put the glass down.

“*Here?*”

“*A little higher.*”

He adjusted his grip.

Manuel tightened something, opened the door carefully and frowned. Then he took the hinge off altogether.

Taiga crouched beside him.

One of the screw holes had widened enough that the screw no longer held.

He pointed.

Manuel nodded, pleased that he had seen it, and began explaining what he intended to do. Taiga understood almost none of the explanation, but the objects in Manuel’s hands helped.

He watched, passed things when asked and eventually learned the word for screwdriver through sheer repetition.

Leo came in while they were testing the door.

“What are you doing?”

“Repairing your inheritance.”

“My inheritance is a cupboard?”

“Depends how well this goes.”

Manuel asked what they had said. Leo translated, and his father laughed before telling him something longer.

Leo leaned against the counter.

“He says you’re a better assistant because you don’t keep suggesting other things.”

“Sounds like informed criticism.”

“I was twelve.”

Manuel added something.

Leo looked at him.

“Okay, fifteen.”

Taiga smiled and held the door while Manuel checked the hinge again.

From the patio, Dani called Leo.

He answered without moving.

She appeared in the doorway a moment later, holding her phone and talking quickly. Taiga caught *send* and *tomorrow*, then lost the rest.

Leo looked at the screen she offered, asked a question and went outside with her.

Manuel handed Taiga the screwdriver.

“*Try.*”

Taiga tightened the final screw and opened the door. It moved cleanly.

Manuel closed it again with obvious satisfaction.

“*Good.*”

They put the tools away together. Manuel had set the small box down in the living room, then forgotten doing it, so Taiga found it while he checked the kitchen.

When Taiga carried it back, Manuel smiled and tapped his own forehead.

“*Too many things.*”

Taiga understood.

“*Me too. Sometimes.*”

Manuel shook his head and pointed at him.

“*Young.*”

“*Still forget.*”

That made Manuel laugh.

They found Leo at the patio table with Dani and Carmen. Dani had apparently finished whatever she needed, because she was scrolling through something unrelated while Carmen mixed something in a bowl and dictated a message.

Leo held Carmen’s phone and typed.

Taiga sat down with his water.

He could follow enough to understand that they were arranging food for someone. Carmen named two dishes, then changed one. Leo erased something and started again.

Manuel went inside to wash his hands.

Taiga listened until the names and quantities tangled together, then opened his notebook to write down *screwdriver* before he forgot it.

Leo glanced at the word.

“Papá teach you that?”

“Several times.”

“That’s usually how it works.”

Carmen touched Leo’s arm and began another sentence. He looked back at the phone.

Later, after the kitchen had filled with people making dinner, Taiga went upstairs to put the notebook away. Leo was sitting at the desk with the monitor off.

He had one earbud in.

Taiga stopped beside him.

“You on a call?”

Leo shook his head and took it out.

“No. Nothing playing.”

Taiga set the notebook down.

“Fair enough.”

Leo reached for his hand and held it loosely, his thumb moving once across the knuckles. For a while Taiga stayed standing beside the chair.

Then he sat on the bed, close enough that Leo did not have to let go.

Downstairs, Ramón laughed at something.

Leo looked toward the door, then back at Taiga.

“Papá said you fixed the cupboard.”

“He fixed it. I held things.”

“Still. You’re getting adopted for your practical value.”

“Your grandmother uses me to move plants.”

“She uses everyone to move plants.”

“I’ve noticed.”

Leo smiled, but the conversation stopped there.

Taiga looked at the blank monitor. He had left a glass on the desk earlier, so he took it when he stood.

“Want water?”

“Yeah. Thanks.”

When Taiga returned with two full glasses, Leo had the monitor on and was checking one of the places they had saved that morning.

He turned the screen slightly.

“This one has another room available. Cheaper, but no balcony.”

Taiga sat beside him and looked.

The room was ugly.

They rejected it together.

---

Saturday began with Leo standing at the wardrobe holding two shirts.

Taiga watched him consider them.

“We’re getting breakfast.”

“I know.”

“Outside. Not at a ceremony.”

“One of these has a stain.”

“Then wear the other.”

Leo turned the second shirt around.

It had a smaller stain.

Taiga laughed.

“You washed those.”

“Apparently without accomplishing much.”

He found a third shirt, pulled it on and reached for his phone.

Taiga stood, took the phone from his hand and slipped it into Leo’s pocket.

“Shoes first.”

“Yes, sir.”

They went out before the house could feed them. Carmen saw them crossing the patio, asked where they were going and accepted the answer once Leo promised they would eat something substantial.

Taiga did not know what qualified as substantial in her system. He suspected the answer changed depending on whether she had prepared it.

They walked farther than the bakery, past the court and along a street Leo had pointed out earlier in the week. Taiga recognized where they were until the last turn.

The place Leo chose had a few small tables and a fan positioned so that it served the counter better than the customers. They sat near the open doorway.

Taiga studied the menu while Leo ordered drinks.

He could read more than he could confidently pronounce. When the woman returned, he chose something, corrected himself halfway through the order and managed the rest without looking at Leo.

She asked a short question.

He understood it.

“*Yes, please.*”

After she left, Leo rested his forearms on the table.

“You sounded good.”

“Don’t sound so surprised.”

“I wasn’t surprised.”

“You were smiling.”

“I’m allowed to enjoy things.”

Taiga nudged his shoe beneath the table.

The food arrived, and for a while neither of them said much. Leo stole something from Taiga’s plate, got his wrist caught on the second attempt and negotiated an exchange.

He had his phone facedown beside his glass.

It vibrated twice during breakfast. The first time he ignored it. The second, he glanced at the screen, typed a short reply and put it down again.

“Who won the scheduling war?” Taiga asked.

“What scheduling war?”

“Samuel’s birthday.”

“Oh. Nobody yet. Mariela thinks the second Saturday, but Luis might be working.”

“Put them in a group chat.”

“They’re in a group chat.”

Taiga stopped chewing.

Leo smiled.

“Yeah.”

“Then why are they all talking to you?”

“Luis sent me his schedule because Mariela keeps changing the date. Samuel hasn’t got next month’s yet.”

“That sounds like a reason to wait.”

“Probably. I told her that.”

His phone vibrated again. Leo looked at it, laughed at something and turned the screen toward Taiga.

Mariela had sent a photograph of Enzo looking deeply offended, with a caption Taiga could only partly understand.

“What does the last bit mean?”

“Basically that this is what happens when you ask him to contribute money.”

“That part I understood from his face.”

Leo put the phone away.

They stayed over coffee, talking about the trip and then about nothing connected to it. Taiga told him what Caleb had said about the shelving unit. Leo wanted to know whether the diagram really had been wrong.

“I didn’t let him show me.”

“Coward. We could have had an answer.”

“We could have had a forty-minute demonstration.”

“That’s also an answer.”

When they left, Leo reached for his hand as they crossed the road.

Taiga let him keep it once they reached the other side.

They bought a few things on the way back and stopped at the bakery because the smell made Leo turn his head. Taiga pointed this out. Leo denied it while opening the door.

By the time they got home, they were carrying more food than they had intended.

Carmen inspected the bag.

Whatever she said made Leo nod gravely.

“What?”

“She approves of our breakfast.”

“She didn’t see our breakfast.”

“She’s looking at the evidence.”

---

That afternoon, Leo went to a cousin’s house with Ramón.

Taiga had met the cousin at the birthday. He remembered glasses, a child who had fallen asleep across two chairs and a discussion of football that had required Enzo to translate. That was as much as he could reliably attach to the invitation.

Leo asked him to come while looking for his wallet.

Taiga was sitting at the desk, working through something on his phone with the notebook open beside him.

“I might stay. I want to finish this.”

“More Spanish?”

“Mostly. Then I promised Caleb I’d look at what he sent.”

Leo found the wallet beneath the shirt he had rejected that morning.

“Okay. We’re not going far.”

“I know where you live.”

“Good. Saves me writing it down.”

He came over, tipped Taiga’s chin up and kissed him.

Taiga caught his wrist before he moved away.

“Did I forget something?”

“Your other shirt.”

Leo looked down.

He had changed into the one with the smaller stain.

Taiga pointed.

Leo sighed.

“Fine. Fourth option.”

After they left, Taiga finished the exercise, read through Caleb’s message and realized the question was easier to understand if he stopped assuming he remembered the example correctly.

He wrote a reply, deleted half of it and tried again.

An hour passed without anybody asking him where Leo was.

Then Antonio appeared in the doorway.

“*Leo?*”

“*Out. With your father.*”

Antonio nodded but did not leave.

Taiga waited.

The kid pointed at the monitor, then at the controller beside it.

Taiga considered him.

“*You play?*”

Antonio answered with a sentence delivered at considerable speed.

Taiga caught *yes*, *Leo* and *win*. That seemed sufficient.

He moved the chair and connected the controllers.

Antonio chose a car without hesitation.

Taiga chose his own.

Halfway through the first race, he discovered that Antonio had memorized a shortcut Leo had never shown him.

The kid took it, emerged ahead and started laughing.

Taiga stared at the screen.

“Your cousin is a fucking criminal.”

Antonio glanced at him.

Taiga searched for the Spanish.

“*Leo. Bad teacher.*”

Antonio agreed enthusiastically.

They played three races. Taiga won the second, narrowly, and lost the third because he tried the shortcut without knowing where it ended.

Antonio demonstrated afterward, driving slowly enough for him to follow.

Taiga watched.

“*Again.*”

The kid did it again, explaining with gestures as much as words.

By the time Isa called him downstairs, Taiga could take the turn.

He intended to make Leo regret the omission.

Leo and Ramón came back near six. Taiga heard their voices in the courtyard while he was making tea.

Leo entered the kitchen carrying a container.

“More food?” Taiga asked.

“She sent some for Abuela.”

“Does anybody ever return empty-handed?”

“Occasionally. We try not to encourage it.”

Leo put the container down and leaned against the counter.

“How was it?”

“Good. Her little one remembered you.”

“Which little one?”

“The one who fell asleep at my birthday.”

“Strong first impression.”

“She asked why your hair was red.”

“What did you tell her?”

“That you’d been very angry for a long time.”

Taiga looked at him.

Leo smiled.

“It was a good explanation.”

“It was slander.”

“You should’ve been there to defend yourself.”

Taiga poured another cup.

Leo took it with both hands.

For the first time that afternoon, Taiga looked at him properly. He was still smiling, but he had lowered himself against the counter as if standing had become an unnecessary addition to the conversation.

“Sit down,” Taiga said, nodding toward the table.

“In a minute. I’m taking this to Abuela.”

“The food isn’t going anywhere.”

“I know.”

Leo picked up the container and carried it across the patio with the tea in his other hand.

Taiga took his own cup outside.

Mercedes asked Leo something when he gave her the food. He answered, pulled up a chair and began telling her about the cousin’s daughter. Mercedes laughed at the part Taiga assumed concerned his hair.

Taiga sat beside them.

After a while, Antonio came down to tell Leo about the races.

That required a demonstration.

Leo put his cup down, listened to the description twice and looked at Taiga with growing amusement.

“You found the shortcut?”

“He found the shortcut. You concealed it.”

“I thought you knew.”

“Bullshit.”

Antonio said something else, pointing at the stairs.

Leo laughed.

“All right. Show me.”

The three of them went upstairs.

Taiga beat Leo on the second race.

He enjoyed it enough to forget about the tea.

Later, after Antonio had gone back to his parents and dinner had brought everyone downstairs again, Leo asked if Taiga wanted to watch something.

“Your choice,” Taiga said.

“Dangerous permission.”

“I reserve the right to complain.”

“You always do.”

They settled on Leo’s bed with the laptop balanced where both could see it. The sofa mattress beside them had become a convenient place for the things they were supposed to put away.

Leo chose an action film, then spent the opening five minutes insisting Taiga should give it time.

Taiga did.

At the first explosion, he looked over to make a comment and found Leo’s eyes closed.

He stopped.

Leo stirred when a voice on the film got louder.

“What happened?”

“Something exploded.”

“Helpful. Why?”

“I was going to ask you.”

Leo rubbed his cheek against the pillow.

“I saw this before. The guy’s brother—wait. No, that’s a different one.”

Taiga paused it.

“We can finish tomorrow.”

“I’m awake.”

“You just asked what happened.”

“I was resting my eyes.”

“You’re twenty, not seventy.”

Leo laughed quietly and pushed himself a little higher against the pillow.

“I’m fine. Put it back.”

Taiga did. For another ten minutes, Leo watched with him and supplied commentary he had not requested.

Then Leo’s head settled against his shoulder.

This time Taiga let the scene finish before closing the laptop.

He moved it onto the desk, turned off the light and lay back down. Leo shifted enough to make room without properly waking.

It had been a long afternoon at the cousin’s. They had gone to bed late the night before. The room was warm.

Taiga pulled the sheet over them and closed his eyes.

---

On Sunday morning, Leo complained that Taiga had watched the film without him.

“I didn’t.”

“You know what happened after the car.”

“Because I was awake during the car.”

“I remember the car.”

“You remember asking why there was a car.”

Leo sat up, hair flattened on one side.

“That sounds like a different kind of question.”

Taiga reached for the laptop.

“I stopped it. Look.”

The progress bar confirmed his account.

Leo inspected it, then leaned over to kiss his cheek.

“You waited.”

“I wanted somebody else to suffer through it.”

“Very generous.”

They finished it after breakfast.

The ending was worse than the beginning. Leo admitted this without giving Taiga the satisfaction of apologizing for his choice.

By then the household had started moving around them. Manuel was doing something outside with Ramón; Isa came down with a bag she needed to return to Carmen; Dani had invited a friend over and was carrying glasses upstairs.

Taiga took his mug to the kitchen and found Mercedes trying to reach a dish on a shelf.

He got it down for her.

She asked for another.

He recognized the word this time.

They carried both outside, and she told him where to put them. Her directions were ordinary Spanish, not especially slow. Taiga still needed her to repeat the last part, but he knew which part he had missed.

Leo passed through on his way to answer the gate.

Taiga did not look up.

He spent the next hour helping with lunch in small ways, sometimes beside Leo and sometimes somewhere else. When Carmen asked where the bottle opener was, Taiga knew. When Ramón asked him a question about Olympus, he answered badly enough that Isa corrected him, then waited while he tried again.

Leo was at the other end of the patio talking to the neighbor who had come through the gate.

Taiga could hear him laughing.

Lunch brought them back beside each other. Leo’s knee rested against his beneath the table, and when Taiga reached for water, Leo moved the jug closer without interrupting what he was saying.

The conversation turned to something from Ramón’s work. Taiga understood a delivery had gone wrong and that Ramón considered somebody else responsible. He failed to identify the guilty party before the story moved on.

Leo began translating.

Taiga shook his head.

“I’m okay. Finish eating.”

“You sure?”

“I got the main part. Somebody else fucked up.”

Leo laughed.

“Pretty much.”

He went back to his food.

After lunch, Taiga took the plates inside while Leo helped Manuel move the table back into the shade. Carmen told them to leave the rest until later. For once, everyone listened.

Taiga went upstairs, read for a while and sent Caleb the explanation he had finally finished. Then he checked the prices on one of the places he and Leo had saved.

There was still a room for two nights.

He carried the phone downstairs to show him.

Leo was sitting near the gate again, elbows on his knees, looking at nothing in particular. Taiga had not heard him leave the patio.

He sat beside him.

“Found this.”

Leo looked at the screen.

“Same price?”

“A little less if we don’t get breakfast.”

“We’d have to buy breakfast.”

“I know. I’m showing you the option, not proposing starvation.”

Leo smiled and scrolled through the photographs.

Taiga waited while he looked.

After a minute, Leo handed the phone back.

“Let’s check the route tonight.”

“Works for me.”

Leo leaned against the wall.

Taiga could hear Dani and her friend laughing upstairs. From the kitchen came the sound of a cupboard closing cleanly, without its old scrape.

He stretched his legs out.

Leo yawned, covering it with the back of his hand.

“You’re tired.”

“Little bit.”

“You fell asleep during your own film.”

“It wasn’t that good.”

“You defended it for half an hour.”

“I had to give it a chance.”

Taiga smiled, then looked at him again.

Leo had his eyes closed. He was not sleeping; his foot moved when somebody passed beyond the gate, and he opened his eyes when Taiga shifted beside him.

“Think I’ll sleep in tomorrow,” he said.

“Sounds ambitious in this house.”

“I can ignore people until nine.”

“Can you?”

“I’m going to attempt it.”

They sat a little longer. Taiga put his phone away instead of showing him another room.

When they stood, Leo stretched both arms overhead and held the stretch until his back clicked.

“Better,” he said.

They went back across the patio.

Isa came down the stairs as they reached the table. She was talking to Ramón, with Antonio walking behind her carrying a small plastic car.

Taiga caught *tomorrow* and *early*. Ramón answered, checked something on his phone and shook his head.

Leo paused.

Isa looked at him and asked a question.

Taiga understood Antonio’s name, *school*, and enough of the rest to know what she wanted.

Leo answered at once.

“*Yes, I can take him.*”

Isa thanked him, then spoke to Antonio. The kid appeared far less pleased by the prospect of Monday than anybody else.

Ramón said something to Leo and pointed toward the gate. Leo nodded.

They continued upstairs. Antonio rolled the car along the railing until his mother told him to carry it properly.

Taiga looked at Leo.

“Didn’t you just say you were sleeping in?”

“Just tomorrow. Tía has to leave early, and Tío’s got a delivery the other way.”

“You’re taking Antonio?”

“Yeah. It’s not far.”

Leo picked up the empty glasses from the table.

Taiga took two before he could stack them.

“I know where the school is.”

“Then you know it won’t take long.”

He said it easily, without irritation, and carried the glasses inside.

Taiga followed.

There was nothing particularly unreasonable about taking Antonio to school. Isa had asked because the morning was awkward, Leo knew the way, and neither he nor Taiga had anywhere they needed to be.

Taiga rinsed the glasses while Leo put them away.

The part that stayed with him was how quickly Leo had answered.

He had still been rubbing sleep out of one eye.

“Come on,” Leo said, touching his back as he passed. “Let’s look at that route before I forget.”

Taiga dried his hands and followed him upstairs.

Leo sat at the desk and opened the map. Taiga took the edge of the bed beside him, close enough to see the screen.

He thought of Friday evening, when Leo had been sitting in front of the blank monitor. Last night’s film. The laundry he had meant to do on Monday and finally done on Tuesday, after Taiga had already gone out for bread.

None of it had seemed worth thinking about separately.

Leo turned the phone toward him.

“That connection looks annoying, but the other place is farther.”

Taiga leaned closer.

They compared the routes, then the rooms, then the cost of staying two nights instead of one. Leo became more animated when they found a photograph of a place they could walk to nearby. He put his hand on Taiga’s leg and started describing something he remembered from another visit.

Taiga listened.

He liked hearing him like this. He liked the way Leo included him in the picture without stopping to ask whether he wanted to be there.

But when Leo paused to read something, his shoulders dropped again.

Taiga had spent the week finding ways to need him less. He could get food, do his washing, talk to people and spend an afternoon without waiting for Leo to give it a shape.

Yet Leo seemed to have less energy left than when Taiga had needed him to explain almost everything.

He looked at the hand resting on his leg, then back at the screen.

“Show me the walk,” he said.

Leo did.
