# II - Disrupture

***

### Chapter 13

The first month of university had stretched itself into approximately a year and a half. Every day had contained a new building, a new person, a new rule, some new way to embarrass himself in front of Coach Mercer, Professor Hall or whichever random stranger happened to be standing nearby when Taiga forgot how normal people behaved.

And yet, as soon as he arrived on the gravel path, no time at all seemed to have passed.

Taiga ended up arriving earlier than everyone else. 
He stopped under the familiar entrance arch. A smile automatically appeared on his face. 

"Taiga!"

He looked down. Yoshinori was waving at him outside the office cabin.
Taiga raised a hand. His smile didn't leave.

As if summoned by hearing that name, Yuri popped out of one of the cabins.

"Did you call—TAIGAA!"

She immediately rushed toward him and hugged him tight before he could even realize what was happening. 

"Oh my God, look at you! Three months of college and you're already barely recognizable!"

Taiga raised an eyebrow. "I haven't gotten any taller in three months."

"Unfortunately no, but..." She pinched his cheek. "You definitely look different."

"Yeah. Whatever."

"Come on, Yuri. Let him settle a second before attacking him."

Yoshinori appeared by her side.

"It's good to see you! You look good."

"Yeah, you too."

He smiled. "Come, give me your bag. I'll leave it in your cabin. You must be hungry after the trip. Aiden's already cooking and the others are in the mess hall."

Taiga handed him his duffel and followed Yuri into the mess hall.

"Hey guys! Look who's come back!" she announced.

Three heads turned toward him. Lloyd smiled first and waved at him with both hands.

"Taiga! Welcome back! It's so good to see you."

Darius raised a hand. "Hello, welcome back."

"Took your sweet time, Dynamite," Yoichi said with his arms crossed.

Taiga couldn't help but smile again. "Hey."

He sat at the table with the rest of them. The familiarity of the mess hall hit him all at once. Everything was still as he'd left it, except someone had put Christmas decorations everywhere. 

Lloyd immediately wanted to know every detail about Olympus's architecture, since he'd seen pictures and was astonished by the grandiosity of the structure. Darius was curious as well, but let Lloyd ask the questions. 
Yoichi didn't see how a couple of stones could be that interesting. 
Yuri was evidently holding back with her questions. Probably saving the worst for later.
How merciful.

Ten minutes later, Aiden came out of the kitchen in his usual apron, still holding a spatula.

"Taiga! I thought I heard your voice! How's it going, buddy?"

"Hey, Aiden. I'm fine. It's good to be back."

Aiden smiled widely. "Glad to hear it! Well, I won't keep you waiting for long, you must be starving! Be back in a jiffy!" He disappeared back into the kitchen.

Yoshinori joined them as well. "So, how was the trip?"

Taiga shrugged. "Normal. Boring, I guess."

"I can imagine."

They kept catching up for the next fifteen minutes, then the door opened and Goro came in. His expression was stern as usual, but when he saw Taiga it softened slightly.

"Ah, hello, Taiga. Welcome back."

"'Morning, Goro."

He hung his leather jacket and took his place at the table.

"I hope your trip was alright."

"Pretty much, yeah."

"Good. So, how was college?"

"Dad!" Yuri said.

Goro blinked. "What?"

"You're already doing the parent thing."

"The parent... thing?"

"Yeah. The parent thing of asking children how school was as soon as they came back home."

Taiga looked between the two. Seeing Yuri roasting Goro never got old.

Goro switched from confused to slightly embarrassed.

Taiga smiled again. 
Yeah, he was home.

***

Camp in winter was different. Last year, there was an entire construction site. Now, just snow and silence.
There were no scouts running between cabins. No whistles, no shouting from the lake, no breakfast line, no engineers or people with yellow helmets.

Winter made the place feel like backstage after the audience had gone home.

Taiga liked it. He helped Aiden clean up the kitchen, then spent the afternoon with Yoshinori going through old equipment that apparently needed to be sorted into three categories—usable, repairable and “absolutely not, Taiga, that rope is older than you are”.

On the second morning, Lloyd spotted him walking past the maintenance shed and waved both arms like Taiga might otherwise fail to notice him from ten meters away.

“Taiga! Perfect timing!”

Darius was beside him with a toolbox open at his feet. “He got back yesterday, Lloyd.”

“I know! I'm welcoming him professionally.” Lloyd held out a screwdriver.

Taiga looked at it. “By giving me work?”

“Exactly!”

Darius pointed toward one of the shed doors. The top hinge had started pulling away from the frame. “It'll take five minutes.”

“You're both scoutmasters now,” Taiga said.

Lloyd put a hand to his chest. “Scoutmasters can believe in practical education!”

Darius shrugged. “And hinges.”

Taiga took the screwdriver.

Shortly after, he heard a car pull into the parking lot, followed by a familiar voice immediately announcing that peace was over.

“WHY IS THE PARKING LOT ICE?!”

Taiga heard Hiro before he saw him. Yoichi, walking a few meters away, heard it too.

"Because it's winter, you stupid raccoon."

The front door of the car opened and Hiro appeared, carrying two bags and wearing a coat bright enough to function as emergency equipment.

“I know what winter is, Wolfboy! I'm asking why nobody salted the part where human beings are expected to waaAAAAH-”

He slipped mid-sentence and landed flat on his ass.

Yoichi smirked. "Tch. Skill issue."

"Hiro! Don't talk while you're walking on ice!"

Keitaro appeared with a suitcase, a camera bag and a resigned expression. He helped Hiro up.

"You did it on purpose, you stinky dog," Hiro said.

"I don't make ice, idiot."

"You didn't salt the road!"

"Not my job."

"Actually, Yoichi, I asked you to do it yesterday," Aiden said, appearing from one of the cabins.

"I was busy."

"Yeah. Of course."

"BIG BRO!" Hiro shouted.

Aiden smiled widely. "Hey, little chef! Keitaro!"

Hiro rushed toward him, somehow managing not to slip again. Aiden hugged him tight. 

"How are you doing?"

"Good!"

Then he lifted his bags. "I brought supplies!"

"Of course you have."

Keitaro approached Taiga and hugged him. 
Taiga tolerated it for three seconds, then four, before shoving lightly at his shoulder.

“Okay, enough.”

Keitaro let go, smiling. “You look good!”

Hiro put both bags down and opened his arms dramatically.

“Do I get one or have we used the seasonal affection allowance?”

Taiga stared at him. “You get punched.”

“I'll take it.”

Hiro hugged him anyway, and Taiga complained throughout. This did not cause Hiro to stop.

"Hey buddy," Aiden said, looking at the bags. "Are you going to stand there squeezing Taiga all morning, or are you going to come explain why you brought enough food to feed twenty people?”

Hiro released Taiga immediately. “Oh, you'll see!”

"Okay, I'm intrigued now."

"Come on, lunch is already behind schedule!"

Aiden blinked. "You've really turned into a proper chef now."

Hiro disappeared into the kitchen with Aiden. 

Yoichi looked at Keitaro. "He's still broken."

Keitaro smiled. “Pretty much.”

Hunter arrived two hours later, stepping through the mess hall door with one duffel bag and enough layers to survive an arctic expedition.

Yuki hit him at speed.

“Oh! Yuki, wait, wait—”

Too late. Hunter staggered backward against the door while Yuki attempted to become one with his coat.

Yoichi called from one of the chairs, “She missed you, Twinkerbell.”

“I can tell!”

Hunter laughed and crouched even as the dog immediately put both paws on his shoulders. “Hi. Yes. Hi. I missed you too. Please don't eat my scarf.”

Keitaro came around the corner with his camera already in hand. Hunter looked up.

“Oh, come on!”

Click.

“Keitaro.”

“Sorry.”

Click.

“You're not sorry.”

“Not really.”

Natsumi arrived at 12:17. Taiga knew the exact time because Natsumi apologized for it.

The door opened and Natsumi stepped inside with a navy duffel over one shoulder and a small paper bag in his hand.

“Good morning, everyone. I'm very sorry I'm later than I originally planned. The first train was cancelled, and the replacement stopped for nearly twenty minutes outside—”

Yoichi pointed at him. “Nobody asked, Mr. Perfect.”

Natsumi stopped, looked at him, then smiled. “Good morning to you too, Yoichi."

Hiro came out of the kitchen holding a wooden spoon. “NATSUMI!”

Natsumi barely had time to put the bag down before Hiro collided with him.

“Hiro, careful, I have—”

“You took forever!”

“I'm less than twenty minutes late.”

“Exactly. Forever.”

Natsumi laughed and returned the hug. Keitaro appeared behind Hiro, Hunter came down the hallway, and Yoichi stayed where he was because standing up voluntarily might have damaged his reputation.

For a few seconds everyone talked over everyone else. Hiro wanted to know about the cancelled train and whether Natsumi had eaten; Hunter asked about the meet before break; Keitaro took photographs. Natsumi attempted to answer three questions in order and failed because Hiro kept adding new ones.

Normality resumed.

***

After less than one day, everyone was already back to their usual habits.

Hiro took over one section of the kitchen and then insisted he had not taken over anything. Aiden encouraged him until Yoshinori walked in, looked at the number of containers on the counter and asked whether they were expecting a second camp to arrive for dinner.

“I'm planning ahead!” Hiro said.

Yoshinori looked at three trays of roasted vegetables, two pots of soup and something rising beneath a towel. “For what event?”

“Eating, of course.”

Aiden nodded solemnly. “Important event. Happens daily.”

Yoshinori stared at him. “You're encouraging this.”

“Absolutely.”

From the doorway, Goro cleared his throat. Everyone looked over as he surveyed the kitchen, then Hiro, then Aiden.

“I assume there is a reasonable explanation for why the winter inventory appears to have become a culinary examination.”

Hiro straightened slightly. “Goro, sir, I can explain—”

“He was bored,” Yoichi said from the table.

Hiro turned. “Wolfboy!”

Goro's expression did not change. “That is not what I would describe as a reasonable explanation.”

Yuri appeared behind him, looked into the kitchen and immediately gasped. “Kyaa! Hiro, sweetheart, is that bread? Oh, please tell me that's bread!”

Goro turned his head. “Yuri.”

“Dad, let the boy cook. Look how happy he is!”

“I am not objecting to cooking. I am asking whether we are using supplies allocated for the next staff rotation.”

Hiro looked briefly horrified. “No! I brought most of this. I have receipts!”

Taiga, passing the doorway, stopped. “You brought receipts to Camp Buddy?”

“I am a culinary student now. Ingredient accountability matters.”

Then Yoshinori asked the rest of them to take a look at the storage room, because it had been neglected for a while, even during the renovations. 

They went through a big pile of boxes that had accumulated when every cabin had been emptied out for restructuring. Most of them were full of actual trash, stuff campers had left behind, activity props. One of the containers looked like it had been opened recently. It didn't have the same amount of dust as the others.

"No way," Hunter said, holding a pair of bunny ears.

Keitaro smiled and pulled out a white football uniform. Natsumi was looking at a blue officer hat.

Yeah, it was the box containing all their costumes from that "Halloween" event.
Yuri must've found it before them.

They spent more than fifteen minutes talking about that night, before eventually putting everything back and moving on.

Taiga's eyes stopped on a box labeled *Fundraising*. He gulped and opened it.
The first thing he saw was a folded green cardboard sign.

**BUDDY COOKIES**

For half a second nobody reacted. Keitaro took it and smiled widely.

"No way, they kept these things."

"Why would they keep this?" Taiga asked.

"Tch. Evidence of child labor," Yoichi said.

“We were eighteen.”

“Still felt illegal.”

Natsumi brushed dust from one corner. “The fundraiser did work.”

"Wait until Hiro sees this," Hunter said.

"Torch-Head's probably gonna burst into tears."

"Oh, look!"

Hunter pulled out an old sketchbook. The paper had gone soft at the creases, and most of the pencil had smudged into gray ghosts.

Natsumi leaned closer. “Is that yours?”

Hunter's ears pinked. “I think so. It's the campfire circle for the fundraising poster.”

Yoichi looked over his shoulder. “Looks like a bunch of rocks.”

“It was a sketch.”

“Still rocks.”

Hunter frowned at him. “Why do I ever show you anything?”

“Because you crave validation, Twinkerbell.”

“That is not—” Hunter stopped and considered. “Okay, maybe a little.”

One thing kept bringing back another memory. Natsumi found the camera they'd used for filming, while the small cart Yoichi had used to carry the cookies around was tucked into a corner of the storage room.

Eventually, an activity that should have lasted a couple of hours took up the whole afternoon. At some point, Hiro barged in.

"What the hell is taking you so long?"

Then he saw the fundraising box still open and set aside, with the sign peeking out.

"No fucking way. They still have it?"

He picked it out and stared at it for a while.

"Try not to cry, Torch-Head," Yoichi said with a smirk.

"Fuck you. These cookies saved the camp."

"Yeah," Keitaro added. "With Aiden flirting with people until they bought six boxes at a time."

“He called it aggressive hospitality.”

“That was marketing for him.”

Taiga smiled. 
Hiro looked down at the sign, his expression shifting slightly. “...That was a weird week.”

“Understatement,” Taiga said.

Keitaro touched one faded corner of the sign. “Yeah.”

Yoichi clicked his tongue. “There we go. Frogboy's getting sentimental.”

Keitaro looked betrayed. Hiro tucked the cookie sign under one arm.

Taiga stared at him. “You're taking that?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because apparently nobody here understands archival preservation.”

Yoshinori looked at the clock when they returned, then at the six of them.

“Was there a structural problem with the shed?”

Taiga shrugged. “Historical contamination.”

Hiro held up the sign. “Look what they did to my legacy!”

Yoshinori stared at **BUDDY COOKIES**, then smiled. “I wondered where that went.”

“You knew it was still here?”

“No. I hoped we'd thrown it away.”

Hiro gasped.

Aiden, passing behind him with a toolbox, called, “Hey, those cookies saved our asses!”

Goro's voice came from the office doorway. “Flynn.”

Aiden did not even turn around. “Saved our *finances*, President Goro.”

“Better.”

Yuri leaned around her father's shoulder. “Oh! The little cookie sign! I remember that! Hiro, sweetheart, you should make those little animal ones again sometime. The frog was adorable.”

Hiro immediately forgot to be offended by Yoshinori. “I can make them better now.”

Yeah, that was probably true.
Hunter could probably redraw the campfire circle without erasing every second line. Hiro could make fundraiser cookies that belonged in an actual bakery. Natsumi could teach someone to swim instead of simply being the best person in the water. Keitaro could take a photograph and know why it worked.

Even Yoichi had learned how to replace rotten planks.

***

Before the sun fully set, Keitaro wanted photographs of the lake. Obviously.

The winter sun had dropped low enough to turn everything pale gold, and without summer leaves the water was visible between the trees from halfway across camp. Hunter was excited as well because the light was "actually kind of ridiculous". The others followed automatically.

They reached the pier.
Taiga stopped. One foot simply failed to take the next step.

Two boards near the end were newer than the rest. The posts had been resealed, and somebody had replaced one warped edge with fresh timber that still looked too clean.
For one ugly second, Taiga felt his stomach clench.

 The others spread along the dock: Hiro immediately complained that the wind was worse over the water, Hunter crouched near one railing to look at the reflection between the boards, Natsumi asked Yoichi which sections had been replaced, and Keitaro walked all the way to the end. Taiga stayed halfway down.

His phone buzzed in his pocket.

**Leo:** hey camp boy
**Leo:** you survived the wilderness?

Ah, right. He had asked. 

**Taiga:** pretty much
**Taiga:** you survived enzo?

**Leo:** ugh barely
**Leo:** now i'm trying to survive family

He sent a photo of a huge patio taken from somewhere higher. At least ten people were there, setting up a big table in the center.

**Taiga:** that's all family?

**Leo:** ...part of it 💀

Taiga blinked.

**Taiga:** insane

**Leo:** can't argue
**Leo:** btw you promised me pics :3

**Taiga:** i didn't

Then he opened the camera and snapped a picture of the lake with all the guys from behind, looking at the sun setting.

**Leo:** wow
**Leo:** okay
**Leo:** now you're showing off

**Taiga:** you asked

**Leo:** i didn't expect THAT 😭😭😭

"Who are you smiling at?" Hiro said, turning back to him.

"I'm not smiling."

He pointed at Taiga's face. "What do you call that?"

"Exasperated look."

Hiro didn't look convinced.

"It's just Leo bothering me. Happy now?"

His face brightened up. "OH! The mummy guy?"

"Yeah."

"Come on, let's send him a selfie together!"

"Absolutely not."

Hiro came over anyway. Taiga rolled his eyes.
He made the most bothered face he was able to, while Hiro grinned with his index and middle fingers raised in a V.

**Leo:** hey hiro!
**Leo:** omg you forced taiga into a selfie
**Leo:** put that in your cv

Hiro laughed hard. "Oh, he knows you pretty well."

**Taiga:** fuck you

Then to Hiro: "Fuck you."

He shoved the phone in his pocket and reached the others on the pier.

Eventually, Hiro decided his ears were freezing off and announced that he was going back before he became “the first culinary student to lose both ears to nostalgia.”

"That's not exactly how frostbite works, Hiro," Natsumi pointed out.

"The distinction will be extremely comforting when my ears fall into the soup."

Hunter laughed and followed because he was getting cold as well. Natsumi and Yoichi followed them.

Keitaro remained at the end of the dock with his camera resting against his chest. Taiga stayed too.
For nearly a minute neither of them spoke.

"We didn't come here much this summer," Keitaro said.

Of course not.

"I still fucking hate this place."

Keitaro turned. "Really?"

"Why wouldn't I?"

"Well... it's been a while. Time has passed, hasn't it?"

"Yeah."

"Do you... still think about that day?"

Taiga exhaled and looked at the pier. "...Yeah, sometimes."

Keitaro's expression saddened. "Taiga..."

"Your face, when I burned your journal. There are times when it just comes up in my mind and won't go away."

"...Sometimes I think about it too."

Of course he did. He was the one who got hurt by it, idiot.

"And that makes me see how you've come such a long way since then, Taiga."

"Your journal is still sitting half burned somewhere."

"Yes, but I also have yours. And I like what that one reminds me of a lot more. It represents the lengths you guys would go to make me feel better."

"Besides," he continued, "after everything you told me this summer about Kieran, I can see where that came from. But that's behind us, right?"

"Yeah... I hope so."

Keitaro smiled. "Come, let's go back. I'm starving."

"Right."

Keitaro lifted the camera. The shutter clicked, then he turned and they went back together.

Taiga still hated that pier.

***


On New Year's Eve, Yuri decided the mess hall looked depressing. This became everyone else's problem.

By four in the afternoon, strings of warm lights hung between beams, paper decorations had appeared along the windows, and Yuri had recruited Hunter into painting a sign despite Hunter repeatedly explaining that being an art student did not mean he had become a sign-printing service.

“But sweetheart, your letters are so much prettier than mine!”

Hunter looked at the brush, then at Yuri. “You already painted half of it.”

“Exactly! Now you can rescue it.”

Across the room, Goro watched Aiden stand on a chair to attach lights.

“Aiden.”

Aiden looked down. “Yep?”

“There is a ladder in the storage room.”

“Chair's closer, Gramps.”

Goro's stare sharpened. “And the ladder is designed for climbing.”

Taiga carried another box past them. He had somehow become responsible for extension cables; nobody remembered deciding this.

By seven, the mess hall looked less depressing and more like Yuri had declared war on empty surfaces.

Hiro and Aiden had covered most of one table with food. Yoshinori had eventually stopped asking whether all of it was necessary after Hiro answered yes for the fourth time. Lloyd and Darius came in last from checking the heating in one of the cabins, both still carrying gloves and looking considerably less festive than the room around them.

Lloyd stopped in the doorway.

“Whoa.”

Yuri beamed. “Isn't it cute?”

“I was going to say aggressive.”

Darius looked around. “It's both.”

“See? He gets it.”

Dinner lasted long enough for Hiro to insist twice that everyone try the bread while it was still warm, for Aiden to steal food off Yoshinori's plate and deny it while chewing, and for Goro to discover that somebody had tied a gold ribbon around the back of his chair.

Yuri denied responsibility much too quickly.

Afterward, people started standing up.

Yuri clapped her hands.

“No, no, no! Nobody's disappearing yet!”

Yoichi froze halfway out of his chair. “Why.”

“Because it's New Year's Eve!”

“That's not an answer.”

“It absolutely is.”

Hunter glanced at the leftover paper and paint still sitting near the windows. That was his mistake.

“We could draw something, I guess.”

Yuri gasped.

Hunter's face fell immediately. “I shouldn't have said that.”

Five minutes later, Yoshinori was cutting scrap paper into squares while Yuri wrote prompts on them and dropped them into an empty bowl.

Natsumi watched closely. “What are the rules?”

“Easy! You pick one and draw it. Everyone else guesses.”

“How much time do we have?”

“Thirty seconds.”

Hunter looked at her. “Thirty?”

“Forty-five!”

“That wasn't my objection.”

“No words, no letters, no numbers,” Natsumi continued.

Yuri paused. “Right. Those too.”

“So those are the rules?”

“Unless we need more.”

Natsumi nodded slowly. “Understood.”

Taiga didn't think he did.

Hunter went first because Yuri claimed this had all been his idea.

He unfolded the paper.

“Oh, come on.”

“What?” Keitaro asked.

Hunter turned the slip around so nobody could see it. “Nothing.”

Yoshinori started the timer.

Hunter drew three lines, a curve and something that looked annoyingly intentional.

“Canoe,” Natsumi said.

Hunter put the brush down.

Yuri stared at him. “That was seven seconds.”

“Was it?”

“That is completely unfair.”

“You told me to draw.”

“With your other hand from now on.”

Hunter looked offended. “That's discrimination.”

“Against art students, yes.”

Yoichi snorted.

His own turn went worse.

He stared at the paper, then attacked the sheet with the marker.

A triangle appeared. Then another line. Then several violent scratches underneath it.

“Mountain,” Keitaro guessed.

“No.”

“Campfire?” Hiro said.

“No!”

“Pizza?” Aiden offered.

Yoichi turned around. “HOW DOES THAT LOOK LIKE PIZZA?”

Yoshinori pointed at the timer. “No talking to the audience.”

“I'm not talking to the audience, I'm correcting stupidity.”

“Ten seconds.”

Yoichi added two more lines.

“...Tent?” Hunter tried.

“FINALLY.”

Hunter blinked. “Why is it on fire?”

“Those are stakes, Twinkerbell.”

“They're above the tent.”

“Perspective.”

Taiga laughed before he could stop himself.

Yoichi pointed the marker at him. “You draw next, Dynamite.”

“Why?”

“Because you laughed.”

Apparently that was a rule too.

Taiga pulled a slip.

**Camera.**

He drew a rectangle and a circle.

“Camera!” Keitaro shouted immediately.

Taiga dropped the marker. “This game sucks.”

Keitaro looked pleased with himself anyway.

Natsumi's turn lasted longer because he spent the first five seconds apparently planning.

Then he drew a long horizontal line, a smaller rectangle under it and four circles.

“Bus,” Taiga said.

Natsumi smiled. “Correct!”

Yuri frowned. “That was suspiciously normal.”

“I followed the rules.”

“Exactly. Suspicious.”

Hiro got **birthday cake** and spent so much time adding layers that the timer ran out before he drew candles.

“That could've been anything!” Yoichi said.

“It had structure!”

“It was a rectangle.”

“It was a *layered* rectangle.”

“Still a rectangle.”

Hiro looked genuinely offended. “You have no respect for pastry.”

Aiden leaned over the paper. “I would've eaten it.”

“Thank you, bro.”

Goro lasted through all of this with the expression of someone supervising an activity rather than participating in it.

Then Yuri held the bowl out to him.

“No.”

“Dad.”

“No.”

“Dad.”

Goro looked at Yoshinori, apparently expecting institutional support.

Yoshinori smiled. “It might be fun.”

Betrayal registered.

Goro took a slip.

His prompt was **Yuki**.

What appeared on the paper had four legs, one extremely long body and a head shaped like a shovel.

Silence.

Yuki, lying beside the table, lifted her head.

Aiden squinted. “Crocodile?”

Goro's jaw tightened.

“Table?” Hiro tried.

“It has legs,” Goro said.

“So does a table,” Darius pointed out.

Lloyd covered his mouth.

Goro added two triangular ears.

Keitaro's eyes widened. “Yuki?”

“Yes.”

Everyone looked at Yuki.

Then at the drawing.

Yuri was the first to break.

“Oh my God, Dad.”

“It is recognizable.”

Yuki got up and walked away.

Aiden folded over the table laughing.

Goro put the marker down with considerable dignity. “The dog is not a cooperative model.”

“Sure, Gramps.”

“Aiden.”

When Lloyd's turn came, he picked his prompt, stared at it for half a second and immediately leaned over the paper with the concentration of someone beginning paid work.

Thirty seconds later, he had drawn a front elevation, two windows, roof pitch, siding lines and what looked suspiciously like hinge placement.

Yuri stared.

“Sweetheart. What is that.”

Darius barely glanced up. “Maintenance shed.”

Lloyd spun around. “YES!”

Everyone protested at once.

“Oh, come on!” Hiro said.

“That's cheating,” Hunter added.

“How is that cheating?” Lloyd demanded. “I communicated visually!”

“You drew construction plans,” Taiga said.

“Good design communicates!”

Darius looked at the paper. “You spent too much time on the hinges.”

“They were relevant!”

“They were not.”

Yuri pointed at Lloyd. “No architectural notation.”

“That wasn't one of the rules!”

“It is now.”

Natsumi opened his mouth.

“Don't,” Hunter told him.

Natsumi closed it again.

Then Darius drew.

Three lines. A small square. One diagonal mark.

Lloyd answered before Yoshinori had even finished starting the timer.

“Camp bell.”

Darius nodded. “Yeah.”

The room exploded again.

Taiga looked at the drawing. “That is literally nothing.”

“It was enough,” Darius said.

“For him!”

Darius shrugged. “He's good at this.”

Lloyd looked unbearably pleased.

“That is not the point,” Goro said, now apparently invested in competitive integrity.

Yuri put both hands on her hips. “Fine. New rule. Lloyd and Darius can't guess each other's drawings.”

Lloyd gasped. “That's targeted discrimination!”

Darius patted him once on the shoulder. “We'll survive.”

The game stopped having rules sometime after nine.

Keitaro forgot his turn because he was taking pictures of everyone else's. Hunter started doodling in the margins with his right hand again when Yuri wasn't looking. Hiro brought out leftovers and somehow turned them into a second dinner. Yoichi began keeping his own score because he accused Yoshinori of being “institutionally biased.” Goro objected to three separate points awarded by Yuri and then insisted he was not being competitive.

By ten-thirty, nobody remembered who had won.

At eleven, Lloyd and Darius went outside to get the fire going at the campfire circle. Yoshinori followed with blankets, while Aiden and Hiro filled two insulated dispensers with hot chocolate.

Goro stopped them at the door.

“Water.”

Aiden looked at the drinks. “We have—”

“For the fire.”

“Oh.”

“New Year's Eve does not suspend fire safety.”

“Never said it did, Gramps.”

Goro handed him a bucket.

Natsumi made a slow pass around the room asking everyone whether they had gloves. Hunter had forgotten his in the cabin. Keitaro had forgotten his because he had been carrying his camera instead. Yoichi claimed he didn't need any until Natsumi stared at him long enough that he swore and went to get them.

Yuri brought the leftover battery lights outside because apparently the forest also needed decorating.

Taiga ended up carrying one of the hot-chocolate dispensers.

Of course he did.

By the time they reached the campfire circle, Lloyd and Darius already had the fire going. Warm light moved across the snow and the empty benches. Lloyd was adjusting one of the logs with a stick while Darius stood beside him.

“See?” Lloyd said. “Perfect.”

Darius looked at the fire. “It's a fire.”

“A *good* fire.”

“Sure.”

People settled wherever they found space. Blankets moved from shoulders to laps. Hiro passed around cups. Yuki curled up near Yoichi's boots as if she'd been assigned there.

Keitaro checked the time.

“Eleven forty-eight.”

“Plenty of time!” Yuri said.

For once, nobody seemed to be in a hurry to fill it.
