# III - Undertow

***

### Chapter 21 - Uncharted paths

Tuesday morning, the day after their talk, Taiga stood for a second in the classroom doorway.
Then he shook his head and continued.

He looked at their usual row and their seats were empty. Looking around, he didn't see Leo anywhere else. So, he took his usual seat and opened his Discrete Structures notes.
One minute before the lesson started, someone arrived panting.

"Holy shit, I made it just in time."

He turned. Leo was standing in the aisle beside their row holding two paper cups.

Taiga smiled automatically. "Morning."

Leo hesitated for a split second, then he took the chair beside him and put both cups on the desk.

"Hey, I, uh... brought coffee. Black, obviously."

He pushed one cup toward him.

Taiga stared at it. Why did he do that? Leo had never brought him coffee to class before.

"What for?"

When he saw the surprise on Leo's face, he suddenly realized that there was the tiny possibility he had reacted like a dick.

"Sorry, I mean... thanks."

Leo smiled uncertainly. "No problem. The line was infinite, though. Don't people have classes in the morning? Ugh."

He took out his laptop and put the bag on the ground. At the same time, the professor arrived.

"Great, I don't even have time to prepare psychologically."

Taiga was still staring at the cup standing on the desk. Did he do it because of what they said yesterday...?

The professor turned on the projector and the lecture started. Leo stopped talking and opened his notes.

That was stupid. It's just coffee.
Taiga picked up his cup and took a sip before starting to take notes.

When class was over, they started packing. Leo had occasionally sworn about something he didn't understand from the lecture they'd missed last Thursday because of Pinetree. But apart from that, they had stayed focused on their notes.

"Heading to Chemistry?" Leo asked.

"Unfortunately."

He zipped his bag and stood. "See you in the lab, then."

"Yeah. Later."

***

That afternoon, Taiga was opening the assignment when Leo came in with Rachel.

"—because if one elevator breaks, everybody just stands in front of the other one. There were stairs right there."

"Did you take them?" Rachel said.

"Jesus, of course not! My room is on the fourth floor. The others should've!"

Rachel laughed and went to her desk. Leo stopped beside Taiga. "Hey. Is this...?"

Taiga moved his bag off the empty chair.

Leo took out his laptop and plugged in the charger. Taiga went back to the assignment while Owen brought up the handout.

"Rotations today. The diagrams are on the second page. Please look at them before you start moving things around."

"Wow. Owen has chosen dictatorship today."

"He knows who he's dealing with."

"That sounds... targeted."

Taiga shrugged. "I said nothing."

"Of course."

For a while, there was only typing beside Taiga, interrupted once by a quiet swear. He had reached the second exercise when Leo leaned back.

"I miss arrays so bad."

"You said you hated arrays."

"Past me was blissfully ignorant. You could at least draw the boxes. Look at this." Leo pushed his notebook toward him. Three trees crowded the bottom of the page, their branches running into each other.

"How do you even understand what's going on here?"

"Tch. I wonder if yours looks any better."

Taiga closed his notebook and pushed it out of Leo's reach.
For a moment, he expected Leo to lean over him and try to reach it. Instead, Leo just narrowed his eyes.

"Coward."

Taiga smiled and went back to his exercise.

When class ended, they picked up their bags and went outside.
Leo was still complaining about the last exercise when they passed in front of the Lecture Hall's cafeteria.

Taiga looked at it and hesitated.

"—and why on earth should one decide to merge two trees when—"

Leo noticed Taiga had stopped.

"You... up for a coffee before training?" Taiga asked.

"...Oh. Yeah, sure."

Why was his stupid heart beating so fast?

***

Nora had claimed a table near the mess hall windows on Wednesday. By the time Taiga reached it, she had spread a notebook, two articles and the remains of her lunch across most of the available space.

"I need an opinion," she said, moving an article off the empty chair. "On one sentence."

"You've never asked for an opinion on one sentence in your life."

She turned the notebook toward him. The sentence ran over four lines.
Taiga put his tray down and read it, then went back to the beginning.

"Who does 'they' refer to?"

"The committee."

"You mentioned the students more recently."

"Yes, but the students can't approve the proposal."

"Then why make me work it out?"

Nora took the notebook back, frowning at the page. She crossed something out while he started eating, rewrote it, then held the pen still.

"Would you still object if the context made it clear?"

"Depends on the context."

"Because the paragraph before this explains—"

"There it is."

She smiled and pushed the notebook aside. It came back twice during lunch. Taiga disagreed with the essay's argument once he had heard it, and Nora spent several minutes defending it before asking him which part he actually objected to.

Eventually she closed the notebook.

"What are you doing for spring break?"

"Probably camp."

"Cool, your friends coming?"

"I don't think so. Plus, once winter's over, preseason starts. So it's gonna be mostly work."

"Do scout camps have preseason?"

Taiga shrugged. "Dunno. This one does."

"Okay, I still don't know what that is."

"Basically, maintenance work before summer arrives. Fixing winter damage, organizing stuff, cleaning things and so on."

"So, a five-star hotel for you."

"You're welcome to try."

Nora giggled. "No thanks. I'm going home for a few days. My sister wants us to go away for a weekend, but every hotel she's sent me costs more for one night than my train tickets."

"Send her somewhere cheaper."

"I did. Apparently it lacked *atmosphere*."

"What does that mean?"

"I asked. She sent me a photograph of a bathtub."

Taiga laughed. Nora began gathering the articles, still explaining the price of the room with the bathtub.

"So," she said after taking a sip of her drink. "You never told me how it went with Leo. Because you talked, right?"

"Yeah."

"Yeah what?"

"We talked."

Taiga scratched the back of his head until she gave him an impatient look.

"...And?"

"And nothing. We're good."

"You're good."

"Yes, we're good."

"Jesus Christ, Taiga."

"Oh, for fuck's sake. We cleared things up and now we're talking again."

"Did you... tell him?"

"What?"

Nora let out an exasperated sigh. "That you like him?"

Taiga's face went red almost instantly. He immediately looked away.

"...Kinda? I said he wasn't wrong, like... about me wanting him around."

She blinked.

"Look, I didn't say those *exact* words. But neither did he. We just agreed to carry on with... whatever we were doing."

Nora didn't look convinced. Not even a little.

"So now what are you doing? Like, are you dating or...?"

"No," he answered, way too quickly.

She stared for a couple of seconds.

"I guess... we'll just see what happens?"

"Alright. As long as you two are fine."

Taiga didn't answer.

***

Leo came out of the kitchen with his phone to his ear.

"Uh—tell me again, guys, how many pizzas?"

Taiga, Zoe and several others raised their hands. Leo counted them off, then gave a thumbs-up and disappeared back into the kitchen.

"Third time's the charm," Zoe said.

Taiga snorted. "I bet he'll still get it wrong."

Friday evening, after training, they were in Apollo's common room.

Leo came back after a minute. "Okay, they said about forty minutes."

Enzo groaned. "Oh my God. I'll scavenge the kitchen for snacks before I bite someone."

"Heh, good luck. Freezer was pretty empty last time I checked."

"Shut up and help me."

Leo grimaced. "You're useless, you know?" And he followed him inside.

"So," said Zoe, leaning closer to Taiga. "I see you two are talking again."

"Apparently."

She smiled. "I'm happy for both of you. Seriously."

Taiga was already regretting involving Nora and Zoe. He looked away.

"You know what? We should hang out sometime. The four of us. Me, you, Leo and Nora."

"What? Why just the four of us and... why Nora?"

"Because I'm sick of being the only pussy in the room."

"You chose CS. You knew what you were signing up for."

"Yeah, and I also happened to become friends with a soccer player. Come on!"

Taiga frowned. "Look, I don't need you or anyone else to... I don't know, set up stuff or anything."

"Okay, then how about this: I want to hang out with Nora because I don't have many female friends and I don't want to be surrounded by soccer players. Does that sound better?"

He shrugged. "Whatever."

Leo came out a few minutes later, with Enzo carrying a disposable aluminum tray filled with fries.

Gav turned. "Where did those come from?"

"Some forgotten corner of the fridge," Leo said.

Enzo smirked. "I told you I remembered we still had something!"

"Enzo, these are ancient."

"Nothing that an oven can't fix."

Gav grimaced. "That's not really how food safety works..."

Enzo ignored him and put them on a table. Leo put a hand over his face and sat down beside Taiga on the couch.

Zoe leaned over. "Leo. What are you doing on Sunday?"

Leo turned. "Hm? Uh... nothing comes to mind, I think. Hold on—"

He pulled out his phone and checked his calendar.

Taiga giggled. "You're learning."

"Ha ha ha. Funny. Anyway, nothing scheduled. Besides the freaking Rao assignment."

When Zoe asked Taiga the same question, he stopped laughing immediately.

"I'm busy."

"Of course you are. Doing what, if I may?"

"You may not."

"I've wanted to see Acropolis for a while now. How about it?"

"Oh! Me too. I've only seen the Christmas markets!" Leo said.

"Good. Then it's a deal! Taiga, you tell Nora."

"Wh—I didn't say yes."

Leo nudged his shoulder. "Aw, come on, I bet it'll be fun!"

Taiga gave him a betrayed look.

Leo put on a pleading smile. "Please?"

Fuck it. How was he able to make a face like that?
Taiga sighed. There was no way he could say no to that.

He pulled out his phone.

**Taiga:** you free on sunday?

**Nora:** What happened?

**Taiga:** nothing

**Nora:** Then I'm even more worried

**Taiga:** just answer already

**Nora:** Yeah, I am. Why?

**Taiga:** zoe wanted to see acropolis
**Taiga:** leo's coming too

**Nora:** Sounds nice! Are we visiting the ruins?

Right. Nora had mentioned the ruins almost a lifetime ago.

"She wants to see the ruins."

"Sounds good to me," Zoe said.

"Yep, same."

**Taiga:** apparently now yes

**Nora:** Then I'm in 😁

Leo leaned over and looked at the screen.

"So it's a deal!"

Taiga sighed and put the phone back.

***

Sunday morning, Taiga met Leo in the common hall of the Residential Building. They waved at each other and waited for the girls on a couch. For a minute, neither of them talked.

"So..." Leo began, "slept well?"

Taiga fidgeted with his thumbs. "Alright. Uh- you?"

"Oh, super. Good, I mean, I slept well."

"Good."

"Yeah..."

They looked at each other for a second until they both snorted.

"That's still a bit weird," Leo said.

"Yeah, a bit," admitted Taiga.

At least he wasn't the only one.

Nora and Zoe arrived two minutes later and they started towards the station. On the train, Zoe asked:

"So, we head straight to the ruins?"

"Yeah, maybe it's less crowded in the morning. I don't think it'll take long, I bet we'll be done by lunch," Nora answered.

"You've already been there?"

"Yes. Once, with my sister."

"Oh, cool!"

Leo was watching the window, apparently lost in his thoughts. Taiga just followed his gaze.

The park looked different without the market stalls. He recognized the fountain first, then the space where the rink had been. Leo glanced at him as they passed it.

"Looks much safer now."

"You're the one who stopped in front of me."

"Because you told me to!"

Nora looked between them. "You went skating?"

"One of us did," Leo said.

Taiga pushed his arm. Leo caught his sleeve, grinning. 

"You're a bad teacher."

"Hey, I got you away from the railing, didn't I?"

"And you nearly died because you had to skate backwards."

"Did he crash into someone?" Zoe asked.

"Yeah. Me."

The path to the ruins narrowed after the fountain. Nora went first, with Zoe behind her; Taiga and Leo had to stop arguing long enough to let a family coming the other way get past.
When they caught up, Nora was standing beside an information board. Beyond it, low walls divided a stretch of grass into uneven squares. Leo went to the railing and looked down.

"Those are rooms?"

"The walls would've been higher," Zoe said.

"Thank you, I got that part."

Nora left the board and came to look. "That's probably a storeroom."

"Probably?" Taiga asked.

"The sign doesn't say."

"So you made it up."

"It's a reasonable guess. There's no entrance from the passage."

Zoe leaned over the railing. "There is on the other side."

Nora followed her finger. For a moment, all four of them studied the gap in the stones.

"Fine," Nora said. "It could be a bedroom."

They followed the path around the foundations. At the next board, Zoe stopped to compare the reconstruction with the walls.

"I wish they'd put one room back together. Just one. I'd like to stand in it."

"Then you'd be standing in something somebody built now," Nora said.

"Yeah, but it would have the vibes!"

Nora shook her head, but she was smiling when she moved on.

They continued wandering in the ruins for a while.

At the far end of the path, Leo stopped by a shallow flight of steps and lowered himself onto the bottom one. Zoe immediately sat beside him.
Nora saw a board with a map and approached it.

"I think we've seen almost everything."

"My feet say hurrah," Leo said.

Zoe shook her head. "So much for an athlete."

"I train to walk on grass, not stone slabs."

"I don't see Taiga complaining."

Leo glanced at him.

"He is. He just won't admit it."

"Would that make you feel better?" Taiga said.

 "Hey, I'm wasted on cross-country. We short people are natural sprinters, very dangerous over short distances."

Zoe snorted. Taiga chuckled as well, then he held out his hand to Leo.

"Come on, time for second breakfast."
