### Chapter 3 - 

"I still think this is a very bad idea."

"It'll be just an hour. And we'll be close by."

Caleb had made the mistake of agreeing to attend a floor party. 
Past-Taiga made the larger mistake of attending as well and now Present-Taiga was complaining. 

"I thought you hate parties."

"I don't hate parties."

“You left orientation because somebody was playing music too loudly.”

“That was different.”

“How exactly?”

“There were two speakers playing different songs.”

Taiga considered.

“That is actually criminal.”

“That's exactly it.”

The party was hosted on the second floor of the residence hall. It was no big event, at least that's what Caleb said, but Taiga could still eyeball at least forty people.
He sighed and grabbed something to drink. Almost everything was alcoholic.
Taiga found soda and poured himself a glass.
He had nothing against alcohol, he simply disliked being less aware of himself around strangers.

Caleb had a beer and appeared to be conducting a detailed conversation with a woman about subway systems. Taiga had missed the transition. He walked past them twenty minutes earlier while they were discussing rent.

Now Caleb was showing her a map on his phone.

“No, because if you extend the line west here, ridership density collapses.”

The woman leaned closer.

“What if they added a transfer?”

“That would make it worse.”

“How exactly?”

Caleb zoomed in. Taiga watched from across the room. Apparently he had made a friend. Or was giving an unsolicited lecture. Possibly both. Someone beside Taiga said:
 
“You look miserable.”

He turned. Nora. Of course. She wore jeans and a green top, hair down for once.

“What are you doing here?”

“I know people.”

“I assumed you lived in the library.”

“You're very charming.”

“I've been told.”

Taiga glanced toward Caleb again. The crowd had grown around him. Three people were now looking at the transit map. Caleb seemed perfectly content. Interesting. Nora followed his gaze.

“You know him?”

“Yeah, he's my roommate.”

“He looks intense.”

“You have no idea.”

Suddenly, another voice came from behind him. Familiar and loud enough to make Taiga regret even more leaving his room.

"Mr. AG!"

Taiga closed his eyes and exhaled. Then he said without turning,

"I told you. You're not calling me like that."

Leo suddenly appeared in his field of view. Taiga was taken aback for a second.
It was weird seeing him outside the pitch, without soccer uniform.

He was wearing a loose white zipped hoodie on a black tank top. He had a small golden pendant representing the outline of a sun with a red lace around his neck and white shorts.
Nothing in his hair had changed. It still looked perfectly messy.

He was smiling very loudly.

"It still sounds very appropriate!"

"You're really asking me to live up to it."

"What are you doing here? I would never expect to see you at a floor party."

"I'm regretting my life choices."

"Sounds more like it."

Nora snorted.

"AG?"

Taiga rolled his eyes.

"Forget it."

Nora ignored him. She turned to Leo.

"Nora."

"Hi! Leo."

"What's with that nickname?"

Leo laughed.

"Teammates dark humor."

Taiga narrowed his eyes a bit. He appreciated him not telling, though. Nora raised an eyebrow.

"Oh you play soccer with him?"

"Unfortunately," said Taiga.

"He'll never admit it, but he joined because of me."

"I saw your shot hitting the moon."

"Exactly! Very inspiring."

Nora asked Leo:

"Is he actually good?"

Taiga glared at him.
Leo considered for a second.

“He’s new. But he learns stupidly fast.”

Taiga blinked. Nora raised an eyebrow. 

“So yes?”

Leo grinned.

“I’m not giving him that much satisfaction.”

"But that me-"

A cheer erupted from the other side of the room. Someone had started a drinking game. Everybody suddenly began shouting.
Taiga winced. He saw Caleb visibly stiffening. Tiny movement. Caleb's shoulders rose. He looked toward the speaker. Then toward the hallway.

Taiga waited another twenty seconds, then he put his cup down. 

"That's my cue."

Leo and Nora looked at him confusedly. He didn't wait for their answer and crossed the room.

“Caleb.”

Caleb turned immediately.

“What?”

“You ready?”

A pause.

“Yes.”

The woman said, “Already?”

Caleb hesitated. Taiga could almost see calculation happening.

“Yeah,” Taiga said. “Gotta go early to library tomorrow.”

Caleb looked at him. Taiga stared back. The woman shrugged.

“Okay. Nice meeting you.”

“You too,” Caleb said.

They left. The hallway door closed behind them. Noise dropped by half. Caleb exhaled. Taiga walked toward the stairs. After several seconds:

“We don't have any library appointments tomorrow.”

“I know.”

“Why did you lie?”

Taiga looked over.

“You looked like you wanted to leave.”

“Yes.”

“So we left.”

Caleb walked another few steps.

“I could've said that.”

“You weren't.”

“No.”

“Then whatever.”

They reached the third floor. Caleb stopped outside their door.

“Thank you.”

Taiga immediately felt uncomfortable.

“You don't need to make it a thing.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

“Good.”

Caleb opened the door. He sat on the edge of his bed and removed his shoe. Taiga sat at his desk.

“You didn't want to stay either.”

Taiga looked over.

“What?”

“At the party.”

“So what?”

“So you made it sound like you left for me.”

Taiga stared. Caleb stared back.

“Can you stop analyzing every fucking thing I do?”

“No, I can't.”

Taiga sighed. Caleb opened his economics textbook. Then added:

“It was mutually beneficial.”

“Great.”

“That makes it less emotionally complicated.”

Taiga laughed despite himself.

“Thanks.”

“You're welcome.”
