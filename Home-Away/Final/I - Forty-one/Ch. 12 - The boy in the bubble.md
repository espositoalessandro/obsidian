### Chapter 12 - The boy in the bubble

December arrived and it brought snow and finals.

The campus turned white after a rather intense blizzard went on for a whole night. Everybody had to deal with thirty centimeters of snow.

Unfortunately, people didn't have the time to wander around and actually enjoy the scenery. 
A few days before the finals week, no one asked "how are you?" anymore. The answer was always the same.

After the last training of the year, House Apollo's common room had stopped being a soccer house at all.

There were still boots by the door, jackets hanging over chairs and somebody's ball trapped under one of the couches, but every table in the common room was covered with laptops, notes and half-empty cups. Scott had taken the coffee table and was working through flashcards. Gav had three different books open around him. Enzo had somehow built a spreadsheet to calculate one of his grades and was now asking Leo why eleven slash eleven had turned into a date.

Taiga looked at the scene: Leo was desperately trying to make Enzo understand why the spreadsheet kept interpreting eleven slash eleven as a date, while Enzo was deflecting every bit of information trying to reach him.
That lasted fifteen minutes, before Leo surrendered and just fixed the damn sheet.
Taiga laughed once and went back to Hall's review.

He had taken the end of the long table near the wall. Political Institutions was Monday morning. Chemistry was Tuesday. Calculus was Thursday. Foundations came last on Friday.

Hall's review sheet was open in front of him with two pages of notes beside it.
The questions were broad enough to be annoying. Institutional legitimacy, emergency power, federalism, executive authority.
Monday was going to be cruel.

***

"I really hope I don't have to build any bridge here, Mr. Akatora."

Hall was smiling at him. Taiga put the sheet on the desk, on top of the others.

"I wrote them."

"We'll see."

That sounded ominous enough that Taiga wanted to leave as soon as possible.
Outside, Nora waited until the classroom door closed.

"That's basically affection from him," she said, smiling.

"That's basically a threat."

"Same thing with professors."

With Political Institutions over, Nora had already finished all her exams. She had a relaxed, smug expression that made her more unbearable than usual.

"When are you going home?" Taiga asked.

"My mum's gonna pick me up in a couple of hours. You?"

"My train's Saturday morning."

"So, still the same plan? Home first, then camp for New Year's?"

"Yeah."

She smiled. "You're gonna miss me, won't you?"

Taiga just gave her an eloquent look.

"I knew it."

"Say hello to Oliver from me."

"You'll call me and say that yourself."

"You wish."

"You will."

Taiga shook his head. A whole semester had passed, yet she was still annoying as hell.

***

That afternoon brought the last soccer session of the semester. 

Mercer shortened the last session to less than an hour. It was cold enough that Enzo had spent most of warmup complaining about his fingers, even though Aaron kept reminding him he did not need his fingers to play soccer.

At the end, Mercer blew twice and called everyone in.
Gav started passing out sheets.

"Break program," Mercer said. "Three weeks. You are not going to lose all your fitness in three weeks."

Mercer continued explaining the schedule. Three sessions a week. Running, strength work, touches when possible. Nothing complicated.
Taiga took a sheet and glanced through it.

"Rest but don't slack off. I'm not starting on January trying to recover three weeks of nothing. So, follow the sheet or January will be harsh."

Someone groaned, but Mercer ignored them.

"You also know the conference season starts next semester. Olympus is playing in it, obviously. Some of those matches could bring in scouts from professional teams. We've got everything we need to compete near the top, so be ready for it."

Most of the freshmen exchanged surprised and scared looks.

"That's all. Get out of here."

***

Foundations ended Friday just after noon.
Professor Rao watched the last few students submit and then closed her laptop.

"If any of you email me tonight asking whether your final compiled correctly after you already submitted it, I will assume the answer is no."

Rao continued. "Enjoy the break. Touch grass. Preferably without trying to optimize it."

That made a few people laugh. Taiga felt a bit insulted, even if he couldn't exactly articulate why.

He left and stopped outside the building.

That was it.

No more classes. No training. Nothing due.
For the first time in months, there was nowhere he was supposed to be.

Campus was already half empty. He went back to the dorm, taking a quick detour to wander around the central park. 

Without the oppressing thought of the finals, the campus was actually really nice when covered in snow. Also, with half the people, it was also significantly quieter.
The fountain at the center was shut and the water frozen in the concrete basin.

Around him, there was almost nobody. Just a couple strolling in the distance and a few people reaching the entrance gate with suitcases and duffels.

Caleb had left that morning with Maya around nine. When Taiga got back to his room, the silence was different.

Caleb's chair was pushed all the way under the desk. His charger was gone. The glass of water that normally sat beside his laptop had disappeared with everything else.

Taiga dropped his bag and pulled the suitcase farther out from under the bed.

He had put two shirts inside when his phone buzzed.
Once.
Again.
Apparently peace had lasted too long.

**Leo:** taigaaaa
**Leo:** pls tell me you're still here 😭

**Taiga:** where else should i be?

**Leo:** i dunno everybody left at once 😭😭😭
**Leo:** i just finished fcs and suddenly i'm alone

**Taiga:** aren't you leaving with enzo tomorrow?

**Leo:** you really want to leave me all day with enzo? 
**Leo:** i already have to suffer through 10 hours tomorrow
**Leo:** i need a break

**Taiga:** like?

**Leo:** acropolis rink is open 
**Leo:** there's also a christmas village :3

**Taiga:** congratulations to the rink

Three dots appeared and stayed there for a while. Then:

**Leo:** wanna come with me?

He stared at the screen for a second. Leo continued before he could answer:

**Leo:** PLEASE
**Leo:** apparently everybody suddenly has families 🥲

**Taiga:** you also have a family

**Leo:** yes and they have been informed i arrive tomorrow

Taiga looked at the two shirts in the suitcase.
Packing would take twenty minutes. Maybe thirty.
He could do it later.

**Taiga:** when

**Leo:** 4:00 common hall?

**Taiga:** 4:10, i need a shower

**Leo:** thankssssss 😭😭😭😭😭

Taiga locked the phone, put another shirt into the suitcase and left it open.

***

Taiga got out of the elevator and saw Leo already sitting on a couch waiting for him.
He wasn't hard to spot. He was wearing a very yellow scarf Taiga had never seen before.

Leo jumped up immediately when he saw him.

"Hey!"

"Hey."

They started towards the entrance. Leo caught Taiga glancing at the scarf a couple of times.

"I know. I know. You can say that you hate it."

"I don't."

"You keep looking at it."

"It's very yellow."

"My mom gave it to me."

"That explains nothing."

"It explains that it was not my choice and that I'm required to send her at least one photo of me wearing it."

Taiga chuckled. "That sounds like punishment."

"Don't mention it. She made this like four years ago, but I've never had the chance to wear it at home."

"Why?"

"Because it's Caribbean."

"Huh, right."

"So the very moment I mentioned that Olympus gets actually cold in winter, she immediately picked it up from the wardrobe."

"How cold does it get in winter?"

"Back at my home? Enough to stop sweating in a t-shirt."

"Sounds like hell."

"Well, if you're born there you get used to it eventually."

"Did you?"

"Well, it's not like I don't suffer in summer... I just learned to live with it I guess?"

Taiga raised an eyebrow.

"What about you, then? How cold is your hometown?"

"Well, both my house and camp are near mountains. So, snow in winters, heat in summer. Summer is tolerable, though."

"Does it snow like this?", Leo said pointing around him.

"Sometimes more, sometimes less."

"Sounds nice. I like snow."

Taiga nodded. "Yeah, it's pretty cool."

They arrived at the station and took the train for Acropolis Park.
The carriage was almost empty.
They took seats across from each other by the window. Snow streaked over the glass while the train pulled away from Olympus.

Leo checked his phone.

"My mother has asked if my flight is still on four times."

"Why four times?"

"Because it snowed."

"That doesn't cancel flights automatically."

"Tell her that."

He typed something quickly and put the phone away.

"So, when are you going to camp?"

"Around the 27th I guess."

"And you're all staying there?"

"Yeah. We're doing a sort of reunion for about a week."

Leo nodded and watched the window for a moment.

"How about you?", Taiga continued. "Any plan for the break?"

Of course he had. He launched in a list of plans that looked impossible already from day one. Friends, family members, activities, old soccer club. 

"How do you actually manage to fit all of those in a few days?"

"I'll figure it out."

Taiga shook his head. Leo had apparently managed to build a full schedule before even getting home.
He looked the window. The snow was beginning to stay on rooftops.

Acropolis Park had been turned into a winter market.
Lights wrapped around the trees. Temporary wooden stalls lined both sides of the main path. Somebody had covered the fountain in white and yellow lights, and a large illuminated arch near the entrance announced 

**WINTER WEEK**.

Leo stopped under it.

"This is nice."

"It is crowded."

"Still nice."

Taiga looked around. "We'll see."

The rink had been set up on the broad paved area beyond the fountain.
They rented skates and sat on one of the benches near the entrance.
Taiga turned one over in his hands.

Leo glanced over. "You've done this before?"

"Nope."

"You live among the mountains."

"And?"

"Makes me laugh that you've never done it while I did."

Taiga groaned. "Good for you."

Leo grinned. "Come, I'll teach you then!"

"I'm good."

"Eheh, alright. We'll see."

Obviously, he was not. Taiga immediately hated the ice the moment he stepped onto it. The skate moved before he wanted it to. His right foot slid sideways and he caught the barrier with one hand.

Leo skated past him with too much confidence for a Caribbean boy.

"How the fuck do you know this?"

Leo laughed. "Sometimes they set up an ice rink in the city during winter."

"Great."

Taiga tried to move again. This time, he nearly fell on his ass. 
Leo was standing there with the face of someone who's trying so hard not to laugh.

"This was a fucking stupid idea."

"Just try and bend your knees more."

Taiga tried.
His left skate moved. The right did not.
He ended up grabbing the barrier for dear life again.
A child passed behind him at a speed Taiga couldn't even imagine reaching.
Leo moved in front of Taiga and started skating backward.

"Push sideways."

"I am."

"You're pushing back."

"I'm trying not to fall."

"That's why you're not moving."

Taiga tried again.
His left skate slipped too far.
Leo immediately reached forward and took both of his hands.

"I got you."

"I don't need—"

His right foot drifted again.
Leo tightened his grip before Taiga could reach for the barrier.

"Looks like you do."

Taiga scowled.
Leo started moving backward, pulling him slowly away from the wall.

"Push. Don't step."

Taiga pushed.

"Yeah! Again."

The movement felt wrong, but at least this time he kept going.

"Better."

"Don't sound proud."

"I am a little proud."

"Fuck off."

Leo kept guiding him around the first curve.
Taiga could barely think about anything except his feet.
The whole thing felt backwards. On grass, his body knew how to recover before he even thought about it. Here, every correction made the skates slide somewhere else.

"Stop looking down."

"I need to see what I'm doing."

"Your feet aren't going anywhere."

Taiga looked up.
Leo was moving backward in front of him, arms extended between them.

"Good," Leo said.

Taiga tried another push. Then another.
Slowly, the panic around every small slide started disappearing.
He stopped treating each movement like an emergency.

By the time they had gone most of the way around once, he could keep his feet under him without the barrier.

"See? You're getting the hang of it."

Taiga looked at him. Before he answered, he noticed that Leo was still holding his hands. His grip changed slightly whenever Taiga drifted to one side. His thumbs rested over Taiga's knuckles.
They were kinda warm. His grip was tight, but somehow gentle.

Why was he looking at that?

He looked up again. Leo was still talking, saying something about turning. Taiga had lost the first half of the sentence.

He looked back down and his stomach tightened a bit.
He suddenly became too aware of how long they'd been holding hands.

Taiga cleared his throat. "Right."

Leo stopped talking.

"What?"

Taiga loosened his grip.

"I think I got it. Let me go."

It came out faster than he intended.
Leo released him immediately.

"Okay."

He kept skating backward.
Taiga moved on his own. Push. Glide.
His balance held.

"See?" he said.

Leo smiled. "Yeah. Look at you."

Taiga raised one middle finger and almost lost his balance.
That made Leo laugh.

A few minutes later, Taiga could finally turn without having to think through every part of it.
Leo was still skating backward in front of him, correcting little things whenever he noticed them.

"Okay, try to push more with the left. You're favoring the right."

Taiga looked up at him with a frown. He was about to answer, then he saw another skater coming up behind Leo. A guy in a dark coat was moving quickly while looking sideways at the person beside him.

"Because if you do it that way—"

"Leo, stop! Behind you," Taiga said, pointing at the guy.

Leo looked over his shoulder and stopped immediately.
The guy swerved past without touching him.
Taiga, on the other hand, hadn't reached the part where he learned how to stop.

"SHIT—"

He went straight into Leo. 
Leo's arms came around him before either of them could fall. Taiga grabbed the front of his jacket.

"Woah."

They held onto each other while Leo tried to catch his balance again. After a couple of dramatic seconds, they settled.

As panic washed away, Taiga noticed his face was merely centimeters from Leo's. He could even see snowflakes melting in his hair.
For a moment, neither moved.
Taiga saw every detail. The color across his cheeks from the cold. The small crease between his eyebrows. His mouth, slightly open from the impact.
Their eyes locked for a split second, until Taiga realized he was staring.
He also became aware of Leo's arms still around him.
Heat hit his ears almost immediately.

What the fuck was he doing?

He blinked, then let go of his jacket.
Still breathing hard, he muttered, "Sorry..."

Leo let go and briefly shook his head. "I stopped too fast."

Taiga noticed he wasn't smiling. Actually, he was slightly red as well.

"No, you stopped. I didn't."

Leo finally let out a laugh. "Yeah, I forgot to teach you a very necessary maneuver."

"Yeah, how did you stop so fast?"

Leo showed him and slowly the awkwardness went away. They went back to skating. This time, Leo mostly stopped skating backward and stayed beside him. 
For the next half hour, Taiga was irritatingly aware of the distance between them.

Eventually, 'one more lap' actually became the last one.
They returned the skates and walked back toward the market.

***

They strolled around the market for a bit, until Leo saw a stand selling hot chocolate. He immediately went for one and Taiga followed.

They found an empty section of a bench near the edge of the market, where the crowd was thinner and the music from the fountain was mostly background noise.

Snow had thickened enough to collect along the roofs of the stalls.
Leo took a drink too early and immediately pulled the cup away.

"Fuck."

Taiga looked at him. "You watched them pour it."

"I was cold. And also slightly hopeful that thermodynamics stopped working."

"Wishful thinking."

"A bit."

Taiga shook his head and waited another minute before trying his own. 
For a while they watched people move between the stalls.
A family passed with two small kids in matching hats. A couple near the rink entrance were trying to take a picture without dropping either of their cups. Somebody farther down the path was playing guitar badly.

Then Leo's phone rang. He checked the screen and groaned.

"Ugh, again."

"What?"

"My mother."

He exhaled and answered.

"Hola, mami."

Taiga was taken aback. He knew Leo spoke Spanish, and he could imagine that he didn't speak English with his mother. Still, hearing it caught him off guard.

"Sí, mañana. Ya revisé."

Leo closed his eyes.

"Mami, ya te mandé el boleto."

The voice on the other end got louder for a moment.
Leo looked down at the yellow scarf.

"Sí, tengo la bufanda."

A pause.

"Sí, esa."

Taiga smiled into his hot chocolate.
Leo kicked lightly at the side of his shoe without interrupting the call.

"Sí, comí."

Another pause.
His expression softened.

"Yo también te quiero."

Taiga looked toward the rink again.

"Sí. Chao. Chao, mami."

A second passed.

"Mami."

The call finally ended.
He lowered the phone and sighed.

"And that's my mum."

"Still concerned for the flight?"

"Yeah."

Taiga smiled. Now that he heard Leo speaking his native language, the contrast with his English became slightly clearer. 

"How come I've never heard you speaking Spanish with Enzo?"

"We try not to when we're with others. Sometimes it's tempting, especially when I'm tired. But I don't really like excluding others."

"Fair enough. Did you go to a bilingual school or something?"

"Yeah. Both of us, English since we were kids."

"I figured. You basically talk like the rest of us."

Leo looked genuinely pleased.

"Seriously?"

"Yeah, you don't even have an accent."

"Oh, wait until you hear me talking early in the morning or when I'm tired. Enzo says that I lose about 20% of my vowels."

Taiga snorted. "You didn't sound different after Halloween."

"Pst. I was barely speaking after that. And I wasn't *that* tired."

"Yeah, sure."

"Thanks though. I'll still take it as a compliment."

"Take it as you want."

Leo looked toward the lights.

"So, what are you guys gonna do the whole week?"

Taiga shrugged. "Dunno. The usual I guess. Light treks, maybe help with something. Chill, mostly. Then there's New Year's Eve."

Leo nodded. "You said you stayed a whole year there before Olympus, right?"

"Pretty much."

"What were you actually doing all year?"

"I volunteered. Last year camp was pretty much broke. Somehow we managed to get a sponsor and we started fixing and expanding the place."

"How did you get a sponsor?"

"He was the father of one of our scout friends. Although, it was mainly Keitaro who convinced him."

"How...?"

"He had been writing a journal for the whole term. The guy, Clermont, saw it and decided it was worth publishing."

"Wait. Clermont? Like, are you talking about William Clermont?"

"Yeah. You know him?"

"No fucking way. I've read a couple of his books, they're dope."

"Well, he published a book from Keitaro's journal. That helped as well, since Keitaro devolved all his royalties to camp."

"That's insane. I mean, getting published by Clermont? And he donated everything to the camp?" 

"Yeah, he's pretty intense. It was a shame that I-"

He stopped. Nope, definitely not.

Taiga cleared his throat. "Anyway, long story short, I stayed with Yoichi after the term to help with the renovations."

"If you don't mind me asking, why did you stay?"

Taiga turned to look at the people on the rink.

"I-..." He exhaled through his nose. "Let's just say that last year's term I wasn't exactly in a good place mentally. I fucked up a thing or two and the camp helped me through it. I felt like I wanted to stay there and do something in return."

Snow landed on the edge of Leo's scarf and disappeared into the fabric.
Taiga watched it melt.

"So... why leave?"

"Because I realized that I wasn't staying there because I wanted to. Well, I actually wanted to, but mostly because of guilt. This summer, I saw everyone coming back after a whole year at college. They went on with their lives, while I was still stuck."

Taiga took a drink from his chocolate.

"Clermont overheard two of my scoutmasters talking about convincing me to go somewhere and suggested that I apply here."

He paused and smiled.

"Yoshinori wrote something like a nine-page reference letter. Clermont signed it as well. Took me something like eleven days and a threat from Keitaro to fill out the application form and send it."

"That's kind of dramatic."

"Fuck off."

Leo smiled. "Now I get it though, why you looked so out of place when I first saw you."

Taiga took another sip of chocolate. "Yeah. It sucked at first."

He thought about how he felt the first week. That seemed like a whole life ago.
He then noticed that Leo was looking at him, smiling.

"What?"

"I was considering that you may have outgrown the Angry Guy."

"Fuck you."

Leo laughed. Then the conversation moved somewhere else.

He talked about how loud his house was, because apparently multiple houses were merged together in a single building. Then he complained about his sister always embarrassing him in front of people. Taiga told him about some activities they did during the summer term, how Hiro once almost set himself on fire while cooking for Keitaro.

Leo had snorted at that. "That feels familiar."

"Yeah. You're both dangerous with open flames, apparently."

The conversation wandered for a while.

At some point Taiga checked his phone: it was after eight.

"Fuck."

Leo looked down at his own screen.

"Oh."

Leo finished the last of his hot chocolate. 

"'Kay, let's go back then."

They stood and went toward the station.

***

The return train was busier. They stood near the doors because most seats were taken and talked about nothing important for most of the trip back.

Campus was almost empty when they stepped out of Olympus Station.
Snow had settled over Central Park and along the roofs of the Residential Zone.

"So, when are you coming back here?" Leo asked at some point.

"Probably around the 3rd or 4th of January."

Leo nodded.

"You?"

"I was considering the 4th or the 5th, depending on the flights. But it may as well be the 3rd or the 6th."

"Right."

They arrived outside the dorm.

"Thanks for coming, by the way," Leo said. "I had fun today."

"You almost risked your life."

Leo giggled. "Worth it."

Taiga glanced at him. Leo looked away and fiddled with his fingers.

"I- I mean, still worth it. It was fun anyway."

"Yeah."

They went silent for a moment. For some reason, Taiga could feel the same uneasiness from the rink crawling back.

"Text me when you get to camp. I want to see pics."

"Tch. Sure."

"I'll make sure to remind you."

"And there you go."

Leo chuckled. He put his hands in his pockets.

"Then, I guess see you after the break?"

"Why was that a question?"

"I don't know."

"Dumbass. Yeah, see you."

Leo hesitated for a second, then turned toward his lift. Taiga watched him go until he disappeared behind the doors.

He went back to his room.

Again, he was immediately hit by the missing presence of Caleb. It still felt weird.
He took a shower, then went back to pack his bag.

When he was done, he looked around to check if he'd missed anything.
He was suddenly hit by the memory of when he first entered that room. 

It had looked more or less like this. 
Same walls, same furniture, no personal items.

And yet, for some reason, it felt more welcoming than before.
