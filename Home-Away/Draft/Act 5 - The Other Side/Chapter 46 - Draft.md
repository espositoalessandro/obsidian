On the way back, Leo fell asleep against the bus window with one hand still tangled in Taiga's. He woke when they stopped in Santo Domingo and insisted he'd only been resting his eyes. Taiga showed him the photograph he'd taken as evidence.

"Delete it."

"You said that about the hat."

"You still have the hat one?"

"Obviously."

Leo tried to reach across him for the phone, got his elbow caught on the armrest, and nearly knocked the bag of sweets off Taiga's lap. The woman in the seat ahead glanced back. They behaved until the bus stopped. They changed buses in Santo Domingo and made it home with the sweets intact.

It was quieter at the house than Taiga had expected. Mercedes was at her table in the shade, and Antonio's voice carried from somewhere inside Ramón and Isa's place. Leo went straight to his grandmother, kissed her cheek, and began telling her about the beach before either of them had put down a bag.

"*And you?*" Mercedes asked Taiga.

"*I survived his volleyball.*"

"*Was he that bad?*"

"*He was on the other team.*"

Mercedes nodded as though that answered the question. Leo complained, then dug into his bag for the sweets they'd brought. By the time Antonio came out, Mercedes had already chosen hers and was telling him that the rest belonged to the people who had actually gone on the trip.

"*There are some for you,*" Leo said.

Antonio inspected the packet. "*I want to see the pictures.*"

Leo showed him the one with the improvised goalposts. Antonio demanded to know why they'd played with a fruit instead of a ball, then asked who had won. Taiga and Leo gave him different answers. Antonio took Taiga's side, which made Leo ask whether anybody in the house remembered whose family he belonged to.

In the evening Carmen arrived with Manuel and wanted to hear what they'd eaten. Leo told her about the volleyball instead. Carmen let him get as far as Taiga's lucky shot before returning to the food. Taiga offered the name of the place where they'd had lunch on Sunday; Leo remembered what they'd ordered. Between them, they satisfied her.

The house filled up around them. Dani took a sweet and said it was too sweet. She took another on her way out. Ramón announced that he'd once played on the very same beach and that the other team had asked him to stop serving because nobody could return the ball. Isa asked whether this had happened before or after he pulled a muscle getting out of the water. Ramón said it was a different trip. Mercedes said it was a different person.

Leo laughed hard enough to lean against Taiga. A little later, when Carmen needed a bag taken to her kitchen, Leo picked it up before Manuel could reach it. He was still talking to Ramón as he carried it inside.

Upstairs, their room had kept the heat while they were away. Leo opened the window, then gave up and turned on the fan. Taiga laid out his damp clothes so they wouldn't stay damp in his suitcase.

"Flight," he said, sitting on the edge of the bed with his phone. "I told my mum I'd book it when we got back."

Leo sat beside him. They looked at the options together and settled on Friday. Taiga bought the ticket before he could start measuring the remaining days against it.

Leo's thumb rested against his knee. Taiga put down the phone.

The next afternoon, Leo turned on his game console and dropped into the chair at his desk.

"I'm finally finishing this," he said. "No interruptions."

"You say that every time you turn it on."

"Because every time, you interrupt me."

Taiga was on the sofa with his Spanish notebook. "I haven't said anything."

"You were about to."

Leo had just reached a screen full of flashing instructions when Isa called up from the courtyard. She wanted help getting a narrow table from her place into the storage room. Leo kept his hands on the controller for a moment. He pressed pause, went to the window, and asked how heavy it was.

Isa looked up at him. "*It's easier with two people. Ramón can do it when he gets home if you're busy.*"

"*No, I'll come down.*"

He put the controller on the desk.

"I'll be five minutes," he told Taiga.

"I'll start a clock."

Leo pointed at him and went downstairs.

Taiga finished half an exercise before curiosity sent him after Leo. The table was already in storage. Leo and Antonio were in the courtyard with Antonio's bicycle turned upside down between them. Its chain hung loose beside the rear wheel, and Leo was showing him how to lift it back onto the teeth.

"*Keep that wheel still.*"

"*I am.*"

The wheel spun. Leo looked at Antonio. Antonio looked at the wheel.

"*Try again.*"

Taiga sat on the step with his notebook. Leo got the chain on, turned the pedals, and heard something scrape. He stopped, adjusted it, and turned them again. Antonio wanted to try. Leo let him undo what they'd just fixed so he could learn to put it back.

Taiga completed the exercise, found three words he didn't trust, and went upstairs to check them. When he came down again, Antonio was riding across the courtyard in a circle that was much too small for the speed he'd chosen. Leo ran after him, calling for him to brake before the wall did it for him.

He caught the handlebars in time. Antonio protested that he had been about to turn. Leo turned the bicycle around and sent him off again, more slowly.

"Your five minutes are up," Taiga said.

"I know. We got distracted."

"You? Never."

Leo wiped a black streak of grease onto an already dirty rag. "I'm going back up."

But he stopped to straighten the tools Antonio had left on the ground. One of the bicycle's brakes caught when he pressed it, and he crouched to look. Taiga held out the rag. Leo took it, glanced at the house, and then at the brake again.

By the time he came upstairs, the light had shifted across the floor. He picked up the controller, found the game still waiting on the same screen, and laughed once.

"It was just a table," Taiga said.

"Antonio's chain was off."

"I saw."

Leo sat down and played. He got through the flashing instructions, lost almost immediately, and put the controller aside when someone called them to eat. He didn't say he'd finish later.

After dinner, Isa came into the courtyard carrying a glass and found Leo at the tap, trying to get the grease out from under his nails. Taiga was rinsing their plates beside him. Antonio had gone inside, and Ramón's voice came from their open window.

"*Did the table fit?*" Isa asked.

"*Yeah. There's room for the other one, too, if you want me to move it tomorrow.*"

"*I don't. It's staying where it is.*" She looked at his hands. "*You were out there with the bicycle for hours.*"

"*The chain kept slipping.*"

"*You fixed it.*"

"*Antonio did most of it. Eventually.*"

"*I thought you were going upstairs when we finished with the table.*"

Leo rubbed a thumbnail with the corner of the towel. "*So did I.*"

Isa set her glass down. "*You could have left the bicycle for Ramón. He bought the patch kit.*"

"*Antonio wanted to ride it today.*"

"*Then he could have asked his father when he got home.*"

Leo opened his mouth and closed it again. From the window, Ramón called something about knowing where the patch kit was. Isa didn't turn.

"*And you could have told me you didn't want to move the table,*" she said.

"*You needed help.*"

"*I needed another pair of hands for two minutes. I said Ramón could do it later.*"

"*He'd just got in from work by then.*"

"*He would have been fine.*"

Leo looked at the towel. "*I know.*"

Isa waited. Taiga stacked the rinsed plates and left them to drain.

"*I was going to play something,*" Leo said at last. "*Nothing important.*"

"*You'd already decided to do it.*"

"*It's a game, Tía.*"

"*Then why are you annoyed you missed it?*"

Leo stared at her. He looked over at Taiga, who stayed where he was. Leo let out a breath through his nose.

"*Because I wanted to.*"

Isa picked up her glass. "*Okay.*"

"*Okay?*"

"*I thought you wanted to help me. Next time tell me if you don't.*"

She went inside with her glass. Ramón immediately asked where she'd put something. Isa told him to look in the cupboard behind him.

Leo turned off the tap. His hands were clean enough by then, except for the line of grease beside one nail.

"I did want to help," he said to Taiga.

"I know."

"I wanted to play the game, too."

"I know that as well."

Leo flicked a little water at him. Taiga stepped out of range.

"You could have told me when I came back down."

"You were busy teaching Antonio how to take the chain off again."

"That was the useful part. Now he knows how to fix it."

"And does he know how to brake?"

"We're working on it."

The following morning, Carmen stopped in the courtyard on her way out with her bag over one shoulder and a list folded in her hand. Taiga and Leo were sitting near Mercedes with their coffee. Taiga had been telling Mercedes about a Spanish word Leo had insisted was useful and which she had never heard anyone say.

"*Where did you teach him that?*" she asked Leo.

"*At school.*"

"*Which school?*"

Carmen cut across Leo's answer. "Julio, *come with me to the shops. I need a few things for dinner.*"

Leo glanced at Taiga. Taiga was still trying to work out whether Leo had invented the word. Leo looked back at his mother.

"*No. I'm staying here today.*"

Carmen paused with the bag strap in her hand. "*Are you feeling all right?*"

"*I'm fine. I just don't want to go out.*"

She studied him for a second. "*All right. Do you need anything while I'm there?*"

"*More coffee,*" Mercedes said.

"*You have coffee.*"

"*Not enough, apparently.*"

Carmen looked at Taiga's empty cup, then at the pot on the table. "*I'll see what they have.*" She checked her list. "*Julio, tell your father I took the car.*"

"*Okay.*"

She was through the gate a moment later. Leo followed her with his eyes, waiting until the gate clicked shut.

Mercedes slid the coffee pot toward him. "*There's some left.*"

"*I don't want any more.*"

"*Then pass it to Taiga.*"

Leo did. Taiga poured himself another cup. The conversation about the word resumed because Mercedes still wanted to know which school Leo meant.

That afternoon, Leo finished the part of his game he'd been stuck on. He shouted loudly enough for Dani to call from the other side of the courtyard that she'd heard him lose often enough to know it wasn't a difficult game. When Carmen came back, he went to help bring in the shopping; she handed him two bags and told him not to crush the bread.

Taiga followed with the last bag. Carmen was putting food away when Leo stopped beside her.

"*I wanted to ask you something about camp.*"

"*The summer term?*"

"*After it. Taiga says we could stay there until it's time to go back to Olympus. If Yoshinori agrees.*"

Carmen set down the packet she was holding. "*How much longer would you be gone?*"

"*About six weeks.*"

"*And you'll have a place to sleep that whole time?*"

"*We'll ask Yoshinori. We haven't promised anything yet.*"

"*Then ask him. I want to know the dates once you have them.*" Carmen took the bag from Taiga. "*I'll miss you, Julio, but you can decide where to spend your summer.*"

Leo nodded. "*Okay. Thank you.*"

"*You still have to visit when you come home,*" she said, and sent both of them out so she could finish putting things away.

By Thursday, Taiga's clothes were coming off the sofa and returning to his suitcase. He packed his Spanish notebook last. Leo found it open on the bed and read out a sentence Taiga had crossed through three times.

"Stop. That one isn't done."

"I can tell. You called the bus a cabbage."

"You told me those sounded different."

"They do."

"To you."

Leo laughed and gave the notebook back. Taiga zipped his suitcase before Leo could find anything else in it.

They ate with the family that night. Carmen asked Leo how long they meant to stay at Camp Buddy after the summer term, then had him repeat the dates because he'd given her the month and skipped the day. She had already agreed to the plan. She wanted to know where they'd sleep, who would be there, and whether they intended to eat anything besides camp food. Taiga answered what he knew. Leo promised to send her the rest once Yoshinori confirmed it.

After dinner, Antonio challenged Taiga to a game with his repaired bicycle parked beside them as a goalpost. They played in the courtyard until the ball struck Manuel's chair and everybody agreed the match had ended. Antonio disagreed. Mercedes declared a draw without having watched enough of it to know the score.

When the others went inside, Taiga stayed to bring in the cups. Mercedes was still at the table, watching Leo try to persuade Antonio to put the ball away.

"*He's supposed to be in bed,*" she said.

"*He'll keep playing until Leo goes upstairs.*"

"*Leo knows.*"

Leo caught the ball, held it above Antonio's reach, and walked him toward Ramón and Isa's door. Antonio followed, complaining all the way.

"*I watched him wait for Carmen to call him back yesterday,*" Mercedes said.

Taiga followed her gaze. "*She didn't.*"

"*No. He finished his coffee instead.*"

Mercedes held out her cup. Taiga took it with the others.

"*Thank you, Doña Mercedes.*"

"*You're still calling me that?*"

"*It's your name.*"

"*Part of it.*"

Taiga looked down at the cups to avoid her face. "*I've practiced saying it.*"

"*You can learn another word.*"

"*I know the word.*"

"*Then use it.*"

He could hear Leo at the door, making one final deal with Antonio about the ball. Mercedes waited. Taiga had watched her wait for Leo to come to her on his birthday; she looked exactly as patient now.

"*Thank you, Abuela.*"

"*There.*" She stood and took the cup from his hand. "*That wasn't difficult.*"

"*It was worse than the cabbage.*"

She laughed. Leo came back across the courtyard in time to see her take the cups into her kitchen.

"What happened?"

"Your grandmother says I need to study harder."

"She says that to me, too."

Taiga reached for his hand. Leo took it and led him upstairs.

In the morning, Carmen inspected Taiga's suitcase after he closed it and added a small packet of food to the bag he was carrying onto the plane. Taiga protested that he'd be home before he needed a meal. Carmen said that airports had a way of changing plans and closed the bag herself. Dani told him he could leave the packet in the overhead compartment if it was something embarrassing. Manuel checked twice that Taiga had his passport, then had to go back inside for his keys.

Mercedes came out to the gate while they waited. She straightened Taiga's collar even though it was already straight.

"*Send a message when you're home,*" she told him.

"*I will, Abuela.*"

Leo turned so quickly that Taiga knew he'd heard. Mercedes looked delighted with herself.

"When did that happen?" Leo asked.

"Yesterday. You weren't paying attention."

"I was putting Antonio to bed."

"You were trying," Mercedes said, and kissed Taiga on the cheek.

Manuel returned, keys in hand. By the time they pulled away, Mercedes and Carmen were still at the gate and Antonio was waving the ball over his head from the courtyard.

Leo sat beside Taiga in the back of the car. Their shoulders knocked whenever Manuel took a turn too quickly. Most of the ride Leo talked to his father about the route, the weather, whether the clouds meant anything, and a place near the airport that had closed since the last time they'd passed. Taiga answered when they included him. When the airport signs appeared, Leo took his hand below the level of the window.

Manuel unloaded the suitcase at departures. He hugged Taiga and stepped away to let them say goodbye, though he stayed close enough to keep an eye on the time.

"Two weeks," Leo said.

"You'd better have your ticket by then."

"I do have my ticket. You watched me buy it."

"Show me again."

Leo pulled up the booking on his phone and held it in front of Taiga's face. Taiga moved the phone aside and kissed him. Leo let the phone fall against his leg and put an arm around him.

"You're really staying at my place first," Taiga said when they separated.

"For a whole night. Your family's going to love me."

"You've changed your mind since yesterday."

"No. I just want to sound confident when I say it."

Taiga kissed him again, shorter this time. Manuel made a show of looking at the departures board.

"Text me when you land," Leo said.

"Your mother already told me."

"She said when you get home. I want to know when you land."

"Bossy."

"I learned from her."

Taiga picked up his suitcase. He got halfway to the door before he looked back. Leo had stayed where he was, holding his phone loosely in one hand. He raised the other and waved. Taiga waved back, then went inside.
